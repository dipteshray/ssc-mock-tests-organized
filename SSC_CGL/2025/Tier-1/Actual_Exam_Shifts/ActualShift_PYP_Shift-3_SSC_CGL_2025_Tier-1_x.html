• Launched on 26th July 2013 by the Ariane-5 VA-214 rocket from Kourou, French Guiana, it is equipped with advanced weather monitoring instruments.

• The satellite is fitted with a 6-channel imager and a 19-channel sounder to provide detailed information on atmospheric conditions such as temperature, humidity, and cloud patterns.

• INSAT-3D enables accurate cyclone prediction, rainfall estimation, and fog detection, making it a cornerstone of India's meteorological services.

• The satellite operates in the geostationary orbit and provides continuous coverage over the Indian Ocean region, enhancing disaster preparedness and response systems.

• It was primarily designed for conducting experiments in space science and communication technology.

• Aryabhata was not equipped for weather forecasting and was focused on scientific research.

• Cartosat: The Cartosat series comprises remote sensing satellites designed for earth observation, especially for cartographic applications.

• It provides high-resolution imagery for urban planning, infrastructure development, and disaster management.

• While essential for geographical mapping, it does not perform weather forecasting tasks.

• Mangalyaan: Also known as the Mars Orbiter Mission (MOM), this satellite was launched on 5th November 2013 by ISRO.

• Mangalyaan focuses on exploring Mars and studying its atmosphere and surface, not weather forecasting.

• It was a landmark mission, making India the first Asian nation to reach Mars orbit and the first nation globally to achieve this feat in its maiden attempt.

• INSAT Series: The INSAT (Indian National Satellite System) series is a group of multipurpose geostationary satellites developed by ISRO.

• These satellites are employed for telecommunications, television broadcasting, meteorology, and disaster warning services.

• INSAT-3D and its successor, INSAT-3DR, are specifically focused on weather monitoring and climate studies.

• Key Features of INSAT-3D: It provides day and night imaging capabilities for weather monitoring.

• The 19-channel sounder helps in atmospheric profiling, providing data on temperature and humidity at different altitudes.

• This new legislation aims to modernize the criminal justice system in India by making it more aligned with contemporary societal needs, values, and constitutional principles.

• The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita Bill, along with two others (Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita Bill and Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam Bill), was introduced in August 2023 in the Indian Parliament.

• One of its key objectives is to eliminate colonial influences and ensure a more victim-centric and citizen-friendly approach to justice delivery.

• The bill incorporates provisions for speedy trials, stringent punishment for crimes against women and children, and promotes the use of technology in the judicial process.

• Indian Penal Code (IPC), 1860: The Indian Penal Code was enacted in 1860 and came into force in 1862 during British rule in India.

• It served as the primary criminal code in India, defining various crimes and their corresponding punishments.

• The IPC was drafted by the first Law Commission of India under the chairmanship of Lord Macaulay.

• Key Features of Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita: It abolishes outdated provisions that were irrelevant in the current legal context.

• It introduces stringent punishments for crimes such as mob lynching, sexual crimes, and crimes against children.

• The bill promotes the use of modern technology in investigations and trials, such as video recordings and electronic evidence.

• It also emphasizes the protection of the rights of victims and witnesses in the legal process.

• Other Related Bills: Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita: Replaces the Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC), 1973, focusing on procedural reforms to ensure speedier justice.

• Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam: Replaces the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, aiming to update laws related to evidence gathering and admissibility in court proceedings.

• Rationale Behind the New Laws: The purpose of these reforms is to remove the colonial legacy and align the laws with the democratic ethos of modern India.

• It seeks to reduce delays in the justice delivery system and ensure transparency and accountability.

• Need for Reform: The IPC was enacted in colonial times and contains outdated laws that are no longer relevant in modern India. The new laws aim to address these gaps.

• Victim-Centric Approach: The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita introduces provisions to make the justice system more focused on the rights and needs of victims of crimes.

• Use of Technology: The new law encourages the use of technology in judicial processes, such as electronic communication and digital evidence.

• Recent Example: The introduction of these new laws in 2023 marks a significant step toward legal modernization in India, replacing three key colonial-era laws.

• A high Revenue Deficit implies that the government needs to borrow or generate funds from other sources to meet its operational costs, such as salaries, pensions, and interest payments.

• Revenue Receipts include tax revenues (such as income tax, GST, etc.) and non-tax revenues (such as dividends, interest receipts, etc.).
