19. In which of the following case, law of demand fails?

A. Giffen goods

B. Normal goods

C. Inferior goods

D. Both Giffen and Inferior goods

Answer D

Solution

The Law of Demand fails in the case of inferior and Giffen goods. In the case of inferior goods, there exists an inverse relationship between the quantity demanded and the income of the consumer. For example, soap and shower gel. Now when a consumer's income is low he will buy soap but when his income increases he will go for shower gel because now he can afford it and for him now soap is an inferior good. Similarly, in the case of Giffen goods, there exists a direct relationship between quantity demanded and price rise.

20. Match the following: - 1. Oligopoly a) Large no. of sellers and buyers 2. Monopoly b) A small-large sellers and a large no. of buyers 3. Perfect competition c) One seller but large no. of buyers

A. 1-b, 2-c, 3-a

B. 1-c, 2-a, 3-b

C. 1-a, 2-b, 3-c

D. 1-b, 2-a, 3-c

Answer A

Solution

The solution describes three different market structures: oligopoly, perfect competition, and pure monopoly. An oligopoly occurs when a small number of large sellers dominate a market, often resulting in higher prices for consumers. Perfect competition is a market structure with a large number of buyers and sellers, leading to maximum competition. On the other hand, a pure monopoly occurs when there is only one seller in a market with no close substitutes, allowing the seller to charge whatever price they want. Understanding these different market structures is important for analyzing and regulating markets.

C. 17

D. 21

Answer C

Solution
