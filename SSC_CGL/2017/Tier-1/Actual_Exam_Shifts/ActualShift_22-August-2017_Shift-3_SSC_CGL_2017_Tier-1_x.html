55. If a person walks at 12 km/hr instead of 10 km/hr, he would have walked 1 km more in the same time. What is the actual distance (in kms) travelled by him at 10 km/hr in the same time?

A. 8

B. 10

C. 5

D. 12

Answer C

Solution

Shortcut Trick When time is constant, the distance travelled is directly proportional to the speed (D ∝ S). Ratio of speeds = 10 : 12 ⇒ 5 : 6. Ratio of distances = 5 : 6. Difference in distance = 6 − 5 = 1 unit. According to the question, 1 unit = 1 km, so actual distance = 5 units = 5 km. ∴ The correct answer is 5 km. Alternate Method Given: Actual speed = 10 km/hr New speed = 12 km/hr Difference in distance = 1 km Formula: Time = Distance ÷ Speed Let the actual distance travelled be D km. ⇒ Time taken at 10 km/hr = D ÷ 10 ⇒ Time taken at 12 km/hr = (D + 1) ÷ 12 ⇒ Since the time is the same in both cases: ⇒ D ÷ 10 = (D + 1) ÷ 12 ⇒ 12 × D = 10 × (D + 1) ⇒ 12D = 10D + 10 ⇒ 12D − 10D = 10 ⇒ 2D = 10 ⇒ D = 10 ÷ 2 = 5 km ∴ The correct answer is 5 km. Additional Information Relationship between Speed, Distance and Time Speed is defined as the rate at which an object covers distance. Formula: Speed = Distance ÷ Time. Direct Proportionality Case When Time is constant, Distance (D) is directly proportional to Speed (S). Ratio formula: D1/D2 = S1/S2. Inverse Proportionality Case When Distance is constant, Speed (S) is inversely proportional to Time (T). Ratio formula: S1/S2 = T2/T1. Average Speed Formula If a person covers the same distance at two different speeds x and y, the Average Speed = 2xy ÷ (x + y).∴ The correct answer is 5 km.
