26. Animals in which the cells are arranged in three embryonic layers are called ________.

A. Diploblastic

B. Ectoderm

C. Triploblastic

D. Endoderm

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Triploblastic. Layer Characteristics Triploblastic Diploblastic Ectoderm Endoderm

• It is in the Triploblastic form of animals that the embryo is arranged in a three-layered structure.

• These layers are ectoderm, mesoderm, and endoderm.

• Most multicellular animals are triploblastic except sponges and coelenterates.

• The term diploblastic refers to animals that have a body derived from only two embryonic cell layers, the ectoderm, and the endoderm.

• Examples of diploblastic animals are – jellyfish, corals, sea anemone, etc.

• The bodies of more complex animals are triploblastic, having developed from mesoderm as well as endoderm and ectoderm germ layers.

• The ectoderm is a germ layer( tissue layer), that forms in an animal embryo during development.

• The ectoderm is the germ layer that covers the outside of the embryo ('ecto' meaning outside).

• The ectoderm gives rise to a number of both internal and external structures.

• The endoderm gives rise to the epithelium of the pharynx, including the Eustachian tube, the tonsils, the thyroid gland, parathyroid glands, and thymus gland), the urinary bladder, the vagina (in females), and the urethra.
