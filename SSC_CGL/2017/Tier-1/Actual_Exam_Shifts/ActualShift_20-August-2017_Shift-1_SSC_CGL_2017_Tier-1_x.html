26. _________ is an example of intrusive igneous rock.

A. Conglomerate

B. Shale

C. Sandstone

D. Granite

Answer D

Solution

• Granite is an example of Intrusive Igneous Rocks.

• Granite is a common type of felsic intrusive igneous rock that is granular and phaneritic in texture.

• Granites can be predominantly white, pink, or grey in colour, depending on their mineralogy.

• When molten magma cools it forms Igneous Rocks.

• There are two types of Igneous Rocks. They are: Extrusive Igneous Rock.

• Intrusive Igneous Rock.

• Extrusive Igneous Rock: When Molten Lava reaches earth crust, it cools down rapidly and becomes solid and rocks formed in this process are called Extrusive Igneous Rock. Example: Basalt

• Intrusive Igneous Rock: When Molten Magam cools down deep in the earth without reaching the earth surface, these types of rocks are called Intrusive Igneous Rock. Example: Granite

• Granite is an igneous rock that forms when magma cools relatively slowly underground. It is usually composed primarily of the minerals quartz, feldspar, and mica. When granite is subjected to intense heat and pressure, it changes into a metamorphic rock called gneiss.
