1. Which of the following statements is INCORRECT regarding Fundamental Rights?

A. Fundamental Rights can be enforced by Law.

B. Fundamental rights are derived from the Britain Constitution.

C. The Fundamental Rights are mentioned in constitution from article 12 to 35.

D. Article 21 provides right to life.

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Fundamental rights are derived from the Britain Constitution.. Key Points Additional Information

• Fundamental Rights are enshrined in Part III of the Indian Constitution, from Article 12 to 35.

• These rights are essential for the overall development of individuals and the preservation of human dignity.

• Article 21 of the Indian Constitution guarantees the Right to Life and Personal Liberty.

• Fundamental Rights are justiciable, meaning they can be enforced through the courts of law in India.

• Fundamental Rights Fundamental Rights are the basic human rights guaranteed to all individuals by the Constitution of India. These rights are crucial for the protection and development of individuals.

• They are meant to promote the ideal of political democracy and prevent the arbitrary use of power by the state.

• Some of the key Fundamental Rights include the Right to Equality (Articles 14-18), Right to Freedom (Articles 19-22), Right against Exploitation (Articles 23-24), Right to Freedom of Religion (Articles 25-28), Cultural and Educational Rights (Articles 29-30), and the Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32).

• These rights are enforceable by the courts, and individuals can approach the judiciary to seek protection and enforcement of their Fundamental Rights.

• Enforcement of Fundamental Rights Fundamental Rights are justiciable, meaning individuals can approach the courts to enforce these rights if they are violated.

• The Supreme Court and High Courts have the power to issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights.

• Writs such as habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, quo warranto, and certiorari are used to enforce these rights.

• Right to Life (Article 21) Article 21 of the Indian Constitution guarantees the Right to Life and Personal Liberty.

• This article ensures that no person shall be deprived of their life or personal liberty except according to the procedure established by law.

• The Right to Life under Article 21 has been interpreted by the Supreme Court to include various rights such as the right to live with human dignity, right to livelihood, right to health, right to education, and more.

2. Under which scheme are collateral free loans of up to ₹10 Lakh extended by the Member Lending Institutions (MLIs) in India?

A. National Strategy for Financial Inclusion

B. Pradhan Mantri MUDRA Yojana

C. Stand Up India scheme

D. Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Pradhan Mantri MUDRA Yojana. Key Points Additional Information

• Pradhan Mantri MUDRA Yojana (PMMY) is a scheme launched by the government of India to provide loans up to ₹10 Lakh to non-corporate, non-farm small/micro enterprises.

• These loans are classified as MUDRA loans under PMMY and are given by commercial banks, RRBs, small finance banks, MFIs, and NBFCs.

• The scheme was launched on 8 April 2015 by Prime Minister Narendra Modi.

• MUDRA stands for Micro Units Development and Refinance Agency Limited, which provides funding support to the micro-enterprise sector.

• National Strategy for Financial Inclusion It is a strategy prepared by the Reserve Bank of India to provide access to formal financial services in an affordable manner.

• The strategy aims at promoting financial education, financial literacy, and consumer protection.

• It includes various stakeholders like banks, non-banking financial companies (NBFCs), micro-finance institutions (MFIs), and insurance companies.

• It emphasizes the need for a robust digital financial ecosystem to achieve universal financial access.

• Stand Up India scheme It was launched on 5 April 2016 by the Government of India.

• The scheme aims to promote entrepreneurship among Scheduled Castes/Scheduled Tribes and women by providing loans between ₹10 lakh and ₹1 crore.

• It ensures that each bank branch provides at least one loan to SC/ST and one to women borrowers.

• Stand Up India scheme provides handholding support and guidance to borrowers during the loan tenure.

• Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana Launched on 28 August 2014, it aims to provide universal access to banking facilities with at least one basic banking account for every household.

• The scheme promotes financial inclusion by ensuring access to financial services such as bank savings and deposit accounts, remittance, credit, insurance, and pension.

• It allows direct benefit transfer (DBT) to accounts opened under PMJDY, ensuring transparency and reducing leakages.

• The scheme also provides an overdraft facility of up to ₹10,000 to account holders.

3. Which of the following festivals observed in Assam is majorly celebrated by Vaishnavites?

A. Bihu

B. Doljatra

C. Ambuchi Mela

D. Rongker

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Doljatra. Key Points Additional Information

• Doljatra, also known as Dol Purnima or Dol Yatra, is a major festival celebrated by the Vaishnavites of Assam.

• It is observed on the full moon day in the month of Phalguna (February-March).

• Doljatra marks the arrival of spring and is a festival of colors, similar to Holi.

• During this festival, people worship Lord Krishna and celebrate his divine play (Leela) with Radha and the Gopis.

• Bihu Bihu is the most important festival of Assam, celebrated thrice a year as Rongali Bihu, Kongali Bihu, and Bhogali Bihu.

• It is primarily an agricultural festival associated with the changing seasons.

• Rongali Bihu is celebrated in April to mark the Assamese New Year and the onset of spring.

• Kongali Bihu is observed in October and is a solemn occasion with less merrymaking.

• Bhogali Bihu, celebrated in January, marks the end of the harvest season and is characterized by feasts and bonfires.

• Ambuchi Mela Ambuchi Mela is a significant festival held at the Kamakhya Temple in Assam.

• It is celebrated during the monsoon season in June and marks the annual menstruation of the goddess Kamakhya.

• Devotees from all over the country visit the temple to seek blessings during this time.

• The temple remains closed for three days, and on the fourth day, the doors are opened with great festivities.

• Rongker Rongker is a traditional festival of the Karbi tribe in Assam.

• It is celebrated to appease local deities and ensure a bountiful harvest.

• The festival involves elaborate rituals, prayers, and sacrifices to seek the blessings of the gods.

• Rongker is usually observed in the month of April or May.

4. Which of the following group of months is the coldest in North India?

A. February and March

B. September and October

C. December and January

D. October and November

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is December and January. Key Points Additional Information

• December and January are the peak winter months in North India.

• During these months, temperatures can drop significantly, often reaching sub-zero levels in hilly areas.

• These months typically experience the lowest average temperatures compared to other months.

• Cold waves and frost are common during December and January, making it the coldest period in the region.

• Winter Season in North India The winter season in North India typically starts in late November and lasts until February.

• December and January are considered the coldest months due to the high pressure areas that develop over the region, leading to cold waves.

• Temperature variations are significant, with some plains experiencing night temperatures close to freezing, while day temperatures remain relatively moderate.

• Snowfall is common in the Himalayan regions, attracting tourists to hill stations like Shimla, Manali, and Gulmarg.

• Climate Influences The climate of North India is influenced by the Himalayas and the Thar Desert. The Himalayas act as a barrier to the cold winds from Central Asia, while the Thar Desert brings dry and cold air.

• Western disturbances, which are weather phenomena originating from the Mediterranean region, also bring cold air and precipitation to North India during the winter months.

• Impact on Daily Life The cold weather affects daily life, especially for those who do not have adequate heating facilities. It can lead to health issues such as hypothermia and respiratory problems.

• Agricultural activities are also impacted as frost can damage crops, leading to economic losses for farmers.

5. Some of the talas invented by _________ include Trimukhi, Panchamukhi, Saptamukhi and Navamukhi.

B. Hariprasad Chaurasia

C. Yashraj

D. Bhimasen Joshi

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is M. Balamuralikrishna. Key Points Additional Information

• M. Balamuralikrishna was an Indian Carnatic vocalist, musician, multi-instrumentalist, playback singer, composer, and character actor.

• He was known for his deep knowledge of both the theoretical and practical aspects of Carnatic music.

• He invented new ragas as well as talas, including the ones mentioned: Trimukhi, Panchamukhi, Saptamukhi, and Navamukhi.

• His contributions to Carnatic music earned him several prestigious awards, including the Padma Vibhushan and the Sangeet Natak Akademi Award.

• It is one of the two main subgenres of Indian classical music that evolved from ancient Hindu traditions, the other subgenre being Hindustani music.

• The main emphasis in Carnatic music is on vocal music; most compositions are written to be sung, and even when played on instruments, they are meant to be performed in a singing style (known as gayaki).

• Tala Tala, literally meaning "clap," is the term used in Indian classical music to refer to musical meter, that is, any rhythmic beat or strike that measures musical time.

• It is a series of beats that are cyclically repeated.

• Talas can have different numbers of beats, and the cycle of beats is divided into sections called "angas," which can be of varying lengths.

• Instruments Played by M. Balamuralikrishna Besides being a renowned vocalist, M. Balamuralikrishna was proficient in playing the violin, viola, mridangam, and kanjira.

• His multifaceted talent in playing various instruments added to his versatility and depth as a musician.

6. In which year was the temple entry movement started by Bhimrao Ambedkar?

A. 1935

B. 1918

C. 1927

D. 1922

Solution

The correct answer is 1927. Key Points Additional Information

• The temple entry movement was started by Dr. Bhimrao Ambedkar in the year 1927.

• This movement was aimed at allowing untouchables or Dalits to enter Hindu temples.

• Dr. Ambedkar led several Dalits to enter the Kalaram Temple in Nashik, Maharashtra, which was significant for its religious importance.

• The movement was part of a broader effort by Dr. Ambedkar to fight against the caste system and for the rights of Dalits.

• Dr. Bhimrao Ambedkar Dr. B.R. Ambedkar was an Indian jurist, economist, politician, and social reformer who inspired the Dalit Buddhist movement and campaigned against social discrimination towards the untouchables (Dalits).

• He was the principal architect of the Constitution of India and served as the first Law and Justice Minister of independent India.

• Ambedkar’s efforts to eradicate social evils like untouchability and caste restrictions were profound and were recognized by the nation, which posthumously awarded him the Bharat Ratna, India’s highest civilian award.

• Temple Entry Movement The Temple Entry Movement was part of the broader Dalit movement in India aimed at securing equal rights for untouchables.

• It was a social movement aimed at ensuring that Dalits had the right to enter temples and participate in religious activities, which were traditionally denied to them due to caste-based discrimination.

• The movement was significant in highlighting the issues faced by Dalits and in pushing for social reforms.

• Kalaram Temple Satyagraha The Kalaram Temple Satyagraha was a landmark protest led by Dr. Ambedkar in 1930, where he and his followers attempted to enter the Kalaram Temple in Nashik.

• The protest was aimed at challenging the traditional prohibitions that barred Dalits from entering temples.

• Though the attempt faced significant resistance, it marked a crucial step in the fight against caste-based discrimination and temple entry restrictions.

• Impact of the Movement The Temple Entry Movement significantly contributed to the social reforms in India, paving the way for the eventual abolition of untouchability.

• It also helped in raising awareness about the plight of Dalits and inspired future generations to continue the fight for equality and justice.

• The movement laid the groundwork for legal and social changes, including the enactment of laws that prohibited untouchability and discrimination.

• This ocean is unique because it is defined by a current, the Antarctic Circumpolar Current, which flows from west to east around Antarctica.

• The office of the CAG is a constitutional body established under Article 148 of the Constitution of India.

• The CAG audits all receipts and expenditure of the Government of India and the state governments, including those of bodies and authorities substantially financed by the government.

• The CAG ensures that the financial administration of the country is carried out in accordance with the Constitution and the laws made by the Parliament and State Legislatures.

• It states that the executive power shall be exercised by the Governor either directly or through officers subordinate to him in accordance with the Constitution.

• Spirogyra is a genus of filamentous green algae that is commonly found in fresh water.

• The gametes in Spirogyra are isogamous, meaning they are morphologically similar or identical.

• Isogamous gametes in Spirogyra are non-flagellated, meaning they do not have flagella and thus do not have the ability to move independently.

• During reproduction, Spirogyra undergoes a process called conjugation, where gametes from different filaments join together to form a zygote.

• Isogamy: Isogamy refers to a type of sexual reproduction involving gametes of similar morphology (shape and size), differing only in their allelic composition.

• In isogamous species, the gametes are often referred to as "+" and "-" types rather than male and female.

• Flagella: Flagella are long, whip-like appendages that protrude from the cell body and enable many protozoa, bacteria, and spermatozoa to swim.

• Conjugation in Spirogyra: Conjugation in Spirogyra is a form of sexual reproduction where two filaments align side by side and form conjugation tubes.

• Zygospores are thick-walled and can survive harsh conditions. Upon germination, they undergo meiosis to produce new haploid filaments.
