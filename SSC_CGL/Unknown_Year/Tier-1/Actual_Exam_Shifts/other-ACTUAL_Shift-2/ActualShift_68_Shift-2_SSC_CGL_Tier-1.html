1. _____ is the provision of small credit to those who would have no other access to financial services.

A. Microloan

B. Microfinance

C. Homeloan

D. Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Microfinance. Key Points Additional Information

• Microfinance refers to the provision of financial services to low-income individuals or those who do not have access to typical banking services.

• It includes a range of services such as microloans, savings, insurance, and money transfers.

• The primary aim of microfinance is to provide small loans to impoverished individuals to help them start or expand small businesses, thereby reducing poverty.

• Microfinance institutions (MFIs) play a critical role in providing these financial services, especially in developing countries.

• Microloan Microloans are small loans given to individuals who typically do not have access to traditional banking services.

• These loans are often used to start or grow small businesses and are a subset of microfinance services.

• Microloans can range from a few dollars to several thousand dollars, depending on the borrower's needs and the lender's policies.

• The concept of microloans was popularized by Nobel Laureate Muhammad Yunus and the Grameen Bank in Bangladesh.

• Homeloan A home loan, or mortgage, is a loan given by a bank or financial institution to individuals to purchase a home or property.

• Home loans are typically long-term loans with a repayment period ranging from 10 to 30 years.

• They require collateral, usually the property being purchased, to secure the loan.

• Interest rates on home loans can be fixed or variable, depending on the lender's policies and the borrower's agreement.

• Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana (PMAY) PMAY is an initiative by the Government of India aimed at providing affordable housing to the urban poor.

• The scheme was launched on June 25, 2015, with the goal of constructing 20 million affordable houses by March 31, 2022.

• It has two components: PMAY-U (Urban) for the urban poor and PMAY-G (Gramin) for the rural poor.

• Beneficiaries under this scheme are identified through the Socio-Economic and Caste Census (SECC) of 2011.

2. The variation between a high air temperature and a low temperature that occurs during the same day is called ______ temperature.

A. diurnal

B. annual

C. wet bulb

D. durian

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is diurnal. Key Points Additional Information

• Diurnal temperature variation refers to the fluctuation between the highest and lowest temperatures that occur within a single day.

• Such variations are common in many parts of the world and are influenced by factors such as altitude, latitude, and local weather patterns.

• Diurnal temperature changes are more pronounced in desert regions due to the lack of moisture which prevents the air from retaining heat.

• Understanding diurnal temperature variation is important for agriculture, meteorology, and various environmental studies.

• Annual Temperature Variation: This refers to the changes in temperature that occur over the course of a year.

• It is influenced by factors such as seasonal changes, earth's tilt, and orbit around the sun.

• Annual variations are more significant in temperate regions compared to tropical regions.

• Wet Bulb Temperature: It measures the lowest temperature that can be reached by evaporating water into the air.

• It is important in understanding the effects of heat and humidity on humans and is used in calculating heat index.

• Durian: Durian is a large, spiky fruit known for its strong odor.

• It is unrelated to temperature variations and is often called the "king of fruits" in Southeast Asia.

5. Which of the following is the best definition of ecological efficiency?

A. The amount of energy utilised at different trophic levels in a food chain

B. The amount of energy stored at different trophic levels in a food chain

C. The ratio between the mass and the energy flow at different trophic levels in a food chain

D. The ratio between energy flows at different points in a food chain

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is The ratio between energy flows at different points in a food chain. Key Points Additional Information

• Ecological efficiency refers to the efficiency with which energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next in a food chain.

• Typically, only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level is passed on to the next level; this is known as the 10% law.

• This concept helps in understanding the energy dynamics and the structure of ecosystems.

• Higher trophic levels receive less energy, which explains why there are typically fewer organisms at higher levels in a food chain.

• Trophic Levels In ecology, a trophic level is the position that an organism occupies in a food chain.

• Primary producers (plants) are at the first trophic level, primary consumers (herbivores) at the second, secondary consumers (carnivores that eat herbivores) at the third, and so on.

• Energy Flow Energy flow in an ecosystem is one-way: from the sun to producers, then to consumers, and finally to decomposers.

• Energy is lost at each trophic level, primarily as heat resulting from metabolism.

• 10% Law The 10% law states that only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level is transferred to the next trophic level.

• This rule was first introduced by Raymond Lindeman in 1942.

• Food Chain A food chain is a linear sequence of organisms through which nutrients and energy pass as one organism eats another.

• In a food chain, each organism occupies a different trophic level.

6. Articles 245 to 255 in Part XI of the Indian Constitution deal with the __________.

A. emergency provisions

B. financial relations between the Centre and the states

C. legislative relations between the Centre and the states

D. tribunals

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is legislative relations between the Centre and the states. Key Points Additional Information

• Articles 245 to 255 in Part XI of the Indian Constitution deal with the legislative relations between the Centre and the states.

• These provisions outline the distribution of legislative powers between the Union and the State legislatures.

• Article 245 states the extent of laws made by Parliament and by the Legislatures of States.

• Article 246 deals with the subject-matter of laws made by Parliament and by the Legislatures of States, emphasizing the three lists: Union List, State List, and Concurrent List.

• Emergency Provisions (Articles 352 to 360) These articles cover the proclamation of emergency by the President of India.

• They include National Emergency (Article 352), President's Rule (Article 356), and Financial Emergency (Article 360).

• Financial Relations (Articles 268 to 293) These articles outline the financial relations between the Centre and the States.

• They cover topics such as distribution of revenues, grants-in-aid, and borrowing powers of the Centre and States.

• Tribunals (Articles 323A to 323B) These articles provide for the establishment of administrative tribunals for the adjudication or trial of disputes and offenses.

• Article 323A deals with administrative tribunals, while Article 323B deals with tribunals for other matters.

7. In which group of states does the Luni River flow?

Solution

The correct answer is Rajasthan and Gujarat. Key Points Additional Information

• The Luni River is one of the major rivers in the western part of India, specifically in the states of Rajasthan and Gujarat.

• The river originates in the Pushkar Valley of the Aravalli Range near Ajmer, Rajasthan.

• The Luni River flows southwest and ultimately discharges into the Rann of Kutch in Gujarat.

• The river is known for its saline and brackish water, especially in its lower reaches.

• Geographical Origin The Luni River originates from the Naga hills of the Aravalli Range, near Ajmer in Rajasthan.

• The river is formed by the confluence of two streams, the Saraswati and the Sagarmati.

• Course and Tributaries The river flows in a south-westerly direction through the Thar Desert, covering a distance of about 495 kilometers.

• It receives several tributaries such as the Jojari, Guhiya, and Bandi rivers.

• Salinity and Endorheic Basin The Luni River is known for its high salinity, particularly in its lower reaches due to the high evaporation rate and low rainfall in the region.

• It is an endorheic river, meaning it does not drain into an ocean but ends in an inland basin, specifically the Rann of Kutch.

• Economic Importance The river's water is extensively used for irrigation in the arid and semi-arid regions of Rajasthan and Gujarat.

• Despite its saline nature, it supports a variety of agricultural activities and local ecosystems.

9. For a chemical reaction with rise in temperature by 10°, the rate constant becomes nearly ________________.

A. Double

B. Triple

C. one-fourth

D. half

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Double. Key Points Additional Information

• For many chemical reactions, the rate constant approximately doubles with a 10°C increase in temperature.

• This phenomenon is often referred to as the "rule of thumb" in chemical kinetics.

• The temperature dependence of the rate constant is described by the Arrhenius equation.

• According to the Arrhenius equation, the rate constant (k) is related to temperature (T) through the exponential factor involving activation energy (Ea) and the gas constant (R).

• Arrhenius Equation: The Arrhenius equation is k = A * exp(-Ea/(RT)), where k is the rate constant, A is the pre-exponential factor (frequency of collisions), Ea is the activation energy, R is the gas constant, and T is the temperature in Kelvin.

• This equation shows the relationship between the rate constant and temperature, indicating that as temperature increases, the rate constant increases exponentially.

• Activation Energy (Ea): Activation energy is the minimum energy that reacting molecules must possess to undergo a chemical reaction.

• Higher activation energy means that fewer molecules have sufficient energy to react at a given temperature.

• Gas Constant (R): The gas constant (R) is a physical constant that appears in many fundamental equations in physical sciences, such as the ideal gas law and the Arrhenius equation.

• The value of R is 8.314 J/(mol·K).

• Rule of Thumb in Chemical Kinetics: The "rule of thumb" in chemical kinetics suggests that for many reactions, the rate constant approximately doubles for every 10°C rise in temperature.

• This empirical rule helps chemists estimate the effect of temperature changes on reaction rates without detailed calculations.

13. Which of the following is an example of prokaryotic cells?

A. Plasmodium

B. Leishmania

C. Yeast

D. Bacteria

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is Bacteria. Key Points Additional Information

• Bacteria are single-celled organisms that lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles, making them prokaryotic.

• Bacteria are among the most ancient and diverse forms of life on Earth, thriving in various environments.

• Bacteria can be classified into various shapes such as cocci (spherical), bacilli (rod-shaped), and spirilla (spiral-shaped).

• Bacteria play crucial roles in processes like nutrient cycling, fermentation, and as part of the microbiome in various organisms.

• Prokaryotic Cells Prokaryotic cells are primitive cells that lack a well-defined nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.

• They have a simple cell structure with genetic material (DNA) present in a nucleoid region.

• Examples include Bacteria and Archaea.

• These cells are more complex and are found in organisms such as animals, plants, fungi, and protists.

• Examples include Plasmodium (a protist), Leishmania (a protist), and Yeast (a fungus).

• It causes diseases known as leishmaniasis in humans.

• Yeast Yeast is a type of fungus that is used in baking, brewing, and scientific research.

• It is a eukaryotic microorganism with a well-defined nucleus and other organelles.

14. As per Mendeleev's prediction, atomic mass of eka-aluminium was:

A. 100

B. 44

C. 72

D. 68

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is 68. Key Points Additional Information

• Dmitri Mendeleev, a Russian chemist, predicted the existence of elements that were not yet discovered in his time.

• He named these elements using the prefix "eka-", which means "one beyond" in Sanskrit, based on their position in the periodic table.

• Eka-aluminium was predicted to be the element below aluminium in the periodic table.

• Mendeleev estimated the atomic mass of eka-aluminium to be around 68, which later turned out to be very close to the actual atomic mass of gallium (69.723).

• Mendeleev's Periodic Table In 1869, Dmitri Mendeleev created the first version of the periodic table, which arranged elements by increasing atomic mass.

• This arrangement revealed recurring (periodic) properties of the elements, which led to the periodic law.

• Mendeleev's table had gaps for elements that were not yet discovered, and he used the properties of neighboring elements to predict the properties of these unknown elements.

• Eka-Elements To predict the properties of undiscovered elements, Mendeleev used the term "eka" followed by the name of the element in the same group but in the previous period (e.g., eka-aluminium).

• Eka-aluminium was later discovered and named gallium.

• Other predicted elements included eka-boron (scandium) and eka-silicon (germanium).

• Gallium Gallium was discovered by French chemist Paul-Émile Lecoq de Boisbaudran in 1875.

• It has an atomic number of 31 and an atomic mass of 69.723.

• Gallium has a melting point of 29.76 °C (85.57 °F), which is low enough to melt in the hand.

• It is used in electronics, particularly in semiconductors and LEDs.

• The period is generally considered to span from 1947, the year the Truman Doctrine was announced, to 1991, the year the Soviet Union collapsed.

• It was marked by a rivalry between the two superpowers, which did not result in direct large-scale fighting between the two, but involved proxy wars, the arms race, and ideological competition.

20. In chess, which of the following may only move forward to the unoccupied square immediately in front of it?

B. The Queen

C. The Pawn

D. The Rook

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is The Pawn. Key Points Additional Information

• A pawn can only move forward to the unoccupied square immediately in front of it on the same file.

• On its first move, a pawn has the option to move forward two squares.

• Pawns capture diagonally, one square forward and to the left or right.

• If a pawn reaches the opposite end of the board, it can be promoted to any other piece, typically a queen.

• Chess Pieces and Their Movements: The Bishop: The bishop moves diagonally any number of squares. Each bishop starts on one color (light or dark) and must always stay on that color.

• The Queen: The queen can move any number of squares along a rank, file, or diagonal. It is the most powerful piece in terms of mobility.

• The Rook: The rook can move any number of squares along a rank or file. Rooks also play a crucial role in the endgame due to their ability to control large portions of the board.

• The Knight: The knight moves in an "L" shape: two squares in one direction and then one square perpendicular. Knights are unique because they can "jump" over other pieces.

• The King: The king moves one square in any direction. The objective of the game is to checkmate the opponent's king.

• Special Pawn Moves: En Passant: This special pawn capture can occur if a pawn moves two squares forward from its starting position and lands beside an opponent's pawn. The opponent's pawn can capture it "en passant" (in passing), as if it had only moved one square.Pawn Promotion: When a pawn reaches the opposite end of the board, it can be promoted to any other piece, except a king, typically a queen.
