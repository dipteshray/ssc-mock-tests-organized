4. Consider the following statements about Judo: 1) The red-and-white belt represents senior practitioners from Sixth to Eighth Dan. 2) A minimum shared buffer zone of 1 meter is required between adjacent contest areas. Which of the above statements is/are correct?

A. Only 1

B. Only 2

C. Both 1 and 2

D. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Only 1. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Statement 1: The red-and-white belt represents senior practitioners from Sixth to Eighth Dan. In Judo, the belt system is used to signify the rank or skill level of practitioners. The red-and-white belt is specifically reserved for senior practitioners who have achieved the ranks between Sixth Dan and Eighth Dan.

• This belt is a recognition of their advanced skill level, experience, and contribution to the art of Judo.

• It is important to note that the belt system in Judo is internationally recognized and adheres to guidelines established by the governing bodies such as the International Judo Federation (IJF).

• Hence, statement 1 is correct.

• Statement 2: A minimum shared buffer zone of 1 meter is required between adjacent contest areas. In Judo competitions, contest areas are carefully regulated to ensure the safety of participants. While buffer zones are indeed required between adjacent contest areas, the minimum shared buffer zone is generally larger than 1 meter, as specified by official regulations.

• The International Judo Federation (IJF) mandates that the buffer zone between competition areas should be wide enough to prevent any interference or accidents between adjacent contests.

• Thus, the information stating a shared buffer zone of 1 meter is incorrect as it does not align with standard guidelines.

• Hence, statement 2 is incorrect.

• Belt System in Judo: The ranking system in Judo is divided into Kyu (beginner ranks) and Dan (advanced ranks).

• The Dan ranks begin from First Dan (Shodan) and go up to Tenth Dan (Judan).

• Practitioners who achieve ranks from Sixth Dan to Eighth Dan are awarded the red-and-white belt, symbolizing their seniority and mastery in Judo.

• The red belt is reserved for Ninth and Tenth Dan, signifying the pinnacle of achievement in Judo.

• Contest Area Regulations in Judo: The contest area in Judo is square-shaped, typically measuring 8 meters by 8 meters.

• A safety zone surrounds the contest area, extending at least 3 meters from the edge to ensure the safety of competitors.

• Between adjacent contest areas, a buffer zone is required to avoid interference between matches. This buffer zone is generally larger than 1 meter as per official guidelines.

• These regulations are established by the International Judo Federation (IJF) and must be adhered to during competitions.

• History of Judo: Judo was founded by Jigoro Kano in 1882 in Japan.

• It is a modern martial art that focuses on throws, holds, and submissions rather than strikes.

• The word "Judo" translates to "gentle way", emphasizing the principle of using an opponent's force against them.

• International Recognition: Judo is governed globally by the International Judo Federation (IJF), which sets the rules and regulations for competitions.

• It became an Olympic sport in 1964 during the Tokyo Olympics and has since gained immense popularity worldwide.

• Judo emphasizes discipline, respect, and self-improvement, making it not just a sport but also a way of life for practitioners.

Solution

The correct answer is Option 2. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Stalagmite: Stalagmites are formed when mineral-rich water drips onto the cave floor and deposits minerals over time. These structures grow upward from the ground due to the continuous deposition process. This matches the process described in Option 2. Hence, Option 2 is correct.

• Pillar: A pillar is formed when a stalactite and a stalagmite grow and eventually meet, creating a vertical column. It is not a structure that hangs from the roof as described in Option 3. Hence, Option 3 is incorrect.

• Stalactite: Stalactites are formed by mineral deposits from dripping water, not by upward mineral movement as stated in Option 4. Hence, Option 4 is incorrect.

• Pillars: A combination of stalactites and stalagmites that meet over time to form a single vertical column. This can take thousands of years to develop.

• Scientific Studies: Geologists and speleologists study cave formations to understand climate history, erosion patterns, and the chemical processes involved.

11. What is the main function of the Golgi apparatus in a cell?

A. Energy generation

B. Protein modification

C. Fat formation

D. Replication of genetic material

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Protein modification. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• The Golgi apparatus is a cellular organelle found in eukaryotic cells. Its primary function is the modification, sorting, and packaging of proteins for secretion or use within the cell.

• Proteins produced in the rough endoplasmic reticulum (ER) are transported to the Golgi apparatus in vesicles. The Golgi modifies these proteins by adding carbohydrate chains, phosphates, or sulfates.

• The modified proteins are sorted based on their destination, such as for secretion outside the cell, incorporation into the plasma membrane, or use within lysosomes. Hence, protein modification is the primary function of the Golgi apparatus.

• Additionally, the Golgi apparatus plays a role in the synthesis of certain lipids and polysaccharides that are crucial for the cell's structure and function.

• The Golgi is a dynamic structure made up of flattened membrane-bound sacs called cisternae. It receives proteins on the cis-face (near the ER) and releases modified proteins on the trans-face (away from the ER). Hence, statement regarding protein modification is correct.

• While energy generation, fat formation, and replication of genetic material are important cellular processes, they are primarily carried out by other organelles such as mitochondria, smooth ER, and nucleus respectively. Hence, the statements regarding these are incorrect.

• Golgi Apparatus: Discovered by Camillo Golgi in 1898, the Golgi apparatus is also known as the Golgi body or Golgi complex.

• It is present in most eukaryotic cells, except some specialized cells like mature red blood cells in humans.

• Structure of Golgi Apparatus: The Golgi apparatus is composed of stacks of flattened, membrane-bound sacs called cisternae.

• These cisternae are divided into three regions: cis-Golgi (near ER), medial-Golgi, and trans-Golgi (away from ER).

• Functions of Golgi Apparatus: Protein Processing: It modifies proteins by glycosylation (adding carbohydrate chains), phosphorylation, and sulfation.

• Sorting and Packaging: Proteins are sorted based on their final destination and packed into vesicles for transport.

• Lipid Synthesis: The Golgi synthesizes certain lipids, such as glycolipids.

• Polysaccharide Synthesis: It produces polysaccharides like pectin and hemicellulose for the cell wall in plants.

• Other Organelles and Their Functions: Mitochondria: Known as the "powerhouse of the cell," mitochondria generate energy in the form of ATP through cellular respiration.

• Smooth Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER): Involved in lipid synthesis and detoxification processes.

• Nucleus: Contains genetic material (DNA) and is responsible for the replication of genetic material and regulation of cellular activities.

• Relevance of Protein Modification: Medical Applications: Understanding protein modification is crucial for the development of targeted therapies and vaccines.

• Industrial Applications: Modified proteins are used in biotechnology for enzyme production, drug development, and more.

14. Under Article 279A, decisions of the GST Council require what level of majority of members’ weighted votes?

A. Simple majority

B. Three-fourths majority

C. Two-thirds majority

D. Half majority

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Three-fourths majority. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Article 279A of the Constitution of India governs the working of the Goods and Services Tax (GST) Council, which is responsible for making recommendations on GST-related policies and laws.

• The GST Council is a constitutional body established by the 101st Amendment Act, 2016, to ensure a uniform taxation system across India.

• Under Article 279A(9), decisions of the GST Council require a three-fourths majority of the weighted votes of its members to pass. This ensures that major decisions reflect a broad consensus among the members.

• The weighted voting system assigns one-third of the total votes to the Central Government and two-thirds to the State Governments, collectively. This ensures balanced representation of both central and state interests.

• The three-fourths majority requirement prevents unilateral decision-making and ensures that GST-related policies are made collaboratively between the Centre and States.

• This provision promotes a federal structure and encourages cooperation among various governments for effective implementation of GST. Hence, Option 2 is correct.

• Composition of GST Council: Chairperson: The Union Finance Minister serves as the Chairperson of the GST Council.

• Members: The Union Minister of State for Finance and Ministers in charge of Finance or Taxation from all the states are members of the council.

• Voting Mechanism in GST Council: The Central Government holds one-third of the weighted votes, while all the State Governments collectively hold two-thirds of the weighted votes.

• Any decision requires 75% of the weighted votes to be passed, ensuring a collaborative decision-making process.

• Functions of GST Council: The Council makes recommendations on the rates of GST, exemptions, thresholds, and special provisions for certain states.

• It also resolves disputes related to the implementation of GST laws and ensures uniformity in GST-related policies across India.

• Significance of the Three-fourths Majority: The requirement of a three-fourths majority ensures that no single entity (either Centre or States) can dominate the decision-making process, promoting a spirit of cooperative federalism.

• This provision also safeguards the interests of smaller states, ensuring their voices are heard in GST-related decisions.

• Historical Background: The GST Council was established as part of the 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016, which introduced a uniform tax system by subsuming multiple indirect taxes.

• GST was implemented on 1st July 2017, marking a significant reform in India’s tax structure.

• NISAR (NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar) is a joint mission developed by NASA and ISRO. It is designed to observe and monitor changes in the Earth's surface.

• The satellite is set to operate in a Polar Sun-Synchronous Orbit, which ensures it passes over the same areas at the same local solar time. This enables consistent lighting conditions, which is important for radar imaging and observations.

• Polar Sun-Synchronous Orbit is ideal for Earth observation satellites as it provides global coverage and allows the satellite to monitor changes consistently over time.

• This orbit is typically at an altitude of around 600–800 km, and satellites in this orbit travel near the poles, covering the entire Earth’s surface as the planet rotates. Hence, this orbit is chosen for NISAR.

• The radar systems in NISAR will use this orbit to collect highly detailed data about ecosystems, ice-sheet dynamics, biomass, and natural disasters such as earthquakes, tsunamis, and landslides. Hence, the correct orbit is Polar Sun-Synchronous Orbit.

• NISAR Mission Overview: NISAR is a collaborative Earth observation mission between NASA and ISRO. The satellite is equipped with advanced radar imaging systems.

• Its primary focus includes monitoring earthquakes, tsunamis, glaciers, forest biomass, and climate change. It aims to provide high-resolution images and data to enhance our understanding of Earth’s processes.

• Features: NISAR will carry L-band and S-band radar systems to capture data with unprecedented accuracy and wide coverage.

• Polar Sun-Synchronous Orbit: Satellites in this orbit pass near the North and South Poles and cover the entire surface of Earth over time.

• It ensures consistent lighting conditions, which is beneficial for imaging and observations.

• Examples: Many Earth observation satellites, including Sentinel-1 and Landsat, operate in Polar Sun-Synchronous Orbit.

• Other Orbit Types: Geostationary Orbit (GEO): Satellites remain fixed relative to a point on Earth, ideal for communications and weather monitoring.

• This dissolution can also occur if the legislative assembly fails to elect a leader or if there is a political deadlock. Hence, Statement 1 is correct.
