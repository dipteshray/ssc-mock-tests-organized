1. During the Nuakhai festival of Sambalpur in Odisha, the first offering of Nabanna (new rice) is traditionally made to which deity?

A. Maa Samaleswari

B. Maa Mangala

C. Maa Bhuaneswari

D. Maa Budhi Thakurani

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Maa Samaleswari. Key Points Additional Information

• The Nuakhai festival is an important agrarian festival celebrated prominently in the western region of Odisha, particularly in the Sambalpur district.

• During Nuakhai, the first offering of the Nabanna (newly harvested rice) is made to Maa Samaleswari, the presiding deity of the region.

• Maa Samaleswari is considered the goddess of prosperity and harvest, and farmers seek her blessings for a bountiful harvest and prosperity in their lives.

• The term Nuakhai literally translates to "eating of new rice," emphasizing the agrarian roots of this festival.

• The festival is celebrated with great enthusiasm and includes rituals, cultural performances, and traditional food, bringing together communities to offer gratitude to nature and the goddess.

• Maa Samaleswari: Maa Samaleswari is the presiding deity of the Samaleswari Temple in Sambalpur, Odisha.

• She is widely revered as the goddess of wealth, power, and harvest.

• The deity is worshipped not only during Nuakhai but also throughout the year by devotees seeking her blessings for prosperity and well-being.

• Nuakhai Festival: The Nuakhai festival is celebrated on the panchami tithi (fifth day) of the lunar fortnight in the month of Bhadraba (August-September).

• The festival involves nine significant rituals, starting from the preparation for the festival to the offering of Nabanna to the deity, followed by community celebrations.

• The festival has a strong cultural and spiritual significance, reflecting the deep connection between the people and their agricultural practices.

• Other Deities in Odisha: Maa Mangala: She is worshipped as a goddess of protection and well-being, primarily in Kakatpur, Odisha.

• Maa Bhuvaneswari: Known as a form of Shakti, she is associated with power and divine wisdom.

• Maa Budhi Thakurani: She is the presiding deity of the Berhampur region in Odisha and is worshipped for her blessings and protection.

• Importance of Agrarian Festivals: Agrarian festivals like Nuakhai play a crucial role in preserving cultural heritage and fostering unity among communities.

• They emphasize the significance of agriculture in sustaining livelihoods and highlight the harmonious relationship between humans and nature.

2. Which of the following temple style and region pairs are correctly matched? 1. Vesara – Deccan 2. Rekha-Deul – Odisha 3. Bhumija – Malwa 4. Kalinga – Tamil Nadu Which of the pairs given above are correct?

A. 1 and 2 only

B. 1, 2 and 3

C. 2 and 4 only

D. Only 1

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is 1, 2 and 3. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Vesara - Deccan: The Vesara style of temple architecture originated in the Deccan region of India, particularly in Karnataka. It is a hybrid style that combines elements of both Dravidian and Nagara architectural styles. Hence, this statement is correct.

• Rekha-Deul - Odisha: The Rekha-Deul is a prominent architectural style found in the temples of Odisha. It represents the curvilinear spire (Shikhara) of the Nagara style that is a defining feature of temples like the Lingaraja Temple in Bhubaneswar. Hence, this statement is correct.

• Bhumija - Malwa: The Bhumija style of temple architecture developed in regions such as Malwa (present-day Madhya Pradesh). This style is characterized by a tall Shikhara with intricate carvings and a unique arrangement of miniature towers. Hence, this statement is correct.

• Kalinga - Tamil Nadu: The Kalinga style of architecture is associated with Odisha and not Tamil Nadu. Tamil Nadu is known for its Dravidian style of temple architecture. Kalinga temples, such as the Sun Temple at Konark, are distinct from the temples of Tamil Nadu. Hence, this statement is incorrect.

• Vesara Style: The Vesara style emerged during the rule of the Chalukyas of Badami and was further developed by the Hoysalas of Karnataka.

• It combines the Dravidian style (Southern India) and the Nagara style (Northern India).

• Examples: The Durga Temple at Aihole and the temples of Pattadakal.

• Rekha-Deul Style: It is a subset of the Nagara style, characterized by a curvilinear spire (Shikhara).

• Prominent in Odisha and is associated with temples such as the Lingaraja Temple and Rajarani Temple.

• The temples often have intricate carvings and sculptures depicting mythological stories.

• Bhumija Style: It is prominent in Central India, especially in regions like Malwa and Gujarat.

• The style is identified by a tall spire adorned with miniature replicas of the main tower.

• Example: The Nilakantha Temple in Udaipur, Madhya Pradesh.

• Kalinga Style: This style is unique to Odisha and features three distinct types of temples: Rekha Deul, Pidha Deul, and Khakhara Deul.

• The Sun Temple at Konark and the Jagannath Temple in Puri are notable examples.

• It should not be confused with the Dravidian style, which is dominant in Tamil Nadu.

• Dravidian Architecture: It is found primarily in Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh.

• Features include tall gopurams (gateway towers), large courtyards, and water tanks within temple complexes.

• Examples: Meenakshi Temple (Madurai) and Brihadeshwara Temple (Thanjavur).

• Nagara Style: It is predominant in Northern India and is characterized by beehive-shaped Shikharas.

• Examples: Kandariya Mahadev Temple (Khajuraho) and Sun Temple (Modhera).

3. Under the administrative system of the Gupta dynasty, which of the following officer–duty combinations is correctly paired?

A. Prathamakulika – Supervisor of irrigation

B. Gramika (Gramadhyaksha) – Head of the village

C. Talavataka – Treasury officer

D. Pustapala – Land revenue assessor

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Gramika (Gramadhyaksha) – Head of the village. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Gramika (Gramadhyaksha) was the head of the village in the administrative system of the Gupta dynasty. He acted as the chief representative of the village and was responsible for managing local affairs and disputes. Hence, this pairing is correct.

• Prathamakulika was not a supervisor of irrigation. This role involved supervising royal workshops or artisans engaged in temple construction and other works. Hence, this pairing is incorrect.

• Talavataka was not a treasury officer. This term is associated with forest officers or officials responsible for the management of elephants and forests. Hence, this pairing is incorrect.

• Pustapala was not a land revenue assessor. Instead, this officer managed records and documentation, particularly pertaining to land grants and royal orders. Hence, this pairing is incorrect.

• Gupta Administration: The Gupta dynasty (circa 320–550 CE) is considered a golden age in Indian history, marked by significant advancements in art, literature, and governance.

• The administrative system was highly decentralized, with considerable autonomy given to local officials and village heads.

• The king was the supreme authority and had a council of ministers to assist in governance.

• Village Administration: Villages were the smallest administrative units, and the Gramika (Gramadhyaksha) served as the head of the village.

• The Gramika was responsible for resolving disputes, collecting taxes, and maintaining law and order within the village.

• Provincial Governance: The Gupta Empire was divided into provinces called Bhuktis, each governed by a Uparika.

• Each Bhukti was further divided into districts known as Vishayas, managed by Vishayapatis.

• Roles of Officials: Prathamakulika: Managed royal workshops and artisans; not related to irrigation.

• Talavataka: Responsible for forest management and elephants.

• Pustapala: Handled records and royal documentation, particularly of land grants.

• Land Grants: The Gupta rulers frequently issued land grants to Brahmins, temples, and other religious institutions, which were managed and recorded by officials like the Pustapala.

• Revenue System: The revenue system was primarily agrarian, with taxes collected from farmers and a share of the produce given to the state.

• Judicial System: Local disputes were resolved by village assemblies or the Gramika, while higher-level disputes were addressed by provincial or royal courts.

5. As per international standards, what is the official width of a singles badminton court?

A. 6.10 metres

B. 13.40 metres

C. 5.18 metres

D. 12.00 metres

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is 5.18 metres. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• The official width of a singles badminton court, as per international standards, is 5.18 metres (17 feet).

• Badminton court dimensions are standardized by the Badminton World Federation (BWF), which governs the rules and regulations of the game globally.

• For singles matches, the court's width is reduced to 5.18 metres, while its total length remains 13.40 metres.

• The reduced width ensures fair play, as only the inner sidelines are used during singles games.

• This measurement allows players to have a balanced playing area, ensuring that the game is competitive and follows international norms.

• Badminton Court Dimensions: The total length of the court is 13.40 metres (44 feet) for both singles and doubles matches.

• The full width of the court for doubles matches is 6.10 metres, while for singles matches, it is reduced to 5.18 metres (17 feet).

• The court is divided into two halves by a net, which stands at a height of 1.55 metres at the edges and 1.524 metres at the center.

• Singles vs Doubles Court: The primary difference between singles and doubles courts lies in the width. Doubles use the full width of the court, while singles use the narrower width (5.18 metres).

• The service area is also different in singles and doubles matches to accommodate the narrower court in singles.

• Badminton World Federation (BWF): The BWF is the international governing body for badminton, responsible for setting court dimensions, rules, and regulations.

• The organization was founded in 1934 and is headquartered in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia.

• The BWF ensures that all international tournaments, including the Olympics, adhere to the standardized court dimensions.

• Importance of Standard Dimensions: Standardized dimensions ensure uniformity in gameplay, providing a consistent experience for players across the globe.

• They also help maintain fairness and facilitate accurate measurements for scoring and other technical aspects of the game.

• Historical Context: Badminton originated in India during the mid-18th century and was initially called Poonah.

• It was introduced to England in 1873, where it became popularized under its current name.

• Olympic Inclusion: Badminton became an Olympic sport in 1992, with singles and doubles events for both men and women.

• Global Popularity: Countries like China, Indonesia, Denmark, and India have produced world-class players, making badminton a global sport.

• It is particularly popular in Asia and Europe.

D. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Only 1. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Statement 1: The runway in a high jump event must be at least 15 metres long. This is correct as per the international rules and regulations set by the World Athletics. The runway can be extended beyond this minimum length, but it must not be shorter. Hence, statement 1 is correct.

• Statement 2: The crossbar diameter lies between 15 cm and 20 cm. This statement is incorrect. The actual diameter of the crossbar in high jump events, as per World Athletics standards, is between 25 mm and 30 mm. Therefore, the given range in the statement is incorrect. Hence, statement 2 is incorrect.

• In high jump events, the runway length and crossbar dimensions are crucial to ensure standardization and fairness in competitions.

• High jump competitions are governed by the rules of World Athletics, the international governing body for athletics.

• Result: Since only statement 1 is correct, the correct option is Only 1.

• High Jump Overview: The high jump is a track and field event where athletes must jump over a horizontal bar set at predetermined heights without dislodging it.

• The event requires a combination of speed, strength, and technique to clear the bar successfully.

• Fosbury Flop Technique: The Fosbury Flop is the most commonly used technique in high jump events.

• It involves approaching the bar at an angle, jumping off one foot, and arching the back over the bar with the body facing upwards.

• This technique was popularized by Dick Fosbury, who won a gold medal at the 1968 Mexico City Olympics.

• Equipment Specifications: Runway: The runway must have a minimum length of 15 metres but can extend up to 25 metres or more, depending on the venue.

• Crossbar: The crossbar is made of lightweight material like fiberglass and must have a diameter between 25 mm and 30 mm.

• Landing Area: The landing area or mat must be sufficiently cushioned to absorb the impact of the athlete's fall, with a typical size of 5m × 3m.

• World Records in High Jump: The men’s high jump world record is 2.45 metres, set by Javier Sotomayor of Cuba in 1993.

• The women’s high jump world record is 2.09 metres, set by Stefka Kostadinova of Bulgaria in 1987.

• Rules for High Jump: A successful jump requires the athlete to clear the bar without knocking it off the uprights.

• Each athlete is allowed three attempts to clear a height.

• The athlete must take off from one foot; jumping off both feet results in a failed attempt.

• High Jump in Olympics: The high jump has been an Olympic event since the 1896 Athens Games for men and 1928 Amsterdam Games for women.

• It is one of the four jumping events in track and field, alongside the long jump, triple jump, and pole vault.

• Training for High Jump: Athletes focus on improving their explosive power, flexibility, and technique.

• Common exercises include plyometrics, strength training, and jump-specific drills.

A. Kesavananda Bharati case

B. State of West Bengal v. Anwar Ali Sarkar

C. Maneka Gandhi case

D. Golaknath case

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is State of West Bengal v. Anwar Ali Sarkar. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Doctrine of Reasonable Classification: The concept of reasonable classification under Article 14 of the Indian Constitution was established in the landmark case of State of West Bengal v. Anwar Ali Sarkar, decided in 1952.

• The judgment emphasized that laws must ensure equality before the law and should not arbitrarily discriminate between individuals or groups unless there is a valid and reasonable basis for classification.

• In this case, the Special Courts Act, 1950, was challenged as it provided for the establishment of special courts to handle certain cases, leading to alleged discriminatory treatment.

• The Supreme Court held that the classification must be reasonable, meaning it should be based on intelligible differentia and have a rational nexus with the objective sought to be achieved.

• The judgment laid down the principle that arbitrary legislation cannot override the right to equality guaranteed under Article 14. It established that classification must not be discriminatory or arbitrary.

• Article 14 of the Indian Constitution: Article 14 guarantees the Right to Equality, ensuring that the state shall not deny any person equality before the law or equal protection of the laws within the territory of India.

• It applies to all individuals, including citizens, non-citizens, and corporations.

• The principle of reasonable classification is a significant part of Article 14, which permits classification based on intelligible differentia and rational nexus.

• Key Components of Reasonable Classification: Intelligible Differentia: The classification must distinguish individuals or groups based on identifiable and logical differences.

• Rational Nexus: The classification must be connected to the objective sought to be achieved by the law.

• Any law or policy violating these principles can be challenged as unconstitutional under Article 14.

• Other Landmark Cases Related to Article 14: Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978): This case expanded the scope of Article 14 to include procedural fairness and reasonableness.

• Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973): The doctrine of basic structure emerged, stating that the right to equality is part of the basic structure of the Constitution.

• E.P. Royappa v. State of Tamil Nadu (1974): Equality was interpreted as prohibiting arbitrariness in state actions.

• Special Courts Act, 1950: This act was enacted to establish special courts for handling specific cases promptly.

• The act faced criticism in the Anwar Ali Sarkar case, as it was argued to create arbitrary discrimination between individuals facing trial.

• Reasonable Classification vs. Arbitrary Classification: Reasonable classification ensures fairness and equality, while arbitrary classification violates Article 14.

• The judiciary plays a crucial role in determining the validity of classifications under Article 14.

• Role of Judiciary: The Supreme Court of India acts as the guardian of the Constitution, ensuring laws comply with the principle of equality.

• Judicial review is an essential tool for addressing violations of Article 14.

25. Identify the incorrect pair of satellite type and its application:

A. Weather satellite – Cyclone monitoring

B. Communication satellite – Television broadcasting

C. Navigation satellite – Forest fire detection

D. Remote sensing satellite – Land-use analysis

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Navigation satellite – Forest fire detection. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Weather satellites: These satellites are designed to monitor and predict weather patterns, including tracking cyclones, hurricanes, and other meteorological phenomena. Hence, statement 1 is correct.

• Communication satellites: These satellites facilitate television broadcasting, internet services, and other forms of communication across the globe. Hence, statement 2 is correct.

• Navigation satellites: Navigation satellites provide location-based services such as GPS and positioning systems. They are not designed for detecting forest fires, which is primarily the role of remote sensing satellites. Hence, statement 3 is incorrect.

• Remote sensing satellites: These satellites are used for land-use analysis, environmental monitoring, and disaster management, including detecting forest fires, deforestation, and agricultural activities. Hence, statement 4 is correct.

• Weather satellites: These satellites monitor atmospheric conditions and provide real-time data on weather patterns.

• They are crucial for cyclone prediction, rainfall estimation, and studying global climate changes.

• Examples include the Indian INSAT series and NOAA satellites from the USA.

• The data provided is used by meteorological departments worldwide to issue weather forecasts and warnings.

• Communication satellites: These satellites enable telecommunication, broadcasting, and internet services worldwide.

• They are placed in geostationary orbit to maintain a fixed position relative to the Earth.

• Examples include GSAT satellites by ISRO and Intelsat satellites.

• They are essential for providing connectivity in remote areas and for disaster communication.

• Navigation satellites: Navigation satellites are designed to provide location data for GPS systems and mapping services.

• They help in transportation, military operations, and surveying activities.

• Examples include the Indian NAVIC system, GPS from the USA, and GLONASS from Russia.

• These satellites do not detect forest fires but assist in pinpointing locations.

• Remote sensing satellites: These satellites collect data about the Earth's surface and are instrumental in environmental monitoring.

• They are used for detecting forest fires, land-use mapping, urban planning, and disaster management.

• Examples include ISRO’s Resourcesat series and NASA's Landsat series.

• They utilize sensors to detect heat signatures and changes in land cover.

• Forest fire detection: Forest fires are primarily detected by remote sensing satellites using infrared sensors.

• These satellites can identify heat anomalies and smoke patterns from space.

• They play a vital role in disaster response and mitigation strategies.

• Role of ISRO: The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) has developed satellites for weather forecasting, communication, navigation, and remote sensing.

• Examples include INSAT for weather, GSAT for communication, NAVIC for navigation, and Resourcesat for remote sensing.

• ISRO’s satellites are widely used for disaster management and environmental conservation.
