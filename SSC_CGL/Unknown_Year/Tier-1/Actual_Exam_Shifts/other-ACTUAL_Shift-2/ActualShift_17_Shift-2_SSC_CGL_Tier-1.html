B. Bhaga – ruler’s share from agricultural output

C. Hiranya – compulsory service

D. Dhanya – tax on cattle

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is option 2. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Adeya: This was not a levy on land transfer but rather referred to a form of voluntary or religious offering. Hence, the first statement is incorrect.

• Bhaga: It was the ruler’s share from the agricultural output, usually a fixed proportion, typically 1/6th of the produce. This formed the primary source of revenue during the Gupta period. Hence, the second statement is correct.

• Hiranya: It referred to taxes paid in cash, not compulsory service. Taxes under this category were generally collected from those who could pay in gold or cash instead of agricultural produce. Hence, the third statement is incorrect.

• Dhanya: This was not a tax on cattle but a tax on agricultural produce (grain). It was a significant part of the agrarian revenue system. Hence, the fourth statement is incorrect.

• Hence, the correct answer is option 2.

• Revenue Administration During Gupta Period: The Gupta Empire (circa 320–550 CE) had a well-organized system of revenue collection based on agrarian economy. The state’s primary source of revenue was land taxes and taxes on agricultural produce.

• Taxes were often levied as a fixed percentage of the agricultural produce, commonly 1/6th of the yield, known as Bhaga.

• Revenue from trade and commerce also contributed significantly, facilitated by internal and external trade routes. Taxes on goods, tolls, and customs duties formed part of the revenue system.

• The administration also collected taxes in cash or kind, such as grain (Dhanya) or gold (Hiranya), depending on the region and the payer's capacity.

• Specific Characteristics of Revenue Terms: Adeya: Refers to offerings or voluntary contributions, often related to religious or spiritual practices, not a formal land transfer levy.

• Bhaga: The state’s share of agricultural produce, collected directly from cultivators. It was a critical revenue source for maintaining administration and military expenses.

• Hiranya: Denoted cash payments, primarily from urban areas or affluent sections of society, as opposed to payments in kind.

• Dhanya: Represented agricultural produce (grain) given as tax. It was significant in rural regions where cash transactions were less prevalent.

• Gupta Empire Economy: The Gupta period marked the Golden Age of India, with significant advancements in agriculture, trade, and industry. Agricultural taxes like Bhaga and Dhanya formed the backbone of the economy.

• Trade and Commerce: Gupta rulers promoted trade, which was taxed in the form of tolls and customs duties. This revenue was essential for urban development and state administration.

• Administrative Efficiency: The Gupta rulers established an efficient system of tax collection and revenue management, ensuring the sustainability of their empire.

• Revenue collected was used for public works, maintaining a strong military, and supporting cultural and educational endeavors.

4. How are Achal and Chal swaras explained in Indian classical music tradition?

A. All notes remain unchanged in pitch

B. Sa and Pa are constant while others may vary

C. Only Carnatic music uses variable notes

D. Re and Dha never change pitch

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Sa and Pa are constant while others may vary.. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Achal Swaras: In Indian classical music, Sa (Shadja) and Pa (Panchama) are considered Achal Swaras, meaning their pitch remains constant and does not vary. These notes provide the foundation for the musical scale.

• Chal Swaras: The remaining swaras – Re (Rishabha), Ga (Gandhara), Ma (Madhyama), Dha (Dhaivata), Ni (Nishada) – are termed as Chal Swaras. These notes can vary in pitch depending on the raga being performed. Chal swaras are responsible for the unique melodic patterns and emotional expression in Indian classical music.

• The distinction between Achal and Chal swaras is critical in understanding the structure of a raga. While Sa and Pa form the stable points, the Chal swaras introduce variability and flexibility, enabling creative musical exploration. Hence, the statement is correct.

• Carnatic and Hindustani Music: Both Carnatic and Hindustani classical music follow the concept of Achal and Chal swaras. Although the two systems differ in their approach and terminology, the principle of fixed and variable notes remains consistent.

• The concept of Achal and Chal swaras ensures that the musical system is rooted in stability while allowing flexibility for improvisation and raga development. Hence, the correct answer is explained.

• Indian Classical Music Swaras: Indian classical music is based on the system of seven swaras: Sa, Re, Ga, Ma, Pa, Dha, Ni. These swaras are the foundation of melodies and ragas.

• Out of these, Sa and Pa are fixed notes, while the other five swaras can be modified to create shuddha (pure) and vikrut (altered) variations. This flexibility enables the formation of a wide range of ragas.

• Achal Swaras: Sa (Shadja): The tonic note, which serves as the reference point for all other swaras.

• Pa (Panchama): The fifth note in the scale, considered stable and unchanging.

• These swaras are universally constant and do not change regardless of the raga being performed.

• Chal Swaras: Re, Ga, Ma, Dha, Ni: These swaras can vary in pitch and are categorized as shuddha (pure) or vikrut (altered). For example, Ma can be shuddha Madhyama or tivra Madhyama.

• The variability in Chal swaras allows the creation of a wide variety of ragas and contributes to the melodic richness of Indian classical music.

• Examples of ragas with variable swaras include Yaman (uses tivra Ma) and Bhimpalasi (uses komal Ga and komal Ni).

• Role of Achal and Chal Swaras: Achal swaras provide stability and ensure that the musical framework does not deviate from its foundational structure.

• Chal swaras introduce flexibility and artistic freedom, allowing musicians to explore and improvise within the raga framework.

• Examples of Achal and Chal Swaras in Ragas: Raga Bhoop: Contains only shuddha swaras (Sa, Re, Ga, Pa, Dha).

• Raga Todi: Uses vikrut swaras (komal Re, komal Dha, tivra Ma).

• Raga Yaman: Incorporates tivra Ma, showcasing the use of variable Chal swaras.

5. Which statement correctly explains the rule of “en passant” in the game of chess?

A. It is allowed after one waiting move

B. It can be exercised within two turns

C. It must be executed immediately after a two-square pawn move

D. It applies only on light squares

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is It must be executed immediately after a two-square pawn move.. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• “En passant” is a special pawn capture rule in chess that applies only when a pawn moves forward two squares from its starting position and lands adjacent to an opponent’s pawn.

• The key condition is that the opponent must execute the capture immediately on the very next move. If this opportunity is not taken, the chance to capture "en passant" is lost forever.

• The capture is unique because the opponent’s pawn moves diagonally to the square the first pawn passed through, as though it had moved only one square instead of two. Hence, the correct statement is “It must be executed immediately after a two-square pawn move.”

• The rule applies to pawns only and is valid under specific circumstances, making it a rare but powerful tactical opportunity.

• The other options provided in the question are incorrect because: Option 1: “It is allowed after one waiting move” – Incorrect, as the move must be executed immediately.

• Option 2: “It can be exercised within two turns” – Incorrect, as the opportunity expires if not used on the next move.

• Option 4: “It applies only on light squares” – Incorrect, as the rule applies regardless of the color of the square.

• Conditions for en passant: The capturing pawn must be on its fifth rank.

• The opponent’s pawn must move two squares forward from its starting position, landing adjacent to the capturing pawn.

• The capture must be made on the very next move; otherwise, the opportunity is lost.

• Historical Background: The rule of en passant was introduced in the late 15th century when the two-square initial pawn move was added to the game of chess.

• The rule was created to prevent players from avoiding pawn captures by leaping over a square where they would otherwise be vulnerable.

• Practical Examples: Suppose a white pawn is on e5, and a black pawn moves from d7 to d5. White can capture en passant by moving its pawn to d6.

• This captures the black pawn on d5 as if it had only moved one square forward to d6.

• Key Chess Rules Related to Pawns: Pawn Promotion: When a pawn reaches the eighth rank, it must be promoted to a queen, rook, bishop, or knight.

• Two-Square Initial Move: A pawn may move two squares forward from its starting position, provided the squares are unoccupied.

• Diagonal Capture: Pawns capture diagonally, one square forward to the left or right.

• Strategic Use of En Passant: En passant can be used to remove a pawn that is protecting key squares or obstructing a piece's mobility.

• It is a tactical tool that can disrupt an opponent’s pawn structure or defensive plans.

• Significance of 400 km: This altitude is optimal for human spaceflight missions as it minimizes exposure to harmful radiation while providing a stable orbit. It also allows sufficient time for reentry procedures in case of emergencies.

• Comparison to Other Orbits: Unlike the Geostationary Orbit (36,000 km), which is used for satellites like communication satellites, orbits like 400 km are suitable for short-term missions and experiments. This altitude also reduces the energy required for launch and reentry.

• Spacecraft Details: The Gaganyaan spacecraft will be launched into this orbit by the GSLV Mk III launch vehicle, also known as the LVM-3. This is ISRO’s heaviest and most powerful rocket, specifically modified for crewed missions.

• The mission aims to ensure the safety and survival of astronauts for up to 7 days in space, with life-support systems, emergency escape provisions, and robust communication channels. These are critical in maintaining a successful 400 km altitude orbit. Hence, option 2 is correct.

• Low Earth Orbit (LEO): LEO is defined as an orbit at an altitude ranging from 160 km to 2,000 km. It is most commonly used for Earth observation, scientific research, and space missions like the International Space Station (ISS).

• Objects in LEO experience atmospheric drag, which necessitates periodic altitude adjustments to maintain orbit. However, this drag is lower at 400 km compared to altitudes closer to Earth.

• 850 km: This is a higher LEO altitude often used for Earth observation satellites, but it requires more energy for spacecraft launch and reentry.

• 36,000 km: This is the altitude of Geostationary Orbit (GEO), where satellites match Earth’s rotation. It is unsuitable for crewed missions due to high radiation exposure and long travel times.

12. Which among the following does NOT directly influence population change?

A. Fertility rate

B. Mortality rate

C. Migration

D. Employment

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is Employment. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Population change is primarily influenced by three factors: fertility rate, mortality rate, and migration. These are considered direct contributors to changes in population numbers.

• Fertility rate: This refers to the average number of children born per woman during her lifetime. Higher fertility rates lead to population growth, while lower rates contribute to population decline. Hence, this influences population change directly.

• Mortality rate: The number of deaths per thousand people in a population. A higher mortality rate reduces population numbers, while lower mortality rates contribute to population growth. Thus, it directly affects population change.

• Migration: Movement of people from one region to another, either internationally or within a country. Migration can increase or decrease population in specific areas. Hence, it directly impacts population change.

• Employment: While employment opportunities can encourage migration, which in turn indirectly affects population distribution in certain regions, it does not directly influence overall population change. Employment does not increase or decrease the total number of people in the population. Hence, it does NOT directly influence population change.

• Fertility Rate: The fertility rate is a critical demographic indicator. For example, in many developing countries, the fertility rate is high, contributing to rapid population growth.

• The Total Fertility Rate (TFR) of a country often indicates its population growth trends. As of recent data, countries like Niger have a TFR above 6, while nations like Japan have a TFR below 2.

17. What is the effect when an ideal fluid passes through a narrowing horizontal pipe?

A. Velocity falls and pressure rises

B. Velocity rises and pressure falls

C. Both increase

D. Both decrease

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Velocity rises and pressure falls. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• This question is based on the principle of Bernoulli’s equation, which describes the behavior of an ideal fluid in motion. It states that for an ideal fluid, the total mechanical energy (sum of pressure energy, kinetic energy, and potential energy) remains constant along a streamline.

• When a fluid passes through a narrowing horizontal pipe, the cross-sectional area decreases. According to the continuity equation, the velocity of the fluid must increase to maintain a constant flow rate. Hence, the velocity rises.

• As the velocity of the fluid increases, the pressure energy decreases to conserve the total energy, as per Bernoulli’s principle. Hence, the pressure falls.

• Bernoulli’s equation can be mathematically expressed as: P + 1/2ρv² + ρgh = constant, where P is the pressure, ρ is the fluid density, v is the velocity, g is the acceleration due to gravity, and h is the height.

• In this scenario, since the pipe is horizontal, the height (h) remains constant, and the changes in velocity and pressure are inversely related. This confirms the relationship between velocity rise and pressure fall.

• The fluid flow is considered ideal and incompressible, implying no viscosity or energy losses, which is a key assumption for applying Bernoulli's principle in this case.

• Thus, the correct answer is that velocity rises and pressure falls.

• Bernoulli’s Principle: Bernoulli’s principle is a fundamental concept in fluid dynamics. It explains that an increase in the velocity of a fluid results in a decrease in pressure or potential energy.

• This principle applies to steady, incompressible, and non-viscous flow. It is widely used in engineering and physics to analyze the motion of fluids.

• Continuity Equation: The continuity equation is derived from the conservation of mass. It states that the mass flow rate of a fluid remains constant in a closed system.

• Mathematically, it is expressed as: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂, where A is the cross-sectional area and v is the velocity of the fluid.

• For a narrowing pipe, the cross-sectional area (A) decreases, leading to an increase in velocity (v) to maintain the flow rate.

• Applications of Bernoulli’s Principle: It is used in the design of aircraft wings, where the shape of the wings creates a pressure difference to generate lift.

• It explains the working of devices like venturi meters, carburetors, and atomizers, which rely on pressure-velocity relationships.

• It is also applied in understanding natural phenomena like tornadoes and fluid flow in rivers.

• Assumptions in Bernoulli’s Principle: The fluid is incompressible, meaning its density remains constant.

• The flow is steady, with no fluctuations in velocity or pressure over time.

• The fluid has no viscosity, implying no energy is lost due to internal friction.

• The flow occurs along a streamline, ensuring there is no turbulence or chaotic motion.

• Real-life Deviations: In real-world scenarios, factors like viscosity, turbulence, and compressibility can lead to deviations from Bernoulli's predictions.

• In such cases, advanced fluid dynamics equations, such as the Navier-Stokes equations, are used for accurate analysis.

25. Consider the statements: 1. Article 262 allows Parliament to establish tribunals for river disputes. 2. The Supreme Court hears appeals in such disputes. Which is/are correct?

A. Only 1

B. Only 2

C. Both 1 and 2

D. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Only 1. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Article 262 of the Indian Constitution empowers the Parliament to legislate for the adjudication of disputes related to the waters of inter-state rivers or river valleys.

• Under this provision, Parliament can establish tribunals specifically for resolving such disputes. These tribunals serve as the designated authorities to handle water-related conflicts between states. Hence, statement 1 is correct.

• However, Article 262 restricts the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court and other courts regarding inter-state river disputes if Parliament passes a law to exclude them. Hence, statement 2 is incorrect.

• An example of legislation under Article 262 is the Inter-State Water Disputes Act, 1956, which provides the framework for establishing water tribunals to settle disputes between states.

• The Supreme Court does not hear appeals in disputes under Article 262 if a law explicitly excludes its jurisdiction. Tribunals are considered final authorities in such cases.

• Hence, the correct answer is Only 1.

• Article 262 of the Indian Constitution: Article 262 provides Parliament the authority to legislate on matters related to inter-state river disputes.

• This article empowers Parliament to determine procedures for resolving disputes and allows the exclusion of Supreme Court jurisdiction over such matters.

• Examples of its implementation include the Inter-State Water Disputes Act, 1956, which established tribunals such as the Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal and the Kaveri River Tribunal.

• Role of Tribunals: Tribunals serve as specialized bodies to adjudicate disputes over water allocation between states.

• The decisions made by these tribunals are binding and final, ensuring that disputes are resolved efficiently without court interference.

• Exclusion of Supreme Court Jurisdiction: If Parliament enacts a law under Article 262, the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court and other courts is excluded for inter-state river disputes.

• This ensures that disputes are handled exclusively by tribunals, avoiding delays in the judicial process.

• Inter-State Water Disputes Act, 1956: This act was enacted to provide for the adjudication of disputes between states concerning the waters of inter-state rivers.

• It allows the central government to set up tribunals for dispute resolution.

• Examples of Tribunals: Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal: Established to resolve disputes between Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and Puducherry.
