47. Two circles touch each other externally at P. AB is a direct common tangent to the two circles. If A and B are points of contact and ∠PAB = 65°, then ∠ABP is _______.

A. 35°

B. 15°

C. 5°

D. 25°

Answer D

Solution

Shortcut Trick When two circles touch each other externally, the angle subtended by the direct common tangent at the point of contact is always 90°. ∴ In ΔAPB, ∠APB = 90°. Using the angle sum property of a triangle in ΔAPB: ∠ABP = 180° − (∠APB + ∠PAB) ∠ABP = 180° − (90° + 65°) = 25° ∴ The correct answer is 25°. Alternate Method Given: Two circles touch each other externally at point P. AB is a direct common tangent touching the circles at A and B. ∠PAB = 65° Formula/Property Used: 1. Tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal in length. 2. Angles opposite to equal sides in a triangle are equal. 3. The sum of angles in a triangle is 180°. Calculations: ⇒ Let the common tangent at the point of contact P intersect the direct common tangent AB at point T. ⇒ For the left circle, TA and TP are tangents drawn from the external point T. ∴ TA = TP ⇒ In ΔTAP, since TA = TP, the angles opposite to these sides are equal: ⇒ ∠TPA = ∠TAP = 65° ⇒ Similarly, for the right circle, TB and TP are tangents drawn from the external point T. ∴ TB = TP ⇒ Since TA = TP and TB = TP, we have: ⇒ TA = TB = TP ⇒ In ΔAPB, the median PT is equal to half of the opposite side AB (since T is the midpoint of AB and TP = AB/2). ⇒ This property holds true only in a right-angled triangle where the hypotenuse is AB. ∴ ∠APB = 90° ⇒ Now, in ΔAPB, using the angle sum property: ⇒ ∠PAB + ∠ABP + ∠APB = 180° ⇒ 65° + ∠ABP + 90° = 180° ⇒ 155° + ∠ABP = 180° ⇒ ∠ABP = 180° − 155° = 25° ∴ The correct answer is 25°. Additional Information Angle Subtended by Direct Common Tangent When two circles touch each other externally, the direct common tangent always subtends a right angle (90°) at the point of contact of the two circles. Length of Direct Common Tangent (DCT) For two circles of radii R and r with distance d between their centers, the length of the DCT is given by √(d2 − (R − r)2). When they touch externally, d = R + r, simplifying the formula to 2√(R × r). Number of Common Tangents When two circles touch externally, there are exactly 3 common tangents: 2 direct common tangents and 1 transverse common tangent.
