5. If a 12-year-old is found to be working in a factory manufacturing carpets, which of the following Articles of the Constitution of India will be violated?

A. Article 24

B. Article 25

C. Article 23

D. Article 26

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Article 24. Key Points Additional Information

• Article 24 of the Constitution of India prohibits the employment of children below the age of 14 years in any factory, mine, or other hazardous employment.

• This Article is part of the Fundamental Rights enshrined in Part III of the Constitution, which aims to protect children from exploitation.

• It was introduced to safeguard children from engaging in work that can be detrimental to their health, safety, and development.

• Violation of this Article can lead to legal consequences and penalties as per the Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act, 1986.

• Article 23: Prohibits human trafficking and forced labor.

• It states that traffic in human beings, begar (forced labor), and other similar forms of forced labor are prohibited and any contravention of this provision shall be an offense punishable in accordance with the law.

• Article 25: Guarantees freedom of conscience and free profession, practice, and propagation of religion.

• It provides all individuals the right to freely profess, practice, and propagate their religion, subject to public order, morality, and health.

• Article 26: Provides freedom to manage religious affairs.

• It gives every religious denomination or any section thereof the right to establish and maintain institutions for religious and charitable purposes, manage its own affairs in matters of religion, own and acquire movable and immovable property, and administer such property in accordance with the law.

• Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act, 1986: This Act prohibits the engagement of children in certain employments and regulates the conditions of work of children in other employments.

• The Act defines a child as a person who has not completed 14 years of age.

• It lays down a detailed framework for the prohibition and regulation of child labor in India.

6. Bihu festival is the main festival of which state?

A. Kerala

B. Punjab

C. Rajasthan

D. Assam

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is Assam. Key Points Additional Information

• Bihu is the main festival of Assam, celebrated with great joy and enthusiasm.

• There are three major Bihu festivals in Assam: Bohag Bihu (or Rongali Bihu), Kati Bihu (or Kongali Bihu), and Magh Bihu (or Bhogali Bihu).

• Bihu marks the harvest season and is closely related to the agricultural calendar of Assam.

• It is a non-religious festival and is celebrated by all communities irrespective of their religion, caste, or creed.

• Bohag Bihu (Rongali Bihu) It is celebrated in mid-April and marks the beginning of the Assamese New Year.

• Bohag Bihu is the most popular Bihu, characterized by feasts, music, and dancing.

• People wear traditional attire and perform the Bihu dance, showcasing their cultural heritage.

• Kati Bihu (Kongali Bihu) It is celebrated in mid-October and is a more subdued festival compared to Bohag Bihu.

• Kati Bihu is associated with the sowing of crops and is marked by lighting earthen lamps in paddy fields and praying for a good harvest.

• It is a time of introspection and prayer for the welfare of the crops.

• Magh Bihu (Bhogali Bihu) It is celebrated in mid-January and marks the end of the harvest season.

• Magh Bihu is characterized by feasts and bonfires, where people gather to celebrate the bounty of the harvest.

• Traditional games such as buffalo fighting and pot-breaking are also part of the celebrations.

• This provision ensures the independence of the Governor's office from legislative influence.

• Article 158 Article 158 of the Constitution of India pertains to the conditions of the Governor's office.

• Clause (1) of Article 158 states that the Governor shall not be a member of either House of Parliament or of a House of the Legislature of any State specified in the First Schedule.

9. What is the name of the inflammatory condition that causes cracks, crusting and scaling at the corners of the mouth due to vitamin B2 deficiency?

A. Cheilosis

B. Psoriasis

C. Urticaria

D. Atopic dermatitis

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Cheilosis. Key Points Additional Information

• Cheilosis, also known as angular cheilitis, is an inflammatory condition that affects the corners of the mouth.

• It is characterized by the formation of cracks, crusting, and scaling at the mouth corners.

• Cheilosis is commonly caused by a deficiency in Vitamin B2 (riboflavin).

• Other contributing factors can include fungal infections and poor oral hygiene.

• Vitamin B2 (Riboflavin) Riboflavin is a water-soluble vitamin that is essential for energy production and cellular function.

• It helps in the metabolism of fats, drugs, and steroids and is crucial for the conversion of carbohydrates into ATP (adenosine triphosphate).

• Deficiency in riboflavin can lead to symptoms such as sore throat, redness and swelling of the lining of the mouth and throat, and cheilosis.

• Psoriasis Psoriasis is a chronic autoimmune condition that causes the rapid buildup of skin cells.

• This buildup of cells causes scaling on the skin’s surface.

• Inflammation and redness around the scales are fairly common.

• Urticaria (Hives) Urticaria, commonly known as hives, is a skin reaction that causes itchy welts.

• It is usually caused by an allergic reaction to food, medicine, or other irritants.

• Atopic Dermatitis Atopic dermatitis, also known as eczema, is a condition that makes the skin red and itchy.

• It is common in children but can occur at any age.

• Atopic dermatitis is long-lasting (chronic) and tends to flare periodically.

• Delhi Sultanate: The Delhi Sultanate was a Muslim kingdom based in Delhi that ruled large parts of the Indian subcontinent for 320 years (1206–1526).

• The Sultanate is noted for its integration of the Indian subcontinent into the Muslim world and its role in the spread of Islam in South Asia.

• Silver Tanka: The silver tanka was a high-value coin made of silver and used for large transactions.

• It had a standard weight of 175 grains (approximately 11.3 grams).

• The introduction of the silver tanka standardized the monetary system and facilitated trade.

• Copper Jital: The copper jital was a lower value coin made of copper and used for smaller transactions.

• It complemented the silver tanka and provided a comprehensive monetary system covering both high and low-value transactions.

• The copper jital played a significant role in the everyday economy of the Delhi Sultanate.

• Economic Impact: The introduction of these coins by Iltutmish helped to stabilize the economy of the Delhi Sultanate.

• It provided a reliable and standardized medium of exchange, which was crucial for trade and commerce.

• This monetary reform was an important step in the consolidation of the Delhi Sultanate's power and its administration.

15. When you take Lead nitrate powder in a boiling tube and heat it, you will observe the emission of brown fumes that are of _________________.

A. Nitrous oxide

B. Nitric oxide

C. Nitrogen dioxide

D. Dinitrogen trioxide

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Nitrogen dioxide. Key Points Additional Information

• Nitrogen dioxide (NO2) is a brown gas that is emitted when lead nitrate is heated.

• Lead nitrate decomposes to form lead oxide (PbO), nitrogen dioxide (NO2), and oxygen (O2).

• The chemical reaction for this process is: 2Pb(NO3)2 → 2PbO + 4NO2 + O2.

• The brown fumes observed during the heating of lead nitrate are characteristic of nitrogen dioxide.

• Lead Nitrate (Pb(NO3)2): It is an inorganic compound with the chemical formula Pb(NO3)2.

• It is soluble in water and is commonly used in laboratories and industries.

• When heated, lead nitrate decomposes into lead oxide, nitrogen dioxide, and oxygen.

• Nitrogen Dioxide (NO2): It is a reddish-brown gas with a characteristic sharp, biting odor.

• It is one of several nitrogen oxides and is a significant air pollutant.

• NO2 is a major component of smog and can have harmful effects on the respiratory system.

• Chemical Reactions: In chemistry, a chemical reaction is a process that leads to the transformation of one set of chemical substances to another.

• Reactions involve the breaking of bonds in reactants and the formation of new bonds in products.

• Decomposition reactions, like the heating of lead nitrate, involve the breakdown of a single compound into two or more simpler substances.

D. 50 : 50

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is 90 : 10. Key Points Additional Information

• For the North-Eastern and the Himalayan states, the cost sharing ratio between the Central and State Governments is 90:10.

• This scheme aims to provide financial assistance to the homeless and those living in kutcha houses to construct pucca houses.

• It ensures that people living in difficult areas such as hilly terrains and remote regions also have access to housing support.

• It aims to provide financial assistance for constructing pucca houses with basic amenities to homeless families and those living in dilapidated houses.

• The cost of unit assistance is shared between the Central and State Governments for various states in different ratios.

• In plain areas, the cost sharing ratio is 60:40 between Central and State Governments.

• For the North-Eastern and the Himalayan states, the cost sharing ratio is 90:10 between the Central and State Governments.

Solution

• Revenue from the sale of government assets is considered a capital receipt because it involves the sale of existing government assets, which reduces the financial assets of the government.

• Examples of capital receipts include loans raised by the government, disinvestment proceeds, and borrowings from international organizations.

• Capital receipts do not occur regularly and are usually non-recurring in nature.

• Revenue Receipts: These are funds received by the government which do not create any liability or reduce any financial asset.

• Grants-in-aid: These are funds provided by one government to another, typically for a specific purpose.

• Grants-in-aid are considered revenue receipts.

A. May

B. August

C. June

D. July

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is July. Key Points Additional Information

• The Inter Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ) is a region that circles the Earth, near the equator, where the trade winds of the Northern and Southern Hemispheres come together.

• In the summer months, particularly in July, the ITCZ shifts northwards to around 20ºN-25ºN latitude, which is over the Gangetic plain in India.

• This northward shift is due to the intense solar heating of the landmass, which creates low pressure areas that draw the ITCZ northwards.

• The movement of the ITCZ is significant as it influences the monsoon patterns, bringing heavy rains to the Indian subcontinent during this period.

• Inter Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ) The ITCZ is also known as the Equatorial Convergence Zone or the Doldrums.

• It is characterized by convective activity which generates often vigorous thunderstorms over large areas.

• The position of the ITCZ varies seasonally, moving northwards during the Northern Hemisphere summer and southwards during the Southern Hemisphere summer.

• The ITCZ plays a crucial role in the global climate system and is a key component of the monsoon systems in tropical regions.

• Monsoon The monsoon is a seasonal reversing wind accompanied by corresponding changes in precipitation.

• In South Asia, the summer monsoon brings heavy rainfall, which is vital for agriculture but can also lead to floods.

• The monsoon is driven by the differential heating of land and sea, with the ITCZ acting as a major component in its formation and movement.

• The onset and intensity of the monsoon are closely monitored by meteorologists to predict weather patterns and prepare for agricultural planning.

• Gangetic Plain The Gangetic Plain is a large and fertile plain encompassing most of northern and eastern India, parts of Pakistan, and Bangladesh.

• It is formed by the sediment deposits of the Ganges and its tributaries.

• The plain is one of the most densely populated regions in the world and is crucial for India's agriculture.

• The northward shift of the ITCZ during summer months significantly impacts the rainfall distribution in this region.
