23. When did Dmitri Mendeleev invent the periodic table, ordering the symbols of the chemical elements according to their atomic weights?

A. 1794

B. 1869

C. 1890

D. 1779

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is 1869. Key Points Additional Information

• Dmitri Mendeleev invented the periodic table in 1869, organizing elements based on their atomic weights and properties.

• Mendeleev left gaps in the table for elements yet to be discovered, predicting their properties accurately.

• On March 6, 1869, Mendeleev presented his first periodic table to the Russian Chemical Society.

• Mendeleev’s table demonstrated that when elements are arranged by increasing atomic weight, certain chemical properties repeat periodically.

• This periodic arrangement became the foundation for the modern periodic table used in chemistry today.

• Periodic Law: Mendeleev’s periodic table was based on the periodic law which states that the properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic weights.

• Modern Periodic Table: Today, the periodic table is organized by atomic number (proton count) rather than atomic weight, as introduced by Henry Moseley in 1913.

• Predicted Elements: Mendeleev accurately predicted the existence and properties of elements like germanium (eka-silicon), gallium (eka-aluminum), and scandium.

• Contributors to Periodicity: Other scientists like John Newlands (law of octaves) and Lothar Meyer worked on periodic classification, but Mendeleev’s table was more comprehensive and widely accepted.

• Significance: Mendeleev’s periodic table provided a systematic framework for studying chemical elements, aiding in the discovery of new elements and advancing the field of chemistry.
