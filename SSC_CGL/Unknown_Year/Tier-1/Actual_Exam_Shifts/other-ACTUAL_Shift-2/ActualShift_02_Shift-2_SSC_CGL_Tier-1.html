4. Which of the following correctly explains the origin of Indian classical music?

A. Ragas developed from ancient Jatis.

B. Jatis originated from medieval Ragas.

C. Folk scales evolved into Jatis in the modern period.

D. Tala system gave rise to Ragas during the Vedic period.

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Ragas developed from ancient Jatis. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Indian classical music has its roots in the ancient Jatis, which were melodic structures used in early Indian music traditions.

• Jatis served as the foundation for the evolution of Ragas, which are complex melodic frameworks used in classical music to evoke specific emotions and moods.

• The concept of Jatis can be traced back to the Vedic period, where the Sama Veda laid the groundwork for Indian music through chants and hymns. Hence, statement 1 is correct.

• The Raga system developed over time, incorporating the ancient principles of Jatis and expanding them into intricate melodic patterns that form the basis of modern classical music.

• The Tala system, which governs rhythm in Indian classical music, evolved independently alongside Ragas, but did not directly give rise to them during the Vedic period. Hence, statement 4 is incorrect.

• The claim that Jatis originated from medieval Ragas is inaccurate, as Jatis predate Ragas and were the precursor to their development. Hence, statement 2 is incorrect.

• The statement suggesting folk scales evolved into Jatis in the modern period is also incorrect, as Jatis have an ancient origin linked to classical traditions, not folk music. Hence, statement 3 is incorrect.

• Origin of Indian Classical Music: Indian classical music originated from the Sama Veda, which contained hymns and chants that were sung in specific melodic structures.

• Jatis were the initial melodic structures that served as the basis for the development of Ragas.

• Over time, these Jatis evolved into highly complex systems of melody known as Ragas, which are used in both Hindustani and Carnatic music traditions.

• Features of Ragas: Ragas are designed to evoke specific emotions, moods, or times of the day, and are central to Indian classical music performances.

• Each Raga has its own set of rules regarding the ascending and descending notes (Arohana and Avarohana), as well as specific notes and phrases that define its character.

• Evolution of the Tala System: The Tala system, which governs rhythm, evolved separately but complements the melodic aspect of Ragas in performances.

• Talas are rhythmic cycles consisting of beats, and their interaction with Ragas adds depth to musical compositions.

• Difference Between Jatis and Folk Scales: Jatis are classical melodic structures rooted in ancient traditions, while folk scales are simpler and often tied to specific cultural or regional practices.

• Folk music played a role in influencing classical music, but Jatis were primarily derived from Vedic traditions rather than folk scales.

• Key Characteristics of Jatis: Jatis were early melodic patterns that laid the foundation for Ragas, forming the backbone of Indian classical music.

• They were structured and codified in ancient texts like the Natyashastra by Bharata, which is considered a seminal work in Indian performing arts.

• Development of Ragas: Ragas evolved from the principles of Jatis and became highly sophisticated over centuries, influenced by regional, cultural, and philosophical ideas.

• They became prominent during the medieval period, with the advent of Hindustani and Carnatic classical traditions.

12. Which type of landslide involves rotational movement of material along a curved surface?

A. Rockslide

B. Rockfall

C. Slump

D. Debris fall

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Slump. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• A slump is a type of landslide that occurs when a mass of earth or rock moves downhill along a curved surface, which is usually concave in shape.

• In a slump, the material moves as a coherent block or mass, maintaining its internal structure to a certain extent.

• This movement typically occurs due to a combination of gravitational forces and the loss of cohesive strength in the material, often triggered by factors such as water saturation, erosion, or human activities.

• The curved surface of the rupture is a defining characteristic of a slump, distinguishing it from other types of landslides such as rockslides or rockfalls.

• Slumps are more common in loose or unconsolidated soils and sediments, particularly in areas with steep slopes and weak base layers.

• The movement of material in a slump is rotational, meaning the top of the displaced material tilts backward while the bottom moves outward and downward.

• The presence of water often plays a crucial role in triggering slumps. Water reduces the friction between soil particles, making the slope less stable.

• Slumps are often seen in coastal cliffs, riverbanks, and areas with loose, unconsolidated materials, especially after heavy rainfall or during rapid erosion.

• Characteristics of Different Types of Landslides: Rockslide: This involves a rapid downslope movement of large blocks of detached bedrock. It usually occurs on steep slopes and is typically triggered by earthquakes, weathering, or human activities.

• Rockfall: A sudden and fast movement of rocks or boulders from a steep slope or cliff. It is often caused by weathering, freeze-thaw cycles, or erosion.

• Slump: As discussed, this is a rotational movement of soil or rock along a concave surface, often influenced by water saturation or erosion.

• Debris fall: This involves a sudden fall of loose debris, such as soil, rocks, and vegetation, from a steep slope. It is similar to a rockfall but includes a mix of materials.

• Common Causes of Landslides: Heavy rainfall leading to saturation of soil and reduced cohesion.

• Earthquakes that disturb the stability of slopes.

• Human activities such as deforestation, mining, and construction.

• Volcanic eruptions that destabilize surrounding areas.

• Examples: The Thistle landslide in Utah, United States (1983) was one of the largest slumps in U.S. history, caused by heavy rainfall and snowmelt.

• Slumps are common in the coastal cliffs of California, where erosion and wave action contribute to slope instability.

• Factors Influencing Slumps: Slopes with clay or loose soil are more prone to slumps due to their weaker cohesive strength.

• Prolonged rainfall or water seepage can exacerbate the likelihood of slumps by reducing soil stability.

• Human activities such as improper land use, deforestation, or construction can increase the risk of slumping by destabilizing slopes.

• Prevention Measures: Planting vegetation to increase slope stability and reduce erosion.

• Constructing retaining walls to support slopes and prevent slippage.

• Implementing effective drainage systems to reduce water infiltration into the soil.

15. The role of lysosomes in a cell is to —

A. Store genetic material

B. Produce energy

C. Carry out intracellular digestion

D. Form proteins

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Carry out intracellular digestion. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Lysosomes are membrane-bound organelles in eukaryotic cells that contain digestive enzymes. Their primary role is to carry out intracellular digestion.

• These enzymes are capable of breaking down proteins, lipids, carbohydrates, and nucleic acids into their basic building blocks, which the cell can then reuse or eliminate.

• Lysosomes are often referred to as the cell’s “garbage disposal system” because they help remove waste materials and recycle cellular components. Hence, the correct answer is to carry out intracellular digestion.

• They play a crucial role in autophagy, a process where the cell digests its own damaged organelles and proteins to maintain cellular health.

• Lysosomes are also involved in immune responses, helping break down pathogens engulfed by cells like macrophages. Thus, lysosomes are essential for maintaining cellular homeostasis.

• Functions of Lysosomes: Lysosomes are responsible for endocytosis, where external substances are engulfed by the cell and digested.

• They participate in exocytosis, where waste or undigested materials are expelled from the cell.

• Lysosomes also play a role in programmed cell death or apoptosis, a process critical for development and preventing diseases like cancer.

• Structure of Lysosomes: Lysosomes are spherical vesicles surrounded by a single membrane, ensuring the digestive enzymes they contain do not harm the rest of the cell.

• They are acidic inside, with a pH of about 4.5-5.0, which facilitates the activity of their hydrolytic enzymes.

• Discovery: Lysosomes were discovered by Belgian cytologist Christian de Duve in 1955.

• Examples of Lysosomal Disorders: Tay-Sachs disease and Gaucher disease are examples of lysosomal storage disorders caused by enzyme deficiencies.

• Other Functions of Lysosomes: In addition to digestion, lysosomes play a role in cellular signaling and energy metabolism.

• They assist in the repair of the plasma membrane by fusing with it when it is damaged.

• Comparison with Other Organelles: Mitochondria: Unlike lysosomes, mitochondria produce energy in the form of ATP through cellular respiration.

• Nucleus: The nucleus stores genetic material, while lysosomes are primarily involved in digestion and waste removal.

• Ribosomes: Ribosomes synthesize proteins, whereas lysosomes break down cellular components.

17. If the Earth’s radius is reduced by 1% keeping its mass same, the acceleration due to gravity will —

A. Increase by about 2%

B. Decrease by about 1%

C. Remain unchanged

D. Decrease by about 2%

Answer A

Solution

Gravitational acceleration (g) is inversely proportional to the square of Earth's radius (R²). A 1% reduction in radius decreases R² by ~2%, increasing g by ~2%. This concept is crucial for understanding gravity's role in physics and geophysics.

Solution

The correct answer is 18–40 years. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Pradhan Mantri Kisan Maandhan Yojana (PM-KMY): This scheme was launched by the Government of India to provide social security to small and marginal farmers by ensuring them financial stability after the age of 60 years.

• Eligibility Age: Farmers between the ages of 18 and 40 years are eligible to enroll in the PM-KMY. This statement is correct.

• Purpose: The scheme aims to offer a regular pension of INR 3,000 per month to eligible farmers after they attain the age of 60 years.

• Contribution: Farmers need to contribute a monthly amount ranging from INR 55 to INR 200, depending on their entry age. The government makes an equal contribution to the farmer's pension fund.

• Implementation: The scheme is implemented by the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers’ Welfare. It is a voluntary and contributory pension scheme.

• Incorrect Age Brackets: The options like "18–30 years," "21–45 years," and "25–50 years" are incorrect, as the scheme explicitly defines the eligible age group as 18 to 40 years.

• Objective of PM-KMY: The scheme is aimed at providing social security to farmers who do not have any pension scheme after retirement.

• It seeks to safeguard the lives of small and marginal farmers in old age when they are no longer able to earn a livelihood.

• The scheme ensures financial independence and dignified living for farmers post-retirement.

• Features of PM-KMY: Voluntary Scheme: Farmers can voluntarily join the scheme by contributing a monthly premium.

• Age-Based Contribution: Farmers aged 18 years need to contribute INR 55 per month, while those aged 40 years need to contribute INR 200 per month.

• Government Contribution: The central government contributes an equal amount to the farmer's pension account.

• Benefits: After attaining 60 years of age, farmers receive a monthly pension of INR 3,000.

• Eligibility Criteria: The applicant must be a small or marginal farmer owning cultivable land up to 2 hectares.

• Farmers must fall within the age bracket of 18 to 40 years at the time of enrollment.

• The farmer must not be enrolled in any other government pension scheme.

• Enrollment Process: Farmers can enroll through the Common Service Centers (CSCs).

• The enrollment requires Aadhaar and bank account details.

• After verification, the farmer can start contributing to the scheme.

• Small and Marginal Farmers: These are farmers who own cultivable land up to 2 hectares. They are the primary beneficiaries of the PM-KMY scheme.

• Exit from the Scheme: In case of the death of the farmer before 60 years of age, the spouse can continue the scheme or withdraw the contributions with interest.

B. 30 days

C. 60 days

D. 90 days

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is 15 days. Key Points Additional Information

• Under Section 187 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023, a Magistrate may authorize the detention of an accused in police custody for a maximum total period of 15 days.

• Unlike the earlier CrPC, the 15 days of police custody need not be continuous. They may be granted in parts during the initial period of judicial remand, subject to the conditions laid down in the BNSS.

• For offences punishable with: Death, life imprisonment, or imprisonment of 10 years or more, police custody can be authorized in parts within the first 60 days of detention.

• Less than 10 years' imprisonment, police custody can be authorized in parts within the first 40 days of detention.

• However, the aggregate period of police custody cannot exceed 15 days.

• Hence, the correct answer is Option 1 – 15 days.

• BNSS, 2023: It replaced the Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC), 1973.

• It came into force on 1 July 2024.

• Remand under BNSS: The total period of detention during investigation may extend up to 60 or 90 days, depending on the nature of the offence.

• Out of this, police custody is limited to a maximum of 15 days; the remaining period, if any, is judicial custody.

25. Constructing sea walls and raising houses to tackle rising sea levels is an example of —

A. Climate change mitigation

B. Carbon sequestration

C. Geoengineering

D. Climate change adaptation

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is Climate change adaptation. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Climate change adaptation refers to actions and strategies aimed at adjusting or modifying systems, structures, or behaviors to minimize the negative impacts of climate change.

• Constructing sea walls and raising houses to tackle rising sea levels are direct examples of adaptation, as they help communities adjust to current and anticipated changes in environmental conditions caused by climate change.

• Adaptation measures focus on reducing vulnerability to climate-related hazards such as floods, droughts, and rising sea levels. These measures are often location-specific and address the immediate needs of affected communities. Hence, the given example is correct.

• Unlike mitigation, adaptation does not aim to prevent climate change but rather seeks to manage its consequences effectively. For instance, constructing infrastructure like levees, flood barriers, and elevated buildings is a common adaptation strategy.

• Adaptation strategies are often implemented by governments, communities, and organizations in response to specific environmental challenges such as coastal erosion, water scarcity, or extreme weather events. Hence, adaptation is the most suitable term for this example.

• Climate change mitigation: Mitigation refers to efforts to reduce or prevent the emission of greenhouse gases, aiming to slow down or reverse climate change.

• Examples include transitioning to renewable energy sources like solar and wind energy, improving energy efficiency, and reforestation to absorb carbon dioxide.

• Mitigation focuses on reducing the causes of climate change rather than managing its effects. For example, reducing fossil fuel usage is a mitigation effort.

• Carbon sequestration: Carbon sequestration involves capturing and storing carbon dioxide from the atmosphere to reduce greenhouse gas concentrations.

• Examples: Planting trees, which absorb CO2, and using carbon capture and storage (CCS) technology in industrial processes.

• It is primarily used as a mitigation strategy rather than adaptation, as it focuses on addressing the root causes of climate change.

• Geoengineering: Geoengineering includes large-scale interventions in the Earth’s climate system to counteract climate change.

• Examples include injecting aerosols into the atmosphere to reflect sunlight or artificial carbon capture methods.

• Geoengineering is experimental and controversial due to its potential unintended consequences on ecosystems.

• Adaptation Strategies: Adaptation strategies are critical for communities already experiencing the impacts of climate change. Examples include constructing flood-resistant buildings and improving water management systems in drought-prone areas.

• Developing early warning systems for extreme weather events is another effective adaptation measure.

• Global Efforts: The Paris Agreement emphasizes the importance of adaptation alongside mitigation to address climate change comprehensively.

• The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) encourages countries to develop national adaptation plans to safeguard vulnerable populations.
