12. Find the part of the sentence that contains an error: The discrepancy between predicted and actual figures (1)/ raised doubts not only about the calculations (2)/ but also about the premises that (3)/ underlay the entire proposal. (4)

A. -1

B. -2

C. -3

D. -4

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is Option 4. Key Points Therefore, the correct answer is 4. Correct Sentence: "The discrepancy between predicted and actual figures raised doubts not only about the calculations but also about the premises that underlie the entire proposal." Additional Information

• The error lies in the verb "underlay" in part 4 of the sentence.

• While "underlay" is the past tense of "underlie," standard English and competitive exams prioritize the present tense for stating fundamental truths or foundational principles of a proposal.

• The word "underlie" (present tense) should be used to indicate that the premises serve as the base for the proposal, regardless of when the doubts were raised.

• Thus, the grammatical error is located in part 4.

• 1: "The discrepancy between predicted and actual figures" – This part is correct; the subject "discrepancy" is correctly paired with the preposition "between."

• 2: "raised doubts not only about the calculations" – This part is correct and sets up the "not only... but also" correlative conjunction.

• 3: "but also about the premises that" – This part maintains perfect parallelism with part 2, repeating the preposition "about" for clarity.

• Verb Confusion: Note the difference between Lie (to recline) and Lay (to put). Underlie follows the pattern of Lie (Lie-Lay-Lain), but is typically used in the present tense for abstract foundations.

23. Select the correct option for the Direct Speech conversion: He observed that, had market liquidity vanished entirely, the exchange would have suspended trading.

A. He said, “If market liquidity vanishes, the exchange suspends trading.”

B. He said, “Had market liquidity vanished entirely, the exchange would have suspended trading.”

C. He said, “The exchange will suspend trading if liquidity vanished.”

D. He said, “Liquidity vanished so trading stopped.”

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Option 2. Key Points Therefore, the correct answer is Option 2. Correct Sentence: "He said, “Had market liquidity vanished entirely, the exchange would have suspended trading”. Additional Information

• Indirect Speech: He observed that, had market liquidity vanished entirely, the exchange would have suspended trading.

• Key Conversions: "had market liquidity vanished entirely, the exchange would have suspended trading" remains unchanged in direct speech: The conditional clause in indirect speech uses the past perfect tense ("had vanished") and "would have suspended," which translates directly into the same structure in direct speech.

• Direct speech retains the same meaning and tense.

• Reporting verb: Indirect: "He observed that..."

• Converted to: "He said," to introduce the direct speech.

• Punctuation and Format: Direct speech is enclosed in quotation marks.

• The sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with a period inside the quotation marks.

• Option 1: "If market liquidity vanishes, the exchange suspends trading." Incorrect tense: Changes the past perfect condition to present tense, altering the original meaning.

• Option 3: "The exchange will suspend trading if liquidity vanished." Incorrect tense: Mixes future tense ("will suspend") with past tense ("vanished"), making the sentence grammatically incorrect.

• Option 4: "Liquidity vanished so trading stopped." Incorrect: Changes the conditional relationship to a cause-effect relationship, altering the meaning entirely.

24. A sentence is given in direct speech. Choose the most accurate indirect form: He said, “I am reading a book.”

A. He said he is reading a book.

B. He said that he was reading a book.

C. He said that he had read a book.

D. He said he read a book.

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Option 2. Key Points Therefore, the correct answer is Option 2. Correct Sentence: He said that he was reading a book. Additional Information

• Indirect Speech: He said that he was reading a book.

• Key Conversions: "I am reading" becomes "he was reading" Pronoun "I" is converted to "he" based on the subject of the reporting verb.

• Present continuous tense "am reading" is converted to past continuous tense "was reading" as the reporting verb "said" is in the past tense.

• Punctuation and Format: The conjunction "that" is added in indirect speech.

• Quotation marks are removed in indirect speech.

• Option 1: "He said he is reading a book." Incorrect tense: "is reading" (present continuous) is not changed to "was reading" (past continuous).

• Option 3: "He said that he had read a book." Incorrect tense: "had read" (past perfect) changes the meaning of the original sentence.

• Option 4: "He said he read a book." Incorrect tense: "read" (simple past) is not appropriate as it does not represent the continuous action of "reading."

25. Rearrange the following sentences to form a meaningful passage: 1. Solar power is among the most promising renewable energy sources. 2. It causes far less environmental harm than fossil fuels. 3. Solar panels convert sunlight into usable electricity. 4. However, storing solar energy efficiently remains a challenge.

A. 2-1-4-3

B. 1-2-4-3

C. 1-3-2-4

D. 4-1-2-3

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is 'Option 3'. Key Points Therefore, the correct sequence is 1-3-2-4. Additional Information

• 1. Solar power is among the most promising renewable energy sources. This is the Introductory Sentence. It establishes the main subject (solar power) and its status as a renewable energy source.

• 3. Solar panels convert sunlight into usable electricity. This is the Explanatory Sentence. Once the topic is introduced, it is logical to explain the basic mechanism of how it works.

• 2. It causes far less environmental harm than fossil fuels. This is the Supporting Detail. After explaining how it works, the passage highlights a key benefit, justifying why it was called "promising" in the first sentence.

• 4. However, storing solar energy efficiently remains a challenge. This is the Concluding/Contrasting Sentence. The word "However" signals a shift to a current limitation or challenge, which naturally finishes the sequence by adding nuance to the earlier praise.

• Option 1 (2-1-4-3): Incorrect because it starts with a pronoun ("It") without an antecedent.

• Option 2 (1-2-4-3): Incorrect because placing the technical explanation (3) at the very end disrupts the flow after a challenge has already been mentioned.

• Option 4 (4-1-2-3): Incorrect because "However" cannot start a passage without a prior context to contrast.
