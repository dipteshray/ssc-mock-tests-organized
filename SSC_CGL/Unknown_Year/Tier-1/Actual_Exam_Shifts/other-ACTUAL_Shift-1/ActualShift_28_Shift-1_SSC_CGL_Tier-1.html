1. Identify the sentence from the passage which is in the passive voice.

A. The caterpillar sheds its skin.

B. The butterfly pumps fluid into its wings.

C. Most of the caterpillar’s tissues are broken down and rebuilt.

D. The adult butterfly searches for a mate.

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Option 3. Key Points Therefore, the correct answer is 3. Additional Information

• In the passive voice, the subject of the sentence receives the action rather than performing it. The structure typically follows: [Object] + [be verb] + [Past Participle (V3)].

• In Option 3, "Most of the caterpillar’s tissues are broken down...", the "tissues" (subject) are not doing the breaking; instead, the biological process is acting upon them.

• The phrase "are broken down" uses the auxiliary verb "are" (be) + "broken" (past participle), which is the hallmark of passive construction.

• In contrast, the other options use the active voice where the subjects (caterpillar, butterfly, adult butterfly) are the ones performing the actions (sheds, pumps, searches).

• Option 1: "The caterpillar sheds its skin." — Active Voice (Subject 'caterpillar' performs the action).

• Option 2: "The butterfly pumps fluid..." — Active Voice (Subject 'butterfly' performs the action).

• Option 4: "The adult butterfly searches..." — Active Voice (Subject 'butterfly' performs the action).

• Grammar Tip: To identify passive voice quickly, look for a form of 'to be' (am, is, are, was, were, be, being, been) followed by a past participle.

2. Which sentence contains a relative clause?

A. The chrysalis becomes firm before transformation.

B. The larva that feeds constantly grows very fast.

C. Adult butterflies live only for a brief period.

D. Wings expand before the first flight.

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Option 2. Key Points Therefore, the correct answer is 2. Additional Information

• A relative clause is a type of dependent clause that describes a noun. It usually begins with a relative pronoun such as who, whom, whose, which, or that.

• In Option 2, the phrase "that feeds constantly" is a relative clause because it provides extra information about the noun "larva."

• Without the relative clause, the main sentence is "The larva grows very fast." The relative clause identifies or describes which larva we are talking about.

• In the other options, there are no relative pronouns or dependent clauses acting as adjectives to modify a noun.

• Option 1: This is a simple sentence using a prepositional phrase ("before transformation") as a time indicator.

• Option 3: This is a simple sentence with an adjective ("brief") and an adverb ("only").

• Option 4: This is a simple sentence with a subject ("Wings"), a verb ("expand"), and a time phrase.

• Grammar Tip: Relative clauses can be defining (necessary for the sentence's meaning) or non-defining (extra information set off by commas). In this case, "that feeds constantly" is a defining relative clause.

17. Change the sentence from active to passive: They will have completed the bridge by 2027.

A. The bridge will being completed by 2027.

B. The bridge will have been completed by 2027.

C. The bridge has been completed by 2027.

D. The bridge will be completed by 2027.

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is '2' i.e. The bridge will have been completed by 2027. Key Points Correct sentence: The bridge will have been completed by 2027. Additional Information

• The given sentence is in the active voice: "They will have completed the bridge by 2027."

• The passive voice construction for future perfect tense is: Subject + will have been + past participle.

• Thus, the sentence changes to: The bridge will have been completed by 2027.

• Active Voice: The subject performs the action on the object. Example: They completed the bridge.

• Passive Voice: The object becomes the subject, and the action is performed on it. Example: The bridge was completed by them.

• Future Perfect Passive Voice Structure: Subject + will have been + past participle. Example: The project will have been completed by next year.
