1. Find out why white silver chloride turns grey in sunlight.

A. Due to rusting of silver in presence of oxygen

B. Due to redox reaction

C. Due to the decomposition of silver chloride into silver and chlorine by light

D. Due to the displacement of silver chloride to silver oxide

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Due to the decomposition of silver chloride into silver and chlorine by light. Key Points Additional Information

• Silver chloride (AgCl) is a white crystalline solid.

• When exposed to sunlight, AgCl decomposes into silver (Ag) and chlorine gas (Cl2).

• The silver formed is a grey metal, which imparts the grey color to the white AgCl.

• This reaction is a type of photodecomposition reaction, where light energy causes the chemical breakdown of a compound.

• Photodecomposition Reaction This is a chemical reaction in which a compound is broken down into simpler substances by the action of light.

• In the case of silver chloride, light energy breaks the bond between silver and chlorine atoms.

• Such reactions are common in photochemistry and are used in various applications including photography.

• Silver Chloride (AgCl) It is a white crystalline solid that is well known for its low solubility in water.

• AgCl is used in photographic films and papers, where it plays a crucial role in capturing images.

• Photographic Process Silver halides like AgCl are used in photographic films because they are sensitive to light.

• When exposed to light, they decompose to form silver, which creates the image on the film.

• This process is the basis for traditional film photography.

4. Part IV A of the Indian Constitution was inserted in the Indian Constitution through __________ Constitutional Amendment Act.

A. 44th

B. 45th

C. 48th

D. 42nd

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act. Key Points Additional Information

• The 42nd Amendment Act, also known as the Constitution (Forty-second Amendment) Act, 1976, was enacted during the Emergency period in India.

• It is considered one of the most comprehensive amendments to the Indian Constitution and was brought in by the Indira Gandhi government.

• This amendment made significant changes to the Constitution, including the insertion of Part IV A, which deals with the Fundamental Duties of the citizens.

• The 42nd Amendment also attempted to reduce the power of the judiciary and increase the power of the Parliament.

• Part IV A (Fundamental Duties) Part IV A was added to the Constitution of India by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976.

• It consists of Article 51A which enumerates the Fundamental Duties of the citizens of India.

• These duties are inspired by the Constitution of the former Soviet Union and aim to remind the citizens of their moral obligations towards the nation.

• There are currently 11 Fundamental Duties listed in Article 51A.

• 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 Known as a "mini-Constitution" due to its wide-ranging amendments.

• It made extensive changes to the Preamble, Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles, and the structure of the executive and judiciary.

• This amendment curtailed the power of the judiciary and strengthened the central government.

• Many of the provisions of this amendment were later undone by the 44th Amendment Act, 1978.

• Emergency Period The Emergency in India was declared by President Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed upon the advice of Prime Minister Indira Gandhi from 1975 to 1977.

• This period is considered one of the most controversial times in Indian political history, marked by the suspension of elections and civil liberties.

• The 42nd Amendment was passed during this period, and it significantly altered the balance of power between the executive, legislature, and judiciary.

6. The outermost part of the Earth is known as ________.

A. volcano

B. the crust

C. the core

D. the mantle

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is the crust. Key Points Additional Information

• The crust is the outermost layer of the Earth, varying in thickness from about 5 km under the oceans to about 70 km under the continents.

• The crust is made up of a variety of rocks, primarily granite and basalt.

• It forms the solid surface of the Earth, where we live, and is divided into several tectonic plates.

• The Earth's crust is distinct from the underlying mantle and core, which are composed of different materials and have different physical properties.

• Volcano A volcano is an opening in the Earth's crust through which molten lava, ash, and gases are ejected.

• Volcanic activity is a result of the movement of tectonic plates and the presence of magma beneath the Earth's surface.

• Volcanoes can form on land or underwater, and they can create mountains and islands over time.

• The Core The core is the innermost part of the Earth, composed mainly of iron and nickel.

• It is divided into two parts: the solid inner core and the liquid outer core.

• The movement of the liquid outer core generates the Earth's magnetic field.

• The Mantle The mantle is the layer of the Earth located between the crust and the core.

• It is composed of silicate rocks that are rich in iron and magnesium.

• The mantle extends to a depth of about 2,900 kilometers (1,800 miles) and is involved in tectonic activity and convection currents.

9. Why does the milkman add a very small amount of baking soda to fresh milk?

A. To reduce the pH of the fresh milk from 6 to slightly more acidic

B. To increase the pH of the fresh milk from 6 to slightly alkaline

C. To maintain the pH of the fresh milk at 6 for a longer time

D. To reduce the pH of the fresh milk from 6 to slightly alkaline

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Option 2: To increase the pH of the fresh milk from 6 to slightly alkaline. Key Points Additional Information

• Adding baking soda, which is a mild base, to fresh milk increases its pH, making it slightly alkaline.

• This process helps in delaying the souring of milk, which is caused by the production of lactic acid by bacteria.

• By making the milk slightly alkaline, the growth of acid-producing bacteria is slowed down, thereby preserving the milk for a longer duration.

• This practice is particularly useful in warmer climates where milk spoils faster due to higher temperatures.

• pH Scale The pH scale measures how acidic or basic a substance is, ranging from 0 to 14.

• A pH of 7 is considered neutral, below 7 is acidic, and above 7 is basic (alkaline).

• Milk typically has a pH of around 6, making it slightly acidic.

• Baking Soda (Sodium Bicarbonate) Baking soda, chemically known as sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3), is a mild alkaline compound.

• It is commonly used in baking to cause dough to rise by producing carbon dioxide gas.

• In addition to its culinary uses, baking soda is also used for cleaning, deodorizing, and as an antacid.

• Lactic Acid and Milk Souring Lactic acid is produced by lactic acid bacteria during the fermentation of lactose (milk sugar).

• The accumulation of lactic acid lowers the pH of milk, leading to its souring.

• Souring is a natural process that occurs as milk ages and is influenced by temperature and bacterial activity.

10. In the context of periodicity, a unit called picometre is used to measure the ________.

A. atomic radius

B. molar mass

C. atomic density

D. spin quantum number

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is atomic radius. Key Points Additional Information

• The atomic radius is measured in picometres (pm), where 1 pm = 10-12 meters.

• The atomic radius is defined as the distance from the nucleus of an atom to the outer boundary of the electron cloud.

• The atomic radius varies across the periodic table, generally decreasing from left to right across a period and increasing down a group.

• Understanding atomic radius is crucial for explaining the physical and chemical properties of elements, including bonding and reactivity.

• Atomic Radius: The atomic radius is a measure of the size of an atom, usually the mean or typical distance from the nucleus to the boundary of the surrounding electron cloud.

• In a group, the atomic radius increases from top to bottom due to the addition of electron shells.

• In a period, the atomic radius decreases from left to right due to the increase in nuclear charge, which pulls electrons closer to the nucleus.

• Molar Mass: Molar mass is the mass of a given substance (chemical element or chemical compound) divided by the amount of substance, measured in grams per mole (g/mol).

• It is used to convert between the mass of a substance and the amount in moles.

• Atomic Density: Atomic density is the number of atoms per unit volume of a material.

• It is often measured in atoms per cubic centimeter (atoms/cm3).

• Spin Quantum Number: The spin quantum number (s) describes the intrinsic angular momentum (spin) of an electron within an atom.

• It can have values of +1/2 or -1/2, indicating the two possible spin states of an electron.

14. Money supply is a ____ concept.

A. stock

B. measurable

C. non-measurable

D. flow

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is stock. Key Points Additional Information

• Money supply is considered a stock concept because it represents the total amount of money available in an economy at a specific point in time.

• Unlike a flow concept, which is measured over a period of time, a stock concept is measured at one particular time.

• The money supply includes various components such as currency in circulation, demand deposits, and other liquid assets.

• Central banks and government agencies typically measure the money supply to monitor and manage the economy.

• Stock vs. Flow Concepts A stock concept is a quantity measured at a particular point in time. Examples include the money supply, wealth, and the number of goods in inventory.

• A flow concept is a quantity measured over a period of time. Examples include income, expenditure, and production.

• Components of Money Supply M1: This is the narrow measure of money supply, which includes currency in circulation and demand deposits (checking accounts).

• M2: This is a broader measure, which includes M1 plus savings accounts, small time deposits, and non-institutional money market funds.

• M3: This includes M2 plus large time deposits, institutional money market funds, and other larger liquid assets.

• Importance of Money Supply The money supply is a critical factor in determining inflation levels, interest rates, and overall economic stability.

• Central banks, such as the Federal Reserve or the Reserve Bank of India, use measures of the money supply to implement monetary policy.

• Changes in the money supply can affect the economy's performance, influencing everything from consumer spending to business investment.
