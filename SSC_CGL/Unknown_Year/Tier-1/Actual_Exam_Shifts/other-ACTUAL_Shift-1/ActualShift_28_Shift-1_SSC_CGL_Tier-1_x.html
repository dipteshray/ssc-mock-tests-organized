1. The Chabahar Port initiative mainly aims to strengthen which aspect?

A. Defense cooperation in the Indian Ocean

B. Extraction of deep-sea resources

C. Promotion of leisure travel in Iran

D. Expansion of regional trade and connectivity

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is Expansion of regional trade and connectivity. Key Points Additional Information

• The Chabahar Port is strategically located in southeastern Iran, along the Gulf of Oman, and serves as a critical trade and transit hub in the region.

• India has invested in Chabahar Port to bypass Pakistan and access Afghanistan and Central Asia for trade and connectivity purposes, fostering regional economic development.

• The initiative supports India's ambition to strengthen connectivity with energy-rich Central Asia and promotes inter-regional trade cooperation.

• The port is a key part of the International North-South Transport Corridor (INSTC), linking India with Central Asia, Russia, and Europe for better transportation of goods and services.

• The Chabahar Port also contributes to enhancing maritime cooperation between India and Iran, thereby improving geopolitical ties and reducing dependency on other trade routes.

• Defense cooperation in the Indian Ocean: The Indian Ocean is a strategically important region, and defense cooperation often focuses on ensuring maritime security, countering piracy, and facilitating naval exercises.

• India collaborates with several countries, including the United States, Japan, and Australia under frameworks like the Quadrilateral Security Dialogue (Quad) for defense cooperation.

• However, the Chabahar Port initiative is not directly aimed at defense cooperation but is predominantly focused on trade and connectivity.

• Extraction of deep-sea resources: Deep-sea resource extraction involves mining minerals, hydrocarbons, and other valuable resources from the seabed.

• While India is exploring deep-sea mining projects, especially in the Indian Ocean, the Chabahar Port is not linked to this objective.

• The port’s primary goal is to facilitate trade routes and connectivity rather than resource extraction.

• Promotion of leisure travel in Iran: Tourism and leisure travel in Iran are promoted through cultural and historical sites like Persepolis, Isfahan, and Tehran.

• The Chabahar Port initiative does not have any direct link to leisure travel promotion but focuses on economic and trade cooperation.

• While tourism remains an important aspect of Iran's economy, this port is intended to serve as a trade hub rather than a tourist destination.

2. Which scheme is specifically designed to upgrade village road networks in India?

A. Bharatmala Pariyojana

B. Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana

C. National Highways Development Project

D. Setu Bharatam Programme

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana. Key Points Additional Information

• Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana (PMGSY) was launched in December 2000 by the Government of India.

• The scheme is specifically designed to provide all-weather road connectivity to unconnected villages and rural areas across India.

• The primary goal of PMGSY is to bridge the rural-urban divide by enhancing access to essential services like healthcare, education, and markets for villagers.

• Under PMGSY, villages with a population of 500 or more in plain areas and 250 or more in hilly, tribal, or desert areas are targeted for road connectivity.

• The scheme is funded by the Central Government, with contributions from State Governments, and is implemented by local authorities and state-level agencies.

• PMGSY has significantly contributed to rural development by increasing road density in India and improving transportation facilities for villagers.

• Bharatmala Pariyojana Bharatmala Pariyojana is a centrally-sponsored and funded road and highway development program launched in 2017.

• Its primary aim is to develop economic corridors, border roads, coastal roads, and expressways to improve freight movement across India.

• The program focuses on interconnecting major cities, industrial hubs, and ports to enhance India’s logistical efficiency.

• Bharatmala Pariyojana is not limited to rural road development but emphasizes the construction of highways and expressways across the country.

• National Highways Development Project (NHDP) The NHDP was initiated in 1998 by the Government of India to develop and expand the national highway network.

• Its focus is on building and upgrading highways to facilitate better transportation and connectivity between major cities and towns.

• NHDP does not cater to rural or village road networks but primarily concentrates on enhancing national highways and inter-state connectivity.

• It includes projects like the Golden Quadrilateral and North-South and East-West Corridors.

• Setu Bharatam Programme Setu Bharatam Programme was launched in 2016 to ensure the construction of railway over bridges (ROBs) and railway under bridges (RUBs) across the country.

• The program aims to eliminate level crossings on national highways for safer and smoother transportation.

• It primarily targets the removal of bottlenecks in highway traffic caused by railway crossings and is unrelated to village road connectivity.

3. The Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 recognizes electronic records in harmony with which earlier legal framework?

A. Information Technology Act, 2000

B. Indian Evidence Act, 1872

C. Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023

D. Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Indian Evidence Act, 1872. Key Points Additional Information

• The Indian Evidence Act, 1872 provides the foundational legal framework to govern the admissibility of evidence in courts of law in India.

• The Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 recognizes electronic records as admissible evidence, aligning itself with the provisions of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872.

• Under Section 65B of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, electronic records are admissible as evidence if certain procedural requirements are met.

• The Act defines electronic evidence as including data stored in digital formats, such as emails, digital photographs, and computer-generated documents.

• The integration of electronic records into the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 ensures legal recognition of digital evidence in court proceedings, enabling a comprehensive legal framework for the digital era.

• Information Technology Act, 2000 The Information Technology Act, 2000 provides legal recognition to electronic records, digital signatures, and electronic governance in India.

• It lays out the framework for preventing cybercrimes and ensuring the security of data in electronic forms.

• While it recognizes electronic records, its primary purpose is to govern electronic commerce, cybercrimes, and digital communication rather than evidentiary matters.

• Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 This Act focuses on safeguarding personal data and ensuring data privacy for individuals in India.

• It regulates the processing of personal data, establishes rights for data principals, and imposes obligations on data fiduciaries.

• However, it does not specifically govern the admissibility of electronic evidence in judicial proceedings.

• Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 The Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC) provides the procedural framework for criminal trials and investigations in India.

• It deals with the processes of investigation, arrest, trial, and sentencing but does not focus on evidence admissibility for electronic records.

4. Which state is the first to bring a Uniform Civil Code into effect?

A. Gujarat

B. Uttarakhand

C. Maharashtra

D. Goa

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Uttarakhand. Key Points Additional Information

• Uttarakhand is the first Indian state to implement a Uniform Civil Code (UCC), aiming to provide one law for all citizens regardless of religion, caste, or gender.

• In May 2022, Uttarakhand announced the formation of a committee under Justice Ranjana Prakash Desai to prepare a draft for UCC implementation.

• The Uniform Civil Code in Uttarakhand covers various aspects such as marriage, divorce, inheritance, and adoption, ensuring equality and justice for all citizens.

• UCC implementation in Uttarakhand aligns with Article 44 of the Indian Constitution, which encourages the state to secure a Uniform Civil Code across India.

• The decision to implement UCC in Uttarakhand was taken to promote harmony and eliminate legal disparities arising from different personal laws for different communities.

• Goa: Goa is the only Indian state that has a common family law, known as the Portuguese Civil Code of 1867, applicable to all its residents irrespective of religion.

• The civil code governs marriage, divorce, succession, and adoption uniformly, making it distinct from other states in India.

• However, the Goa Civil Code is not considered a full-fledged Uniform Civil Code as it has specific provisions for certain communities.

• Gujarat: While Gujarat has not implemented UCC, it has supported the idea at the national level, with political leaders advocating for its implementation.

• The state government has also backed initiatives for legal reforms in personal laws to ensure equality among citizens.

• Maharashtra: Maharashtra has not introduced UCC but has been actively involved in discussions on legal reforms that aim at gender equality and social justice.

• The state has a significant role in addressing issues of personal laws, especially concerning women's rights in marriage and inheritance.

• Uniform Civil Code (UCC): UCC refers to the proposal to replace personal laws based on the scriptures and customs of each major religious community in India with a common set governing every citizen.

• Personal laws in India currently differ based on religion, covering areas like marriage, divorce, inheritance, and adoption.

• The UCC aims to ensure equality and uniformity in legal matters, promoting secularism and national integration.

• Article 44 of the Indian Constitution mentions the UCC as a directive principle of state policy, urging states to work towards its implementation.

5. The term ‘Permaculture’ is most closely related to which idea?

A. Farming on frozen soils

B. Lab-based cultivation in gel media

C. Cleaning oil spills through microbes

D. Creating sustainable farm ecosystems

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is Creating sustainable farm ecosystems. Key Points Additional Information

• Permaculture is a philosophy and approach to agriculture focused on creating sustainable and self-sufficient ecosystems.

• It incorporates principles that mimic the natural processes of ecosystems, promoting long-term sustainability.

• It emphasizes the integration of land, resources, people, and the environment through mutually beneficial synergies, inspired by natural ecosystems.

• The practice involves techniques like companion planting, water conservation, soil regeneration, and waste recycling to create a closed-loop system.

• Permaculture is not limited to farming but can also be applied to designing sustainable communities, homes, and urban spaces.

• Farming on frozen soils: This concept is not related to permaculture. Farming on frozen soils, also known as permafrost agriculture, is extremely challenging as frozen ground lacks the necessary conditions for plant growth.

• It may involve techniques such as greenhouses, raised beds, and soil thawing methods, which are typically not sustainable or efficient in the long term.

• Lab-based cultivation in gel media: This refers to techniques such as tissue culture, where plants are grown in controlled environments on nutrient-rich gel media.

• It is commonly used in research, plant breeding, and the propagation of disease-free plants but does not align with the principles of permaculture, which focuses on natural ecosystems.

• Cleaning oil spills through microbes: This refers to bioremediation, a process where living organisms, such as microbes, are used to break down or remove harmful pollutants like oil spills.

• While bioremediation is an eco-friendly approach to pollution control, it is unrelated to the agricultural and ecological principles of permaculture.

• Option 1: Rajasthan, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh While Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh are correct, Gujarat does not have significant portions of the Chambal badlands.

• Gujarat is geographically distinct from the Chambal basin and is more known for its arid regions, such as the Rann of Kutch.

• These states are primarily part of the fertile Indo-Gangetic plain, known for agriculture.

• Option 3: Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Gujarat Although Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh are correct, Gujarat is incorrectly included in this option.

• Gujarat is not part of the Chambal basin and does not feature badlands formed by river erosion.

• Main Theme: Chambal Badlands The Chambal badlands are a result of the fluvial erosion caused by the Chambal River and its tributaries over millions of years.

• The term "badlands" refers to areas with heavily eroded, barren landscapes that are unsuitable for agriculture or habitation.

• Such regions are ecologically significant, providing habitats for various species, including the endangered gharial and the Indian skimmer.

• Conservation efforts in the Chambal region have been initiated to protect its unique biodiversity and prevent further soil erosion.

• According to belief, the locals prayed to the Goddess Mahakali to protect them from the plague, and the festival was established to honor her intervention.

• Malaria: Malaria is a mosquito-borne disease caused by Plasmodium parasites, transmitted through the bites of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes.

• It leads to symptoms like fever, chills, and flu-like illness and can cause severe complications if untreated.

• Although malaria has been a major health concern in India, its connection to the Bonalu festival is not established.

• It was eradicated globally by 1980 through extensive vaccination campaigns, becoming the first disease to be eradicated.

• While smallpox has caused significant suffering in the past, it is unrelated to the origins of the Bonalu festival.

• Bonalu Festival: The festival typically takes place during the Ashada Masam (July-August) in the Telugu calendar.

• Bonalu is celebrated at various temples dedicated to the Goddess, including the Simhavahini Mahakali Temple and the Ujjaini Mahakali Temple in Secunderabad.

• Bead production at Chanhudaro involved intricate processes such as cutting, drilling, polishing, and coloring, showcasing the high level of craftsmanship achieved by the Harappans.

• Archaeological findings suggest the presence of bead workshops, tools, and raw materials, indicating that bead manufacturing was a key economic activity in the region.

• Beads produced at Chanhudaro were widely traded across the Indus Valley region and beyond, highlighting the site's significance in the Harappan trade network.

• Textile Production: While textile production was an important activity in the Indus Valley Civilization, it was not the specialized activity at Chanhudaro.

• The Harappans were skilled in producing cotton textiles, evidenced by the discovery of cotton fragments at Harappan sites like Mohenjo-Daro.

• They used spinning wheels and dyes obtained from plants to produce colorful textiles, which were likely traded extensively.

• Agriculture: Agriculture was a fundamental aspect of the Indus Valley Civilization, with crops such as wheat, barley, and pulses being cultivated.

• The Harappans practiced advanced irrigation techniques and plowing methods to sustain their agricultural economy.

• While agriculture was prevalent across the civilization, it was not a specialized activity at Chanhudaro.

• Making Stone Tools: Stone tools were commonly used by the Harappans for various purposes, including hunting, farming, and construction.

• Sites like Burzahom in Kashmir are more closely associated with the production of stone tools rather than Chanhudaro.

• Chanhudaro's specialization in bead manufacturing makes it distinct from other Harappan sites focused on stone tool production.

Solution

• Lord William Bentinck served as the Governor-General of India from 1828 to 1835.

• In 1829, Bentinck enacted the regulation banning the practice of Sati, which was the custom of widows immolating themselves on their husbands’ funeral pyres.

• The regulation was titled Regulation XVII of 1829, which declared Sati illegal and punishable by law.

• Bentinck’s decision to outlaw Sati was influenced by humanitarian considerations and extensive public debate, including efforts by social reformers such as Raja Ram Mohan Roy.

• The abolition of Sati marked a significant step in the modernization of Indian society under British rule.

• Lord Curzon Lord Curzon served as the Viceroy of India from 1899 to 1905.

• He is known for his administrative reforms and partitioning of Bengal in 1905, which led to widespread protests and ultimately the Swadeshi Movement.

• Curzon also focused on improving infrastructure, education, and preserving Indian heritage by establishing the Archaeological Survey of India.

• Lord Dalhousie Lord Dalhousie was the Governor-General of India from 1848 to 1856.

• He is best known for his Doctrine of Lapse policy, which allowed the British to annex princely states in India if they lacked a direct male heir.

• Dalhousie introduced several reforms, including the establishment of railways, telegraphs, and postal services in India.

• He also played a significant role in promoting education by founding institutions such as Engineering Colleges and Universities.

• Lord Canning Lord Canning served as the Governor-General of India from 1856 to 1862 and later became the first Viceroy of India after the Revolt of 1857.

• He is known for his efforts to restore order after the revolt and for introducing the Indian Penal Code and the Code of Criminal Procedure.

• Lord Canning oversaw the transfer of power from the East India Company to the British Crown in 1858.

• The Practice of Sati Sati was a Hindu custom wherein widows voluntarily or forcibly immolated themselves on their deceased husband's funeral pyres.

• The practice was prevalent in certain regions of India and was often associated with notions of honor and devotion.

• Efforts to abolish Sati gained momentum in the early 19th century, led by social reformers like Raja Ram Mohan Roy, who criticized the practice as inhumane.

• Lord William Bentinck’s ban on Sati was a landmark event in promoting social reforms during British rule.

16. The special power of the Rajya Sabha under Article 249 allows Parliament to make laws on a subject from which list?

A. State List

B. Union List

C. Concurrent List

D. Residuary powers

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is State List. Key Points Additional Information

• Under Article 249 of the Indian Constitution, the Rajya Sabha has the special power to authorize Parliament to legislate on matters enumerated in the State List if it deems that it is necessary in the national interest.

• A resolution must be passed by a two-thirds majority of the members present and voting in the Rajya Sabha to enable Parliament to make laws on subjects from the State List.

• Once such a resolution is passed, Parliament can legislate on the specified subject for a period of up to one year, which can be extended further by similar resolutions.

• This provision is an exception to the division of powers under the Seventh Schedule of the Constitution, which normally reserves subjects in the State List exclusively for the states.

• Examples of subjects in the State List include police, public health, agriculture, and trade within the state.

• Union List: This list contains subjects of national importance over which Parliament has exclusive powers to legislate under normal circumstances.

• Examples include defense, foreign affairs, atomic energy, railways, and communication.

• Parliament can legislate on Union List subjects without requiring any resolution from the Rajya Sabha.

• Concurrent List: The Concurrent List includes subjects where both the Union and State governments can legislate, but in case of conflict, the Union law prevails.

• Examples include education, marriage and divorce, bankruptcy, and forests.

• Article 249 does not grant the Rajya Sabha special powers over the Concurrent List, as it already allows joint legislative authority.

• Residuary Powers: Residuary powers refer to subjects that are not enumerated in the Union, State, or Concurrent Lists.

• Under Article 248, Parliament has exclusive authority to legislate on residuary subjects.

• Examples include matters related to cyber laws and space exploration.

• Article 249 does not apply to residuary powers, as Parliament already holds exclusive rights over these subjects.

• Significance of Article 249: Article 249 ensures legislative flexibility in situations where a matter of State List importance affects national interests.

• This provision reflects the federal structure of India while maintaining the supremacy of Parliament in matters of national importance.

B. Convex mirror

C. Concave mirror

D. Cylindrical mirror

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Concave mirror. Key Points Additional Information

• A concave mirror is ideal for concentrating sunlight in a solar furnace as it converges light rays to a focal point.

• Light rays parallel to the principal axis of a concave mirror reflect and meet at the focal point, creating intense heat.

• This property of concentrating light makes concave mirrors suitable for applications requiring high temperatures, such as solar furnaces.

• Solar furnaces use this principle to focus sunlight for heating, melting, or generating energy.

• The efficiency of energy concentration depends on the size and curvature of the concave mirror.

• They are unsuitable for concentrating sunlight as they do not converge or focus light rays.

• Common applications include everyday mirrors, periscopes, and optical instruments.

• Convex mirror Convex mirrors diverge light rays, spreading them outward rather than converging them.

• They are used in rear-view mirrors and security mirrors due to their wide field of view.

• They cannot be used in solar furnaces as they do not focus light to a single point.

• Cylindrical mirror Cylindrical mirrors focus light in one dimension, forming a line rather than a point.

• They are used in optical and imaging devices, but their properties make them unsuitable for solar furnaces requiring concentrated energy at a single focal point.

• Solar furnace A solar furnace is a device that uses concentrated sunlight to generate high temperatures.

• It often involves an array of concave mirrors or lenses to focus sunlight on a specific point.

• Applications of solar furnaces include material testing, power generation, and waste treatment.

25. Which kind of sale involves transferring only a part of government ownership of a Public Sector Undertaking to private investors?

A. Minority sale

B. Complete sale

C. Leasing

D. Auction

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Minority sale. Key Points Additional Information

• A minority sale refers to the process where the government sells a part of its ownership stake in a Public Sector Undertaking (PSU) to private investors, while retaining majority control.

• Through minority sales, the government aims to raise funds without completely privatizing the PSU.

• The government continues to hold more than 50% of the ownership in the entity to retain decision-making power and strategic control.

• Minority sale is often part of the disinvestment strategy by the government to meet fiscal goals or improve the efficiency of PSUs.

• Examples of minority sales include partial divestment of entities like Oil and Natural Gas Corporation (ONGC), Indian Oil Corporation (IOC), and Bharat Petroleum Corporation Limited (BPCL).

• Complete sale: A complete sale involves transferring the entire ownership of a Public Sector Undertaking (PSU) from the government to private investors.

• This results in full privatization of the entity, where the government relinquishes all control and decision-making authority.

• Complete sales are typically undertaken for PSUs that are no longer strategically important or have been consistently underperforming.

• An example of complete privatization is the sale of Air India to Tata Group in 2021.

• Leasing: Leasing is the process of granting temporary rights to use a government-owned asset or property to private entities or investors.

• The government retains ownership of the asset, and the lessee pays periodic rent or lease payments for using the property.

• Leasing is often used for infrastructure projects like ports, railways, or airports, where private entities manage operations without ownership transfer.

• An example includes the leasing of railway stations and airports under Public-Private Partnership (PPP) models.

• Auction: An auction is a competitive bidding process where government-owned assets or shares are sold to the highest bidder.

• Auction sales may involve complete or partial ownership transfer, depending on the terms set by the government.

• This method ensures transparency and maximizes revenue generation for the government.

• Auctioning is frequently used for the sale of spectrum licenses, coal blocks, and other natural resources.

• Main Theme: The question addresses the concept of disinvestment or privatization strategies undertaken by the government for Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs).

• Disinvestment is critical for generating revenue, reducing fiscal deficit, and improving the operational efficiency of PSUs.

• It also helps attract private investment, enhances competition, and ensures better utilization of resources.
