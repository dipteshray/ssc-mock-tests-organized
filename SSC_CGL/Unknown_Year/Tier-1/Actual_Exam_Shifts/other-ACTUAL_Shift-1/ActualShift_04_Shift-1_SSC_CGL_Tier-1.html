1. Which of the following statements about Gwalior Fort are correct? 1. The fort extends across a long ridge covering more than 2 km. 2. The southern ascent has Jain rock-cut images carved into the hillside. 3. The Caturbhuj Temple inside the fort complex follows Vaishnava architectural tradition. Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 and 2 only

B. 2 and 3 only

C. 1 and 3 only

D. 1, 2 and 3

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is 4) 1, 2 and 3. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Statement 1: The fort extends across a long ridge covering more than 2 km. The Gwalior Fort, located in the state of Madhya Pradesh, India, is situated on a long and steep hill known as the Gopachal hill.

• The fort spans a distance of over 2 km in length and is strategically positioned to provide a commanding view of the surrounding plains. It has been a significant stronghold for centuries.

• The fort consists of several palaces, temples, and water tanks, making it a grand architectural marvel. Hence, statement 1 is correct.

• Statement 2: The southern ascent has Jain rock-cut images carved into the hillside. On the southern approach to the fort, there are numerous Jain rock-cut sculptures, which are some of the most remarkable features of Gwalior Fort.

• These images are intricately carved into the hillside and depict various Tirthankaras (Jain spiritual leaders). The sculptures date back to the 15th century and are an essential part of Jain religious art.

• The most prominent among these is the colossal statue of Adinatha, which is over 50 feet tall, making it one of the largest Jain sculptures in the world. Hence, statement 2 is correct.

• Statement 3: The Caturbhuj Temple inside the fort complex follows Vaishnava architectural tradition. The Caturbhuj Temple, located within the Gwalior Fort, is dedicated to Lord Vishnu, a principal deity in the Vaishnava tradition.

• The temple is notable for its Hindu architectural style, which includes intricate carvings and sculptures. It reflects the influence of the Vaishnavite tradition, which emphasizes devotion to Lord Vishnu.

• Interestingly, the temple is also significant historically, as it contains an inscription that includes the earliest recorded use of the number "zero" in India.

• Its architectural and historical significance makes it a valuable part of the fort's heritage. Hence, statement 3 is correct.

• Historical Significance of Gwalior Fort: The fort has a rich history and has been ruled by various dynasties, including the Tomars, Mughals, Marathas, and British.

• It is often referred to as the "Gibraltar of India" due to its immense strength and strategic location.

• Architectural Highlights: The fort houses several remarkable structures, such as the Man Singh Palace, the Gujari Mahal (now a museum), and the Sas-Bahu Temple.

• The Teli Ka Mandir, another temple within the fort, is notable for its unique blend of Dravidian and Nagara architectural styles.

• Religious Importance: The Jain rock-cut sculptures and temples within the fort complex are important pilgrimage sites for the Jain community.

• The presence of Hindu temples like the Caturbhuj Temple also highlights the cultural and religious diversity of the region.

• Location: Gwalior Fort is situated on a hilltop in Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh.

• Age: The fort's origins date back to the 6th century, though much of its current structure was built during the rule of the Tomar dynasty in the 15th century.

• UNESCO Status: While not a UNESCO World Heritage Site, the fort is recognized for its historical and architectural importance.

• Tourism: The fort is a major tourist attraction and is also known for hosting the Tansen Music Festival annually.

9. Gorges are typically formed in which type of geological material?

A. Soft sediments

B. Hard rocks

C. Glacial deposits

D. Volcanic ash

Answer B

Solution

Gorges are deep, narrow valleys primarily formed by river erosion in hard rocks. Hard rocks resist erosion better than soft sediments, allowing rivers to carve steep-sided valleys over time. Examples include the Grand Canyon and Tiger Leaping Gorge. Rivers exploit faults in hard rock formations, creating rugged landscapes ideal for gorge formation.

11. What is meant by the rate of natural increase of population?

A. Birth rate multiplied by death rate

B. Ratio of workers to total population

C. Difference between birth rate and death rate

D. Growth caused only by migration

Answer C

Solution

The rate of natural increase measures population growth caused by reproduction and mortality, calculated as the difference between birth and death rates. High rates are common in developing nations, while developed countries often experience low or negative rates due to aging populations and declining births.

B. Telomere extension

C. DNA damage repair

D. RNA synthesis

Solution

The question explores the primary function of the enzyme telomerase. Telomerase extends telomeres, repetitive DNA sequences at chromosome ends, compensating for shortening during replication. 23. Consider the following statements regarding atmospheric layers: 1. Most weather conditions occur in the stratosphere. 2. The ozone layer is primarily located in the troposphere. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

A. Statement 1 only

B. Statement 2 only

C. Both 1 and 2

D. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is Neither 1 nor 2. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Statement 1: "Most weather conditions occur in the stratosphere." The majority of weather phenomena such as rain, thunderstorms, and clouds occur in the troposphere, which is the lowest layer of Earth's atmosphere.

• The troposphere extends up to approximately 8–15 kilometers from the Earth’s surface, depending on the geographical location.

• The stratosphere, lying above the troposphere, is characterized by stable air and minimal turbulence, making it unsuitable for weather activities.

• As weather conditions do not occur in the stratosphere, this statement is incorrect.

• Statement 2: "The ozone layer is primarily located in the troposphere." The ozone layer is predominantly found in the stratosphere, specifically between 15–35 kilometers above the Earth’s surface.

• This region, known as the ozonosphere, plays a critical role in absorbing harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the Sun.

• The troposphere contains only trace amounts of ozone, primarily formed due to human activities and photochemical reactions near the Earth's surface.

• Since the ozone layer is not located in the troposphere, this statement is incorrect.

• Hence, the correct answer is Neither 1 nor 2.

• Troposphere: The troposphere is the lowest layer of the atmosphere, extending up to 8–15 kilometers, depending on latitude and season.

• It is the layer where weather phenomena such as clouds, precipitation, and storms occur.

• The temperature decreases with altitude in this layer, a phenomenon known as the lapse rate.

• Stratosphere: The stratosphere lies above the troposphere, extending from approximately 15–50 kilometers above the Earth’s surface.

• It contains the ozone layer, which absorbs and blocks harmful UV radiation from the Sun.

• Unlike the troposphere, the stratosphere experiences a temperature increase with altitude due to ozone absorption of solar radiation.

• This layer is stable, making it suitable for commercial aviation.

• Ozone Layer: The ozone layer is primarily located in the stratosphere, between 15–35 kilometers above the surface.

• It plays a vital role in shielding life on Earth from harmful UV radiation, which can cause skin cancer and damage crops.

• The presence of chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) and other pollutants can lead to ozone depletion, resulting in phenomena like the ozone hole.

• Atmospheric Layers: The Earth’s atmosphere is divided into several layers based on temperature and composition: Troposphere, Stratosphere, Mesosphere, Thermosphere, and Exosphere.

• Each layer has distinct properties and functions. For example, the troposphere contains most of the air mass and is the site of weather, while the thermosphere hosts phenomena like the auroras.

• Weather and Climate: Weather refers to short-term atmospheric conditions, including temperature, humidity, wind, and precipitation.

• Climate is the long-term average of weather patterns in a region, influenced by factors like geography and ocean currents.
