9. Consider the following statements on glacial landforms: Statement 1: Hanging valleys often join main valleys at higher elevation levels. Statement 2: Spurs of hanging valleys may appear as sharply cut triangular facets. Which of the statements is/are correct?

A. Only 1

B. Only 2

C. Both 1 and 2

D. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Both 1 and 2. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Statement 1: Hanging valleys often join main valleys at higher elevation levels. Hanging valleys are formed when a tributary glacier meets the main glacier during glaciation. After the glaciers retreat, the tributary valley often remains suspended above the floor of the main valley.

• They are characterized by the abrupt elevation difference between the hanging valley floor and the main valley floor, leading to the formation of waterfalls in many cases.

• This phenomenon occurs because the main glacier erodes its valley more deeply compared to the tributary glacier. Hence, statement 1 is correct.

• Statement 2: Spurs of hanging valleys may appear as sharply cut triangular facets. Spurs are ridges of land that project into a valley. In the case of hanging valleys, the spurs are often truncated or sharply cut due to the erosive action of glaciers.

• These triangular facets are a result of the glacial erosion that slices through the spurs, leaving behind distinct triangular shapes.

• The triangular appearance of these facets is a significant geomorphological feature in glacial landscapes. Hence, statement 2 is correct.

• Hence, the correct answer is Both 1 and 2.

• Glacial Landforms: Hanging Valley: A hanging valley is formed as a result of differential erosion caused by glaciers. The main glacier cuts deeper into the valley floor than its tributaries, leaving the tributary valleys "hanging" above the main valley. Waterfalls are often found at the junction of the hanging valley and the main valley.

• Truncated Spurs: Spurs are land projections formed by river erosion. During glaciation, glaciers cut through these spurs, leaving behind sharply truncated triangular facets. These are a hallmark of glaciated landscapes.

• Other Glacial Features: Apart from hanging valleys and truncated spurs, glaciers also form features like cirques (bowl-shaped depressions), moraines (accumulations of glacial debris), and fjords (deep, narrow sea inlets formed by glacial erosion).

• Formation Process of Hanging Valleys: During glaciation, larger glaciers erode their valleys more deeply than smaller tributary glaciers. This differential erosion results in the formation of hanging valleys.

• Post-glaciation, these hanging valleys are often marked by waterfalls or steep drops as tributary rivers flow into the main valley.

• Significance of Hanging Valleys: Hanging valleys are important geomorphological indicators of past glaciation and provide insights into the erosive power of glaciers.

• They are often tourist attractions due to the waterfalls and scenic views they offer, such as Yosemite National Park in the United States.

• Examples of Hanging Valleys: Yosemite Valley, USA: A classic example of a hanging valley where waterfalls like Bridalveil Fall originate from hanging tributary valleys.

• Norwegian Fjords: Many fjords in Norway have hanging valleys along their sides, contributing to the dramatic landscape.

• Characteristics of Glacial Erosion: Glaciers erode the landscape through processes like plucking (removal of rock blocks) and abrasion (grinding of rock surfaces).

• Features like U-shaped valleys, cirques, and truncated spurs are indicative of glacial activity.

13. Which stage of cellular respiration produces the highest amount of ATP?

A. Glycolysis

B. Krebs cycle

C. Electron transport chain

D. Fermentation

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Electron Transport Chain. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Electron Transport Chain (ETC) is the final stage of cellular respiration and produces the maximum amount of ATP compared to other stages.

• ETC occurs in the inner mitochondrial membrane, where a series of protein complexes and electron carriers transfer electrons, generating energy.

• Electrons are transferred from NADH and FADH2, which are generated in earlier stages (Glycolysis and Krebs cycle), to oxygen via the protein complexes in the ETC.

• The energy released during electron transfer is used to pump protons (H+) across the inner mitochondrial membrane, creating a proton gradient.

• The proton gradient drives the synthesis of ATP by ATP synthase, a process known as oxidative phosphorylation. Approximately 34 ATP molecules are produced in this stage.

• The final electron acceptor in the ETC is oxygen, which combines with electrons and protons to form water.

• As the ETC produces the highest amount of ATP, it is a critical part of energy production in aerobic organisms.

• Glycolysis: This is the first stage of cellular respiration and occurs in the cytoplasm.

• During glycolysis, one molecule of glucose is broken down into two molecules of pyruvate, producing a net gain of 2 ATP and 2 NADH.

• Krebs Cycle (Citric Acid Cycle): This cycle occurs in the mitochondrial matrix and processes the pyruvate generated in glycolysis.

• It produces 2 ATP, 6 NADH, and 2 FADH2 per glucose molecule, which are used in the ETC.

• Fermentation: This is an anaerobic process that occurs when oxygen is unavailable.

• It produces a small amount of ATP (2 ATP) by converting pyruvate into either lactic acid or ethanol, depending on the organism.

• Importance of ATP: ATP is the primary energy currency of the cell, used to power various biochemical processes.

• The ETC ensures efficient energy production, making it essential for the survival of aerobic organisms.

• Role of Oxygen: Oxygen is the final electron acceptor in the ETC. Without oxygen, the ETC cannot function, and ATP production is drastically reduced.

• ATP Yield in Cellular Respiration: The complete breakdown of one molecule of glucose through cellular respiration produces a total of approximately 36-38 ATP molecules (2 from Glycolysis, 2 from Krebs Cycle, and 32-34 from ETC).

22. After the 42nd Amendment, education is placed under which Constitutional list?

A. Union List

B. State List

C. Concurrent List

D. None of these

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Concurrent List. Key Points Additional Information

• The 42nd Amendment Act of the Constitution of India, enacted in 1976, transferred the subject of education from the State List to the Concurrent List. This means that both the Central Government and the State Governments can now legislate on matters related to education.

• The Concurrent List is a list of subjects defined in the Seventh Schedule of the Indian Constitution on which both the Parliament and the State Legislatures are competent to make laws.

• Prior to the 42nd Amendment, education was solely under the State List, allowing only state governments to regulate and legislate on educational policies and practices.

• The amendment aimed to bring uniformity in education across the country and enable the Central Government to play a more significant role in framing educational policies and programs.

• The change was considered essential to address the growing importance of education in nation-building and improving literacy rates across India.

• The Concurrent List now allows the Central Government to implement national-level policies like the Right to Education Act (RTE), 2009, while states retain the power to adapt and implement these policies as per regional requirements.

• Hence, education was placed in the Concurrent List by the 42nd Amendment Act.

• Seventh Schedule of the Indian Constitution: The Seventh Schedule divides the legislative powers between the Union and the States into three lists: the Union List, the State List, and the Concurrent List.

• The Union List contains subjects of national importance, such as defense, foreign affairs, and atomic energy, on which only the Parliament can legislate.

• The State List includes subjects of regional and local importance, such as police, public health, and agriculture, on which only the State Legislatures can legislate.

• The Concurrent List includes subjects on which both the Parliament and State Legislatures can legislate. Examples include education, marriage, and adoption. However, in case of a conflict, the law made by the Parliament prevails.

• 42nd Amendment Act, 1976: The 42nd Amendment is also known as the “Mini-Constitution” due to the extensive changes it made to the Indian Constitution.

• It introduced several changes, including the addition of the words “Socialist” and “Secular” to the Preamble and the establishment of the Fundamental Duties under Article 51A.

• The amendment also aimed to strengthen the power of the Central Government and brought changes to the Seventh Schedule, including transferring education to the Concurrent List.

• Important Points: Significant national-level education policies like the National Education Policy (NEP) and initiatives like Skill India have been possible due to education being on the Concurrent List.

• The Right to Education Act (RTE), 2009, which guarantees free and compulsory education to children aged 6 to 14, is a critical example of the Central Government’s role in education after the amendment.

• States retain flexibility in implementing these policies to address regional and local needs, ensuring a balance of power between the Union and the States.
