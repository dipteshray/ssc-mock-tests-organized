1. Identify the Indian classical dancer associated with Kathak.

A. Vedantam Satyanarayana Sarma

B. Kelucharan Mohapatra

C. Rohini Bhate

D. Sharodi Saikia

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Rohini Bhate. Key Points Additional Information

• Rohini Bhate was an eminent Indian classical dancer renowned for her expertise in the Kathak dance form.

• She was a disciple of Guru Lachhu Maharaj and Shambhu Maharaj, both legendary exponents of Kathak.

• Her dance style was characterized by its technical precision and expressive storytelling.

• She was also a respected teacher and choreographer, contributing significantly to the propagation and evolution of Kathak.

• Kathak Kathak is one of the eight major forms of Indian classical dance.

• It originated from the nomadic bards of ancient northern India, known as Kathakars or storytellers.

• The dance form emphasizes intricate footwork, spins, and expressive gestures to convey stories, often from epics like the Mahabharata and Ramayana.

• Kathak has three main gharanas (schools) - Lucknow, Jaipur, and Banaras, each with its unique style and emphasis.

• Other Indian Classical Dance Forms Bharatanatyam - Originating from Tamil Nadu, it is known for its fixed upper torso, bent legs, and intricate footwork combined with expressive hand gestures and facial expressions.

• Kathakali - A dance-drama from Kerala, characterized by elaborate costumes, detailed gestures, and well-defined body movements presented in the form of a story-play.

• Kuchipudi - Hailing from Andhra Pradesh, it blends fast rhythms and fluid movements with dramatic storytelling.

• Odissi - From Odisha, it is known for its grace, fluid movements, and sculpturesque poses.

• Manipuri - A dance form from Manipur, characterized by smooth and graceful movements, often depicting themes from the life of Lord Krishna.

• Mohiniyattam - A classical dance from Kerala, it combines elements of Bharatanatyam and Kathakali, performed by women with slow, graceful movements.

• Sattriya - Originating from Assam, it is a dance-drama that combines dance, drama, and music.

2. British Parliament introduced Indian High Courts Act in which year?

A. 1860

B. 1861

C. 1859

D. 1858

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is 1861. Key Points Additional Information

• The Indian High Courts Act was introduced by the British Parliament in the year 1861.

• This act led to the establishment of High Courts in India for the first time, replacing the older Supreme Courts at Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras.

• The High Courts were given extensive original and appellate jurisdiction, marking a significant shift in the Indian judicial system.

• These High Courts were modeled after the English judicial system and were intended to provide a higher level of judicial administration in the major cities of India.

• Indian High Courts Act 1861 The Indian High Courts Act 1861 was an act of the British Parliament that led to the creation of High Courts in India in the three Presidency towns of Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras.

• Before this act, the judicial system in these cities was managed by Supreme Courts, which were established under the Charter Act of 1774.

• The new High Courts were granted original civil and criminal jurisdiction, appellate jurisdiction, and supervisory jurisdiction over the lower courts.

• This act was significant as it brought uniformity and improvement in the judicial administration of British India.

• Supreme Courts of Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras These courts were established under the Regulating Act of 1773 and subsequent acts, and they functioned until the High Courts were established in 1861.

• They were the highest courts in British India and had both original and appellate jurisdiction.

• The Supreme Courts were replaced by the High Courts to streamline and improve the judicial system.

• Judicial Administration in British India The British introduced several reforms to the judicial system in India to make it more efficient and aligned with British legal principles.

• The High Courts Act was a part of these reforms, which aimed to enhance the administration of justice in the colonies.

• These reforms were crucial in shaping the modern Indian judicial system.

4. In which of the following years was the Election Commission of India founded?

Solution

• The Election Commission of India (ECI) was established on 25th January 1950.

• It is an autonomous constitutional authority responsible for administering election processes in India at both the national and state levels.

• The ECI ensures the conduct of free and fair elections in the country, which is a fundamental aspect of the democratic framework of India.

• The Election Commission operates under the authority of the Constitution per Article 324, and subsequently enacted Representation of the People Acts.

• Election Commission of India (ECI) The ECI is responsible for the superintendence, direction, and control of the preparation of the electoral rolls for, and the conduct of, all elections to Parliament and to the Legislature of every State and of elections to the offices of President and Vice President held under the Constitution.

• The body administers elections to the Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha, and State Legislative Assemblies in India, and the offices of the President and Vice President in the country.

• It was originally a one-member body but was made a three-member body in 1989.

• The Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) heads the Election Commission of India.

• Article 324 of the Indian Constitution Article 324 vests the power of superintendence, direction, and control of elections in an independent body, which is the Election Commission.

• The Election Commission has the power to take all necessary steps to ensure free and fair elections.

• Representation of the People Acts The Representation of the People Act, 1950 provides for the allocation of seats in the House of the People and in the Legislative Assemblies, the delimitation of constituencies for elections, the qualifications of voters at such elections, and other related matters.

• The Representation of the People Act, 1951 provides for the actual conduct of elections in India, the qualifications and disqualifications for membership of Parliament and State Legislatures, and the resolution of election disputes.

5. In Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi Yojana, beneficiaries are provided with how much amount every year?

B. ₹4,000

C. ₹5,000

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is ₹6,000. Key Points Additional Information

• The Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi Yojana (PM-Kisan) is a central sector scheme launched by the Government of India.

• Under this scheme, income support of ₹6,000 per year is provided to all farmer families across the country.

• This amount is paid in three equal installments of ₹2,000 each, which are directly transferred to the bank accounts of the beneficiaries.

• The scheme aims to support the financial needs of farmers in procuring various inputs to ensure proper crop health and appropriate yields.

• Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi (PM-Kisan) PM-Kisan is a central sector scheme with 100% funding from the Government of India.

• It was launched on 24th February 2019, and the first installment was paid out in December 2018.

• The scheme is implemented by the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers' Welfare.

• All landholding farmer families are eligible for this scheme, provided they meet the criteria set by the government.

• Objectives The main objective of PM-Kisan is to provide financial support to farmers, helping them to procure various inputs for agriculture and allied activities, as well as domestic needs.

• The scheme aims to supplement the financial needs of the farmers in procuring inputs for crop production, ensuring proper crop health and yields.

• It also aims to protect farmers from falling into the clutches of moneylenders for meeting such expenses and ensure their continuance in farming activities.

• Eligibility Criteria All landholding farmer families, which have cultivable landholding in their names, are eligible for this scheme.

• However, certain exclusions apply, such as institutional landholders, farmer families holding constitutional posts, serving or retired officers and employees of state/central government as well as public sector undertakings and government autonomous bodies, and professionals like doctors, engineers, and lawyers.

• Implementation The scheme is implemented through an electronic database containing the details of all the beneficiaries.

9. In which year was the MPLADS scheme implemented?

A. 2003

B. 1983

C. 1993

D. 2013

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is 1993. Key Points Additional Information

• Members of Parliament Local Area Development Scheme (MPLADS) was launched in December 1993.

• The scheme enables Members of Parliament (MPs) to recommend developmental work in their constituencies with emphasis on creating durable community assets based on locally felt needs.

• Initially, the scheme allowed MPs to recommend works for Rs. 5 lakh per annum, which has now been increased to Rs. 5 crore per annum.

• The MPLADS funds can be used for various development activities including the construction of roads, schools, sanitation facilities, and drinking water facilities.

• Objective of MPLADS: The primary objective of MPLADS is to enable MPs to identify and fund small-scale developmental projects in their constituencies, addressing specific local needs and promoting the welfare of the people.

• Implementation: The MPLADS is implemented by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI).

• The funds released under MPLADS are non-lapsable, meaning they can be carried forward to subsequent years if not utilized within the financial year.

• Monitoring and Accountability: District authorities are responsible for the execution of projects recommended by MPs and ensuring that the funds are used appropriately.

• Regular audits and reviews are conducted to ensure transparency and accountability in the utilization of MPLADS funds.

• Recent Amendments: In recent years, the MPLADS guidelines have been amended to include provisions for funding projects related to COVID-19 relief and other national priorities.

Solution

The correct answer is willy-willies. Key Points Additional Information

• Willy-willies is the term used to describe tropical cyclones in Australia.

• Tropical cyclones are intense low-pressure systems that form over warm ocean waters and can cause significant damage to coastal regions.

• These cyclones are characterized by strong winds, heavy rainfall, and can lead to flooding and storm surges.

• The term willy-willies is specifically used in the Australian region, whereas other regions have different terms for similar weather phenomena.

• The term is used primarily in the Caribbean Sea, Gulf of Mexico, and along the eastern coastlines of the United States and Mexico.

• They are known for their destructive power, which includes strong winds, heavy rains, and storm surges.

• They are similar in structure and impact to hurricanes and willy-willies, with strong winds and heavy rainfall.

• The term "typhoon" is derived from the Chinese term "tai fung," meaning "great wind."

• Cyclones The term "cyclone" is a general term used to describe a rotating, organized system of clouds and thunderstorms that originate over tropical or subtropical waters.

• In the Indian Ocean and South Pacific, these systems are referred to as cyclones.

• Cyclones can cause extensive damage, including high winds, heavy rainfall, and coastal flooding.

16. If GNP is 16% higher than the last year and the rate of inflation is 9%, production in the economy has grown by:

A. 4%

B. 6%

C. 5%

D. 7%

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is 0.07. Key Points Additional Information

• The Gross National Product (GNP) is a broad measure of a nation's total economic activity.

• The GNP is 16% higher than the last year, indicating overall economic growth.

• Inflation rate is 9%, which represents the rate at which the general level of prices for goods and services is rising.

• The production growth rate can be determined by adjusting the GNP growth rate for inflation.

• Gross National Product (GNP) GNP measures the market value of all the products and services produced in one year by labor and property supplied by the residents of a country.

• It includes the value of goods and services produced by a country's residents, regardless of the location of the actual production.

• Inflation Inflation is the rate at which the general level of prices for goods and services is rising and subsequently eroding purchasing power.

• Central banks attempt to limit inflation, and avoid deflation, in order to keep the economy running smoothly.

• Economic Growth Economic growth is an increase in the production of economic goods and services, compared from one period of time to another.

• It can be measured in nominal or real terms, the latter of which is adjusted for inflation.

• Calculation of Production Growth Rate To find the production growth rate, we adjust the GNP growth rate for inflation.

• Formula: Production Growth Rate = (1 + GNP Growth Rate) / (1 + Inflation Rate) - 1

• Using the given values: GNP Growth Rate = 16% or 0.16, Inflation Rate = 9% or 0.09

• Production Growth Rate = (1 + 0.16) / (1 + 0.09) - 1 = 1.16 / 1.09 - 1 = 1.0642 - 1 ≈ 0.07

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Typhoid, Cholera, Tuberculosis. Key Points Additional Information

• Typhoid fever is caused by the bacterium Salmonella typhi and is typically spread through contaminated food and water.

• Cholera is caused by the bacterium Vibrio cholerae and is also usually spread through contaminated water and food, leading to severe diarrhea and dehydration.

• Tuberculosis (TB) is caused by the bacterium Mycobacterium tuberculosis and primarily affects the lungs, but can also affect other parts of the body.

• These diseases are treatable with antibiotics, which target and kill the bacterial pathogens responsible for the infections.

• It is a contagious respiratory illness with symptoms like fever, cough, and body aches.

• Vaccines are available to help prevent influenza.

• It leads to symptoms such as high fever, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, joint and muscle pain, and rash.

• Malaria Malaria is caused by Plasmodium parasites, which are transmitted through the bites of infected Anopheles mosquitoes.

• Symptoms include fever, chills, and flu-like illness; if not treated, it can lead to severe complications and death.

• It is characterized by symptoms such as sneezing, runny nose, and sore throat.

• There is no cure for the common cold, but symptoms can be managed with over-the-counter medications.

• During Paryushana, Jains engage in various religious activities such as reading scriptures, attending sermons, and participating in communal prayers.

• The festival concludes with the celebration of Kshamavani, or "Forgiveness Day," where Jains seek forgiveness from others for any harm or hurt caused intentionally or unintentionally.
