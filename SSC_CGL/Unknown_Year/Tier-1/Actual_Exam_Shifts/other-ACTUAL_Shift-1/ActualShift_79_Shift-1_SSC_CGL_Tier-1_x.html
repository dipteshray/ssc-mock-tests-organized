7. The upper portion of the Earth is known as:

A. Stratosphere

B. Troposphere

C. Lithosphere

D. Mesosphere

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Lithosphere. Key Points Additional Information

• The Lithosphere is the rigid, outermost shell of the Earth, encompassing both the crust and the uppermost part of the mantle.

• It is divided into tectonic plates that float on the semi-fluid asthenosphere beneath it.

• The thickness of the Lithosphere varies, being thinnest at mid-ocean ridges and thickest beneath continental crust.

• The Lithosphere is crucial for plate tectonics, as its movements and interactions cause earthquakes, volcanic activity, and the creation of mountain ranges.

• Stratosphere The Stratosphere is the second major layer of Earth's atmosphere, lying above the Troposphere and below the Mesosphere.

• It extends from about 10 km to 50 km above Earth's surface.

• This layer contains the ozone layer, which absorbs and scatters ultraviolet solar radiation.

• Commercial jet aircraft typically fly in the lower stratosphere.

• Troposphere The Troposphere is the lowest layer of Earth's atmosphere, extending from Earth's surface up to about 8-15 km.

• It contains approximately 75% of the atmosphere's mass and 99% of its water vapor and aerosols.

• Weather events occur in the Troposphere.

• Mesosphere The Mesosphere is the layer of the atmosphere above the Stratosphere and below the Thermosphere.

• It extends from about 50 km to 85 km above the Earth's surface.

• This layer is where most meteorites burn up upon entering the Earth's atmosphere.

• The temperature decreases with altitude in the Mesosphere.

• Aman rice is sown during the monsoon season (June-July) and harvested in the winter (November-December).

• This variety of rice depends heavily on the monsoon rains for its cultivation.

D. Information technology

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Agriculture. Key Points Additional Information

• Agriculture is a sector that is highly dependent on seasons and climatic conditions, leading to seasonal unemployment.

• During the off-season, agricultural workers may not find work, leading to temporary unemployment.

• This type of unemployment typically affects rural areas where agriculture is the primary occupation.

• Seasonal unemployment in agriculture can be mitigated by providing alternate employment opportunities during the off-season.

• Seasonal Unemployment: Seasonal unemployment occurs when people are unemployed at certain times of the year because their work is seasonal in nature.

• Common sectors that face seasonal unemployment include agriculture, tourism, and construction.

• In agriculture, this type of unemployment happens between planting and harvesting seasons.

• To mitigate seasonal unemployment, workers often seek alternative employment during the off-season or may participate in government employment schemes.

• This type of unemployment is typically temporary and can be addressed through economic policies that stimulate demand.

• Frictional Unemployment: Frictional unemployment occurs when people are temporarily unemployed while transitioning from one job to another.

• This is a natural form of unemployment and is often short-term.

• Economic Causes: The heavy taxation and exploitation of Indian farmers, artisans, and the destruction of traditional industries led to economic distress.

• Social and Religious Causes: The British social reforms, which were seen as interference in traditional customs and religious practices, angered many Indians.

• Many Indian rulers and soldiers joined the rebellion, seeing it as an opportunity to regain their lost power and territories.

• Suppression of the Revolt The British used superior military force and strategic alliances with some Indian rulers to suppress the revolt.

• By 1858, the British had regained control over most of the rebellious areas, marking the end of the revolt.

• Consequences of the Revolt The revolt led to the end of the East India Company's rule in India, and the British Crown took direct control of the Indian administration.

• The British government made several administrative and military reforms to prevent any future rebellions.

• The revolt also marked the beginning of a stronger nationalistic movement in India, leading to the eventual struggle for independence.

14. In the context of cell division, which chromosomal behaviour takes place at the leptotene stage?

A. Chromosomes are un-synapsed

B. Synapsis is complete

C. Homologous chromosomes pair

D. Chromosomes begin to condense

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is Chromosomes begin to condense. Key Points Additional Information

• During the leptotene stage, chromosomes start to condense and become visible under a microscope.

• This stage is the initial phase of prophase I in meiosis, where chromosomes appear as long, thread-like structures.

• The condensation of chromosomes helps in their subsequent alignment and pairing during meiosis.

• Chromosomal condensation is crucial for the accurate segregation of chromosomes into daughter cells.

• Leptotene Stage The leptotene stage is the first stage of prophase I in meiosis.

• During this stage, chromosomes start to condense and become visible as thin threads.

• It is followed by the zygotene stage, where homologous chromosomes begin to pair and synapse.

• Meiosis Meiosis is a type of cell division that reduces the chromosome number by half, resulting in four genetically distinct daughter cells.

• It consists of two consecutive divisions: meiosis I and meiosis II.

• Meiosis is essential for sexual reproduction and contributes to genetic diversity.

• Chromosomal Synapsis Synapsis is the pairing of homologous chromosomes during prophase I of meiosis.

• It occurs during the zygotene stage, following the leptotene stage.

• Synapsis is crucial for the exchange of genetic material between homologous chromosomes through crossing over.

• These principles are guidelines for the framing of laws by the government.

• They aim to create social and economic conditions under which citizens can lead a good life.

• Although these principles are non-justiciable, meaning they are not enforceable by any court, they are considered fundamental in the governance of the country.

• Examples include the right to equality, right to freedom, right against exploitation, right to freedom of religion, cultural and educational rights, and right to constitutional remedies.

• They are mentioned in Part IV-A of the Indian Constitution under Article 51A.

• There are 11 Fundamental Duties listed in the Constitution.

• They were added by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976, based on the recommendations of the Swaran Singh Committee.

• It is covered in Part V of the Indian Constitution from Articles 52 to 151.

• It includes provisions related to the President, Vice-President, Council of Ministers, Parliament, and other Union bodies.
