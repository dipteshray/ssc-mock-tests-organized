• Silver chloride (AgCl) is a white crystalline solid.

• When exposed to sunlight, AgCl decomposes into silver (Ag) and chlorine gas (Cl2).

• The silver formed is a grey metal, which imparts the grey color to the white AgCl.

• This reaction is a type of photodecomposition reaction, where light energy causes the chemical breakdown of a compound.

• Photodecomposition Reaction This is a chemical reaction in which a compound is broken down into simpler substances by the action of light.

• In the case of silver chloride, light energy breaks the bond between silver and chlorine atoms.

• Such reactions are common in photochemistry and are used in various applications including photography.

• Silver Chloride (AgCl) It is a white crystalline solid that is well known for its low solubility in water.

• AgCl is used in photographic films and papers, where it plays a crucial role in capturing images.

• Photographic Process Silver halides like AgCl are used in photographic films because they are sensitive to light.

• When exposed to light, they decompose to form silver, which creates the image on the film.

• This process is the basis for traditional film photography.
