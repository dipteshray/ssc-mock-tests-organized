3. Navratri festival is dedicated to which of the following Goddesses?

A. Jawala

B. Laxmi

C. Durga

D. Saraswati

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Durga. Key Points Additional Information

• Navratri is a Hindu festival that spans nine nights and is dedicated to the worship of the Hindu deity Durga.

• During Navratri, nine forms of Durga, also known as Navdurga, are worshipped each day.

• The festival symbolizes the victory of good over evil, as it commemorates Durga's triumph over the buffalo demon Mahishasura.

• Navratri is celebrated with great fervor and involves fasting, feasting, dancing (Garba and Dandiya), and various cultural activities.

• Navratri: Navratri, meaning "nine nights," is a major Hindu festival observed in honor of the goddess Durga, who is regarded as the divine mother and the ultimate power in the universe.

• The festival occurs in the lunar month of Ashvin, which typically falls in September or October.

• The nine nights of Navratri are dedicated to the nine forms of Durga: Shailaputri, Brahmacharini, Chandraghanta, Kushmanda, Skandamata, Katyayani, Kalaratri, Mahagauri, and Siddhidatri.

• Each day of Navratri has its own significance, rituals, and practices, reflecting different aspects and attributes of the goddess Durga.

• Durga Puja: Durga Puja is a major festival celebrated particularly in the Indian states of West Bengal, Odisha, Assam, Tripura, and Bihar during the last five days of Navratri.

• It marks the victory of the goddess Durga over the evil buffalo demon Mahishasura, symbolizing the victory of good over evil.

• The festival is characterized by elaborate decorations, artistic displays, cultural performances, and grand processions.

• Devotees offer prayers, perform rituals, and partake in communal feasts during Durga Puja.

• Garba and Dandiya Raas: Garba and Dandiya Raas are traditional dance forms performed during Navratri, especially in the Indian state of Gujarat.

• Garba involves rhythmic clapping and circular movements around a central image or representation of the goddess Durga.

• Dandiya Raas, also known as "stick dance," involves pairs of dancers striking small sticks (dandiyas) together in sync with the music.

• These dance forms are not only a way to celebrate the festival but also a means to seek the blessings of the goddess and enjoy the festive spirit.

• Fasting and Feasting: Fasting during Navratri is a common practice among devotees, symbolizing purification and devotion.

• Different regions have their own fasting rules and dietary restrictions, often involving abstaining from grains, meats, and certain vegetables.

• Feasting on special Navratri delicacies, such as sabudana khichdi, kuttu ki puri, and various fruits, is an integral part of the celebration.

• The end of the fasting period is marked by communal feasts and sharing of food, signifying unity and joy.

Solution

The correct answer is Article 75. Key Points Additional Information

• Article 75 of the Indian Constitution deals with the appointment of the Prime Minister and other ministers by the President.

• It states that the Prime Minister shall be appointed by the President and the other Ministers shall be appointed by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister.

• The article also discusses the collective responsibility of the Council of Ministers to the House of the People (Lok Sabha).

• It mentions that the Ministers shall hold office during the pleasure of the President.

• Article 62 Article 62 deals with the time of holding election to fill a vacancy in the office of President and the term of office of the elected person.

• It specifies that an election to fill the vacancy caused by the expiration of the term of office of President shall be completed before the expiration of the term.

• It also states that an election to fill a vacancy in the office of President occurring by reason of his death, resignation, or removal, or otherwise, shall be held as soon as possible after, and in no case later than six months from, the date of occurrence of the vacancy.

• Article 66 Article 66 deals with the election of the Vice-President of India.

• The Vice-President is elected by the members of an electoral college consisting of the members of both Houses of Parliament.

• The election is held in accordance with the system of proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote and the voting is by secret ballot.

• Article 71 Article 71 deals with matters relating to, or connected with, the election of a President or Vice-President.

• It states that all doubts and disputes arising out of or in connection with the election of a President or Vice-President shall be inquired into and decided by the Supreme Court whose decision shall be final.

11. What was the name of the wind measuring instrument invented for the first time in 1450?

C. Anemometer

D. Ceiling Projector

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Anemometer. Key Points Additional Information

• The anemometer was invented by Italian architect and author Leon Battista Alberti in the year 1450.

• Anemometers are used to measure wind speed and direction.

• The term "anemometer" is derived from the Greek word "anemos," which means wind.

• Anemometers are essential tools in meteorology and are commonly used in weather stations, airports, and on ships.

• Transmissometer A transmissometer is an instrument used to measure the extinction coefficient (the loss of light intensity due to scattering and absorption) in the atmosphere.

• It is commonly used to determine visibility in meteorology and oceanography.

• Dropsonde A dropsonde is a weather reconnaissance device that is dropped from an aircraft to measure atmospheric conditions as it falls to the ground.

• It is equipped with sensors to measure temperature, humidity, pressure, and wind speed and direction.

• Ceiling Projector A ceiling projector is an optical device used in meteorology to measure the height of the cloud base above the ground.

• It projects a beam of light onto the clouds, and the height is determined by measuring the angle of the beam reflection.

16. Which two organic chemists are known for observing the peroxide effect in adding reagents to unsaturated compounds in 1933?

A. Morris S Kharasch and Frank R Mayo

B. C John Cadogen and Luis M Campos

C. B Steven Bachrach and Roald Hoffmann

D. Justus von Liebig and Friedrich Wöhler

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Morris S Kharasch and Frank R Mayo. Key Points Additional Information

• Morris S Kharasch and Frank R Mayo are known for their pioneering work in organic chemistry, particularly in the study of the peroxide effect.

• They observed the peroxide effect in 1933, which is also known as the Kharasch effect.

• This effect involves the addition of reagents to unsaturated compounds in the presence of peroxides, which leads to anti-Markovnikov addition.

• The discovery of the peroxide effect has had significant implications for synthetic organic chemistry, enabling the development of new reactions and synthetic pathways.

• Peroxide Effect (Kharasch Effect) This effect refers to the anti-Markovnikov addition of hydrogen bromide (HBr) to alkenes in the presence of peroxides.

• In the absence of peroxides, HBr adds to alkenes according to Markovnikov's rule, where the hydrogen attaches to the carbon with the greater number of hydrogen atoms.

• However, in the presence of peroxides, the addition follows the anti-Markovnikov rule, where the hydrogen attaches to the carbon with fewer hydrogen atoms.

• This phenomenon is due to the formation of free radicals initiated by the decomposition of peroxides.

• Free Radical Mechanism The peroxide effect proceeds through a free radical mechanism, which involves three main steps: initiation, propagation, and termination.

• Initiation: Peroxides decompose to form free radicals.

• Propagation: The free radicals react with HBr to generate bromine radicals, which then add to the alkene, forming new free radicals.

• Termination: The free radicals combine to form stable products, terminating the chain reaction.

• Importance in Organic Synthesis The peroxide effect is utilized in various synthetic applications, allowing chemists to control the regiochemistry of addition reactions.

• It has paved the way for the development of new reactions and methodologies in organic synthesis.

• One Newton is the force required to accelerate a one-kilogram mass by one meter per second squared.

• The symbol for Newton is "N".

• Joule (J) A Joule is a derived unit of energy in the International System of Units.

• It is equal to the energy transferred when applying a force of one Newton over a distance of one meter.

• The symbol for Joule is "J".

• It is defined as the amount of heat needed to raise the temperature of one gram of water by one degree Celsius.

• It is commonly used as a billing unit for energy delivered to consumers by electric utilities.

25. What is the other name of tetanus caused by toxin producing bacteria called clostridium tetani?

A. Lockjaw

B. Snap jaw

C. Cleft jaw

D. Broken jaw

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Lockjaw. Key Points Additional Information

• Lockjaw is a common name for tetanus, a serious bacterial infection.

• Tetanus is caused by the bacterium Clostridium tetani, which produces a toxin affecting the nervous system.

• The condition is characterized by muscle stiffness and spasms, particularly in the jaw muscles, hence the name "lockjaw".

• Without treatment, tetanus can be fatal, but it is preventable through vaccination.

• Clostridium tetani: It is a spore-forming bacterium found in soil, dust, and animal feces.

• The spores can enter the body through wounds, especially deep puncture wounds, and produce a toxin called tetanospasmin.

• Tetanospasmin interferes with the nervous system, leading to severe muscle spasms.

• Tetanus Symptoms: Symptoms typically appear 3 to 21 days after infection.

• Common symptoms include muscle stiffness, difficulty swallowing, and jaw cramping (lockjaw).

• Severe cases can lead to muscle spasms in the back, neck, and other parts of the body.

• Tetanus Treatment: Treatment usually involves wound care, medications to control muscle spasms, and antibiotics to fight the infection.

• In severe cases, patients may require hospitalization and supportive care, including mechanical ventilation.

• Tetanus Prevention: The best way to prevent tetanus is through vaccination.

• The DTaP (diphtheria, tetanus, and acellular pertussis) vaccine is routinely given to children, and booster shots (Td or Tdap) are recommended for adults every 10 years.

• Proper wound care and hygiene can also help prevent tetanus infection.
