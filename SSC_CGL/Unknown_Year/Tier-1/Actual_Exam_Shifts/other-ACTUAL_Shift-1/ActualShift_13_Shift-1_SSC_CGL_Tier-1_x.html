1. What defensive feature was installed on the gates of Golconda Fort to counter attacks by war elephants?

A. Moats filled with crocodiles

B. Iron-plated swinging doors

C. Sharp metal spikes embedded on gates

D. Hidden underground tunnels

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Sharp metal spikes embedded on gates. Key Points Additional Information

• Golconda Fort, located in Hyderabad, Telangana, is one of the most significant historical forts in India.

• To counter the attacks by war elephants, the gates of the fort were fortified with sharp metal spikes embedded on them. These spikes were designed to incapacitate the attacking elephants by injuring them.

• This defensive feature was a crucial part of the fort's architecture, showcasing the strategic planning and foresight of the builders.

• War elephants were commonly used in ancient Indian warfare to break through gates and walls, but the inclusion of these metal spikes effectively neutralized their impact.

• The spikes were made of iron and positioned at the correct height to target the head and trunk of the elephants during an attack.

• Such defensive mechanisms exemplify the advanced engineering and military architecture of the medieval period.

• The fort is also renowned for its acoustic system, intricate water supply system, and robust defense structures.

• Moats filled with crocodiles Moats were often used as a defensive feature in forts to prevent attackers from reaching the gates easily.

• In some cases, these moats were filled with crocodiles to deter enemies from attempting to swim or cross them.

• However, this was not the defensive strategy used at Golconda Fort.

• Iron-plated swinging doors Iron-plated doors were commonly used in fortifications to provide additional strength and durability against battering rams or fire.

• While Golconda Fort had strong gates, the primary feature to counter war elephants was the spikes, not the iron plating.

• Hidden underground tunnels Hidden tunnels were often used for emergency escape routes or to transport supplies during sieges.

• Although such tunnels existed in some forts, they were not the main feature used at Golconda Fort to counter war elephants.

7. At the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics, P. T. Usha missed winning a bronze medal by what margin of time?

B. One-hundredth of a second

C. One-thousandth of a second

D. Half a second

Answer B

Solution

The Correct answer is One-hundredth of a second. Key Points Additional Information

• P. T. Usha, popularly known as the "Queen of Indian Track and Field", narrowly missed a bronze medal at the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics.

• She participated in the 400m hurdles event and finished in the fourth position.

• The margin by which she missed the bronze medal was just one-hundredth of a second, making this one of the closest finishes in the history of the Olympics.

• Her timing in the race was 55.42 seconds, while the bronze medalist clocked 55.41 seconds.

• This performance was a landmark achievement for Indian athletics, as she was the first Indian woman to reach the final of an Olympic track event.

• She remains a symbol of excellence and perseverance in Indian sports history.

• The 1984 Olympics was particularly significant for India because it marked a turning point in the country's track and field achievements.

• One-tenth of a second One-tenth of a second is equivalent to 0.1 seconds, which is a significant margin in professional sports, especially in short-distance track events.

• In contrast, the margin by which P. T. Usha missed was much smaller, emphasizing the importance of every millisecond in competitive events.

• One-thousandth of a second One-thousandth of a second (0.001 seconds) is generally used in sports like motor racing and swimming, where precise timing devices are required.

• It is even more granular than the margin by which P. T. Usha missed the medal.

• Half a second Half a second (0.5 seconds) is a relatively large time gap in competitive sports, especially in track events where athletes are often separated by milliseconds.

16. Which of the following nutrient-type pairs is correctly matched?

A. Micronutrient – Calcium

B. Macronutrient – Zinc

C. Micronutrient – Boron

D. Macronutrient – Iron

Answer C

Solution

The Correct answer is Micronutrient – Boron. Key Points Additional Information

• Boron is classified as a micronutrient, essential for plant growth and development.

• It plays a key role in cell wall formation, membrane integrity, and energy transfer in plants.

• Boron is critical for the reproductive process of plants, aiding in pollination and seed production.

• The deficiency of boron can lead to stunted growth, poor fruit development, and reduced crop yield.

• Boron is absorbed by plants in its ionic form, Borate (BO3³⁻).

• Common sources of boron used in agriculture include borax, boric acid, and various borate fertilizers.

• Micronutrient – Calcium Calcium is not a micronutrient but a macronutrient, as plants require it in larger quantities.

• It is essential for cell wall stabilization, growth, and development.

• Calcium deficiency can lead to issues like blossom end rot in tomatoes and poor root development.

• Common sources include calcium nitrate and lime.

• Macronutrient – Zinc Zinc is not a macronutrient but a micronutrient, as plants require it in smaller amounts.

• Zinc plays a role in enzyme activation, protein synthesis, and growth regulation.

• Deficiency symptoms include chlorosis and stunted growth.

• Sources include zinc sulfate and chelated zinc fertilizers.

• Macronutrient – Iron Iron is also a micronutrient, not a macronutrient.

• It is vital for chlorophyll synthesis and acts as a co-factor in various enzymatic processes.

• Iron deficiency results in interveinal chlorosis on younger leaves.

• Common sources include iron sulfate and chelated iron fertilizers.

17. Which of the following electromagnetic device pairs is correctly matched?

A. Electric generator – Converts electrical energy into mechanical energy

B. Transformer – Works only on DC supply

C. Electromagnet – Permanent magnet

D. Electric bell – Works on magnetic effect of electric current

Answer D

Solution

The Correct answer is Electric bell – Works on magnetic effect of electric current. Key Points Additional Information

• An electric bell is a device that operates based on the magnetic effect of electric current.

• When the electric current flows through the coil, it produces a magnetic field.

• This magnetic field attracts the iron armature, causing the hammer to strike the bell and produce sound.

• The process works in a cyclic manner, as the electric circuit is continuously broken and completed during the operation.

• This device is widely used in schools, homes, and offices for signaling purposes.

• The underlying principle of the electric bell demonstrates the conversion of electrical energy into mechanical energy through electromagnetic induction.

• Electric generator – Converts electrical energy into mechanical energy This is incorrect. An electric generator converts mechanical energy into electrical energy, not the other way around.

• It works on the principle of electromagnetic induction discovered by Michael Faraday.

• Generators are widely used in power plants to generate electricity.

• Transformer – Works only on DC supply This is incorrect. A transformer only works with alternating current (AC), not direct current (DC).

• It is used to step up or step down voltage levels in an AC circuit.

• Transformers are key components in the electric power distribution system.

• Electromagnet – Permanent magnet This is incorrect. An electromagnet is a type of magnet where the magnetic field is produced by an electric current, and it ceases to exist when the current is switched off.

• In contrast, a permanent magnet maintains its magnetic properties without the need for an electric current.

• Electromagnets are used in devices like motors, generators, and MRI machines.

D. Preamble

Solution

The Correct answer is Directive Principles of State Policy. Key Points Additional Information

• Article 50 of the Indian Constitution falls under the Directive Principles of State Policy, which is enshrined in Part IV of the Constitution.

• The article directs the state to take steps to separate the judiciary from the executive in the public services of the state.

• The Directive Principles of State Policy act as guidelines for the state in the governance of the country and aim to establish a welfare state.

• These principles are non-justiciable, meaning they are not enforceable by any court, but they are fundamental in the governance of the country.

• The inclusion of Directive Principles in the Constitution was inspired by the Irish Constitution.

• The main objective of Article 50 is to ensure the independence of the judiciary, which is crucial for the functioning of a fair and impartial judicial system.

• Fundamental Rights The Fundamental Rights are enshrined in Part III of the Indian Constitution and include Articles 12 to 35.

• These rights are justiciable, meaning individuals can approach the courts if their rights are violated.

• Examples include the Right to Equality, Right to Freedom, Right against Exploitation, and Right to Constitutional Remedies.

• Fundamental Duties The Fundamental Duties are enshrined in Article 51A under Part IVA of the Constitution.

• They were added by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 based on the recommendations of the Swaran Singh Committee.

• Examples include respecting the National Flag, protecting the environment, and safeguarding the cultural heritage of India.

• Preamble The Preamble serves as the introduction to the Constitution and states the objectives such as Justice, Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity.

• It is based on the Objective Resolution moved by Jawaharlal Nehru in the Constituent Assembly.

• Although the Preamble is not enforceable in a court of law, it is used as a guide to understand the spirit of the Constitution.
