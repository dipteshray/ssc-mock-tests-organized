11. Change the following sentence from active to passive voice:The manager might have approved the request.

A. The request might have been approved by the manager.

B. The request might be approved by the manager.

C. The request might have approved the manager.

D. The request was being approved by manager.

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Option 1. Key Points Therefore, the correct answer is Option 1. Correct Sentence: "The request might have been approved by the manager." Additional Information

• Passive voice structure: The object of the active voice sentence becomes the subject of the passive voice sentence.

• In the active voice, the sentence is: "The manager might have approved the request."

• To convert to passive voice: Place the object "the request" at the beginning.

• Change the active verb form "might have approved" to "might have been approved" (perfect modal passive).

• Add the preposition "by" followed by the original subject "the manager."

• Option 2: "The request might be approved by the manager." – This is incorrect because it uses the present modal passive, which does not match the original sentence's tense.

• Option 3: "The request might have approved the manager." – This changes the meaning entirely, making the object the doer.

• Option 4: "The request was being approved by the manager." – This uses the past continuous passive, which does not match the original tense.

21. A sentence is given in direct speech. Choose the correct indirect form:He said, “Water freezes at zero degrees.”

A. He said water froze at zero degrees.

B. He said water freezes at zero degrees.

C. He said that water has frozen.

D. He said water had frozen.

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Option 2. Key Points Therefore, the correct answer is Option 2. Correct Sentence: He said that water freezes at zero degrees. Additional Information

• General Rule for Reporting: In case of universal truths, facts, or habitual actions, the verb remains in the present tense in indirect speech.

• Explanation: The given sentence is a universal truth ("Water freezes at zero degrees").

• Hence, the reporting verb "freezes" remains in the present tense in the indirect speech.

• Reporting Verb: The reporting verb "He said" is not followed by a change in tense as the statement is a universal truth.

• Punctuation: The quotation marks in direct speech are removed in indirect speech.

• The word "that" is used to connect the reporting clause with the reported speech.

• Option 1: "He said water froze at zero degrees." Incorrect, as the verb "froze" is in the past tense, which is not appropriate for universal truths.

• Option 3: "He said that water has frozen." Incorrect, as the verb "has frozen" indicates a recent event, not a universal truth.

• Option 4: "He said water had frozen." Incorrect, as the verb "had frozen" indicates a past perfect action, not a universal truth.
