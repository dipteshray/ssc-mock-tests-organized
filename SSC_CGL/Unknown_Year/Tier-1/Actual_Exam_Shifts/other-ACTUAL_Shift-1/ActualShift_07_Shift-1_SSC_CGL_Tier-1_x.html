Solution

The correct answer is Only statement 2. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Statement 1: "The Solicitor General of India is appointed by the President." The Solicitor General of India is the second-highest law officer of the country, assisting the Attorney General of India.

• While the Attorney General of India is appointed by the President under Article 76(1) of the Constitution, the Solicitor General is not a constitutional post.

• The Solicitor General, along with Additional Solicitors General, is appointed by the Central Government and not directly by the President. This appointment is made under the authority of the Law Officers (Conditions of Service) Rules, 1987.

• This security deposit is forfeited if the candidate fails to secure at least one-sixth of the total valid votes polled.

• This provision ensures that only serious candidates participate in the election process.

• Hence, statement 2 is correct.

• Solicitor General of India: The Solicitor General is the second law officer after the Attorney General of India and represents the Union of India in the Supreme Court.

• Unlike the Attorney General, the Solicitor General is not a constitutional post but is appointed under statutory provisions.

• The Solicitor General is appointed by the Central Government on the advice of the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet (ACC).

• Eligibility Criteria and Security Deposit for Presidential Elections: A candidate contesting the election for the post of the President of India must fulfill specific eligibility criteria as laid down in Articles 58 and 59 of the Constitution of India.

• Eligibility Requirements: The candidate must be a citizen of India.

• The candidate must be at least 35 years old.

• The candidate must be qualified to become a member of the Lok Sabha.

• The candidate must not hold any office of profit under the Government of India, state government, or public authority.

• The security deposit of ₹15,000 ensures that frivolous candidates do not contest the Presidential election.

• The deposit is forfeited if the candidate fails to secure at least one-sixth of the total valid votes polled.

• Role of Solicitor General: The Solicitor General is not eligible to participate in parliamentary proceedings.

• The role of the Solicitor General is limited to advising the government and representing it in courts.

• The Solicitor General does not have executive authority like the Attorney General.

• Presidential Elections: The election of the President of India is conducted by the Election Commission of India through a process of proportional representation by means of a single transferable vote.

• The voters in this election include the elected members of both Houses of Parliament and the elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the States and Union territories with Legislative Assemblies.

• Hola Mohalla is a significant Sikh festival celebrated annually, primarily in Anandpur Sahib, Punjab.

• The festival showcases martial arts, mock battles, and other traditional warrior skills performed by the Nihang Sikhs, who are known for their distinctive blue robes and martial history.

• It also features langars (community kitchens), where free meals are served to thousands of devotees and visitors during the celebrations.

• The event typically includes processions, recitation of religious hymns, and cultural performances, emphasizing the rich heritage of Sikhism. Hence, this statement is correct.

• Statement 4 (observed on the same day as Holi) is incorrect because Hola Mohalla usually takes place a day after Holi, not on the same day. Hence, the correct answer is the second statement.

• Historical Significance of Hola Mohalla: The festival was initiated by Guru Gobind Singh, the tenth Sikh Guru, in the late 17th century. It was meant to instill martial spirit and courage among the Sikh community.

• The term ‘Hola Mohalla’ is derived from ‘Hola,’ which means a military charge, and ‘Mohalla,’ meaning a gathering or an organized procession.

• It also features religious and cultural processions, kirtans (devotional music), and poetry recitals.

• Important Locations: Anandpur Sahib, the birthplace of the Khalsa (the collective body of initiated Sikhs), is the focal point of Hola Mohalla celebrations.

• Cultural and Religious Impact: Hola Mohalla serves as an occasion to remember the Sikh community’s martial traditions and to inspire younger generations to uphold these values.

• The festival is an excellent example of Sikhism’s emphasis on community bonding, equality, and selfless service through activities like langars.

• Tourism and Popularity: The festival attracts thousands of visitors, including tourists from across India and abroad, making it a major cultural and religious event in Punjab.

• It is also recognized for its vibrant atmosphere and the participants’ colorful traditional attire, adding to its appeal.

6. In freestyle wrestling, which action earns a wrestler technical points?

A. Running outside the mat on purpose

B. Pulling the opponent’s singlet

C. Executing a clean takedown

D. Applying an illegal headlock

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Executing a clean takedown. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• In freestyle wrestling, a wrestler earns points through various actions that demonstrate skill, control, and dominance over their opponent.

• Executing a clean takedown is one of the primary ways to earn points in freestyle wrestling. A takedown occurs when a wrestler successfully brings their opponent to the mat and gains control over them.

• A clean takedown typically awards the wrestler 2 points, provided the action is performed without any fouls or infractions. Hence, the correct answer is executing a clean takedown.

• The other options mentioned, such as running outside the mat on purpose or pulling the opponent’s singlet, are considered fouls and do not contribute to earning points. Instead, they may lead to warnings or penalties for the wrestler. Hence, these statements are incorrect.

• Applying an illegal headlock is also against the rules of freestyle wrestling and may result in penalties rather than earning points. Thus, this statement is incorrect.

• Scoring in Freestyle Wrestling: Takedown (2-5 points): A wrestler earns points for taking their opponent to the mat and controlling them. The points awarded depend on the complexity and execution of the move.

• Exposure (2 points): Points are awarded if a wrestler turns their opponent's back to the mat for a brief moment, even without pinning them.

• Reversal (1 point): If a wrestler escapes from their opponent’s hold and gains control, they are awarded points for a reversal move.

• Penalty Points: These are awarded to the opponent when a wrestler commits fouls, such as pulling the singlet, fleeing the mat, or performing illegal moves.

• Rules and Fouls in Freestyle Wrestling: Freestyle wrestling has a strict set of rules to ensure fair play. Actions like illegal holds (e.g., headlocks without an arm), pulling the opponent’s attire, or stalling are not allowed and can result in point deductions or disqualification.

• Running outside the mat intentionally is considered stalling and results in a caution or penalty for the wrestler.

• Freestyle Wrestling: Freestyle wrestling is one of the two styles of wrestling in the Olympic Games, the other being Greco-Roman wrestling.

• In freestyle wrestling, wrestlers can use their legs for both offense and defense, unlike Greco-Roman wrestling, where the use of legs is prohibited.

• Scoring System: Victory by Fall: A wrestler wins if they pin their opponent’s shoulders to the mat.

• Technical Superiority: A wrestler wins if they score at least 10 points more than their opponent.

• Decision: If no fall or technical superiority occurs, the winner is determined by points scored during the match.

Solution

The correct answer is Criminal substantive law. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• The Bharatiya Nyaya Samhita (BNS), 2023, is aimed at reforming and modernizing the criminal laws of India.

• The BNS focuses on consolidating and updating criminal substantive law, which governs offenses and prescribes penalties.

• It seeks to replace the existing Indian Penal Code (IPC), 1860, which has been the cornerstone of criminal law in India for over 160 years. Hence, this statement is correct.

• The BNS introduces new provisions for crimes and penalties, aligning with modern societal and technological changes, such as cybercrime and human trafficking.

• It also seeks to simplify legal language and improve clarity in the law, making it more accessible to citizens and legal practitioners alike. This supports the correct answer.

• Evidence-related rules, civil procedural matters, and contractual obligations are governed by separate laws, such as the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, Civil Procedure Code (CPC), 1908, and Indian Contract Act, 1872, respectively. These areas are not directly addressed by the BNS. Hence, these options are incorrect.

• Therefore, the correct answer is Criminal substantive law.

• About the Bharatiya Nyaya Samhita (BNS), 2023: BNS is part of the Indian government's initiative to overhaul colonial-era laws, such as the Indian Penal Code (IPC), Criminal Procedure Code (CrPC), and Indian Evidence Act.

• The focus is on creating a legal framework that reflects the evolving needs of society and incorporates advancements in technology and global legal standards.

• Key Features of Criminal Substantive Law: Criminal substantive law defines offenses such as theft, murder, and fraud, along with prescribing punishments for these offenses.

• It ensures justice by balancing the rights of the accused and the victim.

• Example: Sections of the IPC define crimes like Section 302 (Punishment for Murder) and Section 376 (Punishment for Rape).

• Modernization in Criminal Law: The BNS aims to address emerging crimes, such as cybercrime, financial fraud, and terrorism, that were not adequately covered under the IPC.

• It incorporates provisions for victim compensation and emphasizes the principles of restorative justice.

• Difference between Substantive and Procedural Law: Substantive law deals with the rights and duties of individuals and the punishments for crimes.

• Procedural law, such as the CrPC, outlines the steps for the enforcement of substantive law, including investigation and trial processes.

• Relevance of the Indian Evidence Act: The Indian Evidence Act, 1872, governs the admissibility of evidence in judicial proceedings.
