29. What do you call the rate of formation of new organic matter by consumers?

A. Tertiary productivity

B. Primary productivity

C. Net productivity

D. Secondary productivity

Answer D

Solution

Secondary productivity refers to the rate at which consumers create new organic matter. This includes organisms such as protists, fungi, and animals like deer, cows, goats, and horses. It is important to note that secondary productivity is distinct from primary productivity, which is the rate at which producers create new organic matter through photosynthesis. Gross primary productivity measures the total rate of photosynthesis and organic matter consumed during respiration, while net primary productivity is the difference between gross primary production and ecosystem organisms' respiration.
