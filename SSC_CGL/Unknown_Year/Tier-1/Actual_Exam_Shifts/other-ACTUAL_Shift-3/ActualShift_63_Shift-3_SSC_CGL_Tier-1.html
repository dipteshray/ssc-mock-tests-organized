10. Which of the following is the most acidic?

A. HCOOH

B. C2H5COOH

C. C3H7COOH

D. CH3COOH

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is HCOOH. Key Points Additional Information

• HCOOH, also known as formic acid, is the simplest carboxylic acid and is the most acidic among the given options.

• Formic acid has the highest acidity due to the absence of electron-donating alkyl groups which decrease the acidity by destabilizing the conjugate base.

• The other options, such as C2H5COOH (propanoic acid), C3H7COOH (butanoic acid), and CH3COOH (acetic acid), have alkyl groups that reduce their acidity.

• Formic acid is widely used in various industrial processes and has a higher acid dissociation constant (Ka) compared to other carboxylic acids listed.

• Carboxylic Acids Carboxylic acids are organic acids characterized by the presence of a carboxyl group (–COOH).

• The acidity of carboxylic acids is due to the resonance stabilization of the carboxylate anion (R-COO−) formed after deprotonation.

• Electron-withdrawing groups increase acidity by stabilizing the carboxylate anion, while electron-donating groups decrease acidity by destabilizing it.

• Acid Dissociation Constant (Ka) The acid dissociation constant (Ka) is a quantitative measure of the strength of an acid in solution.

• It is the equilibrium constant for the dissociation of an acid into its conjugate base and a proton (H+).

• Higher Ka values indicate stronger acids, as they dissociate more completely in solution.

• Formic acid has a higher Ka value compared to the other carboxylic acids listed, making it more acidic.

• Factors Influencing Acidity Inductive Effect: Electron-withdrawing groups increase acidity, while electron-donating groups decrease it.

• Resonance Effect: Resonance stabilization of the conjugate base increases acidity.

• Hybridization: Acidity increases with the s-character of the hybrid orbital holding the acidic hydrogen (sp > sp2 > sp3).

• Solvation: Better solvation of the conjugate base in a solvent increases acidity.

12. Which of the following is NOT a greenhouse gas?

A. Methane

B. Carbon dioxide

C. Nitrous oxide

D. Nitric oxide

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is Nitric oxide. Key Points Additional Information

• Nitric oxide (NO) is not considered a greenhouse gas because it does not have a significant impact on the Earth's temperature or climate.

• Greenhouse gases are those that can absorb and emit infrared radiation, leading to the greenhouse effect. Nitric oxide does not significantly contribute to this process.

• Common greenhouse gases include carbon dioxide (CO2), methane (CH4), and nitrous oxide (N2O), all of which have a much higher potential to trap heat in the atmosphere.

• Nitric oxide is primarily known for its role in the formation of smog and its impact on the respiratory system, rather than its role as a greenhouse gas.

• Greenhouse Gases: Greenhouse gases are those gases in the Earth's atmosphere that trap heat. They allow sunlight to enter the atmosphere freely, but when the Earth's surface emits the energy back as infrared radiation (heat), greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit this radiation, warming the planet.

• The primary greenhouse gases in Earth's atmosphere are water vapor (H2O), carbon dioxide (CO2), methane (CH4), nitrous oxide (N2O), and ozone (O3).

• Human activities, such as burning fossil fuels, deforestation, and industrial processes, have significantly increased the concentrations of these greenhouse gases, leading to global warming and climate change.

• Carbon Dioxide (CO2): Carbon dioxide is a colorless, odorless gas that is essential for plant life through the process of photosynthesis.

• It is produced by the combustion of fossil fuels, deforestation, and various industrial processes.

• CO2 is the most significant long-lived greenhouse gas in Earth's atmosphere, contributing to global warming and climate change.

• Methane (CH4): Methane is a potent greenhouse gas with a global warming potential many times greater than that of CO2 over a 20-year period.

• It is produced naturally through the decomposition of organic matter and human activities such as livestock farming, rice cultivation, and landfills.

• Despite its shorter atmospheric lifetime compared to CO2, methane's impact on global warming is substantial.

• Nitrous Oxide (N2O): Nitrous oxide is a greenhouse gas that is more effective than CO2 at trapping heat in the atmosphere over a 100-year period.

• It is emitted from agricultural and industrial activities, as well as during the combustion of fossil fuels and solid waste.

• N2O also plays a role in the depletion of the ozone layer in the stratosphere.

16. In the year 1925, who extracted lipids from an erythrocyte sample and found that lipid monolayers are good for measuring molecular surface area versus lateral pressure?

A. Gorter and Grendel

B. Carson and Eccles

C. Avery and Buck

D. Margulis and Ruska

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Gorter and Grendel. Key Points Additional Information

• In 1925, Dutch scientists Evert Gorter and François Grendel conducted a groundbreaking experiment involving erythrocyte (red blood cell) lipid extraction.

• They found that the lipids extracted from erythrocytes could form a monolayer, which was pivotal in understanding the molecular surface area versus lateral pressure.

• Their work was fundamental in the development of the fluid mosaic model of cell membranes.

• Gorter and Grendel's experiment was one of the first to suggest that cell membranes are composed of a lipid bilayer.

• Lipid Monolayers and Bilayers: A lipid monolayer is a single layer of lipid molecules arranged on the surface of the water, with their hydrophobic tails in the air and hydrophilic heads in the water.

• A lipid bilayer is a double layer of lipids that form the fundamental structure of cell membranes, with hydrophobic tails facing inward and hydrophilic heads facing outward.

• These structures are crucial for maintaining the integrity and functionality of cell membranes.

• Erythrocytes (Red Blood Cells): Erythrocytes are the most common type of blood cell and are responsible for transporting oxygen from the lungs to the body's tissues and carbon dioxide from the tissues back to the lungs.

• Their membrane composition and properties are essential for their deformability and functionality.

• Surface Area vs. Lateral Pressure: This concept involves measuring the surface area occupied by lipid molecules as a function of the lateral pressure applied to the monolayer.

• It is used to study the physical properties of lipid layers and understand cell membrane mechanics.

• Fluid Mosaic Model: Proposed by Singer and Nicolson in 1972, this model describes the cell membrane as a fluid combination of lipids and proteins.

• The lipids provide a flexible matrix, while proteins float in or on this lipid bilayer, contributing to various cellular functions.

25. Which of the following options indicates the size of PPLO (Pleuro Pneumonia Like Organisms)?

A. About 30 µm

B. About 20 µm

C. About 0.1 µm

D. About 10 µm

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is About 0.1 µm. Key Points Additional Information

• PPLOs (Pleuro Pneumonia Like Organisms) are the smallest free-living organisms known.

• They are characterized by their extremely small size, about 0.1 µm in diameter.

• PPLOs lack a cell wall, which contributes to their small size and flexible shape.

• These organisms are responsible for causing respiratory diseases, particularly in livestock.

• PPLO (Pleuro Pneumonia Like Organisms): PPLOs are a group of bacteria that belong to the class Mollicutes.

• These organisms are known for their lack of a rigid cell wall, making them resistant to antibiotics that target cell wall synthesis.

• They are often referred to as mycoplasmas, with Mycoplasma pneumoniae being a well-known species causing atypical pneumonia in humans.

• PPLOs thrive in osmotically balanced environments due to their lack of a cell wall, which makes them susceptible to osmotic lysis in hypotonic conditions.

• Importance in Research: The small size and simple genetic structure of PPLOs make them important models for studying the minimal requirements for cellular life.

• Research on PPLOs has provided insights into the evolution of bacteria and the mechanisms of pathogenesis in respiratory infections.

• Clinical Relevance: PPLOs are significant pathogens in veterinary medicine, causing respiratory diseases such as contagious bovine pleuropneumonia.

• In humans, mycoplasmas can lead to a range of respiratory and urogenital infections, requiring specific diagnostic and treatment approaches.

• Due to their lack of a cell wall, infections caused by PPLOs are treated with antibiotics that inhibit protein synthesis, such as tetracyclines or macrolides.
