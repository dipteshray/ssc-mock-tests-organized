1. Gangubai Hangal belonged to the ______.

• It is known for its melodious and improvisational style, focusing on the purity of notes and elaborate raga development.

• Gharana The term 'gharana' means 'family' or 'lineage' in Hindi.

• Each gharana has its own distinct style, techniques, and repertoire.

• Hindustani Classical Music It is the classical music of North India, distinct from the Carnatic music of South India.

• It has a rich history dating back to the Vedic times and has evolved over centuries, integrating various cultural influences.

• The music is characterized by its raga (melodic framework) and tala (rhythmic pattern).

3. Which among the following is NOT a classification criterion of drugs?

A. Chemical structure

B. Molecular target

C. Behavioural condition

D. Pharmacological effect

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Behavioural condition. Key Points Additional Information

• Behavioural condition is not typically used as a classification criterion for drugs.

• Drugs are generally classified based on their chemical structure, molecular target, and pharmacological effect.

• Classification based on chemical structure involves grouping drugs with similar molecular frameworks.

• Molecular target classification groups drugs based on the biological molecules they interact with, while pharmacological effect classification is based on the therapeutic effects of the drugs.

• Chemical Structure: This classification method involves grouping drugs with similar chemical compositions or molecular frameworks.

• Examples include beta-lactams, benzodiazepines, and phenothiazines.

• Molecular Target: This method classifies drugs based on the specific biological molecules they interact with.

• For example, drugs that target enzymes, receptors, ion channels, or nucleic acids.

• Pharmacological Effect: Drugs are classified based on the therapeutic effects they produce.

• For example, antihypertensives, analgesics, antipyretics, and antidepressants.

• Gandhi believed in the power of peaceful protest and civil disobedience as tools for social and political change.

6. What happens when two forces act in the opposite directions on an object?

A. The net force acting on the object is the difference between the two objects.

B. The net force acting on the object is the difference between the two forces.

C. The net force acting on the object is the total of the two forces.

D. The net force acting on the object is the sum of the two objects.

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is The net force acting on the object is the difference between the two forces.. Key Points Additional Information

• When two forces act in opposite directions on an object, the net force is the difference between the two forces.

• The direction of the net force is in the direction of the larger force.

• If the forces are equal in magnitude but opposite in direction, they cancel each other out, resulting in a net force of zero.

• The concept of net force is crucial in understanding how forces influence the motion of objects according to Newton's Laws of Motion.

• Force A force is a push or pull upon an object resulting from its interaction with another object. Forces can cause objects to accelerate, decelerate, remain in place, or change shape.

• Forces are vector quantities, meaning they have both magnitude and direction.

• Forces can be categorized into contact forces (e.g., friction, tension) and action-at-a-distance forces (e.g., gravitational, electromagnetic).

• Net Force The net force is the vector sum of all the forces acting on an object.

• It determines the overall effect of all the forces on the motion of the object.

• According to Newton's Second Law of Motion, the net force acting on an object is equal to the mass of the object multiplied by its acceleration (F = ma).

• Newton's Laws of Motion First Law (Law of Inertia): An object will remain at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by a net external force.

• Second Law (Law of Acceleration): The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass (F = ma).

• Third Law (Action and Reaction): For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

• Equilibrium An object is in equilibrium when the net force acting on it is zero.

• In equilibrium, an object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion continues to move at a constant velocity.

• Equilibrium can be static (object at rest) or dynamic (object moving with constant velocity).

Solution

The correct answer is Justice Verma Committee. Key Points Additional Information

• The Justice Verma Committee was formed to identify the existence of legal provisions for the implementation of some of the Fundamental Duties enshrined in the Constitution of India.

• The Committee was set up in 1999 under the chairmanship of Justice J.S. Verma, former Chief Justice of India.

• It reviewed the existing legal framework and suggested measures to make the Fundamental Duties more enforceable.

• The Committee's recommendations emphasized the role of education and awareness in promoting a sense of duty among citizens.

• Fundamental Duties The Fundamental Duties are a set of moral obligations for all citizens of India, enshrined in Part IV-A of the Constitution (Article 51A).

• These duties were added by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976, based on the recommendations of the Swaran Singh Committee.

• There are currently 11 Fundamental Duties, which include respect for the Constitution, national symbols, the promotion of harmony, and the protection of public property.

• The Fundamental Duties are non-justiciable, meaning they are not legally enforceable by the courts.

• Justice Verma Committee Justice J.S. Verma, a renowned jurist and former Chief Justice of India, chaired the committee.

• The Committee's focus was to enhance the implementation of Fundamental Duties through legal and educational reforms.

• It highlighted the need for incorporating the Fundamental Duties into the education system to instill a sense of responsibility in citizens from a young age.

• The Committee's report has been influential in shaping policies and programs aimed at promoting civic responsibility.

• 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 The 42nd Amendment Act is also known as the "Mini-Constitution" due to its extensive changes to the Constitution of India.

• It introduced the Fundamental Duties, provided for the establishment of Administrative Tribunals, and made several changes to the Preamble and other parts of the Constitution.

• The Amendment was passed during the Emergency period under Prime Minister Indira Gandhi's government.

• It aimed to strengthen the central government's powers and bring about socio-economic reforms.

15. Which of the following is a saturated hydrocarbon?

A. C3H8

B. C6H6

C. C2H4

D. C4H8

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is C3H8. Key Points Additional Information

• C3H8 is the chemical formula for propane, which is a saturated hydrocarbon.

• Saturated hydrocarbons contain only single bonds between carbon atoms.

• Propane is an alkane, which is a type of saturated hydrocarbon.

• Alkanes have the general formula CnH2n+2, and propane (C3H8) fits this formula.

• Saturated Hydrocarbons: Saturated hydrocarbons are hydrocarbons in which all the carbon atoms are connected by single bonds. They are also known as alkanes.

• Alkanes have the general formula CnH2n+2, where n is the number of carbon atoms.

• Examples include methane (CH4), ethane (C2H6), and propane (C3H8).

• Unsaturated Hydrocarbons: Unsaturated hydrocarbons contain at least one double or triple bond between carbon atoms.

• Alkenes are hydrocarbons with one or more double bonds (general formula CnH2n), such as ethene (C2H4) and propene (C3H6).

• Alkynes are hydrocarbons with one or more triple bonds (general formula CnH2n-2), such as ethyne (C2H2) and propyne (C3H4).

• Benzene (C6H6): Benzene is an aromatic hydrocarbon, characterized by a ring of six carbon atoms with alternating double bonds.

• Aromatic hydrocarbons have unique stability and properties due to their conjugated pi-electron systems.

• Hydrocarbon Classes: Hydrocarbons can be classified into alkanes (saturated), alkenes (unsaturated), alkynes (unsaturated), and aromatic hydrocarbons.

• Each class has distinct chemical properties and reactivities.

• It is calculated by subtracting depreciation from Gross National Product (GNP).

• Factor Cost refers to the cost of factors of production (i.e., labor, capital, land) used to produce goods and services.

• National Income represents the total income earned by a country’s residents and businesses, including any income from abroad.

• Gross National Product (GNP) It is the total value of all goods and services produced by the residents of a country within a specific time period, usually a year.

• GNP includes the value of products made by a country’s citizens both domestically and abroad.

• Net National Product (NNP) It is calculated by subtracting the depreciation (wear and tear of capital goods) from the Gross National Product.

• NNP = GNP - Depreciation.

• Factor Cost It refers to the costs of the factors of production (labor, capital, land, and entrepreneurship) used in creating goods and services.

• Factor Cost does not include taxes or subsidies on products.

• Market Price (MP) It is the price at which goods and services are sold in the market.

• Market Price includes indirect taxes and excludes subsidies provided by the government.
