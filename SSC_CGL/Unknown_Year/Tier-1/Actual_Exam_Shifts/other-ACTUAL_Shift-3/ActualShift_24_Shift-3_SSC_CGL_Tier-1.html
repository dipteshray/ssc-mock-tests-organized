1. Fill in the blank:The ritual masked dances performed at Hemis monastery symbolise various _____ associated with the Drugpa tradition.

B. royal ancestors

C. reborn teachers

D. clan elders

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is divine protectors. Key Points Additional Information

• The masked dances performed at Hemis Monastery are known as the "Cham dance" and are deeply rooted in Tibetan Buddhism.

• These dances are a form of meditation and an offering to the divine forces, particularly the protectors of the Buddhist faith.

• The Cham dances specifically symbolize the activities and powers of deities and divine protectors associated with the Drukpa (Drugpa) tradition of Tibetan Buddhism.

• The performances are believed to ward off evil spirits and negative energies, bringing spiritual harmony and blessings to the participants and onlookers.

• The Hemis Festival, during which these dances are performed, is dedicated to the spiritual leader Padmasambhava (Guru Rinpoche), who is considered an incarnation of Buddha and a protector of the Buddhist faith.

• Royal ancestors: While Tibetan Buddhist rituals honor historical and religious figures, the concept of royal ancestors does not align with the symbolic meaning of the Cham dance.

• The focus of the dance is on spiritual protection and blessings rather than paying homage to royal lineage or ancestors.

• Reborn teachers: In Tibetan Buddhism, reborn teachers or tulkus are revered spiritual leaders believed to be reincarnations of past masters.

• However, the Cham dance is not primarily focused on these reborn teachers but rather on divine entities and protectors.

• Clan elders: Clan elders are respected figures in many traditional and tribal communities, but this concept is not directly related to the Cham dances performed at Hemis Monastery.

• The dances are more about spiritual protection and blessings than honoring clan elders.

• The Hemis Monastery and the Drukpa tradition: The Hemis Monastery, located in Ladakh, is a significant center of the Drukpa Kagyu lineage of Tibetan Buddhism.

• The Drukpa tradition emphasizes the integration of wisdom and compassion in spiritual practice.

• The Hemis Festival, celebrated annually, showcases the rich cultural and spiritual heritage of this tradition, with the Cham dance being a central attraction.

Solution

• The term "Adeya" refers to compulsory surrender or dues, and this term was significant during the Gupta period.

• In the Gupta administration, Adeya was used to describe taxes or obligations that were mandatory for the subjects to pay to the state.

• It was part of the complex taxation system that supported the empire's administration, military, and infrastructure.

• The Gupta period was known for its well-organized administrative system, including various forms of taxation that ensured the efficient functioning of the state.

• This system of dues and taxes highlights the structured governance and economic policies implemented during the Gupta dynasty.

• It typically referred to a portion of the produce (often 1/6th) given to the king by the cultivators.

• This form of taxation was an essential source of revenue for the Gupta Empire.

• Vaishtika – Forced Military Service: The term "Vaishtika" is not associated with forced military service.

• During the Gupta period, military service was generally not forced; it was a professional duty performed by soldiers, many of whom were paid for their services.

• The Gupta Empire relied on a well-maintained army, including elephant corps, cavalry, and infantry, to defend and expand its territory.

• Dasa-paradha – Irrigation Tax: The term "Dasa-paradha" does not refer to an irrigation tax. In the Gupta period, taxes on irrigation systems were not specifically recorded under this term.

• Instead, the Gupta rulers invested in infrastructure, including irrigation systems, to enhance agricultural productivity.

• Revenue from agriculture, including taxes like Bhaga, played a crucial role in the empire's economy.

• Gupta Administration and Economy: The Gupta Empire (circa 320–550 CE) is often referred to as the "Golden Age" of India due to its advancements in art, science, and governance.

• The economy was primarily agrarian, but trade and commerce also flourished during this period.

• The taxation system was well-structured, with various forms of taxes, including land revenue (Bhaga), trade taxes, and other dues like Adeya.

7. How did Pitt’s India Act strengthen administrative unity in British India?

A. By giving Presidencies autonomy

B. By empowering the Court of Directors

C. By making the Governor-General superior to other Governors

D. By separating civil and military authority

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is By making the Governor-General superior to other Governors. Key Points Additional Information

• Pitt's India Act, enacted in 1784, established greater control over the British administration in India by the Crown, marking a shift from the East India Company’s dominant role to more centralized authority.

• The Act introduced a distinction between commercial and political functions, ensuring that the Governor-General in Bengal had superior powers over other Governors in Madras and Bombay.

• The Governor-General’s Council was strengthened, granting it authority over all provinces, thereby promoting administrative unity across British India.

• It created the Board of Control, which was responsible for overseeing political affairs and ensuring the Governor-General’s supremacy in governance.

• This centralization of power was critical in reducing administrative discrepancies among presidencies and enhancing coordination in decision-making processes.

• Option 1: By giving Presidencies autonomy Pitt's India Act did not grant autonomy to the presidencies; rather, it centralized power under the Governor-General and the Board of Control, reducing the independence of Madras and Bombay presidencies.

• Autonomy of presidencies was considered unsuitable due to the need for unified governance in British India.

• Option 2: By empowering the Court of Directors The Court of Directors retained control over commercial functions under Pitt’s India Act, but their political power was curtailed by the creation of the Board of Control.

• The Act sought to balance the East India Company’s commercial interests with the Crown’s political oversight, rather than empowering the Court of Directors politically.

• Option 4: By separating civil and military authority Pitt's India Act did not specifically emphasize separating civil and military authority.

• Instead, it focused on centralizing governance, where the Governor-General and his Council managed both civil and military affairs with the backing of the Board of Control.

• Main Theme: Administrative Unity under Pitt’s India Act The Act was introduced to address the inefficiencies and corruption prevalent under the East India Company’s administration and to establish a unified governance structure.

• It marked a significant step in British imperial control over India, laying the foundation for subsequent legislative measures that further consolidated British rule.

8. Statement 1:A pawn reaching the final rank can be promoted to any piece except king or pawn. Statement 2:Pawn promotion is restricted to already captured pieces. Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. Only 1

B. Only 2

C. Both 1 and 2

D. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Option 1. Key Points Additional Information

• Statement 1 is correct: A pawn that reaches the final (8th rank for white and 1st rank for black) can be promoted to any piece of higher value except a king or another pawn.

• This rule allows the player to choose between a queen, rook, bishop, or knight, irrespective of whether the original piece is still on the board.

• The most common choice for promotion is a queen because it is the most powerful piece on the board, a process referred to as "queening."

• Pawn promotion is a key rule in chess and significantly impacts the strategy and outcome of the game.

• The player may choose a piece that best fits the strategic needs of the position, such as promoting to a knight for a tactical advantage.

• Statement 2 is incorrect: Pawn promotion is not restricted to pieces that have already been captured. A player can promote their pawn to any piece (queen, rook, bishop, or knight) regardless of whether that piece is still present on the board.

• This rule enables players to have multiple queens or other pieces simultaneously, depending on the promotion choice.

• Important Concepts Related to Pawn Promotion: Underpromotion: Choosing a rook, bishop, or knight instead of a queen during a pawn promotion is called underpromotion. This is often done to avoid stalemates or to achieve a specific tactical or positional goal.

• Queening: This is the term used when a pawn is promoted to a queen, which is the most powerful piece on the board.

• Promotion Strategy: The choice of promotion depends on the specific position and the strategic needs of the game. While queening is most common, underpromotions can be decisive in certain situations.

• Chess Rules Overview: Chess is a two-player strategy game played on a square board with 64 squares arranged in an 8×8 grid.

• Each player controls 16 pieces: 1 king, 1 queen, 2 rooks, 2 knights, 2 bishops, and 8 pawns.

• The objective is to checkmate the opponent's king, whereby the king is in a position to be captured ("in check") and there is no way to remove the threat.

A. 1 and 2 only

B. 2 and 3 only

C. 1 and 3 only

D. All three

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Option 1 (1 and 2 only). Key Points Additional Information

• Statement 1: Tendons attach muscles to bones and consist of dense regular connective tissue. This is accurate since tendons are composed of collagen fibers arranged in parallel bundles, providing strength to withstand tensile forces.

• Statement 2: Ligaments join bones together and possess elasticity. This is correct as ligaments are composed of elastic fibers and collagen, which provide flexibility and stabilization between bones.

• Statement 3: Cartilage is softer than bone and does not contain calcium deposits like bones. Therefore, this statement is incorrect, as cartilage is not harder than bone.

• Cartilage is a flexible connective tissue found in areas like joints, the respiratory tract, and the ear, primarily composed of chondrocytes and extracellular matrix.

• Hence, only Statements 1 and 2 are correct, making Option 1 the right choice.

• Tendons: Tendons are fibrous connective tissues connecting muscles to bones, enabling movement.

• They are composed of dense regular connective tissue, with collagen fibers arranged in parallel bundles.

• Tendons are less elastic compared to ligaments, as their primary function is to withstand tension during muscle contraction.

• Examples include the Achilles tendon, which connects the calf muscle to the heel bone.

• Ligaments: Ligaments are elastic connective tissues that join bones at joints and provide stability while allowing movement.

• They contain both collagen and elastic fibers, giving them flexibility and strength.

• An example is the anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) in the knee joint, which prevents excessive movement and stabilizes the joint.

• Cartilage: Cartilage is a resilient and flexible connective tissue found in joints, the rib cage, nose, ear, throat, and intervertebral discs.

• Unlike bone, cartilage does not contain calcium deposits, making it softer and more flexible.

• Types of cartilage include hyaline cartilage (found in joints), elastic cartilage (found in the ear), and fibrocartilage (found in intervertebral discs).

• Its function is to reduce friction between bones, absorb shock, and provide structural support.

17. Which curve represents combinations of two goods that provide equal satisfaction to a consumer?

A. Isoquant

B. Budget line

C. Indifference curve

D. Cost curve

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Indifference curve. Key Points Additional Information

• An indifference curve represents the combination of two goods that provide the same level of satisfaction or utility to a consumer.

• The concept is based on the theory of consumer behavior, where the consumer is assumed to have a preference ranking for goods.

• Indifference curves are typically downward-sloping and convex to the origin, reflecting the principle of diminishing marginal rate of substitution (MRS).

• Each point on the curve indicates a different combination of two goods that the consumer considers equally preferable.

• Indifference curves cannot intersect because it would violate the assumption that higher curves represent higher satisfaction levels.

• Isoquant An isoquant is a curve that shows all the combinations of two inputs (e.g., labor and capital) that produce the same level of output.

• It is primarily used in production theory to study the trade-offs between different input factors.

• Isoquants are similar to indifference curves but deal with production instead of consumption.

• They are also convex to the origin, reflecting the principle of diminishing marginal returns to inputs.

• Budget Line The budget line represents all the combinations of two goods that a consumer can afford given their income and the prices of the goods.

• It is a straight line with a slope equal to the negative price ratio of the two goods.

• The point where the budget line is tangent to an indifference curve represents the consumer's optimal consumption bundle.

• The budget line shifts outward or inward with changes in income, and its slope changes with changes in the relative prices of goods.

• Cost Curve A cost curve represents the relationship between a firm's output and its associated costs.

• Examples include total cost curves, average cost curves, and marginal cost curves.

• These curves are used in the study of production and cost optimization in economics.

• They differ fundamentally from indifference curves as they deal with costs incurred in production rather than consumer preferences.

25. Which natural process mainly causes auroras near the polar regions?

A. Seismic activity

B. Atmospheric lightning

C. Interaction of solar wind with Earth’s magnetic field

D. Volcanic emissions

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Interaction of solar wind with Earth’s magnetic field. Key Points Additional Information

• Auroras occur due to the interaction between solar wind particles and the Earth's magnetic field.

• Solar wind consists of charged particles (mainly electrons and protons) emitted by the Sun during its energetic activities.

• These particles collide with gases in the Earth's upper atmosphere near the polar regions, exciting the atoms and causing them to emit light.

• The Earth's magnetic field channels the solar wind towards the poles, creating the mesmerizing light displays known as auroras.

• Commonly known as the Aurora Borealis (Northern Lights) in the northern hemisphere and the Aurora Australis (Southern Lights) in the southern hemisphere.

• Seismic Activity: Seismic activity refers to the movement or vibration within the Earth's crust due to tectonic forces, earthquakes, or volcanic activity.

• It does not contribute to aurora formation as it is entirely unrelated to atmospheric or magnetic phenomena.

• Seismic activity primarily impacts the Earth's surface and subsurface regions.

• Atmospheric Lightning: Atmospheric lightning occurs during thunderstorms when electrical charges build up in clouds.

• While lightning produces brief flashes of light, it is unrelated to auroras, which are prolonged light displays caused by solar wind interaction.

• Lightning is a localized phenomenon, whereas auroras occur over large polar regions.

• Volcanic Emissions: Volcanic eruptions release gases like sulfur dioxide and ash into the atmosphere.

• These emissions can affect air quality and climate but do not play a role in aurora formation.

• Volcanic emissions are terrestrial phenomena, whereas auroras are extraterrestrial in origin.

• Main Theme: Auroras are natural light displays caused by the interaction of solar wind with the Earth's magnetic field and atmosphere.

• The color and pattern of auroras depend on the type of atmospheric gas and altitude of the interaction.

• This phenomenon provides valuable insights into the Earth's magnetosphere and solar activity.
