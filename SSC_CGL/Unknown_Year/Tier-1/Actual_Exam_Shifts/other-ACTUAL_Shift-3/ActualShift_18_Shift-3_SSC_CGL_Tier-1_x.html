1. Which of the following statements regarding Mumbai’s built heritage are correct? 1. The Victorian Gothic structures in Mumbai showcase colonial architecture inspired by Neo-Gothic design. 2. Mumbai’s Art Deco buildings emerged during the 19th-century colonial phase. 3. Oval Maidan serves as a spatial divide between Victorian Gothic and Art Deco precincts. Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. Only 1 and 2

B. Only 2 and 3

C. Only 1 and 3

D. 1, 2 and 3

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Only 1 and 3. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Statement 1: The Victorian Gothic structures in Mumbai showcase colonial architecture inspired by Neo-Gothic design. Victorian Gothic architecture was introduced in Mumbai during British colonial rule and is characterized by pointed arches, ribbed vaults, and decorative patterns inspired by Neo-Gothic design.

• Examples include iconic buildings such as the Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus (formerly Victoria Terminus) and Bombay High Court.

• This style symbolizes the British influence on Indian architecture during the late 19th century. Hence, statement 1 is correct.

• Statement 2: Mumbai’s Art Deco buildings emerged during the 19th-century colonial phase. Art Deco architecture in Mumbai emerged in the early 20th century, particularly during the 1930s and 1940s, not the 19th-century colonial phase.

• This style was influenced by the global Art Deco movement, which emphasized geometric patterns, streamlined forms, and modernity.

• Examples include the Eros Cinema and buildings along Marine Drive. Hence, statement 2 is incorrect.

• Statement 3: Oval Maidan serves as a spatial divide between Victorian Gothic and Art Deco precincts. The Oval Maidan, a large open space in South Mumbai, divides the Victorian Gothic buildings on one side and the Art Deco buildings on the other side.

• This unique spatial arrangement highlights the transition between two distinct architectural styles in Mumbai.

• It is a prominent landmark illustrating the city's rich architectural heritage. Hence, statement 3 is correct.

• Thus, the correct answer is Only 1 and 3.

• Victorian Gothic Architecture: Originated in the mid-19th century during the reign of Queen Victoria.

• Key features include pointed arches, intricate carvings, stained glass windows, and spires.

• In Mumbai, this style is prominently seen in colonial-era buildings like the Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus, Bombay University, and High Court.

• Art Deco Architecture: Art Deco emerged in the early 20th century and gained prominence during the 1930s and 1940s.

• Key features include geometric shapes, symmetry, streamlined forms, and bold colors.

• Mumbai’s Marine Drive houses one of the largest collections of Art Deco buildings in the world.

• It represents a modern architectural style adapted to tropical conditions.

• Oval Maidan: Located in South Mumbai, it is a large recreational ground and a significant urban space.

• It serves as a physical division between Victorian Gothic structures (such as Mumbai University) and Art Deco buildings (such as those along Marine Drive).

• Recognized as part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site titled “Victorian Gothic and Art Deco Ensemble of Mumbai.”

• Mumbai's UNESCO World Heritage Status: In 2018, Mumbai's Victorian Gothic and Art Deco architecture were collectively recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

• This honor underscores the city's architectural diversity and historical significance.

• Role of Colonial Influence: The British colonial rule significantly shaped Mumbai’s architectural landscape, introducing Victorian Gothic and later Art Deco styles.

• UNESCO Recognition: The recognition by UNESCO highlights the global importance of preserving Mumbai’s architectural heritage.

• Architectural Evolution in Mumbai: Mumbai's architecture reflects its transformation from a colonial port city to a modern metropolis.

3. Which of the following officer–function pairs from the Gupta administration is correctly matched?

A. Dhruvadhikaranika – Keeper of royal treasury

B. Bhandagaradhikrita – Head of policing

C. Talavattaka – Revenue assessor

D. Utkhetayita – Land measurement officer

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is Option 4. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Dhruvadhikaranika: This officer was responsible for maintaining records and ensuring the proper functioning of the royal treasury. However, this designation was not synonymous with a "Keeper of the Royal Treasury." Hence, this statement is incorrect.

• Bhandagaradhikrita: This officer was tasked with the management of the royal treasury, not policing. Policing duties were handled by other officers such as the Dandapashika. Hence, this statement is incorrect.

• Talavattaka: This title refers to individuals involved in musical or cultural activities, particularly those related to playing cymbals or managing cultural performances. It is not related to revenue assessment. Hence, this statement is incorrect.

• Utkhetayita: This officer in the Gupta administration was responsible for measuring land, often for revenue assessment purposes. Accurate land measurement was crucial for taxation and administrative records. Hence, this statement is correct.

• Gupta Administration: The Gupta Empire (circa 320-550 CE) is known for its efficient administrative system and the decentralization of power to regional and local officers.

• They implemented a systematic land revenue system, which was the primary source of income for the state.

• The empire was divided into provinces called Bhukti, governed by Uparika, and further subdivided into districts called Vishayas.

• Other Key Officers: Dandapashika: Responsible for maintaining law and order in the empire.

• Sandhivigrahika: Minister for war and peace, responsible for treaties and alliances.

• Kumaramatya: High-ranking officials who were often part of the king's council and managed administrative responsibilities.

• Land Revenue System: Land was classified into different types such as Kshetra (cultivable land), Kharvataka (waste land), and others, based on productivity.

• Land measurement was crucial for taxation, and officers like the Utkhetayita ensured accurate records were maintained.

• A portion of the produce, usually one-sixth to one-fourth, was collected as tax.

• Key Features of Gupta Period: The Gupta Empire is often referred to as the Golden Age of India due to its advancements in science, mathematics, literature, and art.

• Famous personalities like Kalidasa (literature), Aryabhata (mathematics and astronomy), and Varahamihira (astronomy) flourished during this period.

• The administrative framework laid down by the Guptas influenced future dynasties in India.

• Key Terminologies: Bhukti: Province in the Gupta Empire.

• Vishaya: District under a province.

• Grama: Village unit, forming the base of the administrative structure.

• Land Ownership: Land was either owned by the state, individuals, or religious institutions.

• Tax exemptions were often provided to Brahmins and temples.

• Gupta Coins: Known for their artistic excellence, Gupta coins were made of gold, silver, and copper and depicted the rulers and deities.

• Decline of the Gupta Empire: The empire weakened due to invasions by the Hunas and internal strife among regional rulers.

4. Which classical text authored by Sharangadeva in the 13th century CE is regarded as a cornerstone of Indian music theory?

A. Natya Shastra

B. Sangita Sudha

C. Sangita Ratnakara

D. Abhinaya Chandrika

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Sangita Ratnakara. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Sangita Ratnakara is a renowned treatise on Indian classical music written by Sarangadeva in the 13th century CE. It is regarded as a cornerstone of Indian music theory.

• The text serves as a comprehensive guide to both Hindustani and Carnatic music traditions, covering their theoretical and practical aspects extensively.

• Sarangadeva composed the Sangita Ratnakara in Deccan India, and its influence spread widely across the Indian subcontinent.

• The treatise is divided into seven chapters, discussing topics like musical notes (Swara), scales (Raga), rhythms (Tala), instruments, vocal music, and dance.

• The Sangita Ratnakara bridges the gap between the ancient and medieval traditions of Indian music, making it an essential text for understanding the evolution of music theory in India.

• Natya Shastra: Authored by Bharata Muni, this ancient text deals primarily with drama, dance, and music.

• It is considered one of the oldest treatises on performing arts, written around 200 BCE to 200 CE.

• The Natya Shastra lays the foundation for the Rasa theory and provides guidelines for theatrical performances, including music as an accompaniment.

• While it contributed to music theory, its primary focus is on drama and dance.

• Sangita Sudha: Sangita Sudha is a treatise composed by Govinda Dikshitar, a musicologist and minister in the Nayak dynasty of Tamil Nadu.

• It primarily focuses on Carnatic music and is regarded as an influential text in South Indian music traditions.

• It is not as comprehensive as Sangita Ratnakara in covering the broader aspects of Indian music.

• Abhinaya Chandrika: This text is primarily focused on dance and gestures rather than music.

• It was authored by Maheshwara Mahapatra and deals with the art of Odissi dance.

• Although it includes some references to musical accompaniment in dance, its main emphasis is on the expressive aspects of dance.

• Features of Sangita Ratnakara: It is considered the last major work of ancient Indian music theory, integrating ancient concepts with medieval practices.

• The text explains technical aspects such as Swara (notes), Raga (melodic frameworks), and Tala (rhythmic cycles).

• It also discusses musical instruments like Veena, Flute, and Mridangam.

• The Sangita Ratnakara serves as a bridge between the older traditions of Samaveda chants and the newer forms of classical music.

• Historical Significance: Sangita Ratnakara is a vital resource for understanding the transition from ancient Vedic music to medieval classical traditions.

• Influence: The text's theories are still studied by musicologists in both Hindustani and Carnatic music systems.

• Legacy: It has inspired numerous other treatises and remains a foundational text for Indian music education.

Solution

The correct answer is Sher Shah Suri. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Sher Shah Suri (original name Farid Khan) was a brilliant Afghan ruler who defeated the Mughal emperor Humayun in the Battle of Chausa in 1539 and the Battle of Kannauj in 1540, forcing Humayun into exile.

• Following his victory, Sher Shah Suri established the Second Afghan Empire in India, which lasted from 1540 to 1555 until Humayun reclaimed the throne.

• Sher Shah Suri implemented a centralized administrative system and introduced reforms that significantly impacted governance, trade, and the economy during his reign.

• He is renowned for constructing the Grand Trunk Road, which connected Bengal in the east to Peshawar in the northwest, facilitating trade and communication.

• His reforms in the revenue system, military structure, and administration laid the foundation for an efficient governance model, influencing later rulers, including the Mughals.

• Sher Shah Suri's Administrative Reforms: He divided his empire into 47 administrative units called sarkars, each further subdivided into parganas.

• The revenue system was reformed to ensure fair tax collection based on the actual produce of the land. Land was measured, and taxes were fixed accordingly.

• He introduced a new silver currency called the Rupiya, which became the standard currency in India and influenced the monetary systems of the Mughals and British.

• Military Reforms: Sher Shah Suri established a strong and centralized military system. Soldiers were recruited directly by the state and paid in cash.

• He maintained a standing army and introduced reforms to ensure discipline and efficiency.

• Public Works: The construction of the Grand Trunk Road (formerly called Uttarapath) connected key trade and cultural centers across India.

• He built sarais (rest houses) along the road for travelers and traders, ensuring their safety and comfort.

• Legacy: Sher Shah Suri's reforms had a long-lasting impact on Indian governance and administration, influencing the policies of the Mughal Empire and even the British colonial administration.

• His short reign is considered one of the most efficient and reformative periods in Indian history.

• Other Options: Humayun: The Mughal emperor defeated by Sher Shah Suri, he later reclaimed the throne in 1555.

• Sikandar Lodi: A ruler of the Lodi dynasty, known for founding the city of Agra but not associated with the Second Afghan Empire.

• Bahlul Lodi: The founder of the Lodi dynasty in 1451, preceding Sher Shah Suri's era.

• Battle of Chausa: Fought in 1539, Sher Shah Suri defeated Humayun, marking the beginning of his rise to power.

• Battle of Kannauj: In 1540, Sher Shah Suri decisively defeated Humayun, forcing him to flee to Persia.

• Second Afghan Empire: Established by Sher Shah Suri, this empire was known for its administrative efficiency and economic reforms.

• Grand Trunk Road: A major contribution by Sher Shah Suri, enhancing trade and connectivity across the Indian subcontinent.

7. What was the chief aim of the Ring-Fencing Policy implemented by the British in India during the 18th century?

A. To annex princely states

B. To monopolize maritime trade

C. To maintain buffer regions for defense

D. To introduce direct colonial administration

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is To maintain buffer regions for defense. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• The Ring-Fencing Policy was an 18th-century British strategy implemented in India to create a buffer around their territories by maintaining friendly relations with neighboring princely states.

• The policy aimed to protect British India from external threats, particularly from the expanding powers of the Marathas, Mysore, and other regional entities.

• It was introduced to ensure that the neighboring states acted as buffer zones between British-controlled territories and potential invaders, such as the French or other European colonial powers.

• This policy marked the beginning of the British strategy to interfere in the internal affairs of Indian princely states, later leading to their political subjugation.

• The policy was prominently applied during Warren Hastings’ tenure as the Governor-General of Bengal (1773–1785).

• Other Objectives of the Ring-Fencing Policy: The policy sought to ensure that the British East India Company did not have to directly involve itself in costly military conflicts with neighboring powers.

• It enabled the British to gain influence over the internal administration of buffer states without directly annexing them.

• Key Examples: The policy was applied in Awadh, which was turned into a buffer state against the Marathas in the late 18th century.

• It was also used in the case of Hyderabad, where the British supported the Nizam to counterbalance the influence of Tipu Sultan of Mysore.

• Warren Hastings’ Role: As the architect of the Ring-Fencing Policy, Hastings focused on alliances with weaker princely states to counter the more powerful regional players like the Marathas and Tipu Sultan.

• He provided military support to these states in exchange for territorial concessions or subsidies.

• Transition to the Doctrine of Lapse: While the Ring-Fencing Policy emphasized maintaining buffer zones, the Doctrine of Lapse (later introduced under Lord Dalhousie) focused on direct annexation of princely states.

• This shift marked a more aggressive phase of British territorial expansion in India.

• Option 1: To annex princely states – Incorrect: The Ring-Fencing Policy was not focused on annexation but rather on creating defensive alliances.

• Option 2: To monopolize maritime trade – Incorrect: This was not the primary aim of the policy, as it focused on land-based defense strategies.

• Option 4: To introduce direct colonial administration – Incorrect: The policy aimed at indirect control through alliances, not direct governance.

Solution

• Statement 1: Transpiration rate increases in dry and windy climates. In dry conditions, the surrounding air has a lower humidity, which increases the water vapor gradient between the leaf and the atmosphere, accelerating transpiration.

• Wind removes the humid air around the stomata, enhancing the diffusion of water vapor out of the leaf. Hence, this statement is correct.

• Statement 2: It facilitates upward water movement through cohesion and tension. Transpiration creates a negative pressure in the leaves, pulling water upward from the roots through the xylem via the cohesion-tension mechanism.

• This process is essential for nutrient transport and maintaining plant hydration. Hence, this statement is correct.

• Statement 3: Transpiration invariably causes wilting. Wilting occurs when water loss through transpiration exceeds water absorption by roots, but it is not an inevitable outcome of transpiration.

18. If the price of a commodity rises by 10 percent and demand declines by 20 percent, how is the demand classified?

A. Elastic demand

B. Inelastic demand

C. Unitary elastic demand

D. Perfectly elastic demand

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Elastic demand. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Elastic demand occurs when the percentage change in quantity demanded is greater than the percentage change in price. In this case, the demand declines by 20% when the price rises by 10%, indicating elasticity.

• The elasticity of demand is calculated using the formula: Elasticity = (% Change in Quantity Demanded) / (% Change in Price). Here, elasticity equals -20%/10% = -2, which is greater than 1 in absolute terms, showing elastic demand.

• Elastic demand is common for goods or services that have close substitutes or are not necessities. Consumers can easily switch to alternatives when prices increase.

• Examples of goods with elastic demand include luxury items such as electronics, branded clothing, and entertainment services.

• Hence, the demand is classified as elastic, which is the correct answer.

• Inelastic demand: Occurs when the percentage change in quantity demanded is less than the percentage change in price (elasticity ).

• Examples include essential commodities such as food, fuel, and medicines, where consumers cannot easily reduce consumption despite price hikes.

• Unitary elastic demand: Occurs when the percentage change in quantity demanded is equal to the percentage change in price (elasticity = 1).

• For example, if a 10% increase in price results in a 10% decrease in demand, it is classified as unitary elastic.

• Perfectly elastic demand: Occurs when even a slight change in price leads to an infinite change in quantity demanded (elasticity = ∞).

• This is rare and usually theoretical, often applicable in perfectly competitive markets.

• Factors affecting elasticity: Availability of substitutes: More substitutes lead to higher elasticity.

• Necessity vs. luxury: Necessities have inelastic demand, while luxuries have elastic demand.

• Time period: Demand tends to be more elastic over the long term as consumers find alternatives.

• Concept of elasticity: Elasticity measures how responsive demand is to changes in price, income, or other factors.

• Types of elasticity: Besides price elasticity, other types include income elasticity (how demand changes with income) and cross elasticity (how demand for one product changes when the price of another product changes).

• Applications: Elasticity is crucial in pricing strategies, tax policies, and market analysis.

• Economic significance: Understanding elasticity helps businesses and governments predict consumer behavior, optimize revenue, and design effective policies.

25. Which of the following is NOT an impact of space weather phenomena such as solar flares and CMEs?

A. Satellite signal disruption

B. Radio communication blackouts

C. Changes in ocean circulation

D. Damage to electrical grids

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Changes in ocean circulation. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Solar flares and Coronal Mass Ejections (CMEs) are space weather phenomena caused by intense activity in the Sun's magnetic fields. These events release massive amounts of energy and charged particles into space, which can affect Earth's magnetosphere and technological systems.

• Satellite signal disruption: High-energy particles from solar flares and CMEs can interfere with satellite systems, affecting navigation, communication, and even satellite integrity. Hence, this is an impact of space weather phenomena.

• Radio communication blackouts: Solar flares emit X-rays and ultraviolet radiation that can ionize Earth's upper atmosphere, disrupting high-frequency (HF) radio communications, particularly during the day. Hence, this is a correct impact.

• Damage to electrical grids: CMEs can induce geomagnetic storms, generating electric currents in power lines and transformers, which can lead to large-scale power outages. For instance, the 1989 geomagnetic storm caused a blackout in Quebec, Canada. This is another valid impact of space weather.

• Changes in ocean circulation: Space weather phenomena, such as solar flares and CMEs, primarily interact with Earth's magnetosphere and atmosphere. They do not directly influence deep ocean currents or global ocean circulation patterns, which are driven by factors such as wind, salinity, and temperature differences. Hence, this is NOT an impact of space weather phenomena.

• Solar Flares: Solar flares are sudden bursts of energy resulting from the release of magnetic energy stored in the Sun's atmosphere.

• They emit electromagnetic radiation across the spectrum, including X-rays and ultraviolet rays, which can disrupt Earth's ionosphere.

• Coronal Mass Ejections (CMEs): CMEs are massive bursts of solar wind and magnetic fields rising above the solar corona and being released into space.

• When directed towards Earth, CMEs can cause geomagnetic storms, auroras, and disruptions in satellite operations.

• Geomagnetic Storms: These occur when solar winds interact with Earth's magnetic field, causing disturbances in the magnetosphere.

• Impacts include power grid failures, satellite communication disruptions, and increased radiation exposure for astronauts and high-altitude flights.

• Ocean Circulation: Ocean circulation is influenced by factors like wind patterns, Coriolis force, temperature gradients, and salinity differences.

• Space weather phenomena do not have a direct role in altering these natural oceanic processes.

• Auroras: Space weather phenomena like solar flares and CMEs create stunning auroras (Northern and Southern lights) by interacting with Earth's magnetosphere and ionizing atmospheric particles.

• Satellites and Spacecraft: High-energy particles from space weather can cause damage to electronic circuits in satellites, leading to malfunctions or loss of functionality.

• Human Health Risks: Astronauts and high-altitude flights are exposed to increased radiation levels during solar storms, posing health risks.

• Technological Vulnerability: As society becomes increasingly reliant on technology, understanding and mitigating the impacts of space weather is crucial for infrastructure resilience.
