1. Which Indian satellite supports weather forecasting?

A. Aryabhata

B. INSAT-3D

C. Cartosat

D. Mangalyaan

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is INSAT-3D. Key Points Additional Information

• INSAT-3D is an advanced weather satellite launched by India on 26 July 2013 onboard Ariane 5 rocket from French Guiana.

• It is equipped with advanced meteorological payloads such as a 19-channel sounder and a 6-channel imager for precise weather forecasting.

• The satellite helps in monitoring cloud motion, fog detection, cyclone prediction, and rainfall estimation.

• INSAT-3D significantly contributes to disaster warning systems, especially for cyclones, floods, and tsunamis, ensuring early preparedness.

• It also supports search and rescue operations by relaying distress signals to ground stations.

• Aryabhata Aryabhata was India's first satellite launched on 19 April 1975 by a Soviet rocket.

• It was primarily designed for conducting experiments in space science and upper atmospheric studies.

• The satellite helped in gathering valuable data related to cosmic rays, solar neutrons, and X-rays.

• However, it was not equipped for weather monitoring or forecasting.

• Cartosat Cartosat satellites are a series of Earth observation satellites designed for remote sensing and mapping purposes.

• They are equipped with high-resolution cameras for applications such as urban planning, land use mapping, and infrastructure development.

• Cartosat satellites are not specifically intended for weather forecasting.

• Mangalyaan (Mars Orbiter Mission) Mangalyaan, launched by ISRO on 5 November 2013, is India's first interplanetary mission to Mars.

• Its objective was to study the Martian surface, atmosphere, and mineral composition.

• While it was a significant achievement in space exploration, it does not support weather forecasting for Earth.

2. Which trade route passes through the Indian Ocean?

A. Panama Canal

B. Suez Canal

C. Bering Strait

D. Drake Passage

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Suez Canal. Key Points Additional Information

• The Suez Canal is a crucial man-made waterway that connects the Mediterranean Sea to the Red Sea, providing the shortest maritime route between Europe and Asia.

• It significantly reduces the journey distance for ships traveling between Europe and the Indian Ocean by avoiding the longer route around the southern tip of Africa (Cape of Good Hope).

• Approximately 12% of the global trade passes through the Suez Canal, making it one of the most important trade routes in the world.

• The canal runs through Egypt and plays a strategic role in connecting the Indian Ocean to European and North American markets.

• It was officially opened in 1869 and has undergone expansions to accommodate larger vessels, including the recent expansion in 2015.

• Panama Canal The Panama Canal is a man-made waterway in Central America connecting the Atlantic Ocean (via the Caribbean Sea) to the Pacific Ocean.

• It is critical for shipping between North and South America and facilitates trade between the Atlantic and Pacific regions.

• Completed in 1914, it allows ships to avoid the lengthy and hazardous Cape Horn route at the southern tip of South America.

• Bering Strait The Bering Strait is a narrow passage of water that separates Russia and Alaska, connecting the Arctic Ocean to the Bering Sea in the Pacific Ocean.

• It is not a significant commercial trade route due to its harsh climate and ice-covered waters for most of the year.

• However, it holds strategic importance for Arctic navigation, particularly as melting ice opens new shipping lanes in the Arctic region.

• Drake Passage The Drake Passage is a body of water between the southern tip of South America (Cape Horn) and Antarctica.

• It connects the Atlantic Ocean with the Pacific Ocean but is known for its rough seas and extreme weather conditions.

• It is not used as a major trade route due to the hazardous conditions and the availability of more efficient alternatives like the Panama Canal.

• Indian Ocean Trade Routes The Indian Ocean is a central part of global trade, linking Asia, Africa, and Europe through key waterways like the Suez Canal, the Strait of Malacca, and the Strait of Hormuz.

• Major goods transported through the Indian Ocean include oil, natural gas, textiles, spices, and manufactured goods.

• India’s strategic location at the heart of the Indian Ocean makes it a vital player in international trade and geopolitics.

3. Which law replaced the IPC, 1860?

A. Uniform Civil Code

B. Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam

C. Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita

D. Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita. Key Points Additional Information

• The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita is the new legislation proposed to replace the Indian Penal Code (IPC), 1860, which has been in place since the colonial era.

• It is part of a set of reforms aimed at modernizing India's criminal laws, including the replacement of IPC, the Indian Evidence Act, and the Code of Criminal Procedure.

• The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita aims to simplify, codify, and modernize the criminal laws to better align them with the values and aspirations of contemporary India.

• It seeks to provide a victim-centric approach to justice, ensuring a faster and more transparent legal process while maintaining the rule of law.

• The draft bill was introduced in the Indian Parliament in August 2023 by the Union Home Minister.

• Uniform Civil Code (UCC) UCC refers to a proposal to replace personal laws based on the scriptures and customs of each major religious community in India with a common set of laws governing every citizen.

• It is enshrined in Article 44 of the Indian Constitution as a Directive Principle of State Policy.

• The aim of UCC is to ensure equality and uniformity in civil laws such as marriage, divorce, adoption, and inheritance for all citizens, irrespective of religion.

• However, UCC has not yet been implemented in India due to its sensitive nature and the diverse cultural and religious landscape of the country.

• Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam This is the proposed replacement for the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, as part of the criminal law reforms introduced in 2023.

• The Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam aims to modernize the rules of evidence, making them more relevant to present-day technological and legal advancements.

• The new law intends to simplify the admissibility of evidence, including digital evidence, and ensure a fair and efficient trial process.

• Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita This is the proposed law to replace the Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC), 1973.

• The Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita aims to streamline the criminal procedure to reduce delays and ensure the speedy delivery of justice.

• It emphasizes citizen-centric reforms to ensure protection of rights while enhancing transparency and accountability in criminal investigations and trials.

13. Which statement(s) on desert erosional landforms is/are correct? 1. Mushroom rocks are water-formed. 2. Table rocks resemble flat-topped plateaus. 3. Pedestal rocks are shaped by wind.

A. Only 1

B. Only 2

C. Only 3

D. 1 and 3

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Only 3. Key Points Additional Information

• Pedestal rocks, also known as mushroom rocks, are formed due to the erosive action of wind, which carries sand particles that erode the base of the rock more than the top, giving it a pedestal-like shape.

• The process of wind erosion, including abrasion and deflation, is responsible for shaping such landforms in arid and semi-arid regions.

• These features are commonly observed in deserts where strong winds dominate and sand particles act as natural tools for erosion.

• Pedestal rocks demonstrate the significant role of wind as a geomorphic agent in arid landscapes.

• Their unique appearance is a result of differential erosion, where the harder rock resists erosion, while the softer rock is eroded faster.

• Option 1: Mushroom rocks are water-formed This statement is incorrect. Mushroom rocks are not water-formed but rather shaped by wind erosion in deserts.

• The erosive action of wind carrying sand particles leads to the distinctive mushroom shape, with a narrower base and wider top.

• Option 2: Table rocks resemble flat-topped plateaus This statement is partially correct but does not relate specifically to desert erosional landforms.

• Table rocks, also known as mesas, are large, flat-topped plateaus formed due to erosion, primarily by water in semi-arid regions, rather than wind.

• Key Concept: Desert Erosional Landforms Deserts are characterized by unique erosional and depositional landforms shaped by wind, a process known as aeolian erosion.

• Some common desert erosional landforms include pedestal rocks, yardangs (streamlined ridges), and deflation hollows (shallow depressions).

• Wind erosion is prominent in deserts because of the lack of vegetation and loose, dry soil.

16. What happens if a satellite has a 24-hour orbital period?

Solution

19. Revenue deficit equals:

A. Revenue expenditure – revenue receipts

B. Revenue receipts – capital receipts

C. Total expenditure – receipts

D. Capital expenditure – revenue expenditure

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Revenue expenditure – revenue receipts. Key Points Additional Information

• The revenue deficit is a measure of the shortfall in government income compared to its revenue expenditure.

• It is calculated using the formula: Revenue deficit = Revenue expenditure – Revenue receipts.

• Revenue expenditure refers to expenses incurred for day-to-day government activities, such as salaries, subsidies, and interest payments.

• Revenue receipts include all income received by the government through taxes and non-tax revenues, such as interest and dividends.

• A revenue deficit indicates that the government’s current income is insufficient to cover its operational costs, highlighting fiscal stress.

• It is an important parameter to assess the government’s financial management and sustainability.

• Option 2: Revenue receipts – capital receipts This formula is incorrect as revenue receipts and capital receipts are two distinct types of government income.

• Revenue receipts include regular income from taxes, duties, and non-tax revenue, whereas capital receipts include loans, disinvestment proceeds, and recoveries of loans.

• The subtraction of revenue receipts from capital receipts does not measure any deficit or surplus.

• Option 3: Total expenditure – receipts This formula does not define revenue deficit but refers to the fiscal deficit.

• Fiscal deficit is calculated as the total expenditure (revenue + capital expenditure) minus the sum of revenue receipts and capital receipts (excluding borrowings).

• Fiscal deficit indicates the total borrowing requirement of the government.

• Option 4: Capital expenditure – revenue expenditure This formula is unrelated to the concept of revenue deficit.

• Capital expenditure refers to spending on assets like infrastructure, machinery, and equipment, while revenue expenditure pertains to daily operational costs.

• The comparison between capital and revenue expenditure is used for budgeting analysis rather than calculating deficits.

• Key Concept: Revenue Deficit Revenue deficit reflects the imbalance between the government’s operational income and expenses.

• It is a critical indicator used to understand whether the government is relying on borrowings or capital receipts to fund operational expenses.

• High revenue deficits can lead to increased borrowing and impact the country’s economic stability.
