1. What role does the Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC) play in India’s space missions?

A. Satellite tracking operations

B. Development of launch vehicles

C. Training of space scientists

D. Mission control management

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Development of launch vehicles. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• The Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC), headquartered in Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala, is the main center of the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) focused on the development of launch vehicles.

• VSSC is responsible for designing and developing various types of rockets and launch systems that are crucial for India's space programs, including PSLV (Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle), GSLV (Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle), and GSLV Mk-III.

• VSSC contributes to the development of propulsion systems, aerodynamics, flight dynamics, avionics, and vehicle integration, which are essential for the success of launch missions.

• The center has played a pivotal role in India's achievements in space exploration, such as the Chandrayaan missions and Mangalyaan (Mars Orbiter Mission), by providing reliable launch vehicles.

• It operates under the Department of Space, Government of India, and is named after Dr. Vikram Sarabhai, regarded as the father of the Indian space program.

• Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC): Located in Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala, VSSC is one of the largest ISRO centers specializing in rocket and launch vehicle development.

• It is involved in the creation of advanced technologies such as cryogenic engines, reusable launch vehicles, and space transportation systems.

• The center has collaborated with other ISRO centers like the Liquid Propulsion Systems Centre (LPSC) and the SATISH Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC) for successful launch operations.

• VSSC played a major role in the Chandrayaan-3 mission, ensuring the successful propulsion of the lunar lander.

• Other ISRO Centers: SATISH Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC): Located in Sriharikota, Andhra Pradesh, this center is responsible for launch vehicle integration and launch operations.

• U R Rao Satellite Centre (URSC): Located in Bengaluru, Karnataka, it focuses on the development of satellites for communication, remote sensing, and navigation.

• Liquid Propulsion Systems Centre (LPSC): Handles the development of liquid propulsion systems used in launch vehicles.

• Indian Space Missions: PSLV: Known as the workhorse of ISRO, PSLV is used for launching satellites into polar orbits.

• GSLV: Used for launching heavier satellites into geosynchronous orbits, powered by cryogenic engines.

• GSLV Mk-III: The most powerful launch vehicle developed by ISRO, capable of carrying heavy payloads, including the Chandrayaan-2 mission.

• Important Contributions: Development of Reusable Launch Vehicles (RLV) and Small Satellite Launch Vehicles (SSLV) for cost-effective space exploration.

• Support in international collaborations such as Artemis missions with NASA.

• India's entry into the commercial satellite launch market through the Antrix Corporation, ISRO's commercial wing.

• Satellite Tracking Operations: Satellite tracking is primarily handled by centers like the Indian Deep Space Network (IDSN) in Bengaluru.

• It ensures communication with satellites and interplanetary missions.

• Training of Space Scientists: Space scientist training is carried out at ISRO's Space Applications Centre (SAC) and Institute of Space Science and Technology (IIST).

• Mission Control Management: Mission control is conducted at ISRO's Mission Operations Complex (MOX), Bengaluru, which handles spacecraft operations and telemetry.

3. The merger of princely states with India after 1947 was influenced by multiple forces. Which factor most weakened the rulers’ desire to remain independent?

A. Strong military pressure from neighbouring countries

B. Continued backing by British authorities

C. Public movements supporting integration with India

D. Economic self-sufficiency of princely states

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Public movements supporting integration with India. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• After India’s independence in 1947, the integration of princely states into India became a crucial task. Public movements strongly influenced this process by advocating for national unity and integration.

• Several princely states witnessed mass movements where citizens demanded merger with India, rejecting the idea of remaining independent or joining Pakistan. This created significant pressure on rulers to accede to India.

• Leaders like Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel and V.P. Menon utilized public sentiment to negotiate with rulers effectively, showcasing the people's desire for integration.

• The role of the Indian National Congress and other political organizations in organizing campaigns and fostering nationalistic fervor further weakened the rulers' resolve to remain independent.

• In many princely states, the public showed overwhelming support for merging with India due to shared cultural, linguistic, and historical ties, which undermined the rulers' efforts to maintain autonomy.

• Integration of Princely States: At the time of independence, there were 565 princely states in India, and they were given the choice to join India, Pakistan, or remain independent.

• Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, India’s first Home Minister, played a pivotal role in persuading the rulers to merge with India.

• Techniques such as negotiation, diplomacy, and military intervention were used to achieve integration.

• Important examples include the accession of Hyderabad (via Operation Polo), Kashmir (via Instrument of Accession), and Junagadh (via plebiscite).

• Role of Public Movements: In states like Junagadh, Kashmir, and Hyderabad, public movements were instrumental in influencing political decisions.

• The Indian National Congress and other organizations mobilized citizens to demand integration, emphasizing the importance of a unified India.

• Mass protests, rallies, and petitions showcased the overwhelming public sentiment against independence or joining Pakistan.

• Other Factors Influencing Integration: Military Pressure: In cases like Hyderabad, military action (Operation Polo) was taken to integrate the state when negotiations failed.

• Economic Constraints: Many princely states lacked economic self-sufficiency and recognized the need to merge with India for stability and development.

• Lack of British Support: With the end of British rule, princely states lost their patronage, making independence unsustainable.

• Key Examples: Junagadh: Public protests led to a plebiscite, where the majority voted for accession to India.

• Kashmir: The decision to accede was influenced by public sentiment and military intervention during the tribal invasion.

• Hyderabad: The integration was achieved through military action following public unrest and political instability.

• Instrument of Accession: This legal document allowed princely states to join India or Pakistan. It outlined the terms of accession and guaranteed certain rights to rulers.

• Most states signed the Instrument of Accession, agreeing to transfer control of defense, foreign affairs, and communications to India.

• Operation Polo: This military operation was conducted in September 1948 to integrate Hyderabad into India following the refusal of its ruler to accede.

• It resulted in the annexation of Hyderabad and demonstrated India’s resolve to maintain unity.

4. The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) was introduced primarily as part of:

A. Reforming family laws

B. Overhauling colonial-era criminal laws

C. Implementing tax reforms

D. Expanding international trade laws

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Overhauling colonial-era criminal laws. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) aims to replace colonial-era criminal laws, specifically the Indian Penal Code (IPC), Criminal Procedure Code (CrPC), and the Indian Evidence Act, 1872.

• Introduced in 2023, the BNS seeks to modernize and adapt laws to India's contemporary socio-political and cultural realities.

• The colonial-era IPC, drafted in 1860, has been criticized for being outdated and unreflective of modern principles of justice and fairness.

• The overhaul under BNS aims to simplify legal procedures, ensure faster delivery of justice, and decriminalize minor offenses to reduce the burden on courts.

• The BNS emphasizes a victim-centric approach, enhancing provisions for victim compensation and rights within the legal framework.

• Colonial-era criminal laws: The Indian Penal Code (IPC), enacted in 1860, served as the backbone of criminal law in India, but its provisions reflect colonial priorities rather than modern Indian realities.

• The Criminal Procedure Code (CrPC), implemented in 1973, governs procedural aspects of criminal law but requires modernization to adapt to modern advancements.

• The Indian Evidence Act, passed in 1872, outlines rules regarding evidence in trials, but some provisions are considered restrictive and outdated.

• Key Features of Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita: Modernization: The BNS seeks to align criminal laws with the Constitution of India and contemporary legal practices.

• Victim-Centric Approach: It emphasizes victim compensation and ensures their rights are adequately protected during legal proceedings.

• Decriminalization: Minor offenses are being decriminalized to reduce the burden on the judicial system.

• Transparency: The laws promote transparent legal processes to ensure fairness and justice.

• Comparison with Other Options: Reforming Family Laws: This relates to personal laws such as marriage, divorce, inheritance, and adoption, but is unrelated to the BNS.

• Implementing Tax Reforms: Tax reforms focus on financial policies like GST, corporate tax, etc., and are not connected to criminal law.

• Expanding International Trade Laws: This pertains to global commerce and trade agreements, which are entirely distinct from the scope of the BNS.

• The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita was introduced to replace outdated colonial laws that no longer align with the modern principles of justice.

• It aims to ensure speedy justice by simplifying legal procedures and focusing on victim rights.

• The BNS aligns criminal laws with the Indian Constitution and modern values, ensuring that they reflect India's current socio-political realities.

6. In Indian classical music, the term “svaravalis” refers to:

A. A fixed rhythmic pattern

B. A melodic exercise using scale notes

C. Decorative musical embellishments

D. A specific raga structure

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is A melodic exercise using scale notes. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Svaravalis in Indian classical music refer to melodic exercises involving the practice of ascending and descending the scale notes (swaras).

• It helps in improving a musician's understanding of pitch, rhythm, and raga structure.

• The svaravali exercises are foundational for students learning Indian classical music, as they develop a solid understanding of basic swaras (Sa, Re, Ga, Ma, Pa, Dha, Ni).

• These exercises are part of the early stages of training in both Hindustani and Carnatic music.

• Svaravalis are instrumental in building a musician’s ability to navigate through the notes with precision and fluency, making them a critical component of voice training and mastery of musical scales.

• Indian classical music: Indian classical music is broadly divided into two traditions: Hindustani music (North India) and Carnatic music (South India).

• Both traditions emphasize the importance of raga (melodic framework) and tala (rhythmic cycles).

• It is a form of improvisational music based on specific rules and structures of melody and rhythm.

• Components of Indian classical music: Raga: A melodic framework for improvisation, consisting of a specific scale and set of rules for note progression.

• Tala: A rhythmic cycle in which beats are organized in repetitive patterns.

• Swaras: The seven musical notes (Sa, Re, Ga, Ma, Pa, Dha, Ni) that form the foundation of melodies.

• Other important terms in Indian classical music: Aalaap: A slow, improvisational introduction to a raga, showcasing its characteristics.

• Gamaka: Ornamentation or embellishment of notes, unique to Indian music.

• Tanpura: A string instrument providing the continuous drone that accompanies the singer or instrumentalist.

• Explanation of other options: Option 1: "A fixed rhythmic pattern" refers to the concept of tala, not svaravalis. Hence, this is incorrect.

• Option 3: "Decorative musical embellishments" describe gamakas, not svaravalis. Hence, this is incorrect.

• Option 4: "A specific raga structure" refers to the framework of a raga, not svaravalis. Hence, this is incorrect.

• Svaravalis are particularly crucial in Carnatic music training, where they form the foundation for learning basic ragas and improvisation.

• Practicing svaravalis helps students achieve precision in pitch and rhythm, which is essential for mastering advanced compositions.

• They are typically practiced in different speeds (vilambit, madhya, and drut laya) to enhance a musician's control and proficiency.

7. Consider the following landforms: 1. Tors 2. Spurs 3. Block Mountains Which of these are found in the Peninsular Plateau of India? Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 and 3 only

B. 3 only

C. 2 and 3 only

D. 1, 2 and 3

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is '1, 2 and 3' Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Tors: Tors are isolated rock outcrops formed due to weathering and erosion, commonly seen in granitic regions.

• They are a characteristic feature of the Peninsular Plateau, especially in areas with ancient crystalline rocks.

• Example: Hampi (Karnataka) is famous for its granite tors and boulder landscapes.

• Spurs: Spurs are projecting ridges that extend from hills or mountains, formed due to river erosion.

• They are present in the dissected plateau regions where rivers have carved valleys.

• Example: The Western Ghats and Eastern Ghats show several spurs formed by river systems.

• Block Mountains: Block mountains are formed due to faulting, where large crustal blocks are uplifted or downthrown.

• The Peninsular Plateau has several such features due to its tectonic stability and ancient faults.

• Example: The Vindhya and Satpura ranges are classic examples of block mountains in India.

• The Peninsular Plateau is composed of ancient igneous and metamorphic rocks, making it geologically stable.

• It has undergone prolonged denudation, leading to the formation of diverse landforms like tors and residual hills.

• Faulting and tectonic movements in the past have contributed to the formation of block mountains and rift valleys such as the Narmada valley.

• The Peninsular Plateau is one of the oldest landmasses of the Earth.

• It exhibits a variety of erosional and structural landforms.

• Hence, all the given landforms—tors, spurs, and block mountains—are found in this region.

• Purana Qila, also known as the Old Fort, is one of the oldest forts in Delhi, India, and is a significant example of Mughal architecture.

• The primary materials used in the construction of the fort were sandstone and marble, which were commonly utilized during the Mughal era for their durability and aesthetic appeal.

• Sandstone, particularly red and buff sandstone, was extensively used for the walls, gateways, and other structural components of Purana Qila, providing the fort with its iconic and majestic look.

• Marble was employed for decorative elements and intricate inlays, adding a touch of elegance and sophistication to the fort's architecture.

• The fort was constructed during the reigns of Sher Shah Suri (1540-1545) and later completed by Humayun, reflecting a blend of Islamic and Afghan architectural styles.

• About Sandstone: Sandstone is a sedimentary rock composed mainly of sand-sized mineral particles or rock fragments. It is highly durable and easy to work with, making it a preferred choice for construction during ancient times.

• In Indian architecture, red sandstone was frequently used, especially during the Mughal period, due to its strength, accessibility, and aesthetic appeal.

• Other prominent Mughal structures, such as the Red Fort and Fatehpur Sikri, also extensively used sandstone.

• About Marble: Marble is a metamorphic rock known for its smooth texture and ability to be polished to a high sheen, making it ideal for decorative purposes and inlays.

• Famous Mughal structures like the Taj Mahal utilized white marble extensively, showcasing its importance in Mughal architecture.

• The use of marble in Purana Qila was primarily for ornamental elements and to add a luxurious touch to the fort.

• Mughal Architecture: Mughal architecture is characterized by the extensive use of red sandstone and marble, intricate carvings, domes, and expansive courtyards.

• The style represents a fusion of Islamic, Persian, and Indian architectural elements, making it distinct and globally renowned.

• Historical Context of Purana Qila: Purana Qila is believed to be located on the site of ancient Indraprastha, a city mentioned in the Mahabharata.

• The fort was initially constructed by Sher Shah Suri and later completed by Humayun, reflecting the rivalry between these two rulers.

• It served as a defensive structure and a symbol of authority, strategically located near the banks of the Yamuna River.

• Other Construction Materials: While sandstone and marble were the primary materials, other materials like lime mortar were also used for bonding and plastering.

• The intricate carvings and decorations on the fort gates and walls highlight the craftsmanship of the era.

• Architectural Features: Purana Qila features three main gateways, each with intricate carvings and ornamental designs.

• The fort also houses a stepwell (Baoli) and a mosque, showcasing the diverse architectural elements within its premises.

10. The drainage system of Mohenjodaro was remarkable because houses were linked to:

A. Underground reservoirs

B. Structural support columns

C. Covered street drains

D. Rooftop water tanks

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Covered street drains. Key Points Additional Information

• The drainage system of Mohenjodaro, part of the Indus Valley Civilization, was highly advanced and considered remarkable for its time.

• Each house in Mohenjodaro was connected to a network of covered street drains, which ensured proper disposal of waste water and maintained hygiene in urban spaces.

• The street drains were constructed using bricks and were covered with stone slabs, which could be removed for cleaning—a feature showing advanced urban planning and sanitation techniques.

• The drains were laid out systematically along the streets and were designed to carry sewage and rainwater away from residential and public areas.

• This innovative drainage system demonstrates the emphasis placed by the Indus Valley Civilization on cleanliness, urban planning, and public health.

• Indus Valley Civilization: The Indus Valley Civilization, also known as the Harappan Civilization, flourished around 2600–1900 BCE in the northwestern regions of South Asia.

• It is renowned for its advanced urban planning, including features like grid-patterned streets, sewage systems, and standardized brick sizes.

• Prominent sites of the civilization include Mohenjodaro, Harappa, Dholavira, and Lothal.

• Features of Mohenjodaro’s Drainage System: Drains were lined with bricks and covered with slabs, ensuring durability and ease of maintenance.

• Manholes were provided at regular intervals to facilitate cleaning, showing the meticulous planning of the system.

• The drainage system was connected to soak pits and larger drains, ensuring efficient waste disposal.

• Comparison with Other Ancient Civilizations: The drainage system of the Indus Valley Civilization was far more advanced compared to contemporary civilizations like Mesopotamia and Egypt.

• While Mesopotamia had open drains, the covered and interconnected system in Mohenjodaro shows greater sophistication.

• Significance of Urban Planning: The planning reflects the civic management and the high priority given to sanitation by the people of the Indus Valley Civilization.

• It demonstrates the use of technology and systematic thinking to enhance public health and urban living standards.

Solution

The correct answer is Green Card causes temporary suspension. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Green Card in Kabaddi is issued as a warning to a player for committing minor violations, and it results in a temporary suspension of the player for a set duration. This statement is correct.

• Golden Raid: It is a tiebreaker situation in Kabaddi, where the raider has a limited time to score points. However, the claim that teams are allowed two Golden Raids is incorrect; only one Golden Raid is permitted. Hence, this statement is incorrect.

• In Kabaddi, the match is not replayed after a fixed number of raids. This statement is entirely incorrect. Matches proceed based on the standard rules and outcomes. Hence, this statement is incorrect.

• Red Card: A red card is issued for major violations, and it results in a player being expelled from the match. The claim that it only gives a warning is incorrect. Hence, this statement is incorrect.

• The Green Card is specifically linked to temporary suspension as a penalty for minor offenses, making it the only correct statement in the provided options.

• Golden Raid: A Golden Raid is used during tiebreakers in Kabaddi when the scores are tied at the end of regular time.

• Each team gets one chance, and the raider must score within 30 seconds.

• If no points are scored during the Golden Raid, the team with the least amount of fouls or a toss may decide the winner.

• Card System in Kabaddi: There are three types of cards used in Kabaddi: Green, Yellow, and Red.

• Green Card: Issued as a warning for minor violations, leading to temporary suspension.

• Yellow Card: Issued for repeated fouls or unsportsmanlike behavior, leading to a longer suspension.

• Red Card: Issued for severe misconduct, resulting in the player being expelled from the match.

• Rules of Kabaddi: The game is played between two teams, with seven players on each side.

• The objective is to score points by raiding the opponent's court and tagging as many defenders as possible before returning to your half.

• Matches are divided into two halves, each lasting 20 minutes, with a 5-minute break in between.

• It is now a recognized international sport, governed by organizations like the International Kabaddi Federation (IKF).

• The game has gained global popularity, with tournaments like the Pro Kabaddi League and the Kabaddi World Cup.

• Temporary Suspension: When a player receives a Green Card, they are suspended for a specific duration, during which their team plays with one less player.

• Pro Kabaddi League: The league has popularized the sport globally and introduced new rules and innovations, making the game more viewer-friendly.

• Role of Referees: Referees are responsible for enforcing rules, issuing cards, and ensuring fair play during the match.

• Scoring in Kabaddi: Points are scored by raiders for tagging defenders and returning safely, while defenders score by stopping the raider.

12. Apart from rocky terrain, what was a major agricultural challenge in the Chola period?

A. Scarcity of seeds

B. Shortage of workers

C. Limited irrigation facilities

D. Frequent pest attacks

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Limited irrigation facilities. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• During the Chola period, agriculture faced significant challenges due to limited irrigation facilities.

• The Chola rulers made considerable efforts to improve irrigation systems, including the construction of tanks, canals, and water reservoirs to support agriculture.

• The rocky terrain in many parts of the Chola empire made it difficult to expand irrigation systems, increasing dependence on monsoon rains for cultivation.

• Agriculture during this period primarily depended on the availability of water from rivers like the Kaveri, and the establishment of irrigation infrastructure was critical for ensuring productivity.

• Despite the challenges, the Cholas were known for their administrative efficiency in maintaining and developing water management systems to overcome these limitations.

• Rocky Terrain: The Chola empire encompassed regions with rocky and uneven terrain, which posed difficulties in agriculture and irrigation development.

• The rocky soil was less fertile compared to alluvial plains, and it required substantial effort to make it suitable for cultivation.

• Irrigation Systems under the Cholas: The Chola rulers were pioneers in developing extensive irrigation infrastructure, including tanks, reservoirs, and canals.

• One of the most famous examples is the Grand Anicut (Kallanai), a stone dam built across the Kaveri River to regulate water flow and aid irrigation.

• The Chola administration ensured regular maintenance of irrigation systems, which contributed to agricultural prosperity despite natural challenges.

• Dependency on Monsoons: Due to the limitations in irrigation facilities, agriculture was heavily reliant on monsoon rains.

• Unpredictable rainfall often led to droughts or floods, impacting the yield of crops such as rice, which was a staple in the region.

• Other Agricultural Challenges: The scarcity of advanced tools and techniques made farming labor-intensive during the Chola period.

• Frequent pest attacks were another challenge, but they were not as significant as issues related to irrigation.

• Grand Anicut (Kallanai): Constructed during the reign of King Karikala Chola, this dam is one of the oldest water management systems in India.

• It is located in Tamil Nadu and is still functional, highlighting the engineering expertise of the Chola dynasty.

• Chola Administration: The Chola rulers were known for their efficient administration, particularly in managing resources for agriculture.

• Their governance included land revenue collection and the maintenance of irrigation systems to boost agricultural output.

In the photoelectric effect, the kinetic energy of emitted electrons depends on the frequency of the incident light, not its intensity. Light intensity affects the number of electrons emitted, but not their individual energies.

• Statement 2: "It supports the particle nature of light" is correct. The photoelectric effect demonstrates the particle nature of light as photons interact with electrons. Each photon transfers its energy to an electron, confirming that light behaves as discrete packets of energy (particles).

• Statement 3: "Below a threshold frequency, emission does not occur" is correct. The photoelectric effect requires that the frequency of incident light exceed a certain threshold frequency, specific to the material. • The energy of a photon is given by the equation E = hf, where h is Planck's constant and f is the frequency of the light.

• If the photon energy exceeds the work function of the material, electrons are emitted. The excess energy becomes the kinetic energy of the emitted electrons.

• It depends on the work function of the metal, which is the minimum energy needed to free an electron.

B. Only statement 2

C. Both statements

D. Neither statement

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Option 3. Key Points Additional Information

• Statement 1: Article 300 of the Indian Constitution permits legal action against both the Union and State Governments of India in the same manner as against any individual. This provision ensures that the government can be held accountable in courts for its actions or omissions. Hence, statement 1 is correct.

• Statement 2: Article 300 also recognizes the "Government of India" and "State Governments" as distinct legal entities. This legal recognition allows them to sue or be sued in their respective capacities, ensuring a clear framework for legal proceedings involving the government. Hence, statement 2 is correct.

• Purpose of Article 300: It provides a legal mechanism for citizens to seek redressal in cases of legal disputes involving the government.

• Legal Framework: The term "legal entity" in this context means that the government has the capacity to enter into contracts, own property, and be subject to legal obligations similar to private individuals or corporations.

• Both statements 1 and 2 are correct, as they together explain the scope and purpose of Article 300 in the Indian Constitution.

• Article 300 of the Indian Constitution: It allows legal proceedings to be instituted by or against the Government of India or the State Governments in the name of the "Union of India" or the "State" respectively.

• The provision is rooted in the Government of India Act, 1935, which allowed legal proceedings against the Crown in British India.

• It ensures that both the Union and State Governments can be held accountable for their actions through judicial processes.

• Legal Entity Status: The recognition of the Government as a "legal entity" implies that it has the capacity to: Enter into contracts.

• Own or manage property.

• Be sued or sue in courts of law.

• This legal recognition is critical for the smooth functioning of a democratic government, ensuring that it operates within the bounds of law.

• Judicial Precedents: The Supreme Court of India has clarified the scope of Article 300 in several landmark judgments, emphasizing the accountability of the government under the Constitution.

• For instance, in cases of breach of contract or negligence, the government can be held liable as a legal entity.

• Exceptions and Immunity: While Article 300 ensures accountability, certain actions of the government may be protected under the doctrine of sovereign immunity, especially when they involve essential state functions like national security or defense.

• The scope of such immunity is determined on a case-to-case basis by the judiciary.
