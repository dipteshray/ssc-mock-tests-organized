Solution

The correct answer is 101st Constitutional Amendment Act. Key Points Additional Information

• The 101st Constitutional Amendment Act was passed by the Indian Parliament in August 2016.

• This amendment led to the introduction of the Goods and Services Tax (GST) in India, which came into effect on 1st July 2017.

• The GST Council was formed as per Article 279A of the Indian Constitution, inserted by the 101st Amendment.

• The GST Council is responsible for making recommendations on the rates of taxes, goods and services that may be subjected or exempted from GST, and other related matters.

• 102nd Constitutional Amendment Act This amendment came into effect in 2018.

• It granted constitutional status to the National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC).

• Article 338B was inserted to provide for the NCBC.

• 100th Constitutional Amendment Act This amendment was passed in 2015.

• It dealt with the exchange of certain enclaves between India and Bangladesh and the demarcation of the boundary between the two countries.

• It facilitated the implementation of the Land Boundary Agreement (LBA) between India and Bangladesh.

• 103rd Constitutional Amendment Act This amendment came into effect in 2019.

• It introduced a 10% reservation for economically weaker sections (EWS) in government jobs and educational institutions.

• Articles 15(6) and 16(6) were added to the Constitution to enable this reservation.

• Goods and Services Tax (GST) GST is a comprehensive, multi-stage, destination-based tax that is levied on every value addition.

• It replaced multiple indirect taxes previously levied by the central and state governments.

• GST is categorized into Central GST (CGST), State GST (SGST), Integrated GST (IGST), and Union Territory GST (UTGST).

• The GST Council, chaired by the Union Finance Minister, is a federal body where both the center and the states participate in the decision-making process.

9. Which coal is low-grade, brown and soft with high moisture content?

A. Metallurgical

B. Lignite

C. Bituminous

D. Anthracite

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Lignite. Key Points Additional Information

• Lignite is a type of coal that is considered to be low-grade due to its relatively low energy content.

• It is brown in color and has a soft texture, making it easier to break apart compared to harder forms of coal.

• Lignite has a high moisture content, sometimes as much as 45-66%, which reduces its efficiency as a fuel source.

• Despite its lower quality, lignite is often used in electricity generation, particularly in power plants located near lignite mines.

• Metallurgical Coal Also known as coking coal, it is primarily used in steel production.

• It has a high carbon content and low impurities, which are essential for producing high-quality coke.

• Metallurgical coal undergoes a coking process where it is heated in the absence of air to remove volatile substances, leaving behind a solid carbon mass called coke.

• Bituminous Coal It is a middle-rank coal between sub-bituminous and anthracite.

• Bituminous coal is used extensively for electricity generation and as a fuel in industries.

• It has a higher carbon content than lignite and sub-bituminous coal, providing more energy when burned.

• Anthracite It is the highest rank of coal due to its high carbon content and low impurities.

• Anthracite is hard, shiny, and has the highest energy content of all coal types.

• It is used for heating residential and commercial buildings and in some industrial processes.

10. Which of the following gases plays an important role in welding titanium, aluminium, stainless steel, and magnesium?

A. Fluorine

B. Neon

C. Argon

D. Chlorine

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Argon. Key Points Additional Information

• Argon is an inert gas, which means it does not react with the materials being welded, making it ideal for welding processes.

• It is used as a shielding gas to protect the weld area from atmospheric gases such as oxygen, nitrogen, and water vapor that can cause defects in the weld.

• Argon provides a stable arc and excellent control over the welding process, which is crucial for welding materials like titanium, aluminium, stainless steel, and magnesium.

• It is often used in Gas Tungsten Arc Welding (GTAW) and Gas Metal Arc Welding (GMAW) processes.

• Gas Tungsten Arc Welding (GTAW) Also known as Tungsten Inert Gas (TIG) welding, GTAW uses a non-consumable tungsten electrode to produce the weld.

• Argon is the most commonly used shielding gas in GTAW due to its inert properties and ability to create a stable arc.

• GTAW is known for producing high-quality, precision welds and is often used for welding thin materials.

• Gas Metal Arc Welding (GMAW) Also known as Metal Inert Gas (MIG) welding, GMAW uses a continuous wire feed as an electrode and an inert or semi-inert gas mixture for shielding.

• Argon is often used in GMAW, sometimes mixed with other gases like carbon dioxide or helium to optimize the welding process for different materials.

• GMAW is widely used in industrial applications due to its high welding speed and ease of automation.

• Importance of Shielding Gas Shielding gases like argon protect the weld pool from contamination by atmospheric gases, which can cause porosity and weld defects.

• They help stabilize the arc, improve weld quality, and ensure a clean and strong weld.

• The choice of shielding gas depends on the materials being welded and the specific welding process used.

Solution

• The term "Blue Line" is a significant part of the game Ice Hockey.

• It refers to the lines that divide the hockey rink into three zones: the defending zone, neutral zone, and attacking zone.

• The blue lines play a crucial role in determining offside violations in the game of ice hockey.

• They are essential in regulating the flow of the game and ensuring that players adhere to the rules related to positioning on the rink.

• Offside Rule in Ice Hockey The offside rule is one of the fundamental rules in ice hockey, aimed at preventing players from gaining an unfair advantage.

• A player is considered offside if they enter the attacking zone before the puck crosses the blue line.

• Understanding the offside rule is crucial for players and coaches as it impacts offensive strategies and game planning.

• Zones on the Rink The ice hockey rink is divided into three zones by two blue lines: the defending zone, the neutral zone, and the attacking zone.

• The defending zone is the area between the blue line closest to a team's goal and the end boards.

• The neutral zone lies between the two blue lines, where much of the game's transition play occurs.

• The attacking zone is the area between the blue line farthest from a team's goal and the end boards, where a team aims to score goals.

• Importance of the Blue Line The blue lines are critical for maintaining the structure and flow of the game, ensuring that players adhere to the rules of positioning.

• They are used to judge offside violations and play a role in determining where faceoffs occur on the rink.

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Released a special stamp, 'Wheat Revolution'. Key Points Additional Information

• The special stamp 'Wheat Revolution' was released to commemorate the success and achievements of the Green Revolution in India.

• The Green Revolution was a significant agricultural reform that began in the 1960s, aiming to increase food grain production in India.

• This period marked the introduction of high-yielding variety (HYV) seeds, chemical fertilizers, and better irrigation techniques, which led to a substantial increase in crop production, particularly wheat and rice.

• The release of the 'Wheat Revolution' stamp symbolized the country's commitment to agricultural advancement and the farmers' pivotal role in achieving food security.

• Green Revolution The Green Revolution refers to a set of research, development, and technology transfer initiatives that occurred between the 1940s and the late 1960s.

• It led to the adoption of new agricultural techniques, including the use of high-yielding variety seeds, synthetic fertilizers, pesticides, and improved irrigation methods.

• The main goal was to increase food production and prevent famine in developing countries, particularly in Asia and Latin America.

• In India, the Green Revolution began in the 1960s and resulted in the country becoming self-sufficient in food grain production within a few decades.

• High-Yielding Variety (HYV) Seeds These seeds are genetically modified to produce higher yields compared to traditional varieties.

• They are more responsive to fertilizers and irrigation, leading to increased productivity per hectare.

• HYV seeds played a crucial role in the success of the Green Revolution, especially for crops like wheat and rice.

• Impact of the Green Revolution in India The Green Revolution transformed India from a food-deficient country to one of the world's leading agricultural producers.

• It increased the production of staple crops, reduced food imports, and helped achieve food security.

• However, it also led to environmental challenges, such as soil degradation, water depletion, and increased use of chemical fertilizers and pesticides.

• Despite these challenges, the Green Revolution is considered a major milestone in India's agricultural history.

23. Which of the following is NOT correct regarding circular flow of income?

A. Savings as well as government expenditure are injections to the circular flow.

B. The income of one sector becomes the expenditure of the other.

C. In a circular flow, leakages are equal to injections.

D. The real flow and monetary flow move in a circular manner in an opposite direction.

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Savings as well as government expenditure are injections to the circular flow.. Key Points Additional Information

• Savings are considered a leakage from the circular flow of income as they represent income not spent on consumption.

• Government expenditure is an injection into the circular flow as it represents spending that adds to the overall demand in the economy.

• In a closed economy with no government intervention, savings would be equal to investment.

• The correct representation would be that investment and government spending are injections, while savings, taxes, and imports are leakages.

• Circular Flow of Income The circular flow of income is a model that depicts how money flows through the economy between households and firms.

• In this model, households provide factors of production (labor, land, capital) to firms, and in return, they receive income (wages, rent, interest, profit).

• Households then use this income to purchase goods and services from firms, completing the cycle.

• Leakages and Injections Leakages are non-consumption uses of income, including savings, taxes, and imports. They represent money that leaves the circular flow.

• Injections are additions to the income of firms, including investment, government spending, and exports. They represent money entering the circular flow.

• In a balanced circular flow, the total value of leakages is equal to the total value of injections.

• Real Flow and Monetary Flow The real flow refers to the flow of physical goods and services and the factors of production.

• The monetary flow refers to the flow of money in the form of income and expenditure.

• In the circular flow model, the real flow and monetary flow move in opposite directions to each other.

24. The type of reaction that is typically found when a material that is required for the reaction to proceed, such as a surface or a catalyst, is saturated by the reactants is called a ________ order reaction.

A. first

B. second

C. zero

D. third

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is zero order reaction. Key Points Additional Information

• In a zero-order reaction, the rate of reaction is independent of the concentration of the reactants.

• These reactions are commonly seen in catalytic processes where the catalyst surface is saturated by the reactants.

• In such reactions, the rate of reaction remains constant until the reactant concentration is significantly depleted.

• Zero-order kinetics is often observed in heterogeneous catalysis and other situations where a limiting step is saturated.

• First-order Reaction A first-order reaction is one where the rate is directly proportional to the concentration of one reactant.

• The rate law for a first-order reaction can be expressed as: Rate = k[A], where k is the rate constant and [A] is the concentration of the reactant.

• These reactions often involve unimolecular processes.

• Examples include radioactive decay and certain substitution reactions in organic chemistry.

• Second-order Reaction A second-order reaction's rate is proportional to the square of the concentration of one reactant or the product of the concentrations of two reactants.

• The rate law can be written as: Rate = k[A]^2 or Rate = k[A][B], depending on the reaction mechanism.

• These reactions often involve bimolecular processes.

• Examples include the reaction between hydrogen and iodine to form hydrogen iodide.

• Third-order Reaction A third-order reaction's rate depends on the concentration of three reactants or the concentration of one reactant raised to the third power, or other combinations summing to three.

• The rate law can be expressed as: Rate = k[A]^3 or other combinations.

• These reactions are less common compared to first and second-order reactions.

• An example is the reaction between nitric oxide and oxygen to form nitrogen dioxide.
