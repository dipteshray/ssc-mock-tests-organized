1. Convert 6.75 into a fraction in its simplest form.

Solution

2. If 20 percent of x equals 40 percent of y, find the ratio x : y.

Solution

A. 59200

B. 60000

D. 56000

Solution

8. A sum becomes 8 times in 9 years at compound interest. In how many years will it become 64 times?

A. 18 years

B. 27 years

C. 36 years

D. 45 years

Solution

8 = (1 + r)9 ⇒ (1 + r) = 21/3 64 = (1 + r)t ⇒ (21/3)t = 26 t ÷ 3 = 6 ⇒ t = 18 years

Solution

13. A sphere is inside a cylinder whose height and diameter equal the sphere’s diameter. If the sphere’s volume is 36π cm³, find the vacant space in the cylinder.

A. 24π

B. 18π

C. 36π

D. 12π

Answer B

Solution

⇒ Volume of the sphere = (4/3)πr3 ⇒ 36π = (4/3)πr3 ⇒ r3 = (36 × 3) ÷ 4 = 27 ⇒ r = 3 cm ⇒ Height of the cylinder = 2r = 2 × 3 = 6 cm ⇒ Volume of the cylinder = πr2h = π(3)2(6) = 54π cm3 ⇒ Vacant space = Volume of the cylinder − Volume of the sphere ⇒ Vacant space = 54π − 36π = 18π cm3

22. Two circles with radii 14 cm and 6 cm have centers 40 cm apart. Find length of transverse common tangent.

A. √1200

B. √1400

C. √1600

D. √1800

Answer A

Solution

Shortcut Trick The length of the transverse common tangent (TCT) is calculated using the direct formula: TCT = √[Distance2 − (Sum of Radii)2] Given: Distance (d) = 40 cm, Radii = 14 cm and 6 cm. ⇒ TCT = √[402 − (14 + 6)2] ⇒ TCT = √[1600 − 400] = √1200 cm. ∴ The correct answer is √1200 cm. Alternate Method Given: ⇒ Radius of first circle (R) = 14 cm ⇒ Radius of second circle (r) = 6 cm ⇒ Distance between centers (d) = 40 cm Formula Used: Length of Transverse Common Tangent (TCT) = √(d2 − (R + r)2) Calculations: ⇒ Sum of radii = R + r = 14 + 6 = 20 cm ⇒ TCT = √(402 − 202) ⇒ TCT = √(1600 − 400) ⇒ TCT = √1200 cm ∴ The correct answer is √1200 cm. Additional Information Direct Common Tangent (DCT) The length of the direct common tangent is given by the formula: DCT = √(d2 − (R − r)2). Touching Circles If two circles touch externally, the distance between centers d = R + r. If they touch internally, d = |R − r|. Tangent Properties The radius of a circle is always perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact (Radius ⊥ Tangent).
