10. Convectional rainfall mainly occurs due to which factor?

A. Interaction of cold fronts with warm winds

B. Air rising due to mountain barriers

C. Heated air rising, then cooling

D. Moist currents from polar regions

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Heated air rising, then cooling. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Convectional rainfall occurs mainly due to the heating of the Earth's surface, which causes air to rise vertically.

• As the sun heats the ground, the air above it becomes warm and rises. This process is known as convection.

• As the heated air rises, it expands and cools due to the decrease in pressure at higher altitudes. When the air cools to its dew point temperature, condensation occurs, leading to the formation of clouds. Hence, statement 3 is correct.

• This type of rainfall usually occurs in tropical regions where the sun's heat is intense and leads to daily cycles of heating and cooling. It often results in afternoon showers.

• Convectional rainfall is characterized by short, intense bursts of rain accompanied by thunder and lightning due to the rapid upward movement of air. Hence, statement 3 is correct.

• Convectional Rainfall Characteristics: Formation Process: It occurs when air is heated due to intense solar radiation, rises, cools, and condenses to form rain.

• Location: Commonly found in tropical regions such as the Amazon Basin, equatorial Africa, and Southeast Asia.

• Time of Occurrence: Typically happens during the afternoons when the ground has absorbed maximum heat.

• Intensity: Often associated with heavy rainfall, thunder, and lightning.

• Comparison with Other Types of Rainfall: Orographic Rainfall: Occurs when moist air is forced to rise over mountain barriers, resulting in cooling and condensation.

• Frontal Rainfall: Happens when warm air meets cold air along a front, causing the warm air to rise, cool, and condense.

• Convectional Rainfall: Driven by the heating of the Earth's surface rather than geographical barriers or fronts.

• Examples of Tropical Areas Experiencing Convectional Rainfall: Amazon Rainforest: Known for regular afternoon convectional showers.

• Equatorial Africa: Experiences high humidity and intense heat leading to convectional rainfall.

• Southeast Asia: Countries like Indonesia and Malaysia experience convectional rainfall, especially during their hot seasons.

• Key Factors Influencing Convectional Rainfall: Solar Radiation: The intensity of the sun's heat significantly affects the rate of convection.

• Humidity: High levels of moisture in the air enhance the likelihood of convectional rainfall.

• Geographical Location: Areas close to the equator are more prone to convectional rainfall due to direct solar exposure.

13. Why does the NISAR mission revisit the same point every 12 days?

A. To support lower-orbit operations

B. To minimize fuel usage on orbits

C. To store collected data before transmission

D. For improved temporal coverage and tracking land deformation

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is For improved temporal coverage and tracking land deformation. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Revisit every 12 days: The NISAR mission is designed to revisit the same point every 12 days to ensure continuous and consistent temporal coverage of Earth's surface. This frequent revisit interval is crucial for tracking changes and anomalies over time.

• Monitoring land deformation: The mission's primary objective is to detect and monitor land deformation, including changes caused by earthquakes, landslides, volcanic activities, and subsidence. A 12-day revisit interval provides sufficient temporal resolution to capture these changes effectively.

• The ability to observe the same location repeatedly allows for interferometric synthetic aperture radar (InSAR) techniques, which are essential for measuring ground movement with millimeter-level precision.

• Improved temporal coverage: Revisiting the same point frequently ensures that data continuity is maintained. This is vital for applications such as agriculture monitoring, water resource management, and urban planning, where changes occur over short periods.

• Data consistency: The 12-day revisit interval ensures that observations are taken under similar conditions, reducing discrepancies due to seasonal or environmental changes. This is critical for accurate data interpretation.

• Global applicability: With this revisit cycle, the NISAR mission can provide consistent and reliable data for scientists and researchers worldwide, allowing them to study various phenomena across different regions.

• About the NISAR Mission: NISAR (NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar) is a joint collaboration between NASA and ISRO, designed to monitor Earth's surface changes with high accuracy.

• The mission employs dual-band radar (L-band and S-band) to measure subtle changes in Earth's surface, offering comprehensive data on various geological and environmental phenomena.

• Applications of NISAR: It supports a range of applications, including disaster management, monitoring climate change, studying vegetation and biomass, and tracking polar ice dynamics.

• NISAR's data is expected to improve our understanding of processes like tectonic plate movements, glacier dynamics, and land subsidence, which are critical for sustainability and disaster preparedness.

• Why 12-day revisit interval? The 12-day cycle is a balance between providing frequent updates and ensuring that the satellite's orbit remains efficient and fuel usage is minimized.

• This interval allows for the effective use of interferometric techniques that rely on comparing radar images taken at different times to detect surface changes.

• Key Features of NISAR: Equipped with the largest reflector antenna ever launched into space, the mission provides high-resolution images to study Earth's dynamics.

• The use of L-band and S-band radar ensures that data can be collected under all weather conditions, during both day and night.

• Partnership Details: The mission is jointly developed by NASA and ISRO, with NASA contributing the L-band radar and ISRO providing the S-band radar and spacecraft platform.

• The satellite is expected to be launched in 2024 aboard the GSLV Mk II rocket from India.

14. Which statement best explains the link between demographic transition and replacement level?

A. In stage three, fertility starts nearing replacement level

B. Stabilisation starts when fertility goes below replacement

C. Moving from high to low growth corresponds to reaching replacement level

D. Replacement level has no association with demographic shift

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Moving from high to low growth corresponds to reaching replacement level.. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Demographic Transition: It refers to the change in a population's growth rate as it transitions from high birth and death rates to low birth and death rates. This shift occurs in four stages: Stage 1: High fertility and mortality rates result in low population growth.

• Stage 2: Death rates decline due to better healthcare, leading to rapid population growth.

• Stage 3: Birth rates start declining, approaching the replacement level. This stage reflects a population stabilizing over time.

• Stage 4: Both birth and death rates are low, resulting in a stable or declining population.

• Replacement Level: Replacement level fertility is the level of fertility at which a population exactly replaces itself from one generation to the next. It is approximately 2.1 children per woman in developed countries.

• Explanation of the Answer: When a population transitions from high growth (high birth and death rates) to low growth (low birth and death rates), it usually involves reaching the replacement fertility level (Option 3 is correct).

• In Stage 3, fertility rates approach replacement level, but the population is still growing due to demographic momentum. This statement is partially correct but does not fully address the link between demographic transition and replacement level (Option 1 is partially correct).

• Stabilization begins when fertility falls below replacement level, as seen in advanced stages of demographic transition. However, this does not explain the direct link (Option 2 is partially correct).

• Replacement level fertility is closely tied to demographic transition and cannot be entirely unassociated with it (Option 4 is incorrect).

• Stages of Demographic Transition: Stage 1: Pre-industrial stage with high birth and death rates due to limited medical knowledge and high infant mortality.

• Stage 2: Death rates decline due to advancements in medicine, sanitation, and food production, leading to a population boom.

• Stage 3: Birth rates begin to decline due to increased access to education, family planning, and urbanization. The population growth rate slows down.

• Stage 4: Birth and death rates are both low, resulting in a stable or declining population size.

• Replacement Level Fertility: Replacement fertility ensures that each generation of women has enough children to replace themselves and their partners.

• In developed countries, this is usually around 2.1 children per woman. In developing countries, it may be slightly higher due to higher mortality rates.

• Examples of Countries: Stage 1: Pre-industrial societies, such as tribal communities.

15. Which biomolecule serves as the chief energy reserve in animals?

A. Glycogen

B. Starch

C. Cellulose

D. Sucrose

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Glycogen. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Glycogen serves as the primary energy reserve in animals. It is a branched polymer of glucose that is stored in the liver and skeletal muscles.

• Glycogen acts as a quick energy source for animals. When energy is required, glycogen is broken down into glucose through a process called glycogenolysis. Hence, this is the correct answer.

• It is highly efficient for energy storage due to its branched structure, which allows rapid mobilization of glucose molecules during metabolic needs.

• The liver stores glycogen to maintain blood glucose levels, while muscles use glycogen for their own energy needs during activities like exercise.

• Other options: Starch, cellulose, and sucrose do not serve as the chief energy reserves in animals. These biomolecules either have different storage roles or are specific to plants. Hence, these options are incorrect.

• Glycogen: Glycogen is a polysaccharide composed of glucose units linked by alpha-1,4-glycosidic bonds with branching points connected via alpha-1,6-glycosidic bonds.

• In humans, approximately 100-120 grams of glycogen can be stored in the liver, while about 400-500 grams is stored in the muscles.

• Glycogen is particularly important during periods of fasting or intense exercise to provide a rapid source of glucose.

• Starch: Starch is the main storage carbohydrate in plants, found in seeds, roots, and tubers. It consists of two types of molecules: amylose and amylopectin.

• Starch is not stored in animals but can be consumed through plant-based diets. It is broken down into glucose in the digestive system.

• Cellulose: Cellulose is a structural polysaccharide found in the cell walls of plants. It is composed of beta-1,4-glycosidic bonds, which cannot be digested by humans.

• Cellulose provides fiber in the diet, aiding in digestion but does not serve as an energy reserve in animals.

• Sucrose: Sucrose is a disaccharide composed of glucose and fructose. It is commonly found in plants and serves as a transport sugar in their vascular system.

• In animals, sucrose is consumed as part of the diet and metabolized for energy but is not stored as a reserve.

• Energy Storage in Animals: Animals store energy primarily in two forms: glycogen and triglycerides (fats). Glycogen provides short-term energy, while triglycerides are used for long-term energy storage.

• The liver and muscles are the two key sites of glycogen storage, while adipose tissue stores triglycerides.

• Comparison with Plants: Plants store energy primarily as starch and use cellulose for structural purposes. Animals lack the enzymes to break down cellulose.

• The metabolic pathways for glycogen synthesis and breakdown are tightly regulated by hormones such as insulin and glucagon.

B. Planetary transits

C. Deep-sky objects

D. Moon phases

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Deep-sky objects. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Deep-sky objects, such as galaxies, nebulae, and star clusters, are faint and require dark skies to be observed effectively. Urban light pollution significantly diminishes their visibility, making them the most affected astronomical objects.

• Light pollution from urban areas creates a bright background in the sky, which interferes with the observation of faint objects. This phenomenon makes viewing deep-sky objects nearly impossible without specialized equipment or remote locations.

• Planetary transits, sunspots, and moon phases are less affected by urban light pollution because they involve brighter celestial phenomena. For example: Planetary transits involve planets passing in front of a star, which is observable with telescopes even in moderately light-polluted skies.

• Sunspots are observed on the Sun's surface, which is bright enough to be unaffected by urban lighting.

• Moon phases involve the illuminated portion of the Moon, which remains visible despite urban lighting due to its brightness.

• Deep-sky objects are particularly vulnerable to light pollution because they require minimal interference and a dark night sky to be properly seen.

• Hence, the correct answer is Deep-sky objects.

• What are Deep-sky objects? Deep-sky objects include galaxies, nebulae, star clusters, and other celestial phenomena that lie beyond our solar system.

• Examples include the Andromeda Galaxy, Orion Nebula, and Pleiades Star Cluster.

• These objects are often faint and require clear, dark skies for optimal observation, making them susceptible to urban light pollution.

• Impact of Urban Light Pollution: Urban light pollution creates a glow in the night sky known as skyglow, which reduces the contrast and visibility of faint celestial objects.

• Major cities with excessive artificial lighting make it difficult to observe deep-sky objects even with telescopes, prompting many astronomers to seek remote locations.

• Observing Planetary Transits: Planetary transits, such as the transit of Venus or Mercury, are observable even in light-polluted areas due to the brightness of the star and planet involved.

• Amateur astronomers often use telescopes equipped with filters to view planetary transits more clearly.

• Sunspots and Moon Phases: Sunspots are temporary phenomena on the Sun's surface. They are bright and easily visible with proper equipment, even in urban settings.

• Moon phases are among the brightest celestial phenomena. The Moon remains visible regardless of light pollution due to its proximity and brightness.

• Light Pollution Reduction: Efforts to reduce light pollution include using shielded lighting, minimizing unnecessary outdoor lighting, and implementing dark sky ordinances in urban areas.

• Organizations like the International Dark-Sky Association (IDA) work to preserve dark skies for astronomical observation.

• Remote Observation Locations: Astronomers often travel to remote locations such as mountaintops or deserts to observe deep-sky objects without interference from light pollution.
