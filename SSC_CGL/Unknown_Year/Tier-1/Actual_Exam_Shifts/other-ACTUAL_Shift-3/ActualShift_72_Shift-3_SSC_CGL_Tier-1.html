1. Which famous experiment was done by Michael Faraday in 1831?

A. Discovery of quantum magnetometers

B. Discovery of law of elasticity

C. Discovery of electromagnetic induction

D. Discovery of natural radioactivity

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Discovery of electromagnetic induction. Key Points Additional Information

• Michael Faraday, an English scientist, conducted the famous experiment in 1831 which led to the discovery of electromagnetic induction.

• Electromagnetic induction is the process of generating an electromotive force (EMF) through the relative motion between a conductor and a magnetic field.

• This discovery paved the way for numerous technological advancements, including the development of electric generators and transformers.

• Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction states that the induced EMF in any closed circuit is equal to the rate of change of the magnetic flux through the circuit.

• Electromagnetic Induction Electromagnetic induction refers to the generation of an electric current by changing the magnetic field. This phenomenon is fundamental to many types of electrical devices, including generators and transformers.

• Faraday's Experiment: In 1831, Faraday discovered that when a magnet is moved in and out of a coil of wire, an electric current is induced in the wire. This is known as Faraday's law of induction.

• Lenz's Law: This law states that the direction of the induced current is such that it opposes the change in magnetic flux that caused it. This is a consequence of the law of conservation of energy.

• Applications: The principles of electromagnetic induction are applied in many devices, such as inductors, transformers, and many types of electrical generators.

• Quantum Magnetometers Quantum magnetometers are devices that measure magnetic fields using quantum effects. These are used in various applications, including medical imaging and geological surveys.

• Law of Elasticity The law of elasticity, also known as Hooke's Law, states that the deformation of a material is directly proportional to the force applied to it, provided the material's elastic limit is not exceeded.

• Natural Radioactivity Natural radioactivity refers to the spontaneous decay of unstable atomic nuclei, resulting in the emission of radiation. This phenomenon was discovered by Henri Becquerel in 1896.

3. Who reconciled Dalton's atomic hypothesis with Gay-Lussac's results on the combination of volumes in 1811?

A. Robert Boyle

B. Amadeo Avogadro

C. Fred Hoyle

D. Jacques Charles

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Amadeo Avogadro. Key Points Additional Information

• Amadeo Avogadro was an Italian scientist who is most famous for his hypothesis known as Avogadro's Law.

• In 1811, Avogadro proposed that equal volumes of gases, at the same temperature and pressure, contain an equal number of molecules.

• This hypothesis was crucial in reconciling Dalton's atomic hypothesis with Gay-Lussac's law of combining volumes.

• Avogadro's ideas were not widely accepted until after his death, but they eventually became a fundamental principle in the field of chemistry.

• Dalton's Atomic Hypothesis Formulated by John Dalton in the early 19th century, this hypothesis proposed that all matter is composed of indivisible atoms.

• Dalton suggested that atoms of a given element are identical in mass and properties, and that chemical reactions involve the rearrangement of these atoms.

• Gay-Lussac's Law of Combining Volumes Joseph Louis Gay-Lussac, a French chemist, discovered that gases combine in simple whole-number ratios by volume under the same conditions of temperature and pressure.

• This empirical law was key to understanding the behavior of gases and provided a basis for Avogadro's hypothesis.

• Avogadro's Law Avogadro's Law states that equal volumes of different gases at the same temperature and pressure contain an equal number of molecules.

• This law helped clarify the relationship between the mass and volume of gases, leading to the concept of the mole and Avogadro's number, 6.022 x 10^23 molecules per mole.

• Importance of Avogadro's Work Avogadro's contributions laid the groundwork for the development of the ideal gas law, which combines several earlier gas laws into a single equation.

• His work also paved the way for the modern understanding of molecular theory and stoichiometry in chemical reactions.

• The Amendment was introduced to avoid jumbo ministries and ensure efficient governance.

• The maximum number of ministers in a state, including the Chief Minister, shall not exceed 15% of the total number of members of the Legislative Assembly of that state.

• Anti-Defection Law (Tenth Schedule) The anti-defection law was introduced by the 52nd Amendment Act of 1985 and later strengthened by the 91st Amendment Act of 2003.

• The main objective of the law is to combat the evil of political defections by elected members.

• According to this law, if a member of a house belonging to a political party voluntarily gives up the membership of his party or votes (or abstains from voting) contrary to the directions issued by his party, he can be disqualified from being a member of the house.

• The decision on disqualification questions on the ground of defection is referred to the Chairman or the Speaker of such House and their decision is final.

8. Who published the logistic equation model of population growth in 1838?

A. Howard Thomas Odum

B. Alfred Russel Wallace

C. Pierre François Verhulst

D. George Evelyn Hutchinson

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Pierre François Verhulst. Key Points Additional Information

• Pierre François Verhulst was a Belgian mathematician and sociologist.

• He is best known for formulating the logistic growth model in 1838.

• The logistic equation is a model of population growth that describes how a population grows rapidly at first and then levels off as it approaches a maximum size, known as the carrying capacity.

• Verhulst’s model was one of the first to incorporate the concept of environmental limits on population growth, making it a foundational concept in the field of ecology.

• Logistic Growth Model The logistic growth model is represented by the equation: dN/dt = rN(1 - N/K)

• In this equation, N represents the population size, r is the intrinsic growth rate, and K is the carrying capacity of the environment.

• The term (1 - N/K) represents the effect of environmental resistance as the population size approaches the carrying capacity.

• The model predicts that populations will grow rapidly when they are small and slow down as they approach the carrying capacity, eventually stabilizing at the carrying capacity.

• Carrying Capacity (K) The carrying capacity is the maximum population size that an environment can sustain indefinitely.

• It is determined by the availability of resources such as food, water, and space.

• When a population reaches its carrying capacity, the birth rate equals the death rate, and the population size remains stable.

• Intrinsic Growth Rate (r) The intrinsic growth rate is the rate at which a population increases in size under ideal conditions, with no environmental limitations.

• It is influenced by factors such as birth rate, death rate, and the age at which individuals begin to reproduce.

• History and Impact The logistic growth model was first proposed by Pierre François Verhulst in 1838 and has since become a fundamental concept in population ecology.

• It has been applied to a wide range of biological populations, including humans, animals, and plants.

• The model has also been used in other fields, such as economics and sociology, to describe the growth of various systems.

11. Which Act was passed by the British Parliament in the year 1773 AD to regulate the activities of the East India Company?

A. Pitt's India Act

B. Indian Slavery Act

C. Age of Consent Act

D. Regulating Act

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is Regulating Act. Key Points Additional Information

• The Regulating Act of 1773 was the first step taken by the British Parliament to control and regulate the affairs of the East India Company in India.

• It marked the beginning of British parliamentary control over the East India Company and its administration in India.

• The Act provided for the establishment of a Supreme Court at Calcutta (now Kolkata) to administer justice.

• The Governor of Bengal was elevated to the position of Governor-General of Bengal, with supervisory authority over the presidencies of Madras and Bombay.

• Pitt's India Act Passed in 1784, this Act was intended to address the deficiencies of the Regulating Act of 1773.

• It established a dual system of control by setting up a Board of Control to oversee the political affairs while the Court of Directors retained control over commercial affairs.

• This Act allowed the British government to have greater control over the administration of India.

• Indian Slavery Act Passed in 1843, this Act aimed to abolish slavery in British India.

• It made the sale, purchase, or transfer of slaves illegal and provided for the protection of the rights of those who had been enslaved.

• Age of Consent Act Enacted in 1891, this Act raised the age of consent for girls from 10 to 12 years in all jurisdictions, whether Hindu or Muslim.

• It was aimed at addressing the issue of child marriage and the associated health risks for young girls.

• The interior of the earth is not considered a major climate control factor because it does not have a direct influence on the surface climate conditions.

• Major climate controls are factors that have a significant impact on the climate of a place.

• These factors include altitude, latitude, ocean currents, and others which directly affect the temperature, humidity, and weather patterns.

• The interior of the earth, while influencing geothermal energy and volcanic activity, does not play a significant role in determining the surface climate.

• Altitude Altitude refers to the height of a place above sea level.
