33. The mole (mol) is used to measure which of the following?

A. Temperature of a substance

B. Pressure of a gas

C. Conductivity of a substance

D. Amount of substance

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is Amount of substance. Key Points Additional Information

• The mole (symbol: mol) is the SI unit used to measure the "amount of substance."

• It is defined as the amount of substance that contains exactly 6.02214076 × 1023 elementary entities (e.g., atoms, molecules, ions, or electrons). This number is called the Avogadro constant.

• One mole of any substance contains the same number of particles as there are atoms in 12 grams of pure carbon-12.

• The mole provides a link between the macroscopic scale (mass in grams) and the microscopic scale (number of particles).

• It is widely used in chemistry, physics, and material sciences to quantify the amount of a chemical substance.

• Avogadro's Constant: Avogadro's constant (6.02214076 × 1023 mol-1) is a fundamental physical constant that specifies the number of particles in one mole of a substance.

• It is named after the Italian scientist Amedeo Avogadro, who first hypothesized the concept.

• Relationship Between Mole and Molar Mass: The molar mass (in grams per mole) of a substance is numerically equal to the relative atomic or molecular mass of the substance.

• For example, 1 mole of water (H2O) has a molar mass of 18 g/mol.

• Applications of the Mole Concept: Used to calculate the amount of reactants and products in a chemical reaction.

• Helps in determining concentrations in solutions (e.g., molarity).

• Other Related SI Units: Kelvin (K): Measures temperature.

• Pascal (Pa): Measures pressure.

• Siemens (S): Measures electrical conductivity.
