14. In a Telescope, compared to the eyepiece lens the objective lens has

A. larger focal length and larger aperture

B. larger focal length and smaller aperture

C. smaller focal length and larger aperture

D. smaller focal length and smaller aperture

Answer A

Solution

Explanation: In a telescope, the objective lens is meant to collect light from distant objects. To gather sufficient light, it must have a larger aperture. To properly focus nearly parallel rays from distant objects, it requires a larger focal length. The eyepiece, in contrast, has a smaller focal length and smaller aperture for magnification. ⇒ Compared to the eyepiece, the objective lens has both a larger focal length and a larger aperture. Therefore, the correct answer is Option 1.

15. Which of the following organelles have their own DNA and ribosomes ?

A. Endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi bodies

B. Mitochondria and Chloroplast

C. Endoplasmic reticulum and cytoskeleton

D. Cell wall and nucleolus

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Mitochondria and Chloroplast. Explanation:

• Mitochondria: Known as the "powerhouse of the cell," mitochondria are responsible for producing energy in the form of ATP through the process of cellular respiration.

• They contain their own circular DNA and 70S ribosomes, similar to bacterial cells, which allows them to produce some of their own proteins.

• Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) is inherited maternally in most organisms.

• Chloroplasts: Found in plant cells and some protists, chloroplasts are the site of photosynthesis, where light energy is converted into chemical energy stored in glucose.

• Like mitochondria, chloroplasts have their own circular DNA and 70S ribosomes, enabling them to synthesize proteins essential for photosynthesis.

• They are believed to have originated from photosynthetic cyanobacteria through endosymbiosis.

23. Process by which substances like carbon dioxide or oxygen can move across the cell membrane is called _________.

A. diffusion

B. osmosis

C. transpiration

D. guttation

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is diffusion. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Diffusion is the process by which molecules move from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration.

• It is a passive process, meaning it does not require energy input (ATP) from the cell to occur.

• In biological systems, diffusion is critical for the exchange of gases like oxygen and carbon dioxide across cell membranes.

• For example, oxygen diffuses into cells from the bloodstream because the oxygen concentration is higher in the blood compared to the cytoplasm of the cells.

• Similarly, carbon dioxide diffuses out of the cells into the blood to be transported to the lungs for exhalation.

• Osmosis: Osmosis is the movement of water molecules across a semipermeable membrane from a region of lower solute concentration to a region of higher solute concentration.

• It is a type of diffusion specific to water and is crucial for maintaining cell turgor and fluid balance in living organisms.

• Example: Absorption of water by plant roots from the soil.

• Transpiration: Transpiration refers to the process by which water is lost from plants in the form of vapor, primarily through stomata in the leaves.

• It helps in the upward movement of water and minerals from roots to other parts of the plant.

• Transpiration also plays a role in cooling the plant and maintaining its temperature.

• Guttation: Guttation is the exudation of water droplets from the edges of leaves, usually occurring at night or in the early morning.

• It happens when root pressure forces water out of the plant's hydathodes (specialized pores).

• Guttation is often observed in small plants like grasses.

• Examples of Diffusion in Everyday Life: The smell of perfume spreading in a room is an example of diffusion in gases.

• The dissolving of sugar in water is an example of diffusion in liquids.

• Passive Transport: Diffusion and osmosis are types of passive transport because they do not require cellular energy (ATP).

• Factors Affecting Diffusion: Concentration gradient: The larger the difference in concentration, the faster the diffusion.

• Temperature: Higher temperatures increase the kinetic energy of molecules, speeding up diffusion.

• Surface area: A larger surface area allows more molecules to diffuse at once.

• Membrane permeability: The nature of the membrane affects how easily substances can diffuse across it.
