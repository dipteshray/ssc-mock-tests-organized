14. Statement 1: In Newton’s equilibrium theory, the centrifugal force generated by the Earth-moon system plays a significant role in creating tidal bulges. Statement 2: The tidal bulges predicted by this theory are stationary and do not move with the Earth’s rotation.

A. Both statements are true

B. Statement 1 is False, but Statement 2 is True

C. Statement 1 is true, but Statement 2 is false.

D. Both statements are false.

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Statement 1 is true, but Statement 2 is false. Key Points Statement 1: In Newton’s equilibrium theory, the centrifugal force generated by the Earth-moon system plays a significant role in creating tidal bulges is true because,- Statement 2: The tidal bulges predicted by this theory are stationary and do not move with the Earth’s rotation is false because,- Additional Information

• Equilibrium Theory: Newton’s equilibrium theory describes the gravitational interactions between Earth, the moon, and the sun, which cause tides.

• Gravitational Forces: The gravitational pull of the moon primarily, and to a lesser extent of the sun, creates tidal forces that cause the water to bulge out on the side of Earth closest to the moon and the side farthest from the moon.

• Centrifugal Force: The Earth and moon orbit around a common center of mass, and this orbital motion generates a centrifugal force, which is most pronounced on the side of Earth opposite the moon.

• The combination of the moon’s gravitational pull and the centrifugal force created by this movement results in the formation of two tidal bulges on opposite sides of the Earth.

• These tidal bulges lead to high tides in the regions of the Earth beneath the bulges.

• Tidal Movement: In reality, the tidal bulges move along with the Earth’s rotation. As the Earth rotates, different areas pass through the tidal bulges, causing the observed rise and fall of sea levels.

• Dynamic Nature: The equilibrium theory assumes that the tidal bulges are fixed relative to the positions of the moon and sun, but due to Earth’s rotation and gravitational interactions, these bulges travel westward with the Earth's rotation.

• Local Geographies: The actual movement of tidal bulges is also influenced by the shape of ocean basins, coastlines, and the Coriolis effect, which cause variations in tidal patterns around the globe.

• Tidal Cycles: This rotation leads to the regular cycles of high and low tides experienced along coastlines approximately twice each day.

• Dynamic Theory of Tides: Unlike Newton’s equilibrium theory, the dynamic theory of tides considers the effects of the Earth’s rotation, the geometry of ocean basins, and frictional forces.

• This dynamic theory provides a more accurate representation of tidal movements by taking into account the fluid dynamics of ocean water.

• Real-World Tides: Tides are also influenced by meteorological conditions such as wind and atmospheric pressure, which can enhance or diminish tidal effects in certain regions.

• Understanding the interaction of multiple forces and dynamic conditions is key to accurately predicting and explaining tidal behaviors observed in different parts of the world.

18. The Southern Oscillation is a seesaw pattern between which of these two oceans?

A. Pacific and Atlantic Ocean

B. Pacific and Indian Ocean

C. Atlantic and Indian Ocean

D. None of the above

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is option 2. Key Points Additional Information Southern Oscillation: How the Southern Oscillation Affects Weather Patterns: Key Impact on India and Surrounding Regions: Interconnectedness of Oceans:

• The Southern Oscillation refers to the seesaw pattern of atmospheric pressure that occurs between the western and eastern Pacific Ocean regions. This phenomenon is closely linked to El Niño and La Niña events.

• The Southern Oscillation Index (SOI) measures the difference in air pressure between Tahiti (in the eastern Pacific) and Darwin (in the western Pacific).

• El Niño and La Niña are the warm and cold phases of this oscillation, which affects global weather patterns, including those in the Indian Ocean. Hence, option 2 is correct.

• The Southern Oscillation (SO) is a natural climate phenomenon that describes the periodic fluctuation in atmospheric pressure over the tropical Indian and Pacific Oceans.

• It is an integral component of the El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) cycle, which is a major driver of climate variability globally.

• The Southern Oscillation is the see-saw pattern between high pressure in the eastern Pacific and low pressure in the western Pacific, which occurs every 2 to 7 years.

• It is directly related to El Niño (warming of ocean temperatures) and La Niña (cooling of ocean temperatures).

• El Niño (Warm Phase): High pressure in the western Pacific (Indian Ocean and Indonesia).

• Low pressure in the eastern Pacific (off the coast of South America).

• Global Effects:

• Droughts in the Indian subcontinent and Australia.

• Heavy rains and flooding in the western coast of South America.

• Disruption of monsoon patterns in India and Southeast Asia.

• La Niña (Cool Phase): The pressure pattern reverses, leading to:

• Increased rainfall in the Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia.

• Drier conditions in South America.

• Cooler sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern Pacific.

• The Southern Oscillation is crucial in determining the Indian Monsoon and can lead to either excessive rainfall or drought conditions depending on the phase of the oscillation.

• For example, El Niño years often correspond with weak monsoon seasons, leading to water scarcity and crop failures in India.

• Pacific Ocean: The eastern side of the Pacific (off the coast of South America) experiences a cooling or warming phase depending on the Southern Oscillation, directly impacting global weather.

• Indian Ocean: The change in the pressure pattern of the Southern Oscillation affects the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD), which can influence the Indian Monsoon and weather patterns in Southeast Asia.
