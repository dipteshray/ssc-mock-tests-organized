4. The occasional development of warm ocean surface waters along the coast of Ecuador and Peru is known as

A. EI Nino and La Nina

B. EI Nino

C. La Nina

D. Southern Oscillation

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is EI Nino Key Points Additional Information

• EI Nino refers to the occasional development of warm ocean surface waters along the coast of Ecuador and Peru.

• This phenomenon typically occurs every few years and can have significant impacts on global weather patterns.

• La Nina is the opposite phase, characterized by unusually cold ocean temperatures in the Equatorial Pacific.

• The Southern Oscillation is a broader climatic pattern that includes both EI Nino and La Nina phases.

• EI Nino and La Nina are part of the El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) cycle, which influences global climate and weather patterns.

• The effects of EI Nino can include increased rainfall in the southern United States and Peru, and drought in the western Pacific region.

• Understanding and predicting these phenomena are crucial for mitigating their impacts on agriculture, water resources, and disaster management.

Solution

The correct answer is 'Jupiter.' Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Jupiter: Jupiter is the largest planet in our Solar System and has the shortest day of all the planets.

• Jupiter completes one rotation on its axis approximately every 9.9 hours (about 10 hours).

• This rapid rotation contributes to its oblate shape, meaning it is slightly flattened at the poles and bulging at the equator.

• The high rotational speed also drives dynamic weather patterns and contributes to its prominent equatorial bands and the Great Red Spot, a giant storm system.

• Earth: Earth takes approximately 24 hours to complete one rotation on its axis, defining the length of our day.

• Mars: Mars has a day length of about 24.6 hours, very similar to Earth's day.

• Jupiter's rapid rotation has significant effects on its atmospheric phenomena and magnetic field.

• Understanding the rotational periods of planets helps in comprehending their weather systems, surface conditions, and overall planetary dynamics.

• Jupiter's short day length compared to its massive size is a fascinating aspect of planetary science, demonstrating the diverse characteristics of celestial bodies in our Solar System.

A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.

C. A is true, but R is false.

D. A is false, but R is true

Answer B

Solution

The correct option is 'Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A'. Key Points Additional Information

• Assertion A: The sun is the ultimate source of heat and light for the solar system. This statement is true.

• Explanation: The sun is the central star of our solar system. It provides the essential heat and light that enables life on Earth and sustains various processes within the solar system. Without the sun, the planets and other celestial bodies would be incredibly cold and dark.

• Reason R: The sun is made up of extremely hot gases. This statement is true.

• Explanation: The sun primarily consists of hydrogen and helium gases. These gases are in a plasma state and undergo nuclear fusion reactions in the sun's core, producing tremendous amounts of energy. This energy is emitted as heat and light.

• Evaluation of the Relationship Between A and R: Both the assertion and the reason are true. However, the reason provided does not directly explain why the sun is the ultimate source of heat and light for the solar system.

• While it is true that the sun is made up of extremely hot gases, this fact alone does not explain why it is the ultimate source of heat and light. The reason it is the ultimate source is due to its role as a massive, energy-emitting star at the center of the solar system, providing the necessary energy for life and planetary processes.

• Therefore, the reason (R) is not the correct explanation of the assertion (A).

• Nuclear Fusion: The sun's core temperature reaches about 15 million degrees Celsius, enabling nuclear fusion reactions. These reactions convert hydrogen into helium and release enormous amounts of energy in the form of electromagnetic radiation (including visible light) and heat.

• Energy Distribution: The energy produced by the sun radiates outward through the solar system, impacting planets, moons, and other celestial bodies, and driving atmospheric and climatic phenomena on Earth.

• Solar Importance: The solar energy received by Earth is crucial for photosynthesis in plants, the water cycle, and the overall climate system, highlighting the sun's role as the fundamental energy source for life on our planet.

20. Which of the following is NOT a part of Wegener's explanation for the origin of folded mountains?

A. Crumpling of the frontal edges of continental blocks

B. Resistance from the sea-floor (sima)

C. Forceful resistance offered by 'sial' against 'sima'

D. Compression due to collision of continental plates

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is ''Forceful resistance offered by 'sial' against 'sima'' Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Wegener's Theory of Folded Mountains: Alfred Wegener proposed the theory of Continental Drift which suggests that continents were once joined together in a supercontinent called Pangaea.

• He explained that the movement and collision of these continental blocks are responsible for the formation of folded mountains.

• The process involves the crumpling and folding of the Earth's crust due to the compression forces at convergent boundaries where plates collide.

• Crumpling of the frontal edges of continental blocks: Wegener argued that when continental blocks move, their frontal edges may crumple, leading to the creation of mountain ranges.

• This process is part of the interaction at convergent boundaries, contributing to orogeny (mountain-building).

• Resistance from the sea-floor (sima): The term 'sima' refers to the oceanic crust made up of silicate and magnesium.

• Wegener included the resistance from this oceanic crust against the moving continental blocks as a contributing factor to mountain formation.

• Compression due to collision of continental plates: Wegener’s theory emphasizes that the collision and subsequent compression of continental plates cause significant folding and crumpling of the crust, forming folded mountains.

• This is considered a primary mechanism in the formation of major mountain ranges like the Himalayas and the Alps.

• The concept of 'sial' and 'sima' relates to the composition of the Earth's crust, where 'sial' stands for silicate and aluminum (continental crust) and 'sima' stands for silicate and magnesium (oceanic crust).

• While Wegener acknowledged the resistance of sima in the context of mountain building, his theory did not focus on 'forceful resistance offered by sial against sima' as a significant factor in the formation of folded mountains.

• Understanding these concepts is crucial for grasping the fundamentals of plate tectonics and the geological processes leading to the formation of various landforms.

• Wegener's theories laid the foundation for modern geology and tectonics, although some aspects of his work, like the specific forceful resistance mechanism, evolved with further scientific advancements.

24. Sattriya music and dance are classical traditions from which Indian state, associated with Vaishnavite monasteries and devotional storytelling performances?

A. Assam

B. Odisha

C. Manipur

D. West Bengal

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Assam. Key Points Additional Information

• Sattriya is a prominent Indian classical dance-drama tradition that originated in the Sattras (monasteries) of Assam during the 15th century.

• The art form was founded by the great Saint-Reformer Mahapurush Sankaradeva as a medium to propagate the Ekasarana Dharma or the Neo-Vaishnavite movement through devotional storytelling.

• It was traditionally performed by male monks known as Bhokots as part of their daily rituals; however, in modern times, it is performed by both men and women on public stages.

• The performance is deeply connected to Ankiya Naat (traditional one-act plays) and centers on themes from the Bhagavata Purana and the life of Lord Krishna.

• On November 15, 2000, the Sangeet Natak Akademi recognized Sattriya as the eighth classical dance form of India, granting it national and international status.

• The dance includes foundational technical elements called Mati Akhora (64 basic exercises) and is accompanied by Borgeets (devotional songs) and rhythmic patterns played on the Khol (drum) and Manjira (cymbals).

• Other Classical Traditions: Odisha: The state is famous for Odissi, a classical dance known for its Tribhanga posture and sculpturesque movements, deeply rooted in the Jagannath culture.

• Manipur: It is the birthplace of Manipuri (Jagoi) dance, which is characterized by its fluid, lyrical movements and the iconic Raslila performances involving the Kumil (barrel-shaped skirt).

• West Bengal: While not home to a Sangeet Natak Akademi-recognized classical dance of the same status as Sattriya, it is famous for Gaudiya Nritya and the martial/folk-classical Chhau dance (Purulia style).

• Musical Accompaniment: The Sattriya tradition utilizes unique musical scales and instruments, most notably the Banshi (flute) and the Cymbals, which guide the Tala (rhythmic cycle).

25. Who among the following muslim leaders first propounded the theory of 'Two nations'?

A. Liaqat Ali

B. Mohammad Iqbal

C. Muhammad Ali Jinnah

D. Rahmat Ali Chaudhary

Answer B

Solution

The theory of 'Two Nations' was first propounded by Mohammad Iqbal. Mohammad Iqbal, a prominent philosopher and poet, introduced the concept in 1930 during his presidential address at the Allahabad session of the Muslim League.
