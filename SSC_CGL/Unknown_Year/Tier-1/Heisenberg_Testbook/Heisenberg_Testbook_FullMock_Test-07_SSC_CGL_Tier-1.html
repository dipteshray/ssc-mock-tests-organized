• At tectonic plate boundaries, such as divergent and convergent zones, magma can escape and form volcanic eruptions.

• The molten rock that reaches the surface is called lava, and it solidifies to create various landforms like volcanoes, lava domes, and plateaus.

• Volcanism is often associated with hotspots, where mantle plumes push magma to the surface, as seen in regions like Hawaii and Yellowstone.

• Volcanic eruptions contribute to the Earth's geological processes, including the formation of new crust, landforms, and mineral deposits.

• Examples of erosion include river valleys, coastal cliffs, and desert landscapes, which are shaped by external forces rather than internal geological processes.

• Glacial activity does not cause volcanism, as it is unrelated to the movement of magma or tectonic processes.

29. Which of the following is true regarding public goods as compared to private goods?

A. Public goods are non-excludable and non-rivalrous

B. Public goods are excludable, while private goods are non-excludable.

C. Public goods are rivalrous, while private goods are non-rivalrous

D. Private goods are non-excludable and non-rivalrous

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Public goods are non-excludable and non-rivalrous.. Key Points Additional Information

• Public goods are characterized by being non-excludable and non-rivalrous. Non-excludable means that individuals cannot be excluded from using the good, and non-rivalrous means that one person's use of the good does not reduce its availability for others. Hence, this statement is correct.

• Private goods, in contrast, are typically excludable and rivalrous. Individuals can be excluded from using the good (e.g., by price), and consumption by one individual reduces its availability for others. This does not describe public goods; hence, this statement is incorrect.

• Public goods are essential for collective welfare, and examples include national defense, public parks, and street lighting. These goods benefit society as a whole and are funded through taxation or public spending. This aligns with the characteristics of public goods.

• Private goods are primarily driven by market mechanisms and are allocated based on individuals’ ability to pay. Examples include food, clothing, and vehicles. These goods are not applicable to the definition of public goods. Hence, this statement does not apply to public goods.

• Public goods often face challenges such as the "free rider problem", where individuals benefit from the good without contributing to its cost, which is a unique issue for non-excludable goods. This characteristic is specific to public goods and underscores their difference from private goods.

• Characteristics of Public Goods: Non-excludability: Once provided, no individual can be excluded from using the good, regardless of whether they have paid for it (e.g., clean air).

• Non-rivalry: The consumption of the good by one individual does not reduce its availability for others (e.g., street lighting).

• Examples: National defense, public infrastructure, air quality, and environmental conservation.

• Challenges Associated with Public Goods: Free Rider Problem: Individuals who benefit from the good without contributing to its cost. This often leads to under-provision of public goods.

• Funding Issues: Public goods are often funded through government taxation, but determining the optimal level of provision can be challenging.

• Private Goods: Excludable: Individuals can be prevented from accessing the good if they do not pay (e.g., a ticketed event).

• Rivalrous: Consumption by one individual reduces the availability of the good for others (e.g., food or clothing).

• Examples: Consumer products, housing, and personal services.

• Comparison of Public and Private Goods: Public goods: Provided by the government, funded by taxes, and aimed at collective welfare.

• Private goods: Provided by private entities, driven by profit motives, and allocated based on individual purchasing power.

• Important Points Public goods often require government intervention: Since market mechanisms alone cannot ensure their optimal provision due to the free rider problem.

• Public goods are crucial for economic stability: They contribute to long-term societal welfare, which private goods cannot solely fulfill.

• Global Examples: International efforts like climate change mitigation or global health initiatives are forms of public goods benefiting all nations.
