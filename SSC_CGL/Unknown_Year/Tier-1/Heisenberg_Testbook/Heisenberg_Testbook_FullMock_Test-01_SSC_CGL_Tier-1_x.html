• TSD is commonly observed in certain reptiles, such as turtles, crocodiles, and some species of lizards. In these species, genetic factors do not directly determine the sex of an individual.

• The temperature at which the eggs are incubated influences whether the embryos develop as males or females. For example: In some species, lower temperatures result in males, while higher temperatures result in females.

• In other species, the relationship may be reversed or more complex, with males produced at intermediate temperatures and females at both extremes.

• TSD is an adaptation that allows reptiles to respond to environmental conditions, potentially optimizing the survival and reproductive success of the population. Hence, temperature is the correct factor influencing the sex of developing eggs in reptiles.

• The other options, such as water, wind, and pressure, do not directly influence the sex determination process in reptiles.
