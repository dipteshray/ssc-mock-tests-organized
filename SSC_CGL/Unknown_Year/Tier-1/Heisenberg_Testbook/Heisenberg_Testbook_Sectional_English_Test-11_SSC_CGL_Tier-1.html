11. Identify the voice of the following sentence: "...the transition is often stymied by bureaucratic inertia and a lack of private sector participation..."

A. Causative Voice

B. Active Voice

C. Reflexive Voice

D. Passive Voice

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is Option 4. Key Points Therefore, the correct answer is 4. Analytical Insight: In high-level exams, formal academic writing (like this passage on energy security) frequently utilizes the Passive Voice. It allows the author to focus on the phenomenon (the transition) rather than the obstructors (the inertia). Additional Information

• To identify the voice, we analyze the relationship between the subject and the action performed in the sentence.

• In the sentence: "...the transition is often stymied by bureaucratic inertia...": The subject "the transition" is not performing the action; it is the recipient of the action (it is being stopped/hindered).

• The verb follows the passive construction: Auxiliary verb 'be' (is) + Past Participle (stymied).

• The agents performing the action are introduced by the preposition "by" (bureaucratic inertia and lack of participation).

• This structure is a clear indicator of the Passive Voice.

• Why other options are incorrect: Option 1: Causative Voice indicates someone causing someone else to do something (e.g., "The policy had the sectors increase participation").

• Option 2: In Active Voice, the sentence would be: "Bureaucratic inertia and a lack of private sector participation often stymie the transition."

• Option 3: Reflexive Voice occurs when the subject and object are the same (e.g., "The transition stymied itself").

• Stymied: Prevented or hindered the progress of.

• Inertia: A tendency to do nothing or to remain unchanged (often used for slow-moving bureaucracy).

• Augment: Make something greater by adding to it; increase.

• Ubiquitous: Present, appearing, or found everywhere.
