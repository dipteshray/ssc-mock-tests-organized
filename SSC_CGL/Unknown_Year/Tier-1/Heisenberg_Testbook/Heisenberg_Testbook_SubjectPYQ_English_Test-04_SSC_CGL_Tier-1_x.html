1. Which of the following temples is also known as "Black Pagoda"?

A. Sun Temple, Konark

B. Meenakshi Temple, Madurai

C. Sri Venkateswara Temple, Tirupati

D. Lingaraj Temple, Bhubaneswar

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Sun Temple, Konark. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• The Sun Temple at Konark, located in Odisha, India, is famously known as the "Black Pagoda" due to its dark-colored appearance and its historical use as a navigational landmark by sailors.

• It was built in the 13th century by King Narasimhadeva I of the Eastern Ganga dynasty.

• The temple is dedicated to Lord Surya (the Sun God) and is designed in the shape of a giant chariot pulled by seven horses, symbolizing the sun's movement across the heavens.

• This UNESCO World Heritage Site is renowned for its intricate carvings and architectural brilliance, depicting scenes of everyday life, deities, and celestial beings.

• The temple's unique design and historical significance make it a major attraction for tourists and scholars worldwide. Hence, this is the correct option.

• Meenakshi Temple, Madurai: Located in Madurai, Tamil Nadu, this temple is dedicated to Goddess Meenakshi (a form of Parvati) and Lord Sundareswarar (Shiva).

• It is famous for its Dravidian architecture, intricate carvings, and towering gopurams (gateway towers).

• The temple is not referred to as the "Black Pagoda." Hence, this option is incorrect.

• Sri Venkateswara Temple, Tirupati: Situated in Tirupati, Andhra Pradesh, this temple is dedicated to Lord Venkateswara (a form of Vishnu).

• It is one of the richest and most visited temples in the world.

• While this temple holds immense spiritual significance, it is not known as the "Black Pagoda." Hence, this option is incorrect.

• Lingaraj Temple, Bhubaneswar: Located in Bhubaneswar, Odisha, this temple is dedicated to Lord Shiva.

• It is one of the oldest temples in Odisha, showcasing Kalinga architecture.

• Although significant, it is not referred to as the "Black Pagoda." Hence, this option is incorrect.

• Key Features of Sun Temple, Konark: Its architectural style is called Kalinga architecture, characterized by intricate sculptures and carvings.

• The temple features wheels of the chariot, which serve as sundials, showcasing advanced ancient engineering.

• It was designed to align with the sun's rays, symbolizing the temple's dedication to the Sun God.

• Despite being partially in ruins, it remains a symbol of India's rich cultural heritage.

• Historical significance: The Sun Temple is believed to have been constructed to commemorate military victories and honor the Sun God.

• UNESCO World Heritage Site: The temple was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1984.

• Naval landmark: Its nickname "Black Pagoda" comes from its use by sailors as a navigational guide.

• Architectural wonder: The temple's design and carvings reflect the advanced craftsmanship and artistic excellence of ancient India.

2. Which of the following is a classical form of Indian music?

A. Bhangra

B. Kuchipudi

C. Hindustani

D. Garba

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Hindustani. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Hindustani music is one of the two principal styles of Indian classical music, the other being Carnatic music.

• It originated in the northern parts of India and has been influenced by Persian and Islamic traditions.

• This classical form encompasses various genres such as Dhrupad, Khayal, Tappa, Thumri, and Tarana, with unique features and approaches to melody and rhythm.

• The foundation of Hindustani music lies in ragas (melodic frameworks) and talas (rhythmic cycles), which define the structure and emotional expression of compositions.

• Prominent Hindustani musicians include Ravi Shankar, Ustad Bismillah Khan, Ustad Zakir Hussain, and Pt. Bhimsen Joshi, who have contributed significantly to its global recognition.

• Bhangra: Origin: Bhangra is a lively folk dance and music form originating from Punjab, India.

• Purpose: It is traditionally performed during harvest festivals and celebrations.

• Features: It incorporates energetic movements and beats, typically played using instruments such as the dhol, tumbi, and chimta.

• Global Reach: Bhangra has gained popularity globally, especially in Bollywood and fusion music.

• Kuchipudi: Origin: Kuchipudi is a classical dance form from Andhra Pradesh, India.

• Characteristics: It combines dance, acting, and storytelling, often narrating tales from Hindu mythology.

• Costumes: Performers wear elaborate costumes and jewelry, reflecting its theatrical nature.

• Key Elements: It includes intricate footwork, graceful movements, and expressions (abhinaya).

• Garba: Origin: Garba is a traditional folk dance from Gujarat, India.

• Occasion: It is primarily performed during the Navratri festival.

• Style: Participants dance in a circular pattern, accompanied by rhythmic clapping and music.

• Instruments: Common instruments used include the dhol, tabla, and harmonium.

• Indian Classical Music: Indian classical music is broadly categorized into Hindustani (North Indian) and Carnatic (South Indian) traditions.

• Both styles rely on the concept of ragas (melodic frameworks) and talas (rhythmic cycles).

• It is deeply intertwined with spiritual and philosophical themes, often aiming to evoke specific emotions (rasa).

• Global Influence: Indian classical music has influenced various global genres, including jazz and contemporary music.

• Renowned artists like Ravi Shankar have introduced Indian music to Western audiences.

• Government Support: The Indian government actively promotes classical music through programs, awards, and institutions like the Sangeet Natak Akademi.

4. What were the coins minted in Delhi during the reign of Tomar and Chauhan called?

A. Rupees

B. Dehliwal

C. Dinar

D. Tanka

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Dehliwal. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• The coins minted in Delhi during the reign of the Tomar and Chauhan dynasties were called Dehliwal.

• The term "Dehliwal" specifically refers to the local coins produced in the Delhi region during the medieval period under the rule of these dynasties.

• These coins were primarily made of silver and were used as a medium of exchange in commerce and trade during that era.

• The Dehliwal coins played a significant role in the economic activities of northern India during the reign of the Tomars and Chauhans, symbolizing their administrative and monetary systems.

• The coins reflected regional identity and sovereignty, and their minting was a mark of local governance and control over trade.

• Tomar Dynasty: The Tomar dynasty ruled Delhi before the Chauhan dynasty and was instrumental in establishing Delhi as a center of political and economic activity.

• They are credited with founding the city of Delhi, and their rule lasted until the Chauhans took over the region in the late 12th century.

• The reign of the Tomars witnessed significant developments in trade, and their coins like Dehliwal were widely used.

• The Tomar kings were known for their administrative capabilities and contributions to local governance.

• Chauhan Dynasty: The Chauhans succeeded the Tomars in Delhi, with Prithviraj Chauhan being one of the most prominent rulers of the dynasty.

• The Chauhan dynasty played a crucial role in resisting invasions, especially from the Ghurid Empire.

• Dehliwal coins continued to be minted during their reign, reflecting the continuity of the monetary system established by the Tomars.

• Prithviraj Chauhan’s reign marked the last phase of the Chauhan dynasty before Delhi fell to Muslim rulers.

• Other Coin Types: Rupees: The term "Rupee" became prominent much later, especially during the Mughal and British periods, and does not pertain to the Tomar or Chauhan era.

• Dinar: Dinar was a gold coin used in the Middle East and parts of India, but it was not associated with the Tomars or Chauhans.

• Tanka: The Tanka was introduced by Muslim rulers after the Chauhan dynasty, particularly by the Delhi Sultanate, and was not relevant to the question.

• Significance of Dehliwal Coins: These coins were symbols of regional autonomy and governance under the Tomar and Chauhan rulers.

• The use of Dehliwal coins highlights the economic and cultural significance of Delhi during the medieval period.

• They represent the early monetary systems in India before the advent of centralized coinage by later dynasties.

• Historical Context: The Tomar and Chauhan dynasties laid the foundation for Delhi’s prominence as a political and trade hub in northern India.

• The transition from Tomar to Chauhan rule marked a continuation of local governance systems, including coin minting practices.

• Legacy: The Dehliwal coins are an important artifact of early Indian history, providing insights into the economic systems of medieval India.

• They reflect the cultural and political autonomy of Delhi before the establishment of Muslim rule in the region.

5. Temporary human settlements are usually built in which of the following regions?

A. Cold desert regions

B. Deep forested areas

C. Rugged mountain slopes

D. All of these

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is All of these. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Temporary human settlements are primarily established in regions where permanent habitation is difficult due to environmental or geographical factors. These include cold deserts, deep forests, and rugged mountain slopes.

• In cold desert regions, such as those in Ladakh or Antarctica, temporary settlements are set up for scientific research, military purposes, or seasonal activities like grazing. These regions experience extreme cold and arid conditions, making permanent habitation challenging.

• Deep forested areas often host temporary settlements for activities like logging, collection of forest produce, or tribal practices. These areas are usually dense, remote, and not conducive to permanent infrastructure development.

• Rugged mountain slopes are used for temporary settlements during trekking, mountaineering, or seasonal farming. The steep terrain and unstable conditions often prevent the establishment of permanent settlements.

• All these regions share common traits such as harsh climates, limited access to resources, and the need for temporary human presence for specific purposes. Therefore, the correct answer is All of these.

• Cold Desert Regions: Examples include Ladakh in India, Gobi Desert in China, and Antarctica.

• These regions are characterized by extreme cold, minimal vegetation, and sparse population.

• Activities like scientific research, defense operations, and seasonal grazing are common reasons for temporary settlements here.

• Deep Forested Areas: Commonly found in tropical regions such as the Amazon Rainforest, Congo Basin, and Western Ghats in India.

• Temporary shelters are created for logging, hunting, gathering forest produce, or tribal rituals.

• Challenges include dense vegetation, wildlife, and lack of basic amenities.

• Rugged Mountain Slopes: Mountain ranges like the Himalayas, Andes, and Rockies often see temporary camps for trekkers, climbers, and nomadic tribes.

• These areas are prone to landslides, avalanches, and extreme weather, which limit permanent habitation.

• Seasonal farming, such as terrace cultivation, is another key reason for temporary settlements here.

• Temporary Settlements: Examples include military camps, refugee camps, research stations, and nomadic shelters.

• These are built using lightweight, portable materials for easy dismantling and relocation.

• Temporary settlements are crucial for human activities in inhospitable environments.

• Nomadic Tribes: Nomads such as the Changpa in Ladakh and the Maasai in Africa often establish temporary settlements while herding livestock.

• Scientific Research: Temporary research stations in Antarctica, like McMurdo Station, support scientists studying climate, geology, and ecosystems.

• Seasonal Activities: Migrant workers and seasonal farmers establish temporary homes during peak agricultural or industrial seasons.

• Disaster Relief Camps: Post-disaster scenarios often require temporary settlements for displaced populations.

• The Second Schedule of the Indian Constitution deals with the provisions relating to emoluments, allowances, and privileges of constitutional officeholders.

• It covers the President, Governors, Judges of Supreme Court and High Courts, Speaker, Deputy Speaker, and members of Parliament, among others.

• The schedule specifies the salaries and allowances for these officeholders to ensure transparency and uniformity.

• The Second Schedule is pivotal for maintaining the financial independence of constitutional authorities.

• Schedules in Indian Constitution: The Indian Constitution initially had 8 schedules, which have now increased to 12.

• Schedules are used to provide detailed information on various provisions like administration, representation, and governance.

• Second Schedule Details: It ensures constitutional officeholders receive appropriate remuneration, promoting independence and impartiality.

• Officeholders like the President and Governors are barred from earning salaries from other sources during their tenure.

• Related Articles: Articles 59 and 158 restrict the President and Governors from holding any other profit-making positions.

• Articles 125 and 221 deal with the salaries of Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts, respectively.

• Amendments: The Second Schedule can be amended by Parliament to adjust emoluments as per changing economic and administrative needs.

• Such amendments help maintain the financial viability of these provisions.

7. When is a 'Vote on Account' necessary?

A. To introduce new taxation policies

B. To ensure continuity of government operations until the budget is passed

C. To reduce fiscal deficit

D. To approve supplementary grants

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is To ensure continuity of government operations until the budget is passed. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Article 116 of the Constitution of India provides for a Vote on Account, which is a grant in advance made by the Parliament to the Executive.

• The primary purpose of a Vote on Account is to ensure the continuity of government operations. Since the financial year begins on April 1st and the Budget (the Annual Financial Statement) usually takes a few months to be debated and passed, the government needs funds to cover its essential expenditure during this transition period. Hence, the statement is correct.

• It serves as a temporary authorization to withdraw money from the Consolidated Fund of India. Without this, the government would legally be unable to spend money after March 31st, leading to a shutdown of public services.

• A Vote on Account does not deal with new taxation policies. Changes in taxes are proposed through the Finance Bill as part of the regular budget process. Hence, statement 1 is incorrect.

• It is not a tool used to reduce fiscal deficit. Fiscal deficit management involves long-term policy decisions, expenditure control, and revenue generation strategies, often guided by the FRBM Act, 2003. Hence, statement 3 is incorrect.

• Supplementary grants are governed by Article 115 and are sought when the amount authorized for a specific service in the Appropriation Act for the current financial year is found to be insufficient. Hence, statement 4 is incorrect.

• Duration and Amount: By convention, a Vote on Account is usually passed for a period of two months. The amount granted is typically one-sixth (1/6th) of the total estimated expenditure presented in the Budget for the entire financial year.

• Interim Budget vs. Vote on Account: While a Vote on Account only deals with the expenditure side of the government's ledger, an Interim Budget (presented during an election year) includes both expenditure and receipts (revenue) estimates.

• Consolidated Fund of India: Defined under Article 266, this fund contains all revenues received by the government, loans raised, and money received in repayment of loans. No amount can be withdrawn from it without an Appropriation Act passed by the Parliament.

• Appropriation Bill: Under Article 114, the Appropriation Bill is introduced in the Lok Sabha after the demands for grants are voted upon. It gives legal authority to the government to withdraw funds from the Consolidated Fund.

• Shift in Budget Date: Since 2017, the Union Budget has been presented on February 1st instead of the last day of February. This change was implemented to complete the legislative process before the start of the new financial year on April 1st, thereby minimizing the need for a Vote on Account in normal years.

• Exclusive Power of Lok Sabha: The Vote on Account is a special provision where the Lok Sabha can pass the grant without a full-scale discussion, ensuring that the administrative machinery does not come to a halt.

• No Policy Changes: It is a formal convention that the government does not announce major policy changes or new schemes during a Vote on Account or an Interim Budget, leaving such decisions to the new government formed after elections.

• Constitutional Requirement: Article 265 states that no tax shall be levied or collected except by authority of law, and Article 266 ensures Parliamentary control over expenditure, making the Vote on Account a vital constitutional mechanism for fiscal accountability.

8. How did licensing policy under the planning era affect the emergence of large industrial conglomerates?

A. It increased FDI inflow

B. It prevented market dominance

C. It allowed firms to block competitors by hoarding licenses

D. It enhanced rural employment

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is It allowed firms to block competitors by hoarding licenses. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Licensing Policy under the Planning Era: During India's planning era, particularly after independence, the government introduced an extensive licensing system to regulate industrial growth.

• Objective: The primary aim of the licensing system was to ensure balanced regional development, prevent monopolies, and encourage equitable resource allocation. However, it had unintended consequences.

• Blocking Competitors: Large industrial conglomerates often misused the licensing system by hoarding licenses. By acquiring more licenses than they could effectively utilize, they blocked potential competitors from entering the market or expanding their operations. Hence, the statement in the question is correct.

• Market Control: This practice allowed these conglomerates to establish market dominance and control critical industries, leading to reduced competition and inefficiencies in the economy.

• Impact on Innovation: The lack of competition discouraged innovation and technological advancement, as firms faced minimal pressure to improve their products or processes.

• Industrial Licensing Policy: Introduced under the Industrial Policy Resolution of 1956, this policy categorized industries into three schedules: those exclusively reserved for the state, those where the state could establish new units, and those open to private participation.

• The licensing policy aimed to regulate private sector growth and align industrial development with national priorities.

• Industries requiring significant capital or impacting public interest were subjected to licensing requirements.

• Consequences of Licensing Policy: Creation of Monopolies: Large firms acquired multiple licenses, which allowed them to dominate markets and prevent the entry of smaller players.

• Red Tape: The licensing system became synonymous with bureaucratic delays and corruption, discouraging entrepreneurship.

• Economic Inefficiencies: Resources were often allocated inefficiently due to the artificial constraints imposed by licensing regulations.

• Economic Reforms of 1991: To address the inefficiencies caused by the licensing system, the government introduced Liberalization, Privatization, and Globalization (LPG) reforms in 1991.

• These reforms significantly reduced the scope of industrial licensing and encouraged competition and foreign investment.

• As a result, India transitioned to a more market-oriented economy with greater emphasis on efficiency and innovation.

• Key Characteristics of Licensing System: It required government approval for establishing or expanding industrial units.

• Priorities were given to public sector undertakings and industries in backward areas.

• The system aimed to align industrial growth with the Five-Year Plans and national objectives.

• Examples of Hoarding Licenses: Large business houses like Tata, Birla, and Dalmia were known to hoard licenses to secure their market positions.

• This practice was criticized for stifling competition and innovation in critical sectors like steel, cement, and textiles.

• Legacy of the Licensing Era: The inefficiencies and distortions caused by the licensing system highlighted the need for economic reforms.

• The reforms of 1991 marked a shift towards a more liberalized and competitive economic environment, reducing the influence of the licensing system.

9. Which branch of geology focuses on the study of fossils and ancient life forms?

A. Petrology

B. Mineralogy

C. Palaeontology

D. Geochronology

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Palaeontology. Key Points Additional Information

• Palaeontology is the branch of geology dedicated to the study of fossils and ancient life forms. It involves examining the remains of plants, animals, and other organisms that lived millions of years ago.

• The primary focus of palaeontology is to understand the evolutionary history of life on Earth by studying fossils.

• Fossils provide valuable information about the past, including the structure, behavior, and interactions of ancient organisms, as well as the environmental conditions in which they lived.

• Palaeontology plays a significant role in determining the age of rocks, correlating rock layers across regions, and reconstructing past ecosystems.

• Modern palaeontological studies use advanced techniques like radiometric dating to determine the age of fossils and molecular methods to analyze ancient DNA.

• Examples of famous fossils studied in palaeontology include the Archaeopteryx (a transitional fossil between dinosaurs and birds) and the Tyrannosaurus rex.

• Palaeontology is interdisciplinary, combining elements of biology, geology, and environmental science to provide a comprehensive understanding of Earth's biological past.

• Other Branches of Geology: Petrology: The study of the origin, composition, and structure of rocks. It involves examining igneous, metamorphic, and sedimentary rocks to understand geological processes.

• Mineralogy: The study of minerals, their chemical composition, properties, and crystal structures. It helps in identifying and classifying minerals found in the Earth's crust.

• Geochronology: The science of determining the age of rocks, fossils, and sediments using dating methods such as radiometric dating.

• Applications of Palaeontology: Reconstruction of Earth's geological history and environmental changes over time.

• Understanding the process of evolution and the development of life forms.

• Providing insights into mass extinction events, such as the extinction of the dinosaurs.

• Helping in the exploration of oil and gas reserves by identifying fossilized microorganisms in rock layers.

• Important Points: The word "palaeontology" is derived from the Greek words "palaios" (ancient), "ontos" (being), and "logos" (study).

• Fossils can be classified into two main types: body fossils (remains of an organism's body) and trace fossils (evidence of an organism's activities, such as footprints or burrows).

• Famous palaeontologists include Mary Anning, who discovered important Jurassic fossils, and Charles Darwin, whose work on evolution was influenced by fossil studies.

21. The rolling plan concept was introduced during which Prime Minister's tenure?

B. Indira Gandhi

C. Morarji Desai

D. Rajiv Gandhi

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Morarji Desai. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• The concept of a Rolling Plan was introduced during the tenure of Morarji Desai, who served as the Prime Minister of India from 1977 to 1979.

• A Rolling Plan implies that the plan would be dynamic and could be revised annually based on the country's economic performance and changing needs.

• This approach was introduced as an alternative to the traditional Five-Year Plans, emphasizing flexibility in planning.

• During Morarji Desai's tenure, the Janata Party government discarded the Fifth Five-Year Plan (1974–79) introduced by the previous government under Indira Gandhi.

• The Rolling Plan was implemented for the years 1978–1980, but it was discontinued after Indira Gandhi returned to power in 1980 and resumed the Five-Year Planning model.

• The Rolling Plan was considered innovative as it focused on short-term adjustments rather than rigid long-term strategies, ensuring better responsiveness to economic fluctuations.

• Five-Year Plans in India: The concept of Five-Year Plans was introduced by the Planning Commission of India in 1951, inspired by the Soviet model.

• India's first Five-Year Plan (1951–56) focused on agriculture, irrigation, and community development.

• Subsequent Five-Year Plans addressed various sectors, including industry, infrastructure, education, and health.

• The Planning Commission was replaced by NITI Aayog in 2015 under the Narendra Modi government, ending the era of Five-Year Plans.

• Morarji Desai: Morarji Desai was India’s fourth Prime Minister and the first non-Congress leader to hold the position.

• He served as Prime Minister from March 1977 to July 1979 during the Janata Party government.

• His tenure emphasized transparency, anti-corruption, and economic reforms.

• He was awarded the Bharat Ratna in 1991 for his service to the nation.

• Concept of Rolling Plan: A Rolling Plan is a continuous plan that is updated annually or periodically to reflect changes in economic conditions and priorities.

• It provides flexibility and adaptability, allowing policymakers to revise objectives and allocations in response to current developments.

• This approach contrasts with fixed long-term plans, which may become outdated if circumstances change.

• Indira Gandhi’s Return to Five-Year Plans: When Indira Gandhi returned to power in 1980, she reinstated the Five-Year Plan model, starting with the Sixth Five-Year Plan (1980–85).

• Her approach focused on long-term planning to achieve sustained economic growth and development.

• Advantages of Rolling Plans: Provides flexibility to adjust to unforeseen economic changes.

• Allows for more accurate forecasting and resource allocation.

• Focuses on short-term goals while maintaining long-term objectives.

• Disadvantages of Rolling Plans: Frequent revisions can lead to administrative complications.

• Lack of long-term vision may hinder sustained development.

• Uncertainty in planning could impact investor confidence.

• The Rolling Plan concept was eventually abandoned due to its challenges and inefficiencies compared to structured Five-Year Plans.

• The three-point arc is a marked area on the court, and its distance varies depending on the league. For example, in the NBA, the arc is located at 23 feet 9 inches from the basket at the top of the key.

• Players often practice extensively to develop their ability to make three-point shots, as it requires precision, strength, and technique. It is considered a high-risk, high-reward shot. Hence, the correct answer is Three-pointer.

• History: The three-point shot was introduced to professional basketball in the 1960s and became prominent in the NBA during the 1979-1980 season. It revolutionized the way the game is played, encouraging teams to space the floor and rely on outside shooting.

• The shot has evolved into a critical component of strategy, with teams and players relying heavily on three-point shooting to maximize scoring opportunities. Hence, the correct answer is Three-pointer.

• Layup: A close-range shot performed by laying the ball up near the basket, often using the backboard for guidance. Layups are typically worth two points.
