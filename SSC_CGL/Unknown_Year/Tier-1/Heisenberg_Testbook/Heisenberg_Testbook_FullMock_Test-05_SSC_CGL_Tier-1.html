1. A sum of money becomes Rs. 6,400 in 3 years and Rs. 8,100 in 5 years on compound interest compounded annually. Find the rate of interest per annum.

A. 12 (3/8) \%

B. 12 (4/8) \%

C. 12 (6/8) \%

D. 12 (5/8) \%

Answer B

Solution

Given: Amount after 3 years = Rs. 6,400. Amount after 5 years = Rs. 8,100. Formula Used: Amount = Principal × (1 + Rate/100)Time Compound Interest for n years = Amount - Principal Calculation: Let the principal be P and the rate be R% per annum. ⇒ P × (1 + R/100)3 = 6,400 ⇒ P × (1 + R/100)5 = 8,100 Dividing the second equation by the first, we get: ⇒ (1 + R/100)5 / (1 + R/100)3 = 8,100 / 6,400 ⇒ (1 + R/100)2 = 8,100 / 6,400 ⇒ (1 + R/100)2 = 81/64 ⇒ 1 + R/100 = √(81/64) ⇒ 1 + R/100 = 9/8 ⇒ R/100 = 1/8 ⇒ R = 12.5% ⇒ R = 12 (4/8) % The rate of interest per annum is 12 (4/8) %. 16. Floor of a room measures 4.5 metres × 3 metres. Find the minimum number of complete square marble slabs of equal size required to cover the entire floor.

A. 4

B. 6

C. 5

D. 3

Answer B

Solution

Shortcut Trick To find the minimum number of square slabs, we need the maximum possible size for each slab. Side of the largest square slab = HCF(4.5 m, 3 m) = 1.5 m Number of slabs = (Length ÷ Side) × (Breadth ÷ Side) ⇒ Total slabs = (4.5 ÷ 1.5) × (3 ÷ 1.5) = 3 × 2 = 6 ∴ The correct answer is 6 slabs. Alternate Method Given: Length of the floor (L) = 4.5 m = 450 cm Breadth of the floor (B) = 3 m = 300 cm Concept Used: To find the minimum number of square slabs, the area of each square slab must be at its maximum. The maximum side of a square slab that can perfectly fit the dimensions is the Highest Common Factor (HCF) of the floor's length and breadth. Calculation: ⇒ Side of the maximum square slab = HCF(450 cm, 300 cm) ⇒ HCF of 450 and 300 = 150 cm = 1.5 m ⇒ Area of the floor = Length × Breadth = 4.5 m × 3 m = 13.5 m2 ⇒ Area of one square slab = Side × Side = 1.5 m × 1.5 m = 2.25 m2 ⇒ Minimum number of slabs required = Area of the floor ÷ Area of one square slab ⇒ Number of slabs = 13.5 ÷ 2.25 = 6 ∴ The correct answer is 6 slabs. Greatest Possible Length When asked to find the greatest possible length that can be used to measure exactly two or more given lengths, always find the Highest Common Factor (HCF) of those dimensions.
