33. The unemployment arising from the mismatch between the jobs available in the market and the skills of the available workers in the market is called _________.

A. Disguised

B. Structural

C. Cyclical

D. Seasonal

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Structural. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• The term Structural Unemployment refers to unemployment caused by a mismatch between the skills of workers and the requirements of available jobs. It is a significant type of unemployment that occurs due to changes in technology, globalization, or other shifts in the economy.

• It arises when workers do not have the required skills to adapt to new job demands, or when certain industries decline while others grow. This mismatch often leads to prolonged joblessness for affected workers.

• For instance, a factory worker who loses their job due to automation may struggle to find new employment if they lack the skills needed for jobs in emerging industries such as technology or services.

• Structural unemployment is often considered a long-term issue because it requires workers to retrain or relocate to find new employment opportunities.

• This form of unemployment is different from other types, such as cyclical unemployment, which is caused by economic downturns, or seasonal unemployment, which occurs during specific times of the year. Hence, the correct answer is Structural.

• Types of Unemployment: Disguised Unemployment: This occurs when more people are employed than are needed for a task, leading to reduced productivity. It is common in agricultural sectors of developing countries.

• Cyclical Unemployment: This is caused by economic downturns and is temporary. It occurs due to lower demand for goods and services during recessions.

• Seasonal Unemployment: This occurs in industries with seasonal demand, like agriculture or tourism. Workers are employed only during specific seasons.

• Frictional Unemployment: This is the short-term unemployment that happens when workers are transitioning between jobs or entering the workforce.

• Structural Unemployment: Caused by technological advancements or shifts in the economy, leading to a skills mismatch.

• Examples of Structural Unemployment: A coal miner losing their job due to the decline of the coal industry and lacking the skills to work in renewable energy.

• A typist becoming unemployed due to the widespread use of word-processing software and computers.

• Solutions to Structural Unemployment: Governments and organizations can invest in education and skill development programs to help workers adapt to new job requirements.

• Relocation assistance can be provided to workers to move to areas with better job opportunities.

• Encouraging industries to create jobs that match the existing workforce's skills can also help reduce structural unemployment.

• Structural Unemployment vs. Cyclical Unemployment: Structural unemployment is long-term and arises from fundamental changes in the economy, such as automation or globalization.

• Cyclical unemployment is short-term and related to economic cycles, such as recessions and booms.

• Impact of Structural Unemployment: It can lead to income inequality as some workers are left behind in declining industries.

• It can also cause regional disparities if certain areas are disproportionately affected by economic changes.

• Addressing Structural Unemployment Globally: Countries like Germany have implemented robust apprenticeship programs to reduce structural unemployment by providing skills aligned with industry needs.

• The United States has focused on community colleges and vocational training to address skill mismatches.
