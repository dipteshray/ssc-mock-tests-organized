1. In the question given below, a statement is followed by two assumptions numbered I and II. You have to consider the statement and decide which of the assumptions is implicit in the statement. Statement: Children should not play games on mobile phone for the whole day. Assumption: I. It can result in digital eyestrain. II. It has bad effect on study.

A. If only assumption I is implicit

B. If only assumption II is implicit

C. If neither I or II is implicit

D. If both I and II are implicit

Answer D

Solution

Given: Statement: Children should not play games on mobile phone for the whole day. Assumptions: I. It can result in digital eyestrain - True (Because advising children not to play mobile games for the whole day implies that prolonged screen time may have harmful effects, such as digital eyestrain.) II. It has bad effect on study - True (Because the advice also assumes that spending the whole day playing mobile games can negatively affect children's studies by reducing the time and attention devoted to learning.) So, both Assumptions I and II are implicit. Hence, the correct answer is "Both Assumptions I and II are implicit."

20. Read the following statement and answer. Which of the following arguments is stronger? Statement: Should candidates be barred from contesting elections if criminal charges are pending against them in a court of law? Argument: 1. Yes, these people should not be allowed to contest elections, because they have already violated the law, if they are elected then the violations will continue at a fast pace. 2. No, they are authorized to contest elections by the respective political parties.

A. Only argument 2 is strong.

B. Only argument 1 is strong.

C. Both argument 1 and argument 2 are strong.

D. Neither argument 1 nor argument 2 is strong.

Answer D

Solution

Statement: Should candidates be barred from contesting elections if criminal charges are pending against them in a court of law? Argument: Argument 1 is weak because it violates the legal principle of "innocent until proven guilty." Since the statement specifies the charges are only "pending," assuming they have "already violated the law" is a logical fallacy. Furthermore, claiming violations will continue at a "fast pace" is speculative, not a factual consequence. Argument 2 is weak because it is circular and irrelevant to the ethical/legal question. The fact that political parties authorize them does not address whether they should be barred by law; it merely describes the current situation rather than providing a strong reason for or against the change. Hence, "Neither argument 1 nor argument 2 is strong".
