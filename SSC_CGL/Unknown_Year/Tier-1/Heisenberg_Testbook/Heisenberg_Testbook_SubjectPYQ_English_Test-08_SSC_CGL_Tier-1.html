1. Choose the option which is similar in meaning to the underlined word. Columbus was convinced that the earth was round.

A. Assured

B. Cynical

C. Skeptical

D. Doubtful

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is '1' i.e. Assured . Key Points Correct sentence: Columbus was convinced that the earth was round, meaning he was assured of this fact. Additional Information

• The word "convinced" in the sentence means being certain or assured about something.

• Option 1, "Assured," is the correct synonym as it conveys the same meaning of being confident or sure.

• Option 2, "Cynical," means being distrustful or skeptical of others' motives, which is the opposite of the intended meaning.

• Option 3, "Skeptical," means having doubts or reservations, which is contrary to the word "convinced."

• Option 4, "Doubtful," also refers to uncertainty, which is not similar to the meaning of "convinced."

• Synonyms for "convinced": Assured, confident, certain.

• Antonyms for "convinced": Doubtful, skeptical, uncertain.

• Understanding the context of the sentence is crucial for identifying the correct synonym.

2. Change the following from active to passive: The storm damaged many houses.

A. Many houses are damaged by the storm.

B. Many houses had been damaged by the storm.

C. Many houses were damaged by the storm.

D. Many houses have been damaged by the storm.

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Option 3. Key Points Correct sentence: Many houses were damaged by the storm. Additional Information

• The given sentence is in the simple past tense (“damaged”).

• In passive voice, the structure for simple past is Object + was/were + past participle + by + subject.

• The object “many houses” becomes the subject in the passive voice.

• Since “houses” is plural, we use “were damaged”.

• Option 1: “are damaged” – Incorrect tense (simple present).

• Option 2: “had been damaged” – Past perfect tense, not matching the original sentence.

• Option 4: “have been damaged” – Present perfect tense, not suitable here.

17. A sentence is provided in indirect speech. From the four given options, choose the one that most accurately conveys the sentence in its corresponding direct speech. She said that she would call me later.

A. She said, "I will call you later."

B. She said, "I shall call you late."

C. She said, "I must call you late."

D. She said, "She would call me later."

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Option 1. Key Points Therefore, the correct answer is Option 1. Correct Sentence: “She said, "I will call you later."” Additional Information

• Indirect Speech: She said that she would call me later.

• Key Conversions: Pronoun Change: The third-person pronoun "she" (the subject of the reported speech) refers to the speaker. In direct speech, it changes back to the first-person pronoun "I".

• The first-person pronoun "me" (the object) refers to the person being spoken to. In direct speech, it changes back to "you".

• Modal/Tense Change: The modal "would" in indirect speech is the past form of "will". To revert to direct speech, "would" changes back to "will".

• Removal of Conjunction: The conjunction "that", which connects the reporting verb and the reported speech in indirect form, is removed and replaced by a comma and quotation marks.

• Punctuation and Format: The spoken words are enclosed in quotation marks.

• The first letter of the direct speech ("I") is capitalized.

• A comma separates the reporting clause from the quoted speech.

• Option 2: "She said, 'I shall call you late.'" Incorrect because it changes the word "later" (comparative adverb) to "late" (adjective/adverb), which significantly alters the meaning of the original sentence.

• Option 3: "She said, 'I must call you late.'" Incorrect because it uses the modal "must" (obligation) instead of "will" (future intent) and also changes "later" to "late."

• Option 4: "She said, 'She would call me later.'" Incorrect because it fails to convert the third-person pronouns ("She") back to first-person ("I") and retains the indirect modal form ("would").
