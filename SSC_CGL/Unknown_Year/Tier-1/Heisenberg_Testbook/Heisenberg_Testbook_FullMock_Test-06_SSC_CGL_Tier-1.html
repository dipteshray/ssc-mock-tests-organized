32. Which folk instrument is made from a dried gourd and played by snake charmers (Kalbeliyas)?

A. Pungi

B. Khanjari

C. Morchang

D. Kamayacha

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Pungi. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• The Pungi, also known as the Been, is a traditional wind instrument primarily used by snake charmers in India, particularly the Kalbeliya community of Rajasthan.

• It is constructed using a dried bottle gourd (calabash) which serves as the air reservoir and resonator. This gourd is hollowed out to allow air to pass through to the pipes. Hence, the statement regarding its composition from a dried gourd is correct.

• The instrument typically consists of two bamboo pipes inserted into the lower end of the gourd. One pipe, known as the Jivala, has finger holes to play the melody, while the other pipe provides a constant drone sound.

• It is played using a technique called circular breathing, where the player inhales through the nose while simultaneously pushing air out from the mouth into the instrument to maintain a continuous, uninterrupted sound.

• The Pungi is the central musical element of the Kalbeliya folk dance, where the movements of the dancers often mimic the fluid, swaying motions of a cobra.

• The Kalbeliya Tribe and Dance: The Kalbeliya are a nomadic tribe traditionally known as snake charmers and catchers. Their dance form was recognized by UNESCO and included in the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in the year 2010.

• Apart from the Pungi, they use other instruments like the Dufli, Khanjari, and Dholak to create rhythmic accompaniment for their high-energy performances.

• Khanjari: The Khanjari is a percussion instrument similar to a small tambourine. It consists of a circular wooden frame covered with a single drumhead made of goat skin.

• It is widely used in devotional music (Bhajans) and folk songs across India. Unlike the Pungi, it does not use a gourd and is not a wind instrument.

• Morchang: The Morchang is a jaw harp or mouth harp made of iron or brass. It is categorized as an idiophone.

• The player places the instrument between their teeth and plucks a metal tongue with their finger while using the mouth cavity as a resonator to change the pitch and timbre. It is a popular rhythmic instrument in Rajasthani folk music and Carnatic music (where it is called Morsing).

• Kamayacha: The Kamayacha is a sophisticated bowed string instrument unique to the Manganiyar community of Western Rajasthan.

• It is carved from a single piece of mango wood and features a large circular resonator covered with parchment (goat skin). It typically has three main strings made of gut and several sympathetic strings made of steel, producing a deep, haunting melody.

• The Pungi was famously banned by Mughal Emperor Aurangzeb in the royal residence because of its shrill and unpleasant sound. This ban reportedly led to the evolution of the Shehnai, which was an improved version of the Pungi.

• In snake charming, the snake does not actually "hear" the music (as snakes lack external ears) but reacts to the vibrations and the visual movement of the instrument.

• The Pungi remains an iconic symbol of Indian folk music, though its use has declined in urban areas due to strict wildlife protection laws (such as the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972) that prohibit the catching and keeping of snakes.

• When light hits the oil film, some of it reflects off the upper surface and some reflects off the lower surface (the interface between oil and water).

• The two reflected waves travel different distances; this difference is known as the optical path difference.

• Angle of Incidence: The color observed changes depending on the viewing angle because the path the light takes through the film varies.

• Refractive Index: The ratio of the speed of light in a vacuum to its speed in the medium (oil) determines how much the light bends and shifts phase.

• It is included in both GDP and National Income as it reflects income generated from ownership of property.

• GDP at market price focuses only on the income generated within the geographical boundaries of a country.
