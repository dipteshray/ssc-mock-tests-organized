6. Which of the following statements is correct about the relationship between total utility and marginal utility? I. MU (marginal utility) is the rate of change of TU (total utility). II. Total utility is maximum when marginal utility is zero.

A. Neither I nor II

B. Only I

C. Both I and II

D. Only II

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Option 3 (Both I and II). Key Points Additional Information

• Marginal Utility (MU) is defined as the rate of change of Total Utility (TU) with respect to the consumption of an additional unit of a good or service. Mathematically, MU = ΔTU/ΔQ.

• Total Utility reaches its maximum point when Marginal Utility becomes zero, indicating the consumer has reached the point of saturation.

• When MU is positive, TU increases; when MU is negative, TU decreases. Thus, MU is directly related to the slope of TU.

• Both statements I and II are correct as they define the relationship between MU and TU based on standard economic principles of utility theory.

• The concept of utility is central to consumer behavior and decision-making, explaining how individuals derive satisfaction from goods and services.

• Utility: It is the satisfaction or pleasure derived from consuming goods and services. Utility is measured in hypothetical units called "utils."

• Total Utility (TU): The cumulative satisfaction obtained from consuming a certain quantity of goods or services.

• Marginal Utility (MU): The additional satisfaction gained from consuming an extra unit of a good or service.

• Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility: As more units of a good are consumed, the additional utility derived from each successive unit tends to decrease.

• The point where MU = 0 is known as the point of maximum utility, beyond which any additional consumption reduces overall satisfaction.

7. Arrange the following belts of the Great Indian Plains from north to south: 1. Khadar 2. Tarai 3. Bhabar 4. Bhangar

A. 3→2→4→1

B. 2→3→4→1

C. 3→2→1→4

D. 4→3→2→1

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is 3 → 2 → 4 → 1. Key Points Additional Information

• The Great Indian Plains are divided into four belts based on their geographical and soil characteristics: Bhabar, Tarai, Bhangar, and Khadar.

• Bhabar: It lies at the foothills of the Himalayas and is characterized by porous and coarse sediments deposited by rivers. It is the northernmost belt.

• Tarai: Found south of the Bhabar, this is a marshy and swampy region with fine silt deposits and high groundwater levels.

• Bhangar: South of the Tarai, this region represents older alluvium with slightly elevated land and less fertile soil compared to the Khadar.

• Khadar: The southernmost belt, consisting of newer alluvium deposited by rivers, is highly fertile and ideal for cultivation.

• The correct north-to-south sequence of these belts is 3 (Bhabar) → 2 (Tarai) → 4 (Bhangar) → 1 (Khadar).

• Bhabar: Located along the foothills of the Himalayas, it is a narrow belt with coarse and porous soil.

• The streams in this region disappear into the ground due to the high porosity of the soil, leading to the formation of underground water channels.

• This region is not suitable for agriculture due to its rocky terrain and lack of water availability on the surface.

• Tarai: This is a swampy and marshy region located south of the Bhabar belt.

• It has a high water table, making it suitable for the growth of dense forests and agriculture (especially sugarcane cultivation).

• However, the region is prone to waterlogging due to excessive groundwater and poor drainage.

• Bhangar: Bhangar is composed of older alluvium (deposited during ancient times) and forms slightly elevated terraces.

• The soil in this region is less fertile compared to Khadar, as it contains kankar (calcareous concretions).

• It is generally used for the cultivation of less water-intensive crops.

• Khadar: Khadar is the younger alluvium deposited by rivers during their seasonal floods.

• The soil is highly fertile and supports the cultivation of crops such as rice, wheat, and sugarcane.

• It is located closest to the river channels and is subject to frequent flooding, leading to the replenishment of nutrients in the soil.

19. UNFCC is an international organization that focuses on reducing global warming. What does UNFCC stand for?

A. United Nations Fund to Conserve and Combat the Change

B. United Nations Framework to Combat with the Climate Change

C. United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change

D. United Nations Fund to Combat the Climate Change

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change. Key Points Additional Information

• The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) is an international environmental treaty adopted at the Earth Summit in Rio de Janeiro in 1992.

• Its primary objective is to stabilize greenhouse gas concentrations in the atmosphere at a level that would prevent dangerous anthropogenic interference with the climate system.

• UNFCCC serves as the parent treaty for key agreements like the Kyoto Protocol (1997) and the Paris Agreement (2015), which aim to reduce global carbon emissions and combat climate change.

• The treaty has near-universal membership, with 198 parties (countries) as of October 2023, reflecting a global commitment to addressing climate change.

• The UNFCCC framework supports negotiations for legally binding limits on greenhouse gas emissions and promotes transparency and accountability through the submission of national reports by member states.

• The UNFCCC organizes annual Conferences of the Parties (COP), where member nations meet to assess progress and negotiate further commitments. For instance, COP26 was held in Glasgow in 2021.

• The framework promotes the principle of “common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities” (CBDR-RC), acknowledging the varying contributions and capabilities of countries in addressing climate change.

• It encourages the development and transfer of technologies to reduce emissions and adapt to climate impacts.

• Key mechanisms under the UNFCCC include the Clean Development Mechanism (CDM) and Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs), which outline individual countries' climate targets.

When the orders of the Delimitation Commission are placed before the Lok Sabha or the State Legislative Assembly, then no amendment can be made in these orders. Which of the above statements is/are correct?

A. Both I and II

B. Only II

C. Neither I nor II

D. Only I

Answer A

Solution

The Correct answer is Both I and II. Key Points Additional Information

• The Delimitation Commission is established by the Government of India under the provisions of the Delimitation Commission Act.

• It is responsible for redrawing the boundaries of the Lok Sabha and State Assembly constituencies based on the latest census.

• The orders issued by the Delimitation Commission are considered final and cannot be challenged in any court of law, ensuring its authority.

• Once the orders are placed before the Lok Sabha or State Legislative Assembly, no amendments or modifications can be made to them, ensuring their binding nature.

• The Commission is an independent body, and its decisions aim to ensure fairness in the representation of constituencies based on population.

• The Commission includes a chairperson, usually a retired judge of the Supreme Court, and other members such as the Chief Election Commissioner and representatives of the concerned states.

• The process is vital for maintaining the principle of equal representation as prescribed by the Constitution of India.

• Delimitation Commission Act This Act provides the legal framework for the functioning of the Delimitation Commission.

• The purpose is to ensure that constituencies are redrawn periodically to reflect changes in population and demographics based on the latest census data.

• It ensures that each constituency has equal representation to maintain the democratic process.

• Role of the Chief Election Commissioner The Chief Election Commissioner is an integral part of the Delimitation Commission.

• They provide expertise and ensure that the process aligns with the electoral guidelines.

• They help uphold the principles of free and fair elections.

B. 3, 2, 1, 4

C. 2, 1, 3, 4

D. 2, 1, 4, 3

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is 2, 3, 1, 4. Key Points Additional Information

• Great Stupa at Sanchi (2): Constructed during the reign of Emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE.

• One of the oldest stone structures in India, originally commissioned to enshrine relics of the Buddha.

• Located in the state of Madhya Pradesh, it serves as a classic example of Buddhist architecture.

• Elephanta Caves (3): Created between the 5th and 8th centuries CE.

• The caves are known for their rock-cut sculptures and are dedicated to the Hindu god Shiva.

• Located on Elephanta Island near Mumbai, Maharashtra.

• Brihadeshwara Temple (1): Completed in the early 11th century CE during the reign of Raja Raja Chola I (985-1014 CE).

• A masterpiece of Dravidian architecture located in Thanjavur, Tamil Nadu.

• Also known as Rajarajeswaram or Peruvudaiyar Kovil, it is part of the UNESCO World Heritage site "Great Living Chola Temples."

• Sun Temple at Konark (4): Built in the 13th century CE by King Narasimhadeva I of the Eastern Ganga Dynasty.

• Located in Konark, Odisha, the temple is designed in the shape of a colossal chariot with intricately carved stone wheels, pillars, and walls.

• The famous Konark Sun Temple in Odisha, India, is also called the "Black Pagoda" due to its construction of black granite

• This architectural marvel is dedicated to the Hindu Sun God, Surya.

• It was later enlarged and faced with stone, and the enclosed circumambulatory path was added.

24. Which is a serious contagious bacterial infection that usually affects the mucous membranes of the nose and throat.

A. Meningococcal

B. Diphtheria

C. Shigellosis

D. Chlamydia

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Diphtheria Key Points

• Diphtheria is a serious bacterial infection affecting the mucous membranes of the nose and throat.

• Widespread vaccination has made diphtheria extremely rare in developed countries, but it remains a concern in areas with limited healthcare and vaccination access.

• It can be treated with medication, but advanced stages may cause heart, kidney, and nervous system damage.

• Symptoms appear 2 to 5 days after infection and include a thick gray membrane on the throat, sore throat, swollen neck glands, difficulty breathing, nasal discharge, fever, and tiredness.

• Some people may carry and spread the infection without showing symptoms, known as carriers.

• Cutaneous diphtheria affects the skin, causing ulcers, redness, and swelling, and is more common in tropical regions or crowded, unhygienic areas.

• Diphtheria is caused by Corynebacterium diphtheriae and spreads through airborne droplets or contaminated personal items.

• Risk factors include unvaccinated individuals, crowded or unsanitary living conditions, and travel to areas with low vaccination rates.

• Diphtheria is rare in vaccinated regions but remains a threat to unvaccinated people who travel or have contact with those from areas with high infection rates
