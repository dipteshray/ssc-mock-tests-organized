1. What is the formula for calculating the momentum of an object? (p = momentum, m = mass, a = acceleration, v = velocity)

A. p = (m/a)

B. p = ma

C. p = mv

D. p = (m/v)

Answer C

Solution

Momentum (p) is calculated using the formula p = mv, where m is mass and v is velocity. It is a vector quantity with both magnitude and direction, proportional to mass and velocity. The unit of momentum is kg·m/s, derived from Newton's Second Law of Motion.

2. What is the major reason for the shrinking of the Himalayan glaciers?

A. Earthquakes

B. Acid rain

C. Global warming

D. Human settlement

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Global warming. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Global warming is identified as the primary driver behind the rapid shrinking and retreat of Himalayan glaciers. This phenomenon refers to the long-term increase in the average surface temperature of the Earth due to human activities. Hence, the statement is correct.

• The Himalayas, known as the Third Pole, hold the largest concentration of frozen water outside the North and South Poles. These glaciers are highly sensitive to climatic fluctuations.

• The primary mechanism of shrinking involves the negative mass balance of glaciers, where the rate of ablation (melting and evaporation) exceeds the rate of accumulation (snowfall) due to rising atmospheric temperatures.

• The accumulation of Greenhouse Gases (GHGs) such as Carbon Dioxide (CO2), Methane (CH4), and Nitrous Oxide (N2O) traps solar radiation within the Earth's atmosphere, creating a warming effect that accelerates the melting of alpine glaciers.

• According to various scientific assessments, the Himalayan region is warming at a rate higher than the global average, leading to the formation of supraglacial lakes and increased risks of Glacial Lake Outburst Floods (GLOFs).

• Black Carbon: Apart from general global warming, the deposition of Black Carbon (soot) on glacier surfaces significantly contributes to melting. Produced by the incomplete combustion of fossil fuels and biomass, these dark particles reduce the albedo effect (reflectivity) of snow, causing it to absorb more solar energy.

• ICIMOD Reports: The International Centre for Integrated Mountain Development (ICIMOD) released a landmark report stating that even if global warming is capped at 1.5 degrees Celsius, at least one-third of the Himalayan glaciers will vanish by the year 2100.

• River Systems: The shrinking of these glaciers directly impacts perennial river systems like the Ganga, Indus, and Brahmaputra. These rivers provide water security to nearly 1.9 billion people across India, China, Pakistan, Nepal, and Bhutan.

• The Gangotri Glacier: Located in the Uttarkashi district of Uttarakhand, it is one of the largest glaciers in the Himalayas. It has been retreating at a rate of approximately 10 to 30 meters per year over the last few decades.

• Impact on Ecosystems: The loss of glacial volume alters the hydrological cycle, leading to unpredictable seasonal water flow, which adversely affects agriculture, hydroelectric power generation, and biodiversity in the Hindu Kush Himalayan (HKH) region.

• Paris Agreement (2015): An international treaty aimed at limiting global warming to well below 2 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels is crucial for the preservation of the cryosphere.

• Satellite Monitoring: Agencies like ISRO (Indian Space Research Organisation) and NASA use Remote Sensing and GIS technology to monitor the health of over 9,000 glaciers in the Indian Himalayan region.

• Albedo Feedback: As white ice melts, it exposes darker land or water underneath, which absorbs more heat, leading to a self-reinforcing cycle of further warming and melting.

• Holocene Epoch: While glaciers have advanced and retreated naturally over geological time scales, the current rate of retreat is unprecedented and primarily anthropogenic (human-induced).

6. A heating element of a heater is made of a material with high resistivity and high melting point. Which of the following material properties is most crucial for its function as a heating element?

Solution

The correct answer is High resistivity. Key Points Additional Information

• High resistivity is crucial for a heating element as it ensures that a significant amount of electrical energy is converted into heat.

• Materials with high resistivity provide resistance to the flow of electric current, which causes the generation of heat due to Joule heating (P = I²R).

• Common materials used for heating elements, such as nichrome, have high resistivity and are efficient at producing heat.

• High resistivity reduces the risk of excessive current flow, thereby protecting the heating element and enhancing its longevity.

• Other properties like high melting point and corrosion resistance complement high resistivity to ensure the durability of the heating element.

• High Melting Point: Heating elements must withstand high temperatures without melting or deforming, ensuring safe and effective operation.

• Thermal Conductivity: While high thermal conductivity is beneficial for heat dissipation, heating elements typically focus on resistivity to generate heat efficiently.

• Specific Heat Capacity: Materials with low specific heat capacity heat up faster, but this property is secondary to resistivity in heating elements.

• Nichrome: An alloy commonly used for heating elements, composed of nickel and chromium, known for its high resistivity and corrosion resistance.

9. Why do single-celled organisms do not need specific organs for taking in food, exchanging gases, or removing wastes?

A. They have complex body structures

B. Their entire surface is in contact with the environment

C. They have a protective wall around them

D. They do not perform these functions

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Their entire surface is in contact with the environment. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Single-celled organisms, such as amoeba, paramecium, or bacteria, are microscopic organisms that consist of only one cell. Their entire surface is in direct contact with the environment, allowing them to carry out all essential life processes without specialized organs.

• Their small size and large surface-area-to-volume ratio enable them to exchange substances like oxygen, carbon dioxide, nutrients, and waste products efficiently through simple diffusion. This is why they do not need specialized respiratory or excretory organs.

• These organisms absorb nutrients from their surroundings through mechanisms like endocytosis, wherein the cell membrane engulfs food particles.

• Waste products are also expelled directly into the external environment via simple processes like exocytosis or diffusion.

• Metabolic processes occur within the cytoplasm, and the absence of complex structures like tissues or organs simplifies their physiological functions.

• Option 1: They have complex body structures This is incorrect because single-celled organisms are inherently simple in structure. They lack the complexity of multicellular organisms, which have specialized tissues and organs.

• Option 3: They have a protective wall around them While some single-celled organisms like bacteria possess a cell wall, it is primarily for protection and structural support. It does not replace the need for organs but rather facilitates interaction with the environment.

• Option 4: They do not perform these functions This is incorrect because single-celled organisms actively perform essential functions like nutrient intake, gas exchange, and waste removal to sustain life.

• Examples of single-celled organisms: Amoeba: Known for its ability to change shape, it engulfs food through pseudopodia.

• Paramecium: Moves with the help of cilia and feeds on smaller microorganisms.

• Bacteria: Perform functions like nutrient absorption and waste expulsion directly through their cell membrane or wall.

• Key Terminologies: Diffusion: The process by which molecules move from an area of higher concentration to an area of lower concentration.

• Endocytosis: The process by which cells engulf external substances by surrounding them with their cell membrane.

• Exocytosis: The process by which cells expel waste or substances by vesicles merging with the cell membrane.

• Surface-Area-to-Volume Ratio: Single-celled organisms have a high surface-area-to-volume ratio, which ensures efficient exchange of gases, nutrients, and waste. Larger organisms require specialized systems due to their lower ratio.

• Energy Efficiency: Simple diffusion and direct environmental interaction allow single-celled organisms to conserve energy compared to multicellular organisms.

• Adaptability: Single-celled organisms exhibit high adaptability to environmental changes because of their simplicity and direct interaction with their surroundings.

• Though humans cannot hear infrasound, it can be felt as vibrations and has applications in seismic monitoring.

• Animals such as bats and dolphins can hear ultrasound and utilize it for navigation and communication.

• Applications of Audible Range Knowledge: Design of audio equipment like microphones, speakers, and headphones is based on the human audible range.

• Technological advancements in fields like telecommunications, music production, and hearing aids rely on understanding the auditory spectrum.

• Important Facts: The human auditory system includes the outer ear, middle ear, and inner ear, working together to process sound waves.

• The study of human hearing is essential in disciplines such as audiology, acoustics, and psychoacoustics.

• Hearing tests, such as pure tone audiometry, are used to determine the audible frequency range of individuals.

17. The object covers unequal distances in equal intervals of time, it is said to be in ____ motion.

A. uniform

B. linear

C. non-uniform

D. circular

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is non-uniform motion. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• In non-uniform motion, an object covers unequal distances in equal intervals of time. This is the defining characteristic of non-uniform motion.

• Non-uniform motion occurs when the speed of an object is not constant and changes as it moves. This can include acceleration or deceleration.

• Examples of non-uniform motion include a car speeding up or slowing down, a falling object under gravity, or a ball rolling down a hill.

• Graphically, the motion is represented by a non-linear path or a curved line when plotting distance versus time.

• The motion can be influenced by external factors like force, friction, or gravity, which cause changes in speed or direction.

• Characteristics of Motion: Uniform motion: An object covers equal distances in equal intervals of time. Examples include a car moving at a constant speed or the Earth revolving around the Sun.

• Non-uniform motion: The speed of the object changes, resulting in unequal distances covered in equal intervals of time. It can include both accelerated and decelerated motion.

• Factors affecting non-uniform motion: Force: A net external force can cause a change in the velocity, leading to non-uniform motion.

• Gravity: Objects falling under gravity exhibit non-uniform motion as their speed increases due to acceleration.

• Friction: Friction can slow down an object, causing non-uniform motion.

• Examples of non-uniform motion: A car accelerating on a highway or decelerating at a traffic signal.

• A roller coaster moving along a track with varying speeds and directions.

• A ball dropped from a height, which gains speed due to gravity.

• Graphical representation: In non-uniform motion, the distance-time graph is typically a curved line, indicating changes in speed over time.

• In contrast, the velocity-time graph may show variable slopes depending on acceleration or deceleration.

• Newton’s Laws of Motion: First Law: An object remains in uniform motion or at rest unless acted upon by an external force.

• Second Law: The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass.

• Third Law: For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

• Real-life applications: Understanding non-uniform motion is critical in designing vehicles, braking systems, and amusement park rides.

• It is essential in calculating trajectories of projectiles and space missions.

23. The amount of heat energy that is required to change 1 kg of a solid into liquid at atmospheric pressure at its melting point is known as the ____ heat of fusion.

A. latent

B. boiling

C. melting

D. absolute

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is latent heat of fusion. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Latent heat of fusion is the amount of heat energy required to change 1 kg of a solid into liquid at its melting point under atmospheric pressure without any change in temperature.

• The energy supplied is used to overcome the forces of attraction between particles in the solid, allowing it to transition into a liquid state.

• This concept is important in understanding phase transitions in substances, such as when ice melts to water or metals are melted for industrial applications.

• The latent heat of fusion is specific to each substance and is usually measured in joules per kilogram (J/kg).

• For example, the latent heat of fusion of water is approximately 334,000 J/kg, meaning it takes this much energy to convert 1 kg of ice at 0°C to water at 0°C.

• Phase Transitions: When a substance transitions between solid, liquid, and gas phases, energy is absorbed or released without changing its temperature.

• Latent heat of vaporization: The energy required to convert a liquid into a gas without a temperature change.

• Sublimation: The direct conversion of a solid into a gas, bypassing the liquid phase. This process also involves latent heat.

• Boiling Point vs. Melting Point: The boiling point is the temperature at which a liquid turns into a gas, while the melting point is the temperature at which a solid turns into a liquid.

• Both processes require energy input, but the type of latent heat involved differs: fusion for melting, vaporization for boiling.

• Applications of Latent Heat: Used in refrigeration and air conditioning systems to absorb and release heat efficiently.

• Plays a critical role in meteorological phenomena, such as the formation of clouds and precipitation.

• Important in industrial processes like metal casting and welding, where substances are melted and reshaped.

• Specific Characteristics of Related Options: Boiling: Refers to the phase transition from liquid to gas, requiring latent heat of vaporization.

• Melting: Refers to the physical transition from solid to liquid, governed by latent heat of fusion.

• Absolute: This term is unrelated to phase transitions; it typically refers to numerical values such as absolute temperature.

• Measurement Units: Latent heat is commonly measured in Joules (J), and its value depends on the substance's physical properties and phase transition.

• Example Values: The latent heat of fusion for ice is 334,000 J/kg, while the latent heat of vaporization for water is approximately 2,260,000 J/kg.

• Scientific Significance: Understanding latent heat is fundamental to thermodynamics, phase equilibria, and material science.

• Practical Use: This concept is utilized in designing systems like heat exchangers, thermal storage devices, and cryogenic cooling systems.

24. The Galaxies are spread over vast distances, which are measured in ____.

A. light years

B. kilometres

C. metres

D. nanometres

Answer A

Solution

The galaxies are spread over vast distances measured in light years. A light year represents the distance light travels in a vacuum in one Julian year, approximately 9.46 trillion kilometers. Terrestrial units like kilometers are impractical due to the immense scale of space.

25. Which of the following planet appears blue from the outer space because of its two-thirds surface is covered by water?

A. Earth

B. Mercury

C. Venus

D. Uranus

Answer A

Solution

Earth appears blue from outer space because approximately 71% of its surface is covered by water. The vast oceans reflect sunlight and scatter blue light, creating its distinct blue appearance. Additionally, Rayleigh scattering by Earth's atmosphere further enhances this effect.
