1. What is a disadvantage of friction?

A. It increases speed

B. It improves machinery efficiency

C. It prevents objects from wearing out

D. It causes wear and tear of materials

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is It causes wear and tear of materials. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Friction is a resistive force that occurs when two surfaces come into contact and move relative to each other. While it has some advantages, it also has significant disadvantages, especially in mechanical applications.

• One major disadvantage of friction is that it causes wear and tear of materials. This happens because the continuous rubbing of surfaces damages their structural integrity over time.

• Friction leads to a reduction in the lifespan of machinery and components, as the constant abrasion deteriorates the material quality.

• In industries, wear and tear caused by friction can result in higher maintenance costs. Frequent replacement or repair of components is required to ensure the machinery functions properly.

• Friction also generates heat, which can further accelerate the degradation of materials, leading to issues such as overheating and thermal damage.

• Hence, minimizing friction through lubrication or advanced materials is a key focus in mechanical engineering to reduce wear and tear.

• Advantages of Friction: Friction is essential for performing tasks such as walking, driving, and holding objects. Without friction, movement would be uncontrollable.

• It helps in the braking systems of vehicles, allowing them to stop effectively.

• Friction plays a critical role in processes such as cutting, grinding, and polishing in manufacturing.

• Methods to Reduce Friction: Lubrication: Using oils or greases reduces the direct contact between surfaces, minimizing wear and tear.

• Using advanced materials such as ceramics or polymers with lower coefficients of friction can decrease the impact of friction.

• Implementing ball bearings or roller bearings reduces friction in rotating systems.

• Types of Friction: Static Friction: The frictional force that prevents relative motion between surfaces in contact.

• Kinetic Friction: The resistive force occurring during relative motion between surfaces.

• Rolling Friction: The friction that occurs when an object rolls over a surface.

• Fluid Friction: The resistance encountered by objects moving through fluids (liquids or gases).

• Applications of Friction: Friction is used in mechanical systems such as clutches and brakes for controlling motion.

• It is also utilized in sports to enhance grip, such as in climbing shoes or car tires.

• In construction, friction is exploited for fastening components, such as nails and screws.

• Heat Generation: Friction converts kinetic energy into thermal energy, which can lead to overheating in machines and devices.

• Energy Loss: Friction results in the loss of energy, reducing the efficiency of systems such as engines and turbines.

• Material Degradation: The repeated action of friction causes scratches, cracks, and deformation of surfaces over time.

• Scientific Study: The coefficient of friction is studied to design systems with optimal performance, balancing frictional force requirements.

B. Only II

C. Both I and II

D. Neither I nor II

3. What is the scientific term for white blood cells?

A. Erythrocytes

B. Leucocytes

C. Thrombocytes

D. Platelets

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Leucocytes. Key Points Additional Information

• White blood cells (WBCs) are scientifically known as Leucocytes.

• Leucocytes play a crucial role in the immune system by defending the body against infections, foreign invaders, and abnormal cells.

• They are produced in the bone marrow and are found in the blood and lymphatic system.

• There are five main types of leucocytes: neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils, and basophils.

• Unlike red blood cells, leucocytes are nucleated and lack hemoglobin.

• Erythrocytes: Also known as red blood cells (RBCs), they are responsible for transporting oxygen and carbon dioxide.

• Erythrocytes contain the protein hemoglobin, which binds oxygen for delivery to tissues.

• Thrombocytes: Commonly referred to as platelets, they play a key role in blood clotting.

• They help prevent excessive blood loss during injury.

• Functions of Leucocytes: They detect and neutralize pathogens such as bacteria, viruses, and fungi.

• Some leucocytes produce antibodies, while others directly destroy harmful cells.

• Leucocyte Count: The normal range of WBCs in a healthy adult is 4,000 to 11,000 cells per microliter of blood.

• An abnormal count may indicate infections, immune disorders, or blood diseases.

C. The paper becomes fireproof when wet

Solution

B. Frequency increases while wavelength stays constant

C. Both frequency and wavelength decrease

D. Speed decreases while frequency increases

7. Which of the following statements is INCORRECT regarding plants?

A. Roots take water from the soil

B. Phloem tubes of cells specialized for water transport

C. Chlorophyll, the green pigment in the leaves, absorbs light

D. Stomata are the little pores in the leaves required for gaseous exchange

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Phloem tubes of cells specialized for water transport. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Roots take water from the soil: This statement is correct. Roots are specialized structures in plants that absorb water and nutrients from the soil. The water is then transported upward through the xylem tissue to other parts of the plant.

• Phloem tubes of cells specialized for water transport: This statement is incorrect. Phloem is responsible for transporting food (mainly sugar) produced during photosynthesis from leaves to other parts of the plant. Water transport is carried out by the xylem tissue, not phloem. Hence, this option is incorrect.

• Chlorophyll, the green pigment in the leaves, absorbs light: This statement is correct. Chlorophyll is a vital pigment found in chloroplasts of plant cells. It absorbs light, primarily in the blue and red wavelengths, to carry out photosynthesis by converting light energy into chemical energy.

• Stomata are the little pores in the leaves required for gaseous exchange: This statement is correct. Stomata are tiny openings present on the surface of leaves that facilitate gaseous exchange, allowing oxygen to exit and carbon dioxide to enter for photosynthesis. They also play a role in transpiration.

• Hence, the correct answer is Phloem tubes of cells specialized for water transport.

• Xylem vs. Phloem: Xylem: Responsible for transporting water and dissolved minerals from roots to other parts of the plant. It consists of vessels and tracheids, which are specialized cells for water conduction.

• Phloem: Transports organic nutrients, especially sucrose, from leaves (where photosynthesis occurs) to other parts of the plant. It consists of sieve tubes and companion cells.

• Water transport mechanism: Water is absorbed by roots through osmosis and transported upward via capillary action in the xylem.

• The transpiration process creates a suction force, pulling water upward through the plant.

• Photosynthesis: Chlorophyll absorbs light energy and uses it to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen.

• This process occurs in the chloroplasts, primarily in leaf cells.

• Stomata and transpiration: Stomata regulate the exchange of gases (CO2 and O2) and water vapor through the process of transpiration.

• They are controlled by guard cells, which open and close the stomatal pores depending on environmental conditions.

• Functions of Xylem and Phloem: Xylem: One-way transport system for water and minerals from roots to leaves.

• Phloem: Bidirectional transport system for nutrients between leaves and other parts of the plant.

• Role of chlorophyll: Chlorophyll absorbs light most efficiently in the blue and red wavelengths, making leaves appear green due to the reflection of green light.

• It is essential for converting light energy into glucose during photosynthesis.

• Stomatal regulation: Stomata play a key role in maintaining plant water balance and enabling photosynthesis.

• Environmental factors like humidity, temperature, and light intensity affect stomatal opening and closing.

11. When focusing on a tree while on an anti-clockwise merry-go-round, from which side does it appear and exit?

A. Right to Leff

B. Always visible

C. Never visible

D. Left to Right

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Right to Left. Key Points Additional Information

• When you are on an anti-clockwise merry-go-round, your position is constantly changing relative to a stationary object such as a tree.

• As the merry-go-round rotates anti-clockwise, the tree will first appear on your right side as you move toward it.

• Due to the continuous rotation, the tree will seem to move from your right side to your left side as you pass it.

• This apparent motion is a result of the relative positioning between the observer (you) and the stationary object (tree) during the anti-clockwise movement.

• The correct interpretation of this phenomenon aligns with the principles of relative motion in physics.

• Relative Motion: The concept of relative motion describes how the position of an object changes with respect to an observer. In this case, the tree’s apparent motion is relative to the observer’s position on the rotating merry-go-round.

• Anti-clockwise Rotation: A rotation is termed anti-clockwise when it proceeds in the opposite direction to the movement of clock hands. This impacts the observer’s perception of stationary objects.

• Frame of Reference: The observer on the merry-go-round is in a non-inertial frame of reference. Stationary objects like the tree appear to move due to the observer’s rotational motion.

• Perception of Motion: The apparent movement of the tree from right to left is a result of the observer’s changing position and orientation relative to the tree.

• Practical Example: Similar observations can be made in other rotational systems, such as carousels or spinning platforms, where objects appear to move in a direction opposite to the rotation.

14. What is the name of India's reusable launch vehicle currently being developed by ISRO?

A. PSLV-R

B. GSLV-X

C. RLV-TD

D. MLV-1

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is RLV-TD. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• The Reusable Launch Vehicle-Technology Demonstrator (RLV-TD) is India's primary project aimed at developing essential technologies for a fully reusable launch vehicle to enable low-cost access to space.

• It is being developed by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) to significantly reduce the cost of launching satellites by recycling the launch vehicle, similar to the Space Shuttle or SpaceX's Falcon series.

• The first experimental flight of RLV-TD, known as the HEX-01 (Hypersonic Flight Experiment), was successfully conducted on May 23, 2016, from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC) SHAR, Sriharikota.

• The configuration of RLV-TD is a winged body vehicle that acts as a flying testbed to evaluate various technologies like hypersonic flight, autonomous landing, and powered cruise flight.

• In April 2023 and June 2024, ISRO successfully conducted the RLV LEX (Landing Experiment) at the Aeronautical Test Range (ATR) in Chitradurga, Karnataka, demonstrating the vehicle's capability to land autonomously on a runway after being released from an Indian Air Force Chinook helicopter.

• PSLV (Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle): Known as the "Workhorse of ISRO," it is a third-generation expendable launch vehicle used to place satellites into Sun-Synchronous Polar Orbits. It has four stages using solid and liquid propulsion alternately.

• GSLV (Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle): A three-stage expendable vehicle with solid, liquid, and cryogenic stages designed to launch heavier communication satellites into Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit (GTO).

• Need for Reusability: Currently, the cost of space transportation is high because launch vehicles are expendable (lost after one use). A reusable system can reduce costs by nearly 80%, bringing it down to approximately $5,000 to $2,000 per kg of payload.

• TSTO (Two-Stage-to-Orbit): The ultimate goal for ISRO is to scale up the RLV technology into a two-stage-to-orbit fully reusable vehicle, where both the first and second stages can return to Earth.

• Technical Features: The RLV-TD consists of a fuselage, a nose cap, double delta wings, and twin vertical tails. It utilizes thermal protection systems, including ceramic tiles, to withstand the extreme heat during atmospheric re-entry.

• HEX Mission: During the 2016 mission, the vehicle reached an altitude of about 65 km and a speed of Mach 5 (five times the speed of sound) before surviving re-entry and landing at a designated spot in the Bay of Bengal.

• Autonomous Landing: In the LEX missions (2023 and 2024), the RLV performed high-speed autonomous landings under conditions similar to those of a vehicle returning from space, proving India's mastery over navigation and control laws.

• Future Phases: Following LEX, ISRO plans to conduct the REX (Return Flight Experiment) and SPEX (Scramjet Propulsion Experiment) to further refine the vehicle's capability to operate as a reusable rocket-plane.

20. To modify the magnitude of an alternating voltage without altering its frequency, which principle is applied in the functioning of a transformer?

A. Self-induction

B. Electromagnetic radiation

C. Mutual induction

D. Capacitive coupling

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Mutual induction Key Points Additional Information

• A transformer is a static electrical device that transfers electrical energy between two or more circuits through electromagnetic induction.

• The fundamental principle behind the operation of a transformer is Mutual Induction. This occurs when a changing magnetic flux produced by the primary winding links with the secondary winding, inducing an electromotive force (EMF) in it.

• The transformer modifies the magnitude of alternating voltage (stepping it up or down) while keeping the frequency of the power constant.

• Self-induction is the phenomenon where a change in current in a single coil induces an EMF in that same coil. While it occurs in the primary winding, the transfer of energy to the secondary is due to mutual induction.

• Electromagnetic radiation involves the emission of energy as waves (like radio waves), which is not the mechanism for power transformation in a transformer.

• Capacitive coupling involves the transfer of energy through an electric field between two points of a circuit, which is different from the magnetic coupling used in transformers.

• A transformer consists of two inductive coils (Primary and Secondary) which are electrically separated but magnetically linked through a ferromagnetic core.

• The relationship between the voltages and the number of turns in the coils is given by the formula: (V_p/V_s) = (N_p/N_s), where V represents voltage and N represents the number of turns.

• Step-up transformers increase the output voltage (Ns > Np), whereas Step-down transformers decrease the output voltage (Ns < Np).

• Transformers operate only on Alternating Current (AC) because Direct Current (DC) does not produce the changing magnetic flux necessary for induction.

• The efficiency of modern transformers is very high, often exceeding 95 percent, as there are no moving parts and thus no mechanical friction losses.

21. If the half-life of a radioactive substance is 10 days, then the time taken for 75% of the original sample to decay is:

A. 5 days

B. 10 days

C. 20 days

D. 30 days

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is 20 days Key Points Additional Information

• Radioactive decay is a spontaneous process by which an unstable atomic nucleus loses energy by emitting radiation.

• The half-life (T1/2) of a substance is the time required for exactly half of the entities to decay.

• Given that the half-life is 10 days, we can track the decay process in steps: After 10 days (1 half-life): 50% of the sample remains, and 50% has decayed.

• After 20 days (2 half-lives): 50% of the remaining 50% decays. This means 25% of the original sample remains, and 75% has decayed.

• Mathematically, the remaining amount (N) is given by the formula N = N0(1/2)n, where N0 is the initial amount and n is the number of half-lives.

• If 75% has decayed, then 25% remains. So, 25/100 = (1/2)n, which simplifies to 1/4 = (1/2)n. This gives n = 2.

• Total time = n × T1/2 = 2 × 10 = 20 days.

• Radioactive decay follows first-order kinetics, meaning the rate of decay depends only on the number of radioactive nuclei present.

• The decay constant (λ) is related to the half-life by the equation: λ = 0.693T_1/2.

• The SI unit of radioactivity is the Becquerel (Bq), which is defined as one disintegration per second. Another common unit is the Curie (Ci).

• Different isotopes have vastly different half-lives; for example, Carbon-14 has a half-life of about 5,730 years, while Polonium-214 has a half-life of about 164 microseconds.

25. What structures are provided in the upper part of the respiratory tract to remove germs, dust, and other harmful particles from inhaled air in humans?

A. Alveoli

B. Bronchi

C. Pleura

D. Cilia

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is Cilia. Key Points Additional Information

• Cilia are microscopic hair-like structures found on the epithelial cells lining the respiratory tract. They play a vital role in trapping and removing harmful particles from inhaled air.

• Cilia are covered by a layer of mucus produced by goblet cells. The mucus traps germs, dust, and other particulate matter present in the air.

• The coordinated movement of cilia, known as the "mucociliary escalator," ensures that trapped particles are moved upwards towards the throat, where they can be expelled or swallowed. This mechanism is essential for maintaining respiratory health.

• Cilia are located in the upper respiratory tract, including the nasal cavity, trachea, and bronchi, making them the first line of defense against airborne contaminants.

• Damage to cilia, due to smoking or exposure to pollutants, can impair this protective function, leading to increased susceptibility to respiratory infections.

• Alveoli: Alveoli are tiny air sacs located at the end of bronchioles in the lungs, responsible for gas exchange (oxygen and carbon dioxide) between the blood and the atmosphere.

• They are not involved in trapping harmful particles or removing germs; their primary function is to facilitate respiration.

• Bronchi: The bronchi are the main passageways for air entering the lungs. While they transport air, they also contain cilia and mucus to trap and expel harmful particles.

• However, the bronchi are not the primary structures for filtering inhaled air; cilia take precedence in this function.

• Pleura: The pleura are two thin layers of tissue surrounding the lungs and lining the chest cavity. They produce pleural fluid, which reduces friction during breathing.

• Pleura do not play a role in trapping or removing harmful particles from inhaled air.

• Respiratory Health Facts: Respiratory diseases such as asthma, bronchitis, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) can result from impaired ciliary function.

• Maintaining clean air quality and avoiding smoking are critical for preserving ciliary function and overall respiratory health.

• Important Points: Cilia are an essential component of the innate immune system, providing a physical barrier against airborne pathogens.

• The mucociliary clearance mechanism is a key defense strategy employed by the respiratory system.

• Regular hydration and a balanced diet support the production of mucus and the proper functioning of cilia.
