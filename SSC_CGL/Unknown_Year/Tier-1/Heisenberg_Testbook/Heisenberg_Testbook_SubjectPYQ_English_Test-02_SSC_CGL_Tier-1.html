Which of the following factors contributes to the low child sex ratio in some economically developed Northern states of India? 1) Increased accessibility to prenatal sex-determination methods 2) High levels of female literacy and empowerment 3) Societal preference for male children in inheritance and family support 4) Strengthened enforcement of gender-equal policies

A. 2 and 3

B. 2 and 4

C. 1 and 3

D. 1 and 4

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is 1 and 3. Key Points Additional Information

• Increased accessibility to prenatal sex-determination methods has contributed to the declining child sex ratio in some states due to misuse for sex-selective abortions.

• A societal preference for male children persists in Northern states, driven by cultural norms favoring sons for inheritance, lineage, and old-age support.

• Despite economic development, deeply rooted patriarchal attitudes often result in discriminatory practices against female children.

• The misuse of medical technologies such as ultrasound imaging for prenatal sex determination has led to skewed sex ratios in economically developed regions.

• Legal frameworks like the Pre-Conception and Pre-Natal Diagnostic Techniques (PCPNDT) Act exist but face challenges in enforcement, allowing misuse to persist.

• Child Sex Ratio (CSR): CSR refers to the number of girls per 1,000 boys in the 0-6 age group. It is a key indicator of gender equality and societal attitudes toward female children.

• Pre-Conception and Pre-Natal Diagnostic Techniques (PCPNDT) Act: Enacted in 1994, this law aims to prevent the misuse of prenatal diagnostic techniques for sex-selective abortions.

• Gender Preferences: Cultural factors in India often place higher value on male children due to their role in family lineage, economic support, and rituals such as funeral rites.

• Skewed Sex Ratio Consequences: A declining child sex ratio can lead to long-term societal issues, including gender-based violence, trafficking, and imbalanced marriage markets.

• Government Initiatives: Campaigns such as "Beti Bachao, Beti Padhao" aim to address gender-based discrimination and promote the survival, protection, and education of girls.

C. Both I and II

D. Neither I nor II

Solution

The correct answer is Both I and II. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Financial Inclusion Index (FI-Index) was introduced by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) in July 2021. It measures the level of financial inclusion in the country.

• The index comprises three parameters: Access, Usage, and Quality.

• Access: This parameter evaluates the availability of financial services, such as the number of bank branches, ATMs, and other financial outlets per population.

• Usage: This parameter measures how actively people use financial services, such as savings accounts, loans, or insurance policies.

• Quality: While not mentioned in the question, this parameter assesses the effectiveness and efficiency of financial services provided.

• The FI-Index is a comprehensive measure that ranges from 0 to 100, where 0 represents complete financial exclusion and 100 signifies full financial inclusion.

• Hence, both I (Access) and II (Usage) are correct.

• Reserve Bank of India: The RBI introduced the FI-Index to provide a comprehensive tool for policymakers and researchers to track financial inclusion progress.

• This index is released annually and incorporates data from 21 parameters, grouped into the three mentioned dimensions.

• Financial Inclusion: Financial inclusion aims to provide affordable financial services to all individuals, especially marginalized and underserved communities.

• It includes services such as banking, credit, insurance, and digital payments.

• Government Initiatives: The Government of India has launched programs like Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY) to enhance financial inclusion.

• PMJDY focuses on providing universal access to banking facilities, financial literacy, and insurance coverage.

• Global Context: Similar indices exist globally, such as the Global Findex Database by the World Bank, which measures financial inclusion worldwide.

• India’s FI-Index aligns with international efforts to track progress toward achieving UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), particularly SDG 8.10.

• Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana: Launched on 28th August 2014, PMJDY aims to provide universal banking access.

• It has led to the opening of over 48 crore bank accounts as of 2023.

16. What does the term "Duckworth-Lewis Method relate to in cricket ?

A. Fielding restrictions

B. Player ranking system

C. Bowling speed calculation

D. Calculating revised target in rain-affected matches

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is Calculating revised target in rain-affected matches. Key Points Additional Information

• The Duckworth-Lewis Method is a mathematical formula used in cricket to calculate a revised target score in rain-affected matches where overs are lost.

• It was introduced by statisticians Frank Duckworth and Tony Lewis in 1997 and is now officially known as the Duckworth-Lewis-Stern (DLS) method after updates by Professor Steven Stern.

• The method takes into account the number of overs remaining and the number of wickets lost to produce a fair target for the chasing team.

• It has been adopted by the International Cricket Council (ICC) for use in limited-overs matches, including One-Day Internationals (ODIs) and Twenty20 Internationals (T20Is).

• The DLS method ensures fairness by considering both resources (overs and wickets) available to the batting team at any point during the match.

• Frank Duckworth and Tony Lewis: The original developers of the method aimed to address the shortcomings of previous rain-rule systems in cricket, such as the "most productive overs" method.

• Steven Stern's Contribution: Professor Steven Stern updated the Duckworth-Lewis method in 2015 to reflect modern cricket scoring patterns, leading to its renaming as the Duckworth-Lewis-Stern (DLS) method.

• Key Principles: The method uses pre-determined tables based on historical data to estimate the resources (overs and wickets) a team has left in a match, providing a fair revised target.

• Criticism: While widely used, the DLS method has faced criticism for its complexity and occasional perceived unfairness in scenarios of extreme weather interruptions.

• Alternatives: Other rain rules, such as the "VJD Method" (used in domestic cricket in India), also exist but have not gained the same level of global acceptance as the DLS method.
