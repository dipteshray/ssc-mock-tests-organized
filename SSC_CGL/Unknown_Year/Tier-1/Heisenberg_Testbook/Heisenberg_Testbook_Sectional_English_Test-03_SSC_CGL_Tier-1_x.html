6. Which of the following sequences correctly represents the layers of the Earth from the outermost to innermost?

A. Crust → Mantle → Core

B. Lithosphere → Hydrosphere → Atmosphere

C. Mantle → Crust → Core

D. Core → Crust → Mantle

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is: Crust → Mantle → Core. The layers of the Earth are organized based on their physical and chemical properties. The Earth is divided into three main layers: the crust, the mantle, and the core. Each layer has distinct characteristics and compositions. Key Points Additional Information

• Crust: The outermost layer of the Earth.

• Composed primarily of solid rocks like granite (continental crust) and basalt (oceanic crust).

• Thickness varies from about 5 km (oceanic) to 70 km (continental).

• Mantle: The layer directly beneath the crust.

• Approximately 2,900 km thick.

• Composed mainly of silicate minerals rich in iron and magnesium.

• Divided into the upper mantle and the lower mantle, with the upper mantle containing the asthenosphere which is semi-fluid and allows tectonic plates to move.

• Core: Innermost layer of the Earth, divided into the outer core and inner core.

• Outer core is liquid, composed mainly of iron and nickel, and responsible for Earth's magnetic field.

• Inner core is solid and also composed primarily of iron and nickel, with temperatures reaching up to 5,700°C.

• Formation of Layers: The Earth’s layers were formed during the early stages of planetary formation and differentiation, where denser materials sank to the center, creating the core, while lighter materials formed the mantle and crust.

• Importance of Classification: Understanding the structure of the Earth is crucial for various fields such as geology, seismology, and plate tectonics. It helps in studying earthquake patterns, volcanic activities, and the movement of tectonic plates.

• Plate Tectonics: The movement of the Earth's lithospheric plates over the semi-fluid asthenosphere in the upper mantle depicts the dynamic nature of the Earth, causing phenomena like earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, and mountain building.

9. Which of the following can conduct electricity? 1. Sugar solution 2. Vinegar solution 3. Lemon juice solution 4. Caustic soda solution

A. 1, 3, and 4 only

B. 2 and 4 only

C. 1 and 4 only

D. 2, 3, and 4 only

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is 2, 3, and 4 only. Key Points Additional Information

• Vinegar solution conducts electricity due to the presence of acetic acid, which ionizes and produces ions in the solution.

• Lemon juice solution conducts electricity because it contains citric acid, a weak electrolyte that ionizes and produces ions.

• Caustic soda solution conducts electricity because it contains sodium hydroxide (NaOH), a strong electrolyte that dissociates completely into ions.

• Sugar solution does not conduct electricity as sugar molecules do not ionize or produce charged particles in water.

• Electrical conductivity in solutions is directly dependent on the presence of free ions that can move under an electric field.

• Electrolyte: A substance that, when dissolved in water, produces ions and conducts electricity. Examples include acids, bases, and salts.

• Strong vs Weak Electrolytes: Strong electrolytes (e.g., NaOH) fully dissociate into ions, while weak electrolytes (e.g., acetic acid) partially ionize.

• Ionic Conductivity: Conductivity in solutions depends on the concentration and mobility of ions present.

• Non-Electrolytes: Substances like sugar or alcohol do not ionize in water and hence do not conduct electricity.

• Applications: Understanding conductivity is crucial in industries like electroplating, battery manufacturing, and water quality testing.

24. Which of the following measures of money supply is the most liquid and easiest for transactions?

A. M2

B. M3

C. M4

D. M1

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is M1. Key Points Additional Information

• M1 is the most liquid measure of money supply and is primarily used for immediate transactions.

• It includes currency in circulation, demand deposits with banks, and other deposits with the Reserve Bank of India (RBI).

• Currency in circulation comprises all coins and paper currency held by the public.

• Demand deposits are funds held in bank accounts from which money can be withdrawn at any time without any advance notice.

• M2: Includes M1 plus savings deposits with post office savings banks and savings accounts.

• M3: Known as broad money, it includes M2 plus time deposits with banks.

• M4: Comprises M3 plus all deposits with post office savings banks excluding National Savings Certificates.

• Liquidity: The ease with which an asset can be converted into cash without affecting its market price.

• Reserve Bank of India (RBI): The central bank of India, which regulates the issue and supply of the Indian rupee and the banking system in India.

25. The name of the process used to manufacture most sulfuric acid is the

A. Contact process

B. Frasch process

C. Esterification

D. None of the above

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Contact process Key Points Additional Information

• The contact process is used for the manufacture of sulfuric acid.

• The various steps in the contact process are as follows: Production of sulphur dioxide.

• Purification of gases.

• Catalytic oxidation of sulphur dioxide.

• Absorption of sulphur trioxide in sulphuric acid.

• Dilution of oleum to obtain sulphuric acid.

• The lead-chamber process is the method of producing sulfuric acid by oxidizing sulfur dioxide with moist air, using gaseous nitrogen oxides as catalysts.

• The Frasch process is a method to extract sulfur from underground deposits. It is the only industrial method of recovering sulfur from elemental deposits.

• Esterification is the process of combining an organic acid (RCOOH) with an alcohol (ROH) to form an ester (RCOOR) and water. This chemical reaction results in the formation of at least one ester product.
