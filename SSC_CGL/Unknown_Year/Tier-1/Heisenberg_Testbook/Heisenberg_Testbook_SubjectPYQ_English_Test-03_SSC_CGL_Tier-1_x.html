1. Who holds the power to dissolve the Lok Sabha prior to the completion of its tenure?

A. Prime Minister

B. Rajya Sabha

C. Speaker of Lok Sabha

D. President

Answer D

Solution

The correct answer is President. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Under Article 85(2)(b) of the Constitution of India, the President has the power to dissolve the Lok Sabha (the Lower House of Parliament) even before the completion of its five-year term. Hence, the statement regarding the President is correct.

• The President generally exercises this power on the advice of the Council of Ministers, headed by the Prime Minister. However, if the Council of Ministers loses the confidence of the house (the majority), the President may use discretionary power to dissolve the house or explore the possibility of an alternative government.

• The Prime Minister is the head of the government and can recommend the dissolution to the President, but the Prime Minister himself does not hold the constitutional authority to dissolve the house. Hence, the statement regarding the Prime Minister is incorrect.

• The Rajya Sabha is the Upper House of the Parliament of India and is a permanent body. According to Article 83(1), it is not subject to dissolution; instead, one-third of its members retire every second year. Hence, the statement regarding the Rajya Sabha is incorrect.

• The Speaker of Lok Sabha is the presiding officer responsible for maintaining order and adjourning the house (sine die or for a specific period), but the Speaker does not possess the legal power to dissolve the Parliamentary body. Hence, the statement regarding the Speaker is incorrect.

• Composition of Lok Sabha: According to Article 81, the maximum strength of the Lok Sabha is fixed at 550 members (following the 104th Amendment Act, 2019, which discontinued the nomination of two Anglo-Indians). Currently, it consists of members elected directly by the people of India on the basis of universal adult suffrage.

• Tenure and Article 83: The normal term of the Lok Sabha is five years from the date of its first meeting. However, during a Proclamation of Emergency, this term can be extended by Parliament by law for a period not exceeding one year at a time.

• Summoning and Prorogation: Article 85 also empowers the President to summon each House of Parliament to meet at such time and place as he thinks fit, ensuring that six months shall not intervene between its last sitting in one session and the date appointed for its first sitting in the next session.

• The Role of the Election Commission: Once the President dissolves the Lok Sabha, the Election Commission of India is responsible for conducting General Elections to constitute a new house, typically within six months of dissolution.

• Discretionary Powers: In a Hung Parliament (where no single party or coalition has a numerical majority), the President plays a crucial role in deciding whether to dissolve the house or appoint a Prime Minister who can prove a majority on the floor of the house.

• Qualifications for Membership: To become a member of the Lok Sabha, a person must be a citizen of India and at least 25 years of age. For the Rajya Sabha, the minimum age requirement is 30 years.

• The 42nd Amendment Act (1976): This Act briefly increased the tenure of the Lok Sabha from five to six years, but the 44th Amendment Act (1978) restored it back to five years.

• Difference between Adjournment and Dissolution: Adjournment terminates a sitting of the House, which is done by the Speaker. Dissolution terminates the very life of the existing House, and a new House is constituted after general elections.

4. Which of the following correctly describes a heavy industry?

A. It uses light raw materials like cotton.

B. It produces light consumer goods.

C. Uses bulky inputs to produce heavy goods.

D. It manufactures cosmetics and toiletries.

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Uses bulky inputs to produce heavy goods. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Heavy industries are characterized by their use of bulky raw materials and heavy machinery to produce large-scale and heavy goods such as steel, machinery, and vehicles.

• These industries often require large capital investments and involve significant industrial processes, including mining, metallurgy, and production of heavy engineering equipment.

• The raw materials used, such as iron ore, coal, and limestone, are bulky and require extensive transportation and handling.

• The products of heavy industries are typically used as inputs for other industries, such as construction, automobile manufacturing, and shipbuilding, making them foundational to industrial development.

• Examples of heavy industries include the steel industry, oil refining, shipbuilding, and machinery manufacturing.

• Characteristics of Heavy Industries: They produce capital goods rather than consumer goods, such as machinery, tools, and construction materials.

• They require a large workforce and create significant employment opportunities in related sectors.

• Often located near raw material sources or transportation hubs to reduce costs.

• They play a critical role in a country’s infrastructure development and economic growth.

• Examples of Heavy Industries: The steel industry, which produces steel used in construction and manufacturing.

• Automobile and shipbuilding industries, which create large vehicles and ships for transportation and trade.

• The mining industry, which extracts and processes raw materials like coal and iron ore.

• The chemical industry, which produces industrial chemicals, fertilizers, and other heavy goods.

• Difference between Heavy and Light Industries: Heavy industries use bulky raw materials and produce goods that are often used in further industrial processes.

• Light industries, on the other hand, use light raw materials and produce consumer goods such as textiles, cosmetics, and electronics.

• Global Importance: Countries like China, the USA, Germany, and India have invested heavily in heavy industries to boost their economic infrastructure and industrial growth.

• Heavy industries form the backbone of export economies, contributing significantly to a nation’s GDP.

• The Industrial Revolution of the 18th and 19th centuries marked the rapid growth of heavy industries, particularly in Europe and North America.

• Many heavy industries are considered energy-intensive and contribute significantly to greenhouse gas emissions, making sustainability a key concern in the modern era.

• Policies such as Make in India and Atmanirbhar Bharat aim to promote the growth of heavy industries in India to reduce dependence on imports and boost self-reliance.

• Technological advancements, such as the use of automation and AI in heavy industries, are improving efficiency and reducing production costs.

A. Women

B. Children

C. Tribal (Adivasi) communities

D. Industrial workers

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is Tribal (Adivasi) communities. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Thakkar Bapa, born as Amritlal Vithaldas Thakkar in 1869, was a prominent social worker and freedom fighter in India.

• He is primarily known for his lifelong efforts towards the upliftment and welfare of the Tribal (Adivasi) communities, focusing on their education, healthcare, and social empowerment.

• Thakkar Bapa founded the Bhil Seva Mandal in 1922, which aimed to improve the socio-economic conditions of the Bhil tribe in Gujarat and nearby regions.

• He worked closely with Mahatma Gandhi during India’s freedom struggle and was deeply influenced by Gandhi’s philosophy of non-violence and rural development.

• His efforts to bring attention to the plight of tribal communities led to the formation of specific policies and programs for their welfare post-independence.

• Contribution of Thakkar Bapa to Tribal Welfare: He was appointed as the General Secretary of the Harijan Sevak Sangh, an organization working for the upliftment of marginalized communities.

• Thakkar Bapa emphasized the importance of educating tribal children and initiated several schools and hostels for them.

• He advocated for the rights of tribal people to land ownership, sustainable livelihoods, and protection from exploitation.

• His work directly influenced the formulation of government policies aimed at tribal welfare and development, including the establishment of tribal welfare departments in several states.

• Key Characteristics of Tribal (Adivasi) Communities: Population: Tribal communities constitute around 8.6% of India’s total population, as per the 2011 Census.

• Geographical Distribution: Major tribal populations are concentrated in states like Madhya Pradesh, Odisha, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, and Rajasthan.

• Languages and Culture: Tribals have their own unique languages, customs, and traditions, and many follow animistic religious practices or are nature worshippers.

• Challenges: Tribals face issues such as lack of access to education, healthcare, and employment opportunities, as well as displacement due to industrialization and urbanization.

• Government Programs for Tribal Welfare: Van Bandhu Kalyan Yojana: Launched by the Government of India to ensure the integrated development of tribal communities.

• Tribal Sub-Plan (TSP): A strategic policy initiative to channelize funds and efforts for the socio-economic development of Scheduled Tribes.

• Eklavya Model Residential Schools (EMRS): Aimed at providing quality education to tribal children in remote areas.

• Forest Rights Act (2006): Recognizes the rights of forest-dwelling communities to use and manage forest resources sustainably.

• Thakkar Bapa’s Legacy: To honor his contributions, the Government of India established Thakkar Bapa Tribal Research and Training Institute in various regions.

• He is remembered as the “Father of Tribal Welfare” in India due to his tireless efforts for the upliftment of tribal communities.

• United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (UNDRIP): This international declaration was adopted in 2007 to protect the rights of indigenous peoples globally.

• It emphasizes the rights of indigenous peoples to maintain their unique cultures, languages, and traditions.

• India, being home to a significant tribal population, has aligned many of its policies with the principles outlined in this declaration.

7. The "Pradhan Mantri Kisan Urja Suraksha evam Utthan Mahabhiyan (PM-KUSUM)" scheme has a focus on the energy sector within agriculture. What is the primary objective of this scheme?

A. To provide free electricity to all agricultural consumers.

B. To promote the use of chemical fertilizers and pesticides for higher yields.

C. To encourage the installation of solar pumps and grid-connected solar power plants in rural areas.

D. To improve the rural electricity grid infrastructure for better power supply.

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is To encourage the installation of solar pumps and grid-connected solar power plants in rural areas. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• The Pradhan Mantri Kisan Urja Suraksha evam Utthan Mahabhiyan (PM-KUSUM) scheme was launched by the Government of India to promote the use of renewable energy in the agricultural sector.

• The primary focus of the scheme is to encourage the installation of solar-powered pumps and grid-connected solar power plants in rural areas.

• It aims to provide energy security to farmers by reducing their dependence on expensive diesel or unreliable electricity grids.

• Under this scheme, farmers can install solar irrigation pumps and even sell surplus electricity generated from solar plants to the grid, thus generating additional income.

• The scheme is divided into three components: Component A: Setting up 10,000 MW of decentralized grid-connected renewable energy plants.

• Component B: Installation of 20 lakh standalone solar pumps.

• Component C: Solarization of 15 lakh existing grid-connected agricultural pumps.

• The scheme also aligns with the government’s vision of achieving 175 GW of renewable energy capacity by 2022.

• Benefits of PM-KUSUM Scheme: Reduces dependency on fossil fuels and promotes the use of clean energy.

• Provides an alternative source of income for farmers by enabling them to sell surplus power to the grid.

• Improves access to reliable energy for irrigation, boosting agricultural productivity.

• Helps in achieving the government’s climate goals by reducing greenhouse gas emissions.

• Key Features of Solar Pumps: Solar pumps are powered by energy from the sun, which is a renewable and sustainable energy source.

• Standalone solar pumps eliminate the need for grid electricity or diesel, reducing operational costs.

• They are ideal for remote areas where electricity supply is unreliable or unavailable.

• Implementation and Funding: The scheme is implemented by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE).

• It provides a combination of Central Financial Assistance (CFA), state government subsidies, and farmer contributions.

• The central government provides up to 30% subsidy, while the state government contributes another 30%, and the farmer bears the remaining cost.

• Global Perspective: Globally, solar-powered irrigation systems are gaining popularity as a sustainable solution to water and energy challenges in agriculture.

• Countries like India, Kenya, and Bangladesh have embraced solar irrigation to support smallholder farmers and reduce reliance on traditional energy sources.

• The PM-KUSUM scheme contributes significantly to the government’s goal of doubling farmers’ income by 2022.

• It also supports the National Solar Mission, which aims to increase the share of solar energy in India’s energy mix.

• By replacing diesel pumps with solar pumps, the scheme can save approximately 1.2 billion liters of diesel annually, reducing the import burden and foreign exchange expenditure.

• The scheme has a potential to generate employment opportunities in the renewable energy sector, particularly in rural areas.

11. "Taxila", the famous centre of learning, was the capital of which Mahajanapada in ancient India?

A. Avanti

B. Gandhara

C. Kamboja

D. Kosala

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Gandhara. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• Taxila, an important center of learning in ancient India, was the capital of the Mahajanapada called Gandhara.

• The Gandhara Mahajanapada was located in the northwestern region of the Indian subcontinent, covering parts of modern-day Pakistan and Afghanistan.

• Taxila was renowned for its world-class educational institutions, attracting scholars and students from across the world, including notable figures like Chanakya, Panini, and Jivaka.

• The city's strategic location on ancient trade routes, such as the Uttarapatha, made it a cultural and commercial hub.

• Taxila flourished particularly during the 6th century BCE to the 5th century CE, under the influence of various dynasties, including the Achaemenids, Mauryas, Indo-Greeks, and Kushans.

• Gandhara Civilization: The Gandhara region was an important cultural and political hub in ancient India, known for its Gandhara art, which combined Indian and Greek artistic influences.

• The Gandhara School of Art is best known for its depictions of Buddha in human form, influenced by Hellenistic styles.

• It was also a center for the spread of Buddhism, with numerous monasteries and stupas built during the reign of the Mauryan emperor Ashoka.

• Taxila University: Taxila housed one of the earliest known universities in the world, offering education in a wide range of subjects, including medicine, law, politics, warfare, mathematics, and philosophy.

• It was unique in that it did not have a formal admission system; students came to study under specific teachers based on their expertise.

• Other Mahajanapadas: Avanti: Located in modern-day Madhya Pradesh, its capital was Ujjayini (modern Ujjain).

• Kamboja: Situated in present-day regions of northern Afghanistan and parts of Central Asia, Kamboja was known for its warrior class.

• Kosala: Located in modern-day Uttar Pradesh, its capital was Ayodhya, and it played a significant role in the early history of Buddhism and Jainism.

• Taxila’s Decline: Taxila's prominence declined after the invasions of the Huns in the 5th century CE, which led to the destruction of the city and its institutions.

• The region subsequently lost its significance as a center of learning and cultural exchange.

• Panini: The ancient grammarian who authored Ashtadhyayi, the foundational text of Sanskrit grammar, was associated with Taxila.

• Chanakya: Also known as Kautilya or Vishnugupta, the advisor to Chandragupta Maurya and author of the Arthashastra, is believed to have studied and taught in Taxila.

• Jivaka: A renowned physician during Buddha's time, who studied at Taxila, is considered one of the earliest surgeons in history.

• UNESCO World Heritage Site: The ruins of Taxila were declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1980, recognizing its historical and cultural significance.

• Archaeological Discoveries: Excavations in Taxila have unearthed numerous artifacts, including stupas, coins, and sculptures, shedding light on its rich cultural heritage.

13. Which Mughal emperor introduced the Charbagh style of gardens?

A. Babur

B. Akbar

C. Jahangir

D. Aurangzeb

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Babur. Key Points Additional Information

• Babur, the founder of the Mughal Empire in India, is credited with introducing the Charbagh style of gardening to the Indian subcontinent. He reigned from 1526 to 1530.

• The term Charbagh is derived from Persian, where Char means four and Bagh means garden. It refers to a quadrilateral garden layout divided into four smaller parts by walkways or flowing water channels.

• In his autobiography, the Baburnama, Babur recorded his deep interest in planning and laying out formal gardens, which were typically placed within rectangular walled enclosures and divided into four quarters by artificial channels.

• The Ram Bagh (originally known as Aram Bagh) in Agra, constructed by Babur in 1528, is the earliest surviving example of a Mughal Charbagh in India. Hence, the statement that Babur introduced this style is correct.

• While later emperors like Akbar, Jahangir, and Shah Jahan refined and expanded this architectural style, the foundational concept was established by Babur to recreate the aesthetic of his homeland in Central Asia.

• Symbolism of Charbagh: The Charbagh layout is a terrestrial representation of the Islamic concept of Paradise (Jannat). According to the Quran, Paradise is a garden through which four rivers flow (water, milk, wine, and honey), which is mirrored by the four water channels in the garden design.

• Evolution under Akbar: During the reign of Akbar (1556-1605), the Charbagh style was integrated into funerary architecture. 15. Consider the following statements under FIDE chess rules: 1. A pawn may be promoted to a queen upon reaching the 8th rank, even if the original queen is still on the board. 2. Castling is allowed only if neither the king nor rook has moved, and the king doesn't pass through or into check. Which of the above statement is/are Correct?

A. Only 1 is Correct

B. Only 2 is Correct

C. 1 and 2 both are Correct

D. Neither 1 nor 2 are Correct

Answer C

Solution

The correct answer is 1 and 2 both are Correct. Key Points Additional Information Important Points

• According to the FIDE Laws of Chess, specifically Article 3.7.5.1, when a pawn reaches the rank furthest from its starting position (the 8th rank for White and the 1st rank for Black), it must be exchanged as part of the same move for a new queen, rook, bishop, or knight of the same color.

• The rules explicitly state that the choice of the piece is not restricted to pieces that have been previously captured. This means a player can theoretically have up to nine queens on the board at the same time if all eight pawns are promoted and the original queen remains. Hence, statement 1 is correct.

• Castling is a unique move in chess involving the king and either of the original rooks. Under FIDE Article 3.8.2, castling is only legal if neither the king nor the rook involved has moved previously in the game.

• Furthermore, the king cannot castle if it is currently in check, if the square it must pass through is under attack by an opponent’s piece, or if the square it lands on would result in the king being in check. This ensures the king never 'jumps' over or into danger during the maneuver. Hence, statement 2 is correct.

• Hence, the correct answer is 1 and 2 both are Correct.

• FIDE (International Chess Federation): Founded on July 20, 1924, in Paris, France, FIDE is the international organization that connects various national chess federations and acts as the governing body of international chess competition. Its headquarters are located in Lausanne, Switzerland.

• Promotion Mechanics: The promotion of a pawn is immediate. As soon as the pawn touches the promotion square, the player must replace it with the chosen piece. The new piece's powers are active instantly, meaning a promoted queen could deliver a check or checkmate on the very same move.

• Castling Variations: There are two types of castling: Kingside castling (also known as short castling) and Queenside castling (also known as long castling). In kingside, the king moves two squares toward the h-file, and in queenside, it moves two squares toward the c-file.

• En Passant: This is another special pawn capture rule. If a pawn moves two squares forward from its starting rank and lands directly adjacent to an opponent's pawn, the opponent may capture it as if it had only moved one square. This move is only legal on the immediate next turn.

• Check and Checkmate: A king is in check when it is under attack by at least one enemy piece. A checkmate occurs when the king is in check and there is no legal move to escape the attack, resulting in an immediate win for the attacking player.

• Stalemate: This occurs when the player whose turn it is has no legal moves and their king is not in check. Under FIDE rules, a stalemate results in an immediate draw.

• Touch-Move Rule: In competitive play, if a player touches one of their own pieces, they must move it if a legal move exists. If they touch an opponent's piece, they must capture it if legal.

• Draw Conditions: Aside from stalemate, a game can end in a draw through mutual agreement, threefold repetition of a position, the 50-move rule (no capture or pawn move in 50 consecutive moves), or insufficient material to force a checkmate.

• The primary contributors to the UHI effect include concrete structures, low vegetation, and vehicular heat, which trap and radiate heat, especially during nighttime.

• Concrete and asphalt surfaces, commonly used in urban construction, absorb and retain heat during the day, releasing it slowly at night, thereby increasing temperatures.

• Lack of vegetation in urban areas reduces natural cooling processes like transpiration and evaporation, further intensifying the heat.

• Cool Roofs: Using reflective materials for roofs to minimize heat absorption.

22. Under the BSA, what is the value of expert opinion in a trial?

B. Used only in civil trials

C. Treated as conclusive proof

D. Ignored by the court

Answer A

Solution

The correct answer is Advisory in nature, but holds persuasive value. Key Points Additional Information

• Under the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023, specifically Section 39 (which corresponds to Section 45 of the erstwhile Indian Evidence Act), the opinion of an expert is considered a relevant fact. However, it is fundamentally advisory in nature and does not bind the court.

• The primary role of an expert is to provide the judge with the necessary scientific or technical criteria for testing the accuracy of their conclusions, so as to enable the judge to form their own independent judgment by the application of these criteria to the facts proved in the case.

• Expert evidence is persuasive because it is based on specialized knowledge in fields like foreign law, science, art, handwriting, or finger impressions. While the court gives due weight to such opinions, it is not conclusive proof, meaning the court can reject the opinion if it finds it inconsistent with other evidence or if the reasons provided are not convincing. Hence, the correct answer is advisory in nature, but holds persuasive value.

• The court is often described as the 'expert of experts'. This means the final decision rests with the Judiciary, and the expert's testimony is treated as corroborative evidence rather than substantive evidence. It cannot be ignored by the court if it is relevant, but it also cannot be the sole basis for a conviction or decree without further scrutiny. Hence, statement 4 is incorrect.

• The value of expert opinion is the same in both civil and criminal trials. In criminal cases, expert opinions from forensic scientists or medical examiners are crucial but must still be weighed against other direct or circumstantial evidence. Hence, statement 2 is incorrect.

• The Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023 replaced the Indian Evidence Act of 1872 to modernize the rules of evidence in India. It was passed by the Parliament of India and came into force on July 1, 2024.

• Section 39 of the BSA defines an expert as a person who is especially skilled in a particular field. The scope of expert opinion under the BSA has been maintained to ensure continuity in legal interpretation while aligning with modern technological advancements.

• Section 40 of the BSA mentions that facts which support or are inconsistent with the opinions of experts are also relevant. This allows the court to test the credibility and consistency of the expert's opinion against the broader context of the case.

• The Supreme Court of India has consistently held that expert opinion is a 'weak' form of evidence. In the landmark case of State of Himachal Pradesh v. Jai Lal, it was emphasized that the expert must provide reasons for their opinion, and a mere assertion without supporting data is of little value to the trial.
