67. Two articles are sold for Rs. 10,005 each. On one, the seller gains 15% and on the other, he loses 13%. What is his overall gain or loss percent. Correct to two decimal places?

D. 0.94% loss

Answer D

Solution

Shortcut Trick When Selling Price (SP) is same for two articles with gain x% and loss y%: Overall % = [100(x − y) − 2xy] ÷ [200 + x − y] Given: x = 15, y = 13 Overall % = [100(15 − 13) − 2(15)(13)] ÷ [200 + 15 − 13] = [200 − 390] ÷ 202 = −190 ÷ 202 ≈ −0.94% ∴ The correct answer is 0.94% loss. Alternate Method Given: Selling Price of each article = ₹10,005 | Gain on 1st = 15% | Loss on 2nd = 13% Formula: Cost Price (CP) = [SP ÷ (100 ± Profit/Loss%)] × 100 ⇒ CP of Article 1 = 10,005 ÷ 1.15 = ₹8,700 ⇒ CP of Article 2 = 10,005 ÷ 0.87 = ₹11,500 ⇒ Total CP = 8,700 + 11,500 = ₹20,200 ⇒ Total SP = 10,005 + 10,005 = ₹20,010 ⇒ Overall Loss = 20,200 − 20,010 = ₹190 ⇒ Loss % = (190 ÷ 20,200) × 100 = 190 ÷ 202 ≈ 0.9405% ∴ The correct answer is 0.94% loss. Successive Percentage Formula For two successive changes a% and b%, the effective change is [a + b + (ab/100)]% (not directly applicable here as base CP is different). Ratio Method Convert percentage to fractions (15% = 3/20, 13% = 13/100) and equate the SP units to find the combined CP and SP ratio.

85. Select the correct passive form of the given sentence. The sailors had never encountered such a rough sea.

Answer B

Solution

The correct answer is Such a rough sea had never been encountered by the sailors. Key Points Hence, the correct answer is option 2.

• In Active Voice, a sentence emphasizes the subject, performing an action.

• In Passive Voice, a sentence emphasizes the action or the object of the sentence.

• The given sentence is in the active voice and 'The sailors' is the subject and 'such a rough sea' is the object.

• When we convert this sentence into passive voice, the subject 'The sailors' of the active voice becomes the object, the object 'such a rough sea' becomes the subject.

• The passive format "had + been + V3 (encountered)" should be used.

• This is the active and passive voice rule for the past perfect tense.
